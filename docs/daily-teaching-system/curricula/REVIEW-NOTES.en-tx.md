# Reviewers' notes: registry `en-tx`

Written by the reviewers of the crosswalk (`scripts/curricula/apply-review.js`). 306 teaching points carry a reviewer's row; the reason for each is in `frontend/content/curricula/en-tx/crosswalk-authored.json`.

## author-math

- Texas places these skills in a LATER grade than the worksheets sit: odd and even by pairing (worksheets K and grade 1, Texas 2.7.A), composing shapes from pieces (K, Texas 1.6.F), number words (grade 1, Texas 2.2.B), number lines (grade 1, Texas 2.2.E/2.2.F), two-digit mental addition and subtraction and bonds to 100 (grade 1, Texas 2.4.B), rounding (grade 2, Texas 3.2.C), grams and milliliters (grade 2, Texas 3.7.E), arrays (grade 2, Texas 3.4.D/3.4.E), dot plots, which Texas does not call line plots (grade 2, Texas 3.8), symmetry (grades 2-3, Texas 4.6.B), right angles (grade 3, Texas 4.6), division with remainders (grade 3, Texas 4.4.H), a dot plot marked in halves (grade 3, Texas 4.9.A) and volume by counting cubes (grade 3, Texas 5.6.A).
- Texas places these skills in an EARLIER grade than the worksheets sit: time to the minute (worksheets grade 3, Texas 2.9.G with a.m. and p.m.), flat or solid and solids in real life (grade 2, Texas K.6.B/K.6.E and 1.6.E), bundling ten ones (grade 2, Texas 1.2.B), skip counting by fives and tens (grade 2, Texas 1.5.B), the equal sign as balance (grade 3, Texas 1.5.E/1.5.F), and area by covering with square units, which Texas starts in grade 2 (2.9.F) and no grade 2 teaching point covers.
- Skills on the site that the Texas mathematics wording does not contain: position words, repeating picture patterns, ordinal numbers, days, months, seasons and the calendar, 24-hour time, temperature, ordering three objects by length, and time to five minutes or the quarter hour as a separate step (Texas goes from the half hour in grade 1 to the minute in grade 2).
- Personal financial literacy is a full strand in every Texas grade (K.9, 1.9, 2.11, 3.9: earning income, wants and needs, spending and saving, giving, deposits and withdrawals, borrowing and lending, credit) and no teaching point covers it; K-3 teachers in Texas will look for it.
- Money in Texas starts earlier and is more detailed than the site's two points: identifying coins by name in kindergarten (K.4), coin values and the cent symbol in grade 1 (1.4.A, 1.4.B), the dollar sign and decimal point in grade 2 (2.5.B) and collections of coins and bills in grade 3 (3.4.C). Only 1.4.A, 1.4.C and 2.5.A are met.
- Texas number ranges are wider than the usual ones: grade 1 works to 120 (1.2, 1.5), grade 2 to 1,200 (2.2, 2.7.B) and grade 3 to 100,000 (3.2.A, 3.2.B, 3.2.D). Sheets that stop at 100, 1,000 and four digits cover only part of each range, and nothing covers place value to 100,000.
- Texas fraction content with no teaching point: eighths in grade 2 (2.3.A, 2.3.D), halves and fourths already in grade 1 (1.6.G, 1.6.H), the more parts the smaller the part (2.3.B) and counting fractional parts beyond one whole (2.3.C). Thirds, which the grade 2 sheets use, are not named by Texas until grade 3.
- Other Texas expectations for K-3 that no teaching point reaches: comparing two written numerals in kindergarten (K.2.H), reciting to 100 by tens (K.5), two-dimensional faces of solids (K.6.C), measuring the same length with two different units and the inverse relationship (1.7.C, 2.9.B), writing a problem situation for a number sentence (1.3.F, 2.4.D), adding up to four two-digit numbers (2.4.B), estimating lengths (2.9.E), a two-digit number times a one-digit number (3.4.G), multiplication as a comparison (3.5.C), area of composite figures (3.6.D) and a missing side from a given perimeter (3.7.B).
- Skill lines too vague to place exactly: K:fact-fluency-colour-code (three ranges in one point), 1:number-walls, 1:check-answers and 3:magic-squares (no number range), 3:balance-equations (operations and range not given), 2:shopping-money (not clear whether coins are counted or how prices are written, so the coin expectations 2.5.A and 2.5.B were not claimed), 2:choose-units (mapped to the strand statement 3.7) and 3:two-attribute-sort (not clear what is sorted; if the objects are shapes, 5.5 would also be a readiness target).
- Four rows use a strand id because no single expectation fits: 2.2 for counting on a chart within 1,000, 3.5 for continuing number patterns, 3.7 for choosing a unit, and 5.8 for letter-number grid codes.

## author-elar-science

- Texas states grammar only inside the editing expectation (K.10.D, 1.11.D, 2.11.D, 3.11.D: 'edit drafts using standard English conventions, including ...'). Rows marked 'teaches' on those parts mean the worksheet practices the named convention; none of the worksheets has a child edit a draft.
- Skills Texas places in a LOWER grade than the worksheets: syllable clapping, splitting and blending (kindergarten only; grade 1 has no syllable-counting line), decoding digraphs (grade 1, the grade 2 digraph pages sit one grade high), naming material (kindergarten), the five senses as body structures (kindergarten), the meaning of -ed and -ing words (grade 1, used in a grade 3 point), plant parts (kindergarten and grade 2, nothing in grade 1).
- Skills Texas places in a HIGHER grade than the worksheets: antonyms and synonyms (first named in grade 2), living and nonliving (grade 1), young animals and their parents (grade 1), the order of the seasons (grade 1), natural and manmade (grade 2), recycling (grade 2 and 3), butterfly and frog life cycles (grade 2), sink or float (grade 3), prefixes dis-, in-, pre- (grade 3) and mis- (grade 4), irregular plurals and irregular past tense (grade 4), the water cycle (grade 4), the Moon's changing appearance (grade 4), day and night from Earth's rotation (grade 5).
- Places where Texas is ahead of or differs from the Common Core / NGSS hint: alphabetizing words to the first or second letter is grade 1; cursive is required from grade 2 (letters and joins) and grade 3 (words and answers); the order of the planets is a grade 3 science expectation; articles are a kindergarten convention.
- Teaching points with no real Texas expectation behind them: question words (grades 1 and 2), shades of meaning, naming a root word, reading cursive, the names of the Moon's phases, the parts of a flower, animal class names (mammal, bird, reptile). They are mapped as 'prepares' or 'extends' only.
- Texas ELAR content that K-3 teachers will look for and no teaching point covers: blending onset and rime, adding, changing or deleting a phoneme or a letter to make a new word, long and short vowels, VCe words, vowel teams and r-controlled vowels as their own units, contractions, abbreviations, spelling high-frequency words, homophones and homographs, idioms, dictionary and glossary use, prepositions, adverbs, proper nouns, capital letters for months and days, commas in a series and in dates, apostrophes, future tense, the genres (folktales, fables, poetry, drama, persuasive text), central idea and text features of informational text, author's purpose, facts versus opinion, and composing poetry, procedural texts and reports.
- Texas science content that K-3 teachers will look for and no teaching point covers: magnets (K), light and shadows (K), rocks and uses of rocks, soil and water (K), wind as moving air (K), heating and cooling and reversible changes (grade 1), pushes and pulls (grades 1 to 3), soils and bodies of water (grade 1), water conservation (grade 1), food chains (grades 1 to 3), solids and liquids and states of matter (grades 2 and 3), sound (grade 2), severe weather and graphing weather (grade 2), pollination and seed dispersal (grade 2), forms of energy, soil formation, rapid changes to Earth's surface, fossils and natural resources (grade 3), and the named scientists and engineers of each grade.
- Skill lines too vague to place firmly: K:sound-position (heard only or written with letters), K:word-picture-match (which words), 1:root-words (which endings), 1:sun-earth-moon (which facts), 2:compare-two-animals (which facts), K:living-nonliving and K:natural-made (whether a reason is asked). Each was judged by the grade of the worksheets and given the weaker relation.
- Nineteen points of social studies, health and art have no rows because Texas holds them in TEKS chapters this registry does not contain (social studies Chapter 113, health Chapter 115, fine arts Chapter 117); body parts and the five senses are also mainly health content in Texas, so their science rows are thin.
- Bilingual word cards belong to Texas's English Language Proficiency Standards and bilingual program rules rather than to ELAR; only their picture-and-word vocabulary use is mapped.
