## 1. The pitch
"A real printer's type case: the capitals live in the UPPER drawer and the small letters in the LOWER drawer — put the right small letter in the slot under its capital, and every letter you drop shows its own other form on its back."

## 2. Why a child will want it again (the fun)
- **The type case is a treasure drawer.** Two wooden drawers of little compartments, chunky metal letters (sorts) that clink when you pick them up, a composing tray. It feels like a grown-up tool.
- **Flip it and see.** Every sort is two-sided: the small letter on its face, its capital on its back. Long-press a sort and it flips with a metallic "tink" — in free play (and as a help after a miss) the child never tires of it ("what's on the back of g?").
- **The press.** When the pair in the column is right, the child pulls the press lever: an ink roller rolls across (a soft "shhhk"), the platen comes down with a "thunk", and a fresh print of the pair (`Bb`) slides out onto the alphabet poster in its place.
- **The poster fills.** Across a pass, the poster on the wall — the whole alphabet of the page's language in faint outline — fills with the child's own prints. At the end of the pass the poster is held up with a little "ta-da" fold.
- **Free play ("Print shop"):** pick any sort from the case, flip it, set a word in the tray and print it.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (RF.K.1.d):** recognise and name all upper- and lowercase letters — know that B and b, A and a, G and g are the same letter. The worksheets add finding every copy of a letter among look-alikes (K-229) and finding and counting a letter inside words (K-327).
- **Misconception 1 — "the small letter is just the big letter made smaller."** True for c/C, o/O, s/S, v/V, w/W, x/X, z/Z — and false for a/A, b/B, d/D, e/E, g/G, q/Q, r/R… **Push back:** a wrong sort dropped in the lower slot tips over and shows the capital on its back — B above, D below — two different capitals, so the child sees the piece belongs to another letter. A shrinking trick never works: the case has no "make smaller" — each pair is learned.
- **Misconception 2 — b/d/p/q are one letter facing different ways.** **Push back:** each of them is a separate sort with a different capital on its back (B, D, P, Q). Flipping b shows B; flipping d shows D. The mirror-twins are revealed as strangers.
- **Misconception 3 — I / l / 1 confusion.** The case is set in a literacy typeface in which capital I has bars, small l has a tail and 1 has a flag, so the look-alike rounds are fair.

## 5. The hand
- **Tap / drag a tray sort** into the empty compartment of the lit column (or tap the sort: it flies there, 240 ms). Within 40 ms it lifts with a "clink"; it drops in with a wooden "tock".
  - It **fits** (right letter): it settles flat; the column's brass frame glows; the press unlocks.
  - It **does not belong** (wrong letter): it settles, then tips over onto its back (280 ms, "tink") showing its other form — so the column reads B over D — holds 1.2 s, and slides back to its tray place face up. (This is the miss; see §7.)
- **Flip a sort** (a long-press, or a tap on the small turn-arrow at its corner): it turns over to show its back (240 ms) and turns back on the next press. In free play flipping is always allowed. In MATCH, LOOK-ALIKES and IN WORDS rounds the sorts are latched face-up until the first miss, because flipping them would let a child match by the back instead of recognising the front; after miss 1 the latch opens and flipping becomes a tool (every flip is the child checking a pair — still case-pair learning).
- **Pull the press** (drag the lever down or tap Check): the column's pair slides onto the press bed (300 ms), the roller rolls (350 ms, "shhhk"), the platen thunks, and the print (`Bb`, capital first, in ink) flies to its place on the poster (500 ms). The case resets for the next round.
- **LOOK-ALIKES:** tap each sort on the stick that is the target letter (in either form if the round says so): every tapped sort — right or not — rises 8 px and gets a brass underline (nothing is judged until the press). Tap again to un-mark. Check (press) prints every marked sort.
- **IN WORDS:** tap each copy of the target letter in the words (the sort rises and gets a brass underline); a counter row of empty dots under the stick (one dot per mark) shows how many are marked — the count chip asks the child to set the number (1–6 keys, 48 px) before pressing.
- **Free play ("Print shop"):** the whole case opens (all compartments of the locale's alphabet, scrolling), drag any sorts to the stick, flip them, print a strip.

LOOK-ALIKES: the stick is re-dealt from the round's set every pass; target copies are never all in the first or last half (gate: each half holds ≥ 1 copy), never in alternating positions throughout, and slot 1 holds a target in ≤ half of a level's rounds (gate over 10 000 deals: each slot holds a target with frequency = count/8 ± 3 %). IN WORDS: counts vary as above, so "always 3" fails.

**Order:** order-only reshuffle per pass; never the same order twice; in Big and small, no more than two same-direction rounds in a row.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check / press with nothing placed:** no miss; the empty compartment pulses; **"Put a letter in the empty slot."** (LOOK-ALIKES/IN WORDS with nothing marked: **"Tap every {letter}."**)
- **Miss 1 — MATCH, wrong sort dropped:** the sort tips onto its back and shows its own capital (or small letter) in the column (§5) — the two forms in the column are visibly different letters. Bubble: **"That one is {letter}, not {target}."** (letter names are written as the letters themselves, e.g. "That one is d, not b.")
- **Miss 2 — MATCH:** the given sort in the column flips over and stays flipped, showing its own other form (B's back shows b), so the child now looks for that exact shape among the look-alikes in the tray. Bubble: **"This is what {target} looks like on the back. Find it in the tray."**
- **Miss 1 — LOOK-ALIKES / IN WORDS (Check with a wrong set):** every wrongly marked sort flips to show its back (its own other form), and the number of copies still unmarked appears as that many hollow dots under the stick. **"{k} more to find."** / **"One of these is not {target}."** IN WORDS with the count wrong: the marked letters number themselves 1, 2, 3 … under the stick. **"Count the letters you found."**
- **Miss 2 — LOOK-ALIKES / IN WORDS:** the target sort in the case turns so its face and back are both visible (both forms side by side), and the stick's unmarked copies get a faint shimmer one after another, left to right, once (a scan path, not a pointer at each). **"Look for both forms: {Target} and {target}."**
- **A new move takes the help away** until the next Check.
- **Success:** the press prints; bubble: MATCH **"B and b — the same letter!"** · LOOK-ALIKES **"You found every b — 3 of them!"** · IN WORDS **"4 a's in the words!"** Pass end: the poster holds up its prints with a little fold: **"Look at all the letters you printed!"**

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — metal letters that clink, flip over to reveal a secret on the back, and a press that thunks out a print onto their own alphabet poster.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — it shows why we SAY "uppercase" and "lowercase", and every wrong pick turns over to show which letter it really belongs to; the look-alike and in-words rounds are exactly K-229 and K-327.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Calm, real letters in a proper school typeface, every language's own letters, no score.
4. **The invention:** a printer's type case where capitals live in the upper drawer and small letters in the lower, and every metal letter carries its other form on its back — so a wrong match flips over and shows whose letter it really is.
