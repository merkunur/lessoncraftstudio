## The skill
A right angle is a square corner; other angles are smaller or bigger than it.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE CORNER CARD: a stiff paper card with one square corner. The child drags it onto any corner of a shape or a picture; it snaps vertex to vertex and rotates to align one side. If the angle is right, the card fits flush and glows; if smaller, the shape's side hides under the card; if bigger, a wedge of light shows between card and side. The child taps every right angle (the 'hunt'), then sorts angles into smaller / right / bigger trays.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Judging angles by the length of their arms or by orientation (a right angle not drawn upright 'isn't one').
- **Pictures:** none (shapes drawn tool-grade); optional library objects with clear corners (book, window, envelope) on a later setting
- **Settings:** Angles: right vs not / smaller-right-bigger sort · Orientation: upright / any turn
- **Rounds:** Generated shapes with 0-4 right angles, ≥ 9 rounds, right-angle count per shape balanced, arms of varied length so length is not a tell.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-341
- **Distinct from:** No angle activity exists; not the shape sieve (which uses square corners only as one test).
- **Build risk:** Low.
