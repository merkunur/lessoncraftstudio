Date: 2026-11-14. Written for the owner of LessonCraftStudio, who delegated this decision.

Who wrote this: an AI agent acting as data-protection counsel. I am not a licensed lawyer and this is not a lawyer's opinion. Where a conclusion is a firm reading of the law I say "firm"; where it depends on a fact I could not check, or on a point the authorities have not settled, I say so.

## A. The decision

**GO for a limited scope, after the required changes in section E. As built today: NO-GO.**

1. **What may be switched on:** class links and results for **numbers-only classes** (a seat is a number and an animal picture; no child's name reaches the server), in all eleven languages and in every country, for any teacher with a usable Teacher plan who ticks the confirmation in E2. No school signature is needed for this mode.
2. **What stays off at launch:** **first-name classes, for everyone.** The server must refuse them, not only hide them (E1).
3. **When first names may be opened later, account by account:** (a) for a school, after a person who may sign for the school has accepted the school agreement of section D.3; or (b) outside the United States, for a private tutor or a home-educating parent who declares that she alone decides (she is the controller herself). Never on a school teacher's own word.
4. **Why not "as built":** the code defaults to first names; the pages a child plays call Google (fonts) and run the site's analytics beacon, and activity pages can load the Pinterest advertising tag; the privacy texts say things the code does not do (section "Document versus code" below). Each of these has a small, exact fix in E.

**Residual risks I accept, and why:**

- A school authority may take the view that results under a seat number are still pupil data that needs its approval. That is correct from the school's side (the teacher holds the key), so the teacher confirms her school allows it (E2) and the processing terms are on offer to every school. The site itself holds nothing that names a child. Acceptable.
- A teacher may type a child's name into the class name. The form says not to (E11), the terms forbid it, and the field is short. Acceptable.
- Illinois, New York and Connecticut require a signed agreement with the district before an operator receives identifiable student data. Numbers-only data is, on a reasonable reading, not identifiable to the operator; a district could argue otherwise. Acceptable for numbers-only; **not** acceptable for first names, which is why they stay off in US schools until the district has signed.
- A child's device address (IP) passes through the server and Cloudflare, as for every visitor of any website. Lawful for delivery and security (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f); COPPA "support for internal operations", 16 CFR 312.5(c)(7)) once E3 and E4 are done.

**Risks I do not accept:** children's first names on the server on an individual teacher's say-so; any third-party request or analytics on a page a child reaches through a class link; a privacy text that promises more than the code does.

**This decision depends on one fact I could not check:** the country in which the company is established (F2). If it is outside the EU/EEA and not in a country with an EU adequacy decision, the numbers-only launch still stands, but an EU representative (GDPR Art. 27) is needed for the site as a whole, and first names for EU schools will additionally need the EU standard contractual clauses in the school agreement.

## B. The eight questions

**1. Roles: is "school = controller, LessonCraftStudio = processor" right for a teacher who subscribes privately and uses it in a public school?**
The model is right; the phrase "or the individual teacher" is wrong for a school teacher. Firm: a teacher who processes pupils' data in her job acts for the body that runs the school; that body is the controller (GDPR Art. 4(7)), and only it, or someone it has authorised, can conclude the Art. 28(3) contract. Who that is: Germany, the school (represented by its head) under the Land's school law; France, the school's authority (DASEN or the head of a secondary school; for primary schools the academic authority); Netherlands, the school board (bevoegd gezag); Italy, the istituto (dirigente scolastico); Sweden, the huvudman (for municipal schools the responsible municipal board); Denmark and Norway, the municipality; Finland, the education provider (usually the municipality). A teacher who pays herself does not become the controller by paying. Consequence: see C. A private tutor or a home-educating parent is the controller herself (a parent with only her own children falls outside the GDPR, Art. 2(2)(c)).

**2. Lawful basis: is a parent notice enough, or is consent required somewhere?**
For numbers-only classes: a notice is enough in all eleven markets, and the notice is the school's duty (Art. 13), which the parent note supports. The school's basis is its public teaching task (Art. 6(1)(e) with national school law) or, for a private school, Art. 6(1)(f) or the school contract. We must never collect consent from a child, and consent is not a valid basis for a public school in most of these countries (imbalance of power, Recital 43). For first-name classes: still no consent where the school has formally adopted the tool; but in several German Länder a tool that is not adopted by the school leadership may be used with identifiable pupil data only with the parents' consent, and the school decides that. This is one reason first names wait for the school's own acceptance.

**3. United States: inside COPPA and FERPA as built? Do state laws require a signed agreement?**
As built: no, because of the fonts, the beacon and the Pinterest tag (COPPA: a persistent identifier may go without parental consent only for the operator's internal operations, 16 CFR 312.5(c)(7); an advertising tag is never that). After E, numbers-only: yes. What is then collected from a child is taps, results under a seat number, and the IP address for delivery; none of it is "personal information" beyond the identifier, and the identifier is used for internal operations only, so neither parental consent nor the school-consent route is needed. The 2025 amended rule (in force 23 June 2025, compliance by 22 April 2026) still requires: a notice that names the internal operations and says the identifier is not used to contact or profile a child, a written retention policy in the notice with no indefinite retention (312.10), and a link to the notice from the children's page (312.4(d)). These are in D.1 and E5, E9. FERPA binds the school, not us; the agreement in D.3 carries the "school official" terms (34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)). First names: yes, a signed agreement with the district is required before we receive them in Illinois (SOPPA, 105 ILCS 85/15), New York (Education Law 2-d and 8 NYCRR Part 121, which also covers click-through terms) and Connecticut (Gen. Stat. 10-234bb); California puts required contract terms on the district (Education Code 49073.1) and direct duties on us (SOPIPA, Bus. & Prof. Code 22584: no targeted advertising, no profile except for school purposes, no sale, security, deletion on request). The FTC's school-consent guidance survives the 2025 rule but was not written into it, and the FTC advises that the school or district, not the single teacher, should decide. So: US first names only after the district has signed.

**4. Brazil and Mexico: first name + results for a school without separate parental consent?**
Brazil: possible in law. LGPD Art. 14 §1 names parental consent, but the authority's Enunciado CD/ANPD 1/2023 allows any basis of Art. 7 if the child's best interest prevails; a school's teaching task qualifies. The school must inform parents, and a transfer of names to a server abroad needs a transfer mechanism (LGPD Art. 33; ANPD Resolution 19/2024). Brazil's 2025 law on children online (Lei 15.211/2025) adds privacy-by-default and no-profiling duties for services children use; numbers-only meets them. Mexico: the 2025 federal law (in force 21 March 2025) requires the school's privacy notice and, for a minor, the consent of the parent through the school; public schools fall under the separate law for public bodies. Decision: numbers-only in both countries at launch; first names only with the school's acceptance. (Overview level, as asked.)

**5. Sub-processors and transfers: does Cloudflare need more than the standard clauses?**
No more is legally required, but the agreement must say exactly what applies. Cloudflare, Inc. terminates the encrypted connection, so class data passes through it readable. It is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (adequacy decision of 10 July 2023, upheld by the General Court on 3 September 2025, appeal pending), and its data processing addendum, which is part of the self-serve agreement, contains the EU standard contractual clauses as fallback. Name both. In a numbers-only class nothing that names a child passes through at all. Hetzner: conclude Hetzner's own data processing agreement in the account if not done (F4); no transfer. Google must not be a recipient at all (E3).

**6. Retention: 14 months unused, 60 days after the plan ends, deletion on request. Acceptable?**
Yes, with one addition. Today a class that stays in use keeps every old result for ever, because the clock is "unused". Storage limitation (GDPR Art. 5(1)(e)) and the amended COPPA rule (312.10) both exclude open-ended keeping, and German school rules expect deletion around the end of the school year. Required: every single result is deleted at the latest 427 days after it was made, even in an active class (E5). Recommended: a reminder at the end of the school year with one button "clear all results".

**7. Age: is an age statement missing?**
No age check of children is needed anywhere: GDPR Art. 8 applies only where consent is the basis and the service is offered to the child directly; here neither is the case. Missing and required: one sentence that accounts are for adults (18 or older) and that children never make an account; the US notice of D.1; and the link from the children's page to the privacy section (E9). The UK children's code is met by the design (no profiling for own purposes, no nudges, high privacy by default) once numbers-only is the default.

**8. Company details.**
The owner fills them in (F1 to F3). Without a legal name, an address and a privacy contact, neither the privacy section (GDPR Art. 13(1)(a); COPPA 312.4(d)(1)) nor the agreement is valid. This blocks the switch.

## C. The roles, and who may switch the feature on

| Setting | Controller | LessonCraftStudio is | Contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| A teacher in a school (public or private), EU/EEA/UK | The school or its authority (B.1). Never the teacher personally | Processor for class data. Controller of its own for delivery, security logs and the teacher's account | Art. 28(3) agreement with the school. The teacher cannot sign it unless the school has authorised her |
| A private tutor, a home-educating parent | The tutor herself; a parent with only her own children is outside the GDPR | Processor (tutor) | The tutor accepts the agreement herself |
| A US public school | The school or district ("educational agency") | "School official" under FERPA; "operator" under COPPA and the state laws, with direct duties | District-signed agreement where state law requires it (IL, NY, CT; contract terms in CA) |

**May an individual teacher switch the feature on without her school having signed anything?**

- **Numbers-only: yes.** Reason: the site receives a seat number, a picture and marks. It has no means reasonably likely to be used to find out which child sits on seat 7 (GDPR Recital 26; CJEU, EDPS v SRB, C-413/23 P, 4 September 2025: whether pseudonymised data is personal depends on whether the recipient can re-identify). For the school it is still pupil data, because the teacher holds the list; so the teacher confirms her school allows such tools (E2), the processing terms apply to every class automatically, and a school can have them countersigned on request. This is the accepted way such tools are used in German, Swedish and Finnish schools, and under COPPA it needs no consent at all (B.3). Firm on the mechanics; the SRB reading for a processor is persuasive, not settled, which is why the terms still apply.
- **First names in a school: no.** A first name with results on an external server is pupil data for everyone. Without an agreement that binds the school, we would process it without the contract Art. 28(3) demands, and the fine risk sits with us as well as with the school (Art. 83(4)(a)). In Illinois, New York and Connecticut we would break the state law directly. A tick-box in which the teacher says "my school agrees" does not create a contract with the school.
- **First names for a tutor or home educator outside the US: yes, on her own declaration**, once the unlock exists. In the US a tutor is not a school, so the school-consent route under COPPA does not apply; keep numbers-only there.
