# Swedish pronoun deck (`pronoun.case.l-1-1-d`) — linguist ruling + defect audit

## VERDICT IN ONE LINE

**The German/Dutch frame does not transfer. Six of the nine rounds, transliterated,
would be dead** — Swedish has a two-form pronoun system that native children acquire
before school, so `*Mig ritar en katt`, `*Oss bygger en koja`, `*Här är mig hatt` are
distractors no Swedish child would ever pick. The Swedish deck must be rebuilt on the
two axes where Swedish children *do* err in writing: **object `honom`/`henne` vs
dialectal subject-form `han`/`hon`**, and **possessive agreement `min`/`mitt`/`mina`**.

---

## 1. Which contrasts are ALIVE in written Swedish at åk 2

| Pair | Native child errs in writing? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `jag`/`mig` (simple clause) | No | **DEAD** distractor |
| `du`/`dig` (simple clause) | No | **DEAD** |
| `vi`/`oss` | No | **DEAD** |
| `ni`/`er` | No | **DEAD** |
| `han`/`honom` | **YES** — colloquial/dialectal object `han` (`jag såg han`, `till han`) is widely attested | **ALIVE — the best round in the deck** |
| `hon`/`henne` | Yes, less frequent than `han` | **ALIVE** (weaker; prefer a preposition frame) |
| `de`/`dem` | Yes, universally — but see §2 | **ALIVE but WRONG GRADE** |
| `jag`/`mig` in a **compound** (`Otto och ___`) | Yes, attested both directions | **ALIVE** — the deck's hardest item |
| `min`/`mitt`/`mina` agreement | Yes — bound to en/ett, and *very* live for the platform's multilingual audience | **ALIVE — strongest åk-2 axis** |
| `sin`/`hans` | Yes, strongly — but see §4 | **ALIVE, WRONG ACTIVITY** |

Orthographic sibling, not a case point: **`mej`/`mig`, `dej`/`dig`, `sej`/`sig`** is a
genuine lågstadiet *spelling* issue. It belongs in a spelling deck, not here.

## 2. `de` / `dem` — EXCLUDE

Both are spoken **`dom`** across nearly all of Sweden, so there is no ear cue at all.
Swedish schools treat this at **mellanstadiet (åk 4-6) and högstadiet**, not lågstadiet.
`dom` is listed in SAOL as a talspråklig pronoun and is **printed in a great many
Swedish children's books and lättläst titles** — so an åk-2 child has legitimately *read*
`dom` and has no basis to choose between `de` and `dem`.

**Including it would force the deck's grade chip to åk 4+.** Keep it out; it is its own
mellanstadiet activity.

## 3. `min`/`mitt`/`mina` — the agreement rule

Agreement is with the **possessed noun**, never the possessor:

- **en-word sg** → `min bok`, `din boll`, `min hatt`, `din jacka`
- **ett-word sg** → `mitt hus`, `ditt bord`, `ditt suddgummi`
- **plural (both genders)** → `mina skor`, `dina vantar`, `mina böcker`

Same paradigm: `min/mitt/mina`, `din/ditt/dina`, `sin/sitt/sina`, `vår/vårt/våra`,
`er/ert/era`. **Invariable** (never inflect): `hans`, `hennes`, `dess`, `deras`, `hens`.

This is a genuine åk 2-3 written-Swedish teaching point because it is welded to the
en/ett judgement, which *is* explicitly lågstadiet content — and it is the single
highest-value round type for a dual-language/immersion audience, where `*min hus` is
routine.

## 4. `sin/sitt/sina` vs `hans/hennes/deras` — RULING: EXCLUDE, build separately

Confirmed: a genuine Scandinavian difficulty with **no English equivalent**.
`Erik tog sin hatt` (his own) vs `Erik tog hans hatt` (someone else's). Reflexive
possessive binds to the subject of its own clause and cannot itself be in subject
position (`*Sin hatt är röd`).

It is within the *letter* of L.1.1.d ("possessive pronouns"), but **it is a different
cognitive operation** — coreference/binding, not case-slot fitting. Decisive engineering
reason: the engine grades by `role` and fires one of three notes; a `sin`/`hans` round is
`possessive` on **both** chips, so the child would be told *"det visar vems det är"* —
true of both options, and therefore not the reason. **The feedback would be actively
wrong.** Its natural placement is åk 3-4.

**Its own activity, with a two-referent picture and the question `vems?`.**

## 5. Compound phrases — ONE round, and it is the object one

`Sam och jag byggde` / `på Sam och mig` are both correct. The hypercorrect
`*på Lova och jag` **is** attested in Swedish children's writing, and `*Otto och mig
byggde` occurs too — but both are markedly rarer than the English equivalents, because
Swedish prepositions govern the object form very strongly.

Worth **one** round, not the German deck's pair. Keep both directions only if you want the
subject side represented at all (see the proposed deck — I keep one of each, because the
compound is the *only* place a Swedish subject-position error genuinely lives).

## 6. Audit of the proposed frames

| Frame | Swedish quality | Round verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `___ springer till boet.` han/honom | `boet` = a bird's **nest**; a hare has no *bo*. Semantically off. | **DEAD** (`*Honom springer` is impossible in every dialect) + fix the noun |
| `___ ritar en katt.` jag/mig | Natural, lovely child Swedish | **DEAD** distractor |
| `___ bygger en koja.` vi/oss | Natural (`koja` is a perfect child word) | **DEAD** distractor |
| `Pat såg ___ i parken.` honom/han | Natural — **but `Pat` is not Swedish** | **ALIVE — the deck's best round.** Rename to `Ella` |
| `Mamma ropade på ___.` mig/jag | Natural | Dead **bare**; **ALIVE as a compound** |
| `Hunden hittade ___ i trädgården.` oss/vi | Natural | **DEAD** distractor |
| `Det är ___ boll.` hans/honom | `*Det är honom boll` impossible | **DEAD.** Replace with `min`/`mitt` |
| `Här är ___ hatt.` min/mig | Natural + on-theme | Dead as min/**mig**; **ALIVE as min/mitt** |
| `Det är ___ jacka.` din/dig | Natural | Dead as din/**dig**; **ALIVE as din/ditt** |

**Names.** `Pat` is undecodable and reads as an abbreviation. Åk 1-2 decodable Swedish
names (short, regular orthography, no `sj/stj/tj/ck` clusters): **Ella, Alva, Maja, Nova,
Lova, Saga, Vera · Otto, Milo, Vide, Loke, Kalle, Pelle.** Swedish grandparent terms are
specific, so `Grandpa` must be a deliberate choice: **`Farfar`** or **`Morfar`**.

## 7. The preposition trap

`på, till, med, hos, åt, av, för, om, i, efter` govern the object form — exceptionless,
and a rule Swedish teachers state out loud.

- For `jag/mig`, `vi/oss`, `du/dig` it makes a dead round **doubly** dead. Do not use.
- For `han/honom` it is **the single best frame in the deck**, because `till han` /
  `på han` / `med han` is exactly where the attested colloquial error lives, and
  *"efter en preposition kommer objektsformen"* is the transferable rule.
- Ship **one preposition-cued** (scaffold) and **one bare-object** (transfer) round.

⚠ Do not count on the ear: `speak()` in `lcs-shell.js` never calls `getVoices()`, so on a
machine without a Swedish voice the sentence is read by the default (English) voice. The
deck must be solvable with the sound off.

## PROPOSED SWEDISH DECK — 9 live rounds

All blanks are mid-sentence, so **capitalisation never leaks the answer**. No round
contains its own answer as a token.

```
OBJECT (4)
1  Ella såg ___ i parken.            honom / han     bare object — hardest
2  Vi cyklade hem till ___.          honom / han     preposition cue (scaffold)
3  Otto ritade ___ på tavlan.        henne / hon     bare object
   (stronger alternative: Boken är till ___.  henne / hon)
4  Mamma ropade på Lova och ___.     mig / jag       compound, hypercorrection

SUBJECT (1)
5  Otto och ___ byggde en koja.      jag / mig       compound

POSSESSIVE — AGREEMENT (4)
6  Här är ___ hatt.                  min / mitt      en hatt   (on-theme)
7  Det är ___ hus.                   mitt / min      ett hus
8  Var är ___ skor?                  mina / min      plural
9  Har du ___ suddgummi?             ditt / din      ett suddgummi
```

**Deliberate design in 6-8:** `min` and `mitt` each appear as *correct* in one round and
as *wrong* in another. This proves to the child that neither chip is "the right word" —
the right word depends on the noun. That is the whole teaching point, made structural.

### `L.sv` strings — and they must NOT copy the German phrasing

```js
sv: {
  q: 'Vilket ord passar i meningen?',
  win: 'Ja! {note}', hear: '🔊 Lyssna',
  noteSubject:    'Här är det någon som GÖR något!',
  noteObject:     'Här händer det något MED någon!',
  notePossessive: 'Det visar vems det är — och det passar ihop med ordet efter!',
  nSubject:    'Nästan! Fråga: vem GÖR något? Läs meningen igen.',
  nObject:     'Nästan! Fråga: vem händer det med? Läs meningen igen.',
  nPossessive: 'Nästan! Heter det en eller ett — eller flera? Läs ordet efter och prova igen.',
  blankWord: 'lucka'        // NEW key — see defect A
}
```

`nPossessive` names the **test**, never the answer. The German and Dutch nudges say
*"Dann passt das andere Wort"* / *"Dan past het andere woord"* — with exactly two chips
and the wrong one already dimmed, that literally instructs the child to tap the remaining
chip. **Swedish must not inherit it.**

`blankWord: 'lucka'` — `lucka` is the standard Swedish worksheet term (`fyll i luckan`),
the exact parallel of German `Lücke`.

### Metadata

- **Årskurs: åk 2**, via a per-activity grade override. Do **not** inherit
  `alignment.grade: "1"`. Lgr22 states centralt innehåll once for the whole åk 1-3 band,
  so the band cannot decide the year — the progression does, and this deck requires
  reading a full sentence while holding a noun in mind. åk 2 also matches every shipped
  Swedish sibling (plural, tempus, affix, meningsbyggnad are all åk 2).
- `title`: **`Hatties hattaffär`** — the English pun survives translation intact
  (`hatt` = hat), it alliterates, and it is decodable.
- `instruction`: `Ge Hattie rätt ord — det som passar i meningen!`
- `slug`: `pronomen-han-honom-och-min-mitt-ak-2`
- `page_title`: `Pronomen – han eller honom, min eller mitt: välj rätt form (åk 2)`
- `page_intro`: name **Lgr22** only (no CCSS code — the SERP gate); claim no freeness.

### Register warning (sv #21 precedent applies)

Object `han` is not a child's *mistake* — in much of Sweden it is the vernacular norm.
Telling a Norrland child *"nästan"* about their own speech is defensible but blunt. The
codebase already has a **third response class** (affirm, do not resolve) from sv #21,
keyed on per-round data rather than `LANG`. Consider `Så säger man ofta! När vi skriver
skriver vi honom.` Optional, not blocking.

## DEFECTS FOUND IN THE ENGLISH SOURCE AND THE ENGINE

**A. English hard-coded into a locale-branching path — twice, and Swedish hits both.**
`pronoun-activity.js:235` (Hear-it) and `:285` (`_srMirror`) carry the *same duplicated*
ternary with **no `sv` arm and an English fallback `'blank'`**. A Swedish child pressing 🔊
hears *"Ella såg **blank** i parken"*. Same rule in two call sites — the shape that gets
half-fixed. Extract to one `txt('blankWord')`.

**B. The screen-reader chip joiner is English too.** `:284` — no `sv` arm, fallback
`' or '`. A Swedish SR user hears **"honom or han?"**. Swedish is `eller`.

**C. ⭐ THE SR MIRROR NAMES THE ANSWER FIRST ON EVERY ROUND, IN EVERY LOCALE.**
`_renderChips:248` renders the **shuffled** `this._chipOrder`; `_srMirror:284` calls
`pnChips(round)`, which returns **`[correct, wrong]` unshuffled**. So the mirror both
(i) announces the options in a different order from the visible chips ~50% of the time,
and (ii) **always names the correct answer first**. A screen-reader user can solve the
entire deck without reading a sentence. Live defect, all seven locales.

**D. `page_intro.en` misdescribes its own deck.** It says the child learns *"he when
doing, him when it is done to him, his when it belongs to him"* — but the EN rounds are
3× `he`, 2× `I`, 3× `they`, 1× `we`. **Two thirds of the deck is not he/him/his.**

**E. The EN deck has no `my` and no `our` round — and it is the only locale that doesn't.**
CCSS L.1.1.d names `my` in its own exemplars ("I, me, my; they, them, their"), and de/fr/
es/pt/it/nl each ship a first-person possessive (`mein Hut`, `mon ballon`, `mi pelota`,
`meu chapéu`, `la mia palla`, `mijn hoed`). English is the odd one out, against the very
standard it is aligned to.

**F. German and Dutch nudges instruct the child to tap the other chip.** `:38-40` /
`:95-97` end *"Dann passt das andere Wort"* / *"Dan past het andere woord"*. With two
chips and the wrong one dimmed (`:250`), the round becomes a no-op after one tap. EN/FR/
ES/PT/IT correctly say "look again" instead.

**G. No round can be failed.** `:254` — a tapped wrong chip is permanently dimmed and
dead, leaving exactly one live chip. Every round resolves in ≤2 taps with no re-reading.
A design call, but the deck's measured difficulty is zero.

**H. ⭐ THE LOCALIZED DECKS BYPASS EVERY CHECK IN THE CORE.** `pnChips`/`pnIsAnswer`
(`:102-103`) read the round's `{correct, wrong}` literals and never touch `PronounCore`;
`CASE_TABLE` is English-only. So for de/fr/es/pt/it/nl — **and for sv** — there is *no*
answer-leak check, *no* two-distinct-chips check, *no* role-validity check. Two mis-built
rounds are already live and nothing caught them:
- `fr-obj-3` `"Léo ___ suit."` correct `le`, wrong **`son`** — a *possessive* used as the
  distractor in an `object` round, so the French nudge asks *"de qui parle la phrase ?"*
  against a possessive.
- `it-pos-3` `"Questo è il ___ cappello."` correct `suo`, wrong **`lui`** — `*il lui
  cappello` is not a possible Italian string. Not a plausible error; word salad.
- `es-sub-1/2/3` use `La`/`Lo`/`Los` as pre-verbal subjects — impossible clitic positions,
  the same "distractor no child could produce" class as `*Oss bygger`.

**I. Both gates are English-only.** `scripts/local-test-pronoun.js:48` drives
`?lang=en`; `verify-pronoun-core.js` runs through `PronounCore`, which the localized
decks bypass (H). **No gate has ever read a non-English pool** — the same class as the
tense defect fixed in `42a07726`. `sentenceNoAnswerLeak` exists (`pronoun-core.js:54`)
and is **never called at runtime**.

**J. `alignment.grade: "1"` contradicts six of its own seven page_titles** (Klasse 3,
CE2, 3.º primaria, 3º ano, classe terza, groep 4).

**K. `es-MX` is silently overridden.** `pronoun-activity.js:236` passes `lang: 'es-MX'`,
but `speak()` (`lcs-shell.js`) does `.split('-')[0]` and re-derives **`es-ES`** from its
own table. The activity's careful es-MX mapping is dead code. (`pt-BR` survives by
coincidence.)

**L. Three simultaneous announcements per tap.** `.pn-line-msg` is `aria-live`, the
`_srMirror` wrapper is `aria-live`, and `_resolve`/`_nudge` also call `api.announce`.
Both `_resolve:266` and `_nudge:276` call `render()`, which rebuilds the stage and
re-inserts the mirror node — so an SR user gets **the whole sentence plus both chips
re-read after every single tap**, on top of the announce.

**M. Adding `sv` requires touching four places, and three are silent if missed:**
`L.sv`, `strings.title.sv`, `strings.instruction.sv`, `strings.q.sv` (`:122-124`).
`strings.q` is seven identical `'{q}'` passthroughs — easy to overlook.

**N. The 🔊 button has no `aria-label`,** so it is announced as "loudspeaker Hear it".
