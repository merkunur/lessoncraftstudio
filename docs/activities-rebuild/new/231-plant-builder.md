## The skill
A plant has parts (roots, stem, leaves, flower, fruit, seed) that each do a job; the foods we eat are different plant parts (fruits hold seeds); a flower has petals, sepals, stamens and a pistil.

## The apparatus and its invention
A POT AND A WATER DROP. The child builds a plant from library parts: roots only click below the soil line, the stem on the roots, leaves on the stem, the flower at the top (a part held anywhere else drifts back). A water drop placed on the soil then travels visibly up through the roots and stem to the leaves; a missing part stops the drop there and that gap glows. WHICH-PART-WE-EAT mode (K-202 merged grow-where, G2-363): a library food (carrot, celery, lettuce, broccoli, apple, tomato, corn) is hung on the part we eat; the plant fills that part with the food's colour; fruits show their seeds. JOBS mode (G2-364): job cards climb to their part. FLOWER mode (G3-392, merged flower-press): the flower opens apart into petal, sepal, stamen, pistil; name tags go onto them, one tag (bark, thorn) is not a flower part.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Thinks the plant drinks through its leaves or that roots are just "the bottom".
- **Pictures:** MISSING: a plant-parts set (root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed as separable layers of one plant) and a flower-parts set (petal, sepal, stamen, pistil). Foods exist: vegetables (carrot, celery, lettuce, broccoli, cauliflower, corn, tomato), fruits (apple, pear).
- **Settings:** Task: build and water / name the parts · Parts: 4 / 5 (+ seed).
- **Rounds:** 8+ plants from different library flowers; the part asked to name varies; tags shuffled.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-376, G2-363, G2-364, G3-392, K-202
- **Distinct from:** Not a duplicate of the Cycle Wheel (growth over time); this is structure and function.
- **Build risk:** Blocked on two library assets. The tomato/cucumber/pepper honesty line ("a fruit of the plant, cooked as a vegetable") is needed in the eat mode.
- **Grade span (settings):** K–3 (settings by grade)

## BLOCKED — needs an operator decision before building
Separable plant parts (root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed) and a flower-parts set (petal, sepal, stamen, pistil) do not exist in the library, and drawing them is hand-drawn objects (rule 5). It now also holds grow-where (fruit/vegetable by plant part, K-202) and flower-press (G3-392), all on one apparatus once the art exists.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 2 "The Root-to-Fruit Ladder — Parts of a Plant" (root-to-fruit): Name the parts of a plant (root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed), what each does, and which part of a plant a food is. — A tall ladder beside a plant column, one rung per part from root (bottom, underground) to seed/fruit (top). Food cards (library vegetables and fruits: carrot, celery, lettuce, broccoli, apple, peas, corn) are hung on the rung of the part we eat; the plant column fills that part in (the root zone shows a carrot shape). Function mode: function cards (takes up water, makes food from light, holds the plant up, makes seeds) climb to their rung, and a little water drop travels up from root to leaf to show the path.
- grade K "The Grow-Where Garden" (grow-where): Tell fruits from vegetables by which part of the plant we eat: fruits hold the seeds, vegetables are roots, leaves and stems. — THE KNIFE AND THE PLANT. A cutting board and a plant diagram (a plant with roots, stem, leaves, flower and a fruit, drawn as apparatus). The child drags a food onto the board; a cut opens it: seeds inside → it is a fruit; no seeds → the child drags it onto the plant part it is (carrot to the root, lettuce to the leaf, celery to the stem). Then it goes into the fruit bowl or the vegetable crate. Culinary rounds (tomato, cucumber) show seeds and are honest: "a fruit of the plant, cooked as a vegetable".
- grade 3 "The Flower Press" (flower-press): Name the parts of a flower (petal, stem, leaf, root, and grade-3 sepal/stamen/pistil) and what each does. — THE FLOWER PRESS: a flower in a pot; the child pulls it from the soil and presses it flat between two glass plates, where its parts separate slightly. A job card appears ('drinks water from the soil', 'makes food from sunlight', 'attracts bees', 'holds the flower up'); the child drags it to the part that does that job, and the part lights and acts (roots draw a blue line of water up the stem; a bee lands on the petals). Label mode: name tags go onto numbered parts, with one tag that is not a flower part to cross out.
