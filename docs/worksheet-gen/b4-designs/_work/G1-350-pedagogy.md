**Boundary (load-bearing).** This family owns exactly one thing nobody has: **a picture-cued NOUN gap inside a panel-authored sentence whose predicate only the pictured noun satisfies** (the picture sits on the SAME row as its frame, so the picture is the key and the bank is only a spelling aid; the child reads a sentence and produces a noun in subject or plain-object position). Structural fences the validator enforces: the gap is ALWAYS a noun (the answer literal is a vocab singular / plural or an `objForms` form, never an adjective, verb, article or function word); the gap sits in the LAST sentence of a frame of at most two sentences; no frame text is a `SENTENCES[loc].frames` literal; no face is a picture card without a sentence, a passage with questions, a true/false judgement, a word-order task or a bare singular/plural pair; the letter-box face (F1) titles itself as a SENTENCE gap in every locale (pt "Complete a frase com o nome da figura", never "escreva o nome da figura"); the plural face (F3) never titles itself with the `singular-plural` head. Visual signature: one picture per row on the LEFT, a sentence with one pencil gap on the RIGHT, a word bank on top; the story face swaps the row picture for a three-picture strip; the match face moves the pictures to a right-hand column.

**The one rule that locks the type.** A frame is a whole panel literal with exactly one `___` and NO answer text: it names `noun` (a vocabKey) + `form` (`sg` = the vocab singular through `displayWord`, `pl` = `objForms[noun].pl`, or an `objForms` inflected form `unique | def | defPl | part | gen | dat | a2`), and the code READS the answer literal from the reviewed bank (`answerFor(loc, noun, form)`), prints it in the bank / chips / boxes, and stamps it; the panel never types a noun form.
