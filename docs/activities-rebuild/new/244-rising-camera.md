## The skill
See that an object seen from above looks different from the side, and match side views to top views — the first step to reading a map.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE SLIDER THAT LIFTS THE EYE. A table carries a few solid objects drawn as apparatus geometry (a cup = cylinder, a book = box, a cone hat, a ball, a table seen as a rectangle). A camera on a vertical slider looks at the table from the side; sliding it up rotates the view in steps until it looks straight down, and the outlines morph (cylinder → circle, box → rectangle, cone → circle with a dot). Task: the stage shows a top view on a map card; the child slides the camera to see the top views and drags each object's side card onto its top-view footprint — or arranges the objects on the table to match a small map.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Expecting the top view to look like the side view (drawing the cup as a cup from above).
- **Pictures:** shapes (cylinder, cone, cube, sphere, pyramid) as side views; kitchen-tools (cup, mug, glass), classroom (book), toys (ball, blocks) as everyday side cards; top views drawn as apparatus geometry (circle, square, ring, circle-with-dot).
- **Settings:** Mode: match side to top / build the map · Objects: 3 / 5
- **Rounds:** 9 rounds; the footprint positions shuffled on the map card, each footprint shape appears in every map position across a pass; one right answer because every solid has a distinct footprint (circle, square, long rectangle, ring).
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-377
- **Distinct from:** No perspective or map activity exists at any grade.
- **Build risk:** Needs a small 3D-to-2D renderer in SVG (orthographic, a handful of solids); keep to solids that read without shading.
