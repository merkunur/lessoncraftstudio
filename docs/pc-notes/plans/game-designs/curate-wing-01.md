## 2. Concept & creative hook
Professor Pip, a small bow-tied penguin, is the youngest museum director in the world — and tonight the museum trusts the child to **name every new acquisition and open the gallery.** Exhibits ride up the brass conveyor as *treasures with stories* (a triangular sail, a hexagonal honeycomb tile, an ancient square mosaic) — each in whatever pose it was crated in: tilted, tiny, stretched, upside-down. The child is the one expert who can tell what each truly **is** — by name — no matter how it's turned.
- **The naming act = route-to-the-correctly-NAMED-pedestal (the rework):** the hall has **≥3 named pedestals**; the conveyor delivers mixed shapes (one active at a time); the child routes each to the pedestal that names it. The hall opens (ribbon-cutting) when every pedestal holds its correctly-named exhibits. **NOT binary accept/reject** (which is brute-forceable + doesn't force naming).
- **Pedestal identity is AUDIO/NAME-BOUND — NO canonical icon (the critical fix):** K children are pre-readers, so the pedestal's identity is carried by **the spoken formal name** (Pip / the pedestal speaks "this is the *square* pedestal" on tap/approach) + a **non-prototype marker** (color + Pip-portrait-with-nameplate, OR a **trio of already-transformed mini-exemplars** of that shape — never a single upright canonical icon). A single upright icon is BANNED: it would let a pre-reader rotate-in-head to silhouette-match the picture and NEVER name (the canonical-pose cheat, which the gate would falsely green-light). The **name→shape audio binding is the spine** of the whole game — audio is the only channel that forces naming for a pre-reader.
- **The shapes are seeded parametric SVG (the load-bearing twist):** every exhibit is randomized at the transform layer (rotation + scale + within-class proportion), so the **canonical pose is the exception** — there's no upright prototype on screen to match against; only the invariant geometry (a square is 4 equal sides + right angles, any tilt) bridges. ≥50% non-canonical post-warm-up.
- **THE SIGNATURE AHA — the spin-to-upright reveal (the premium core, both designers' fight-for):** on a committed-correct routing, the rotated exhibit **rotates upright** — the disguise drops, *"it was a square all along, just tilted"* — and Pip speaks the formal name + the pedestal lights. This is the only moment K.G.A.2's invisible invariance becomes FELT knowledge; it's the one mechanic the free sort can never have → **promoted to a non-optional, gate-asserted, load-bearing mechanic.** It fires ONLY on the committed-correct drop (never on hover/approach/proximity — no pre-commit "you're getting warm" tell that leaks the answer). No explicit side-count/side-ping (that's the 2.G.A.1 lane).
- **Anti-generic statement:** NOT the free E3 shape-sort + a penguin — (a) naming-under-transformation (≥50% non-canonical; the spin-to-upright invariance reveal); (b) audio/name-bound pedestals with NO canonical icon (silhouette-matching structurally defeated); (c) the rhombus/oval impostor forces the full definition (naming-by-exclusion to a positive "Mystery Gallery"); (d) re-seed-on-return defeats brute-force; (e) a board ships only if a **canonical-pose matcher**, a **silhouette-match (icon) solver**, and a **side-count router** ALL fail the gate while an **invariant-namer** passes 100% on first-attempt (§8).

## 3. Core loop & the ≥7 distinct experiences
**Loop:** the conveyor slides one exhibit into the active bay (a big ≥64px drag target) → the child routes it to a named pedestal (Pip/the pedestal speaks its name; the child names + routes) → **committed-correct drop** → the exhibit **spins upright** (the disguise drops) + Pip speaks the formal name + the pedestal lights + a niche fills → next exhibit. **Mis-route = no-shame instruction:** Pip speaks the CORRECT name + gestures ("hmm, that's a *hexagon* — the hexagon shelf is over here"), and the exhibit **gently re-enters the conveyor RE-SEEDED into a NEW transform** (so it looks different on return — cross-return stimulus-memory is useless; only the invariant NAME carries). **No fail, no score, no timer, no Check button.**

**The ≥7 distinct experiences — HONEST re-count (the critic's catch: rotation/scale/proportion are ONE act, not three): distinct = the CHILD'S ACT, NOT the transform knob.** The genuinely distinct acts (the collect-trade family identity earns several):
| # | Experience (the distinct ACT) | Render / act | Why it's distinct & instantiates naming |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ★ | **Transformed-route** (the core naming act) | name an incoming transformed exhibit → its correctly-named pedestal; **rotation / scale / proportion are the INTERNAL variety pool** (the diamond-trap square, the tiny+huge circle, the scalene/obtuse triangle, the long-thin rectangle) | holistic naming under transformation — the canonical pose is the exception; one act, varied stimulus |
| 2 ★ | **Exclude-route** (naming-by-exclusion → the Mystery Gallery) | a non-named shape (rhombus / oval / parallelogram / pentagon) → the **"Mystery Gallery"** ("not a square — its corners are slanted — it goes somewhere special!") | naming requires EXCLUSION (the full-definition discrimination); a positive destination, never a "reject" |
| 3 ★ | **Fine-discriminate** (square vs rectangle) | both the Square + Rectangle pedestals live; near-square rectangles + true squares fed → attend equal-vs-unequal sides under transform | the hardest naming — between two valid names; defeats the side-count shortcut (both are 4-sided) |
| 4 ★ | **Name→shape** (the inverse direction; a visitor trades) | Pip / a visitor names a shape ("the visitor wants a HEXAGON") → the child finds + brings the matching shape (point-up OR flat-up) from a varied set | name→shape (not shape→name) — the inverse act; earns the collect-trade identity (a trade for a named shape) |
| 5 ★ | **Match-the-pair** (same name despite difference) | find the TWO exhibits that share a NAME though they look totally different (huge upright square + tiny tilted square) → shelve them together | match-by-name-across-transform — the literal invariance pedagogy as a matching act |
| 6 ★ | **Confirm/correct-Pip** (judge a naming claim) | Pip names a shape — sometimes WRONG (calls the rhombus a square) → the child agrees or corrects | agree/disagree with a NAMING claim — a judge act, distinct from routing |
| 7 ★ | **The full curated hall** (integration under variety — capstone) | all live named pedestals fed a varied mixed stream → the child distributes everything to its true name + opens the hall | integrated naming under variety — the "I curated a whole gallery" capstone |

- **Honest framing (the §A.13.60 fix):** seven distinct ACTS — the **transformed-route is ONE act** (rotation/scale/proportion are the internal §A.13.60 variety pool, NOT separate experiences); the others are genuinely different child-acts (exclude / fine-discriminate / name→shape / match-pair / confirm-correct / capstone). **Circles never appear as a standalone "drag the circle"** (no orientation trap) — only as the scale-carrier (within #1) + the oval-impostor (within #2). The variety gate keys distinctness on a **`facetAct` enum** (transformed-route / exclude-route / fine-discriminate / name→shape / match-pair / confirm-correct / capstone) where rotation/scale/proportion ALL map to the single transformed-route act (§8). Difficulty ramp: canonical warm-up → rotation → scale → crossed → impostors.
- **Free slice = transformed-route (1) + exclude-route (2) + name→shape (4)** — proves naming-under-transformation + exclusion + the inverse direction.

## 5. Pedagogy
- **K.G.A.2 instantiation:** the child NAMES shapes (routes each to its correctly-named pedestal) across rotation + scale + atypical-but-valid exemplars; the transformed exemplar IS the assessment (≥50% non-canonical). The spin-to-upright reveal makes the orientation/size invariance felt (van Hiele 0→1; breaking the concept image).
- **THE KEYSTONE RULES (non-guessability):** (i) **NAMING, NOT MATCHING** — pedestal identity is audio/name-bound with NO single upright canonical icon (markers are non-prototype: color + nameplate, or a trio of transformed mini-exemplars); a silhouette-match (icon) solver must FAIL. (ii) **THE TRANSFORM IS THE ASSESSMENT** — ≥50% non-canonical; assessed shapes always carry non-trivial rotation/scale (no near-canonical assessed item); a canonical-pose matcher must FAIL. (iii) **NO SIDE-COUNTING** — the square/rectangle/rhombus 4-sided ambiguity defeats a side-count router (it can't separate them); naming is holistic, not attribute-counting (that's 2.G.A.1's lane). (iv) **RE-SEED-ON-RETURN** — a mis-routed exhibit re-enters with a NEW transform (no stimulus-memory; only the invariant name carries) → across-return brute-force defeated; the assessment signal is first-attempt-correct (internal, never shown). (v) **THE SPIN-TO-UPRIGHT FIRES ONLY ON COMMIT** (no pre-commit leak).
- **The shapes (pedagogy):** circle (SIZE clause + oval-impostor) / triangle (ORIENTATION + scalene/obtuse/right proportion — the richest, weight it) / square (the 45° rotation + the rhombus-impostor + the rectangle boundary) / rectangle (long-thin vs near-square proportion + tilt) / hexagon (rotation flat-top-vs-point-top + scale; kept REGULAR). **Rotated square = square** ("diamond" banned as a child label); the **rhombus-impostor** has VISIBLY non-right angles (60/120; never near-90° — fairness) + appears ONLY paired with a genuine rotated square. **Square⊂rectangle OUT for K** — a square NEVER appears as an item in a rectangle context (no inclusion-punishment).
- **Difficulty ramp:** Wave 1 canonical warm-up (establish the task) → Wave 2 rotation + scale (one axis at a time) → Wave 3 crossed (rotated-AND-scaled) + atypical proportions → Wave 4 impostors (rhombus vs genuine rotated square; oval vs scaled circle — visibly unambiguous dose). ≥7 distinct acts + order-only reshuffle.
- **Fairness (developmental fit):** atypical in POSE/PROPORTION, but UNAMBIGUOUS in identity to an attentive K child (no near-degenerate slivers; no near-90° rhombus; no irregular hexagons; no near-circle ovals). The lesson is "turned/stretched but still clearly a square," never "is this even a real shape?"
- **Formal-label doctrine (§A.13.61):** pedestal nameplates + the spoken names use the FORMAL native-confirmed terms (Circle/Triangle/Square/Rectangle/Hexagon per locale); "diamond"/"rhombus" NEVER child-facing (teacher note only); the negation/Mystery-Gallery line is a full per-locale literal phrase (never a fill-in `Not a {shape}` token — the case-language trap). "Oval" distinct from circle.
- **No-shame:** the mis-route is instruction (the correct name spoken), never a buzzer; the impostor goes to a POSITIVE Mystery Gallery (no value-judgment); the spin-to-upright is the intrinsic reward. **§20.8 demonstrate-don't-touch:** the game directly instantiates naming-under-transformation (not a generic sort) — the gate is the structural proof.
