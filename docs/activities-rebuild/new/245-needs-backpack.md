## The skill
Tell needs (what we must have to live: water, food, shelter, clothing) from wants (nice to have).

## The apparatus and its invention
THE FOUR POCKETS. A backpack for a camping trip has four shaped pockets — a water drop, a food bowl, a tent, a shirt — and a small open side net. The child drags library things into it: a need snaps into its pocket and the pocket glows full; a want (a toy car, a game, a lollipop) fits no pocket and goes in the side net. The trip can start only when all four pockets are full; filling the net with wants never starts it. So "need" is defined by the four pockets, not by liking.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Anything I really like is a need; wants are bad.
- **Pictures:** food, fruits, clothing, toys, household (bottle, bread, coat, blanket, tent if present)
- **Settings:** Mode: pack the bag / need or want · Items: 6 / 10
- **Rounds:** 9 rounds, each pocket's needs and 2–4 wants per round, tray shuffled; one right pocket per item.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-212
- **Distinct from:** No economics activity exists.
- **Build risk:** Pocket shapes are apparatus geometry; keep "want" wording neutral (not "bad").
