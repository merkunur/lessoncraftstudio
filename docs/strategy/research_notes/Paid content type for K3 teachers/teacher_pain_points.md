# K-3 teacher pain points and jobs-to-be-done (US + DE/FR/ES/NL/IT/PT-BR/SE/DK/NO/FI), 2023-2026

> **Method note.** WebFetch was blocked; every finding below comes from WebSearch result summaries (36 queries run before the session's search budget was exhausted). Where a number is quoted, it is what the search summary reported for the linked page — treat each as "search-summary-only" unless the URL is a primary report (RAND, OECD, Bosch-Stiftung, OAJ, AOb, DEPP PDFs). Planned but NOT executed (budget hit): Reddit/Facebook/Lehrerforen/forums-enseignants quote mining, Denmark/Norway/Portugal/Brazil quantitative surveys, search-demand (Google Trends / TPT best-sellers), EEF adaptive-teaching evidence, parent-communication time surveys. ## KQ1. What do K-3 teachers report as their most time-consuming weekly tasks?

### Takeaway
Across every locale with data, the two things K-3 teachers say they cannot fit into contracted time are (a) **adapting instruction to a wide ability spread in one class** ("heterogeneity" DE, "écarts de niveaux" FR, "atención a la diversidad" ES, "differentiëren/passend onderwijs" NL, "eriyttäminen" FI, "differentiation/small groups/MTSS data" US) and (b) **administrative/documentation load** (data sheets, MTSS logs, IEP paperwork, parent contact). Lesson preparation itself averages ~7.4 h/week (OECD TALIS 2024), US teachers work ~49-57 h/week, and US teachers add ~5 h/week creating their own materials on top of the curriculum. Behaviour is the #1 stressor in Germany 2026 and a top-3 in the US 2026, but is not a content-product problem.

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- US teachers reported working **49 h/week on average in 2025** (4 h less than 2023/2024), **10 h more than contracted**; 32% work 51-60 h/week vs 5% of other working adults; 46% say the job leaves them too tired for personal activities (vs 13%). — [RAND SoAT 2025, RRA1108-16](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-16.html); [RAND RRA1108-20 (work-life balance)](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-20.html)
- RAND 2025: "limited planning time" and instructional-time requirements leave minimal flexibility; fewer than half of teachers say their school/district is trying to help them manage work/life; teachers most valued leaders "limiting meetings and administrative work". — [RAND RRA1108-20](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-20.html)
- EdWeek Research Center: teachers report a **57-hour work week**, 23 h of it teaching; most want more time on planning/instruction and less on administrative work and non-teaching duties. — [EdWeek, How Teachers Spend Their Time (2022, older)](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-teachers-spend-their-time-a-breakdown/2022/04)
- Teacher quotes on what keeps them up at night (EdWeek, Dec 2025): "constant juggling … Updating data sheets, MTSS data collection and posting, grading or posting grades, calling a parent" with one ball "always being dropped"; "Limited time and so many responsibilities taking away precious plan time — it seems there is always something I didn't get to". After school: meetings, parent calls/emails, lesson plans, grading. — [EdWeek 'Constant Juggling' 2025/12](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/constant-juggling-teachers-share-the-job-stressors-that-keep-them-up-at-night/2025/12)
- Nearly **90% of US public-school teachers add/mix content from the internet** into district-mandated curriculum (RAND); teachers spend **~5 extra hours/week creating their own content** (search-summary figure attributed to survey research cited by Fordham). — [Fordham: the "case for curriculum" is about reducing workload](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/commentary/case-curriculum-about-reducing-teachers-workload); [RAND AIRS 2019-2024 trends](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA134-30.html)
- Older but specific: a survey found teachers spend **7 h/week searching for instructional materials** (Education World, date not given in summary — likely 2017-19 era; label as older). — [Education World](https://www.educationworld.com/a_news/survey-finds-teachers-spend-7-hours-week-searching-instructional-materials-490526015)
- RAND pre-K teacher survey (Apr 2025): "varied curriculum use and time constraints" among public-school pre-K teachers (summary only; no number retrieved). — [RAND press 2025/04](https://www.rand.org/news/press/2025/04/rand-survey-reveals-varied-curriculum-use-and-time.html)
- Only 31% of teachers say they have sufficient time to collaborate with other teachers (EdWeek). — [EdWeek State of Teaching 2025](https://www.edweek.org/the-state-of-teaching/2025)
- Small-group instruction is near-universal in US elementary: in a Scholastic-cited survey, **90% of elementary teachers met with small groups 3-5 days/week**. — [Scholastic small-group topic paper](https://education.scholastic.com/content/dam/education/resources/5252-23_RS_Small-Group_TP_R1_Proofread_Final-INT-singles.pdf)
- Science of Reading has shifted small-group time toward phonics "not as a follow-up for the stragglers, but as initial teaching" (Shanahan). — [Shanahan on Literacy](https://www.shanahanonliteracy.com/blog/should-reading-be-taught-whole-class-or-small-group-1)
- Special-education paperwork: special-ed teachers average **~5 h/week on IEP paperwork (>10% of work time)**; some sources claim 15-20 unpaid hours/week to meet deadlines (source quality uncertain — vendor blogs); 89% say there is insufficient (48%) or no (41%) time to work with other IEP team members; **57% of licensed special-ed teachers used AI for IEPs/504s in 2024-25** (CDT). — [Frontline Education](https://www.frontlineeducation.com/blog/how-special-educators-spend-work-day/); [SETWorks (cites CDT)](https://set-works.com/ai-and-ieps-what-providers-need-to-know/)
- Germany-style behaviour stress also in US: classroom management now named as a job stress by more teachers than low pay (EdWeek, June 2026). — [EdWeek 2026/06](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/more-teachers-name-classroom-management-as-a-job-stress-than-low-pay/2026/06)

**OECD / cross-national (TALIS 2024)**
- Full-time teachers average **41 h/week total; 7.4 h/week on lesson preparation** (OECD avg); Netherlands 5.4 h, Czechia 8.5, Australia 8.4. — [OECD TALIS 2024 "The demands of teaching"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/results-from-talis-2024_90df6235-en/full-report/the-demands-of-teaching_0e941e2f.html)
- Teacher well-being falls most with extra time on **administrative tasks, marking and communicating with parents**, not with teaching or lesson prep. — [OECD TALIS 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/results-from-talis-2024_90df6235-en/full-report/the-demands-of-teaching_0e941e2f.html)
- Share of primary teachers reporting "too much lesson preparation" as a stressor rose since 2018 in five systems and **fell in France**. — same source.
- Netherlands national TALIS report (Oct 2025): Dutch primary teachers spend less time on lesson prep/marking and **more on administration** than the international average; full-time PO teachers work more hours/week than the TALIS average; teachers want initial training to prepare them better for **teaching multilingual groups and differentiating in class** (a repeat finding from earlier rounds). — [Rijksoverheid TALIS 2024 national report](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/rapporten/2025/10/07/nationaal-rapport-talis-2024-de-werk-en-leeromgeving-op-school-in-beeld)
- Italy TALIS 2024: **56% of Italian teachers cite excessive administrative work** as a stressor, followed by marking/assessment load and parent demands; the problem is fragmentation (staggered compliance tasks, multiple platforms, out-of-hours meetings, family communication extending beyond the school day). — [edunews24 on TALIS 2024](https://edunews24.it/scuola/docenti-stressati-dalla-burocrazia-cosa-dicono-i-dati-talis-2024)

**Germany**
- Deutsches Schulbarometer 2025 (Bosch Stiftung/forsa): **Grundschul-Lehrkräfte name "Heterogenität" as their #1 burden at 40%** (2024: 45%). — [Deutsches Schulbarometer Lehrkräfte 2025 PDF](https://www.bosch-stiftung.de/sites/default/files/documents/2025-06/Deutsches%20Schulbarometer_Lehrkr%C3%A4fte_2025.pdf); [Schulportal summary](https://deutsches-schulportal.de/bildungswesen/deutsches-schulbarometer-2026-neue-zahlen-verraten-was-lehrkraefte-am-meisten-belastet/)
- Schulbarometer 2026 (n=1,547, Nov-Dec 2025, all school types): **student behaviour 42%** (#1), **workload/time scarcity 34%** (up from 28% in 2024), **heterogeneity 32%**. — [Bosch Stiftung Schulbarometer 2026 PDF](https://bosch-stiftung.de/sites/default/files/publications/pdf/2026-06/RBSG_Deutsches_Schulbarometer_Lehrkr%C3%A4fte_2026.pdf); [ZDF](https://www.zdfheute.de/politik/deutschland/schulbarometer-lehrer-belastung-verhalten-schueler-100.html)
- VBE/forsa Inklusion 2025 (n=2,737, Mar-Apr 2025): teachers support inclusion but experience "inadequate equipment and lack of support daily"; "when special needs are added to the inherent heterogeneity of primary classes, many teachers and schools reach the limits of what is feasible"; VBE stresses that "increasingly more children with and without migration background struggle with language problems". — [VBE NRW press](https://vbe-nrw.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/2025/forsa-umfrage-inklusion/); [VBE Inklusion 2025](https://www.vbe.de/service/meinungsumfragen/inklusion-2025)
- Mercator-Institut: DaZ-Unterricht lacks teachers, quality standards and training offers. — [Schulportal/Mercator](https://deutsches-schulportal.de/bildungswesen/mercator-institut-fuer-sprachfoerderung-und-deutsch-als-zweitsprache-daz-unterricht-es-fehlen-qualitaetsstandards-und-ausbildungsangebote/)

**France**
- DEPP 2025: full-time primary teachers declare **41.8 h/week** outside holidays. — [DEPP "Le temps de travail des enseignants" (FPE 2022, published 2025)](https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/le-temps-de-travail-des-enseignants-474745.pdf)
- **More than 8 in 10 teachers cite "lack of time to help pupils" and "gaps in levels" (écarts de niveaux)** as factors making teaching difficult; 46% have attended training on differentiation, 45% on difficulté scolaire. Real work includes preparing sessions, correcting notebooks, designing assessments, analysing results and **adapting materials**, plus follow-up of pupils with special needs, coordination with AESH, family meetings, digital data entry. — [DEPP/PRAESCO via IH2EF](https://www.ih2ef.gouv.fr/pratiques-denseignement-du-francais-praesco-en-classe-de-cm2); [OZP on PRAESCO](https://ozp.fr/spip.php?article32914=)

**Spain**
- STEs-i survey (>13,000 teachers): **excessive bureaucracy is the top concern for 77.5%**; **9 in 10 say current ratios prevent adequately attending to an increasingly diverse class**; pupils with specific support needs grew **75% while resources grew 30%**; "impossibility of individualised attention". — [El Mirón de Soria on STEs-i](https://elmirondesoria.es/nacional/sociedad/stes-i-apunta-en-encuesta-problemas-que-sufren-maestros-y-profesores-en-espana)
- Veteran teacher quote (Infobae, Nov 2025): "La burocracia y la adaptación curricular provocan una situación diaria que genera estrés". — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/espana/2025/11/04/eduardo-tras-mas-de-30-como-profesor-la-burocracia-y-la-adaptacion-curricular-provocan-una-situacion-diaria-que-genera-estres/)
- 40% of Spanish teachers report anxiety or depression (international study cited by CCOO). — [FECCOO CyL](https://www.feccoocyl.es/noticias-sindicales/el-40-del-profesorado-en-espana-reconoce-experimentar-ansiedad-o-depresion-segun-un-estudio-internacional)

**Netherlands**
- AOb/ResearchNed (n>7,100 PO+VO teachers): **>80% disagree that they have enough time to give pupils sufficient personal attention**; top causes of workload in regular primary 2023-24: **pupils with behavioural support needs, pupils with learning support needs, and teacher absence (arranging cover, splitting groups)**; 53% of PO teachers with classroom support say it is sufficient, most without it want it. — [AOb position paper Passend Onderwijs 2024](https://www.aob.nl/assets/Brieven-AOb/Position-Paper-AOb-Passend-onderwijs-tbv-debat-29-05-2024-en-bijlage.pdf); [AOb time-use study](https://www.aob.nl/assets/Nieuws/Downloads/AObonderzoek-Tijdsbesteding-leraren-po-en-vo.pdf)
- Onderwijsraad: workload in basisonderwijs too high; advises dropping "one teacher does all lessons" and differentiating task distribution with assistants/specialists. — [AVS on Onderwijsraad](https://avs.nl/actueel/nieuws/onderwijsraad-werkdruk-in-basisonderwijs-te-hoog/)

**Sweden**
- Sveriges Lärare: 36% of teachers stressed because of **too little planning time** (2023); over half often work under unsustainable load, 3 in 10 at breaking point; workload driven by **documentation requirements, too-large groups, too little planning time**; **26% of F-3 teachers** (vs 45% middle school) report struggling with workload. — [Sveriges Lärare "En ohållbar klassrumssituation" 2024 PDF](https://cdn.abicart.com/shop/ws21/49421/art25/213189825-9829a1-SVLT1251-En-ohallbar-klassrumssituation-SVLU018-240611.pdf); [Vi Lärare](https://www.vilarare.se/nyheter/arbetsbelastning/varannan-larare-svarar-vi-kan-inte-utfora-vart-jobb/); [government inquiry SOU 2025:26 on reducing administration](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/6dfbf9bb877b433bbca46ad8c07950fe/tid-for-undervisningsuppdraget--atgarder-for-god-undervisning-och-lararyrkenas-attraktivitet-sou-202526.pdf)

**Finland**
- OAJ Työolobarometri 2024 (n>1,400, Nov 2023): teachers work **40.5 h/week**; "adequately small group size that considers learners needing support is at the top of many teachers' wish lists". — [OAJ 2024 barometer PDF](https://www.oaj.fi/contentassets/476cfb03fec74aa0a9feea4166f4dba7/opetusalan_tyoolobarometri_2024.pdf); OAJ 2026 barometer headline is rising violence — [OAJ 2026](https://www.oaj.fi/ajankohtaista/uutiset-ja-tiedotteet/2026/tyoolobarometri-vakivalta-on-lisaantynyt-huolestuttavasti)

**Denmark / Norway**
- Folkeskolen: Danish teachers have **one hour less preparation per week** (headline only; year/number not retrieved). — [Folkeskolen](https://folkeskolen.dk/arbejdsliv-arbejdstid-efteruddannelse/danske-laerere-har-faet-en-time-mindre-forberedelse-om-ugen/403984)
- DLF member surveys: teachers say more two-teacher arrangements, more prep time and class-splitting would help inclusion; DLF asks annually whether members have enough prep time. — [DLF](https://www.dlf.org/politik-og-presse/tal-og-analyser/)
- Utdanningsforbundet (NO): "teachers get ever less time to plan and deliver teaching and follow up pupils"; the union wants reduced teaching frames and more time for for- og etterarbeid. — [Utdanningsforbundet 2024](https://www.utdanningsforbundet.no/nyheter/2024/-elevene-fortjener-en-skole-der-larerne-har-tid-til-dem/)

**UK (comparator, Teacher Tapp)**
- Teachers spend 2-3 h per weekday on tasks outside lesson delivery; only 33% of primary teachers may take PPA at home; the three biggest unnecessary-workload drivers are **marking, planning & preparation, and data management**; AI-planned lessons rose 14%→20% (Dec 2025), with **EYFS/KS1 teachers doubling 9%→18%**. — [Teacher Tapp](https://teachertapp.com/articles/ai-for-planning-leadership-gaps-plus-tas/); [TeacherToolkit workload 2025](https://www.teachertoolkit.co.uk/2025/12/08/teacher-workload-2025/)

### Inferences
- The only pain point that is (a) top-3 in every locale with data, (b) tied to weekly teacher output rather than school policy, and (c) solvable by a content product is **producing level-differentiated practice for a wide-ability class** ("Heterogenität 40% of Grundschul-Lehrkräfte", "8 in 10 FR", "9 in 10 ES", ">80% NL", TALIS NL "want to be trained to differentiate"). Behaviour, bureaucracy, ratios and pay are bigger stressors but are not purchasable content.
- The measured "extra 5 h/week creating own content" (US) and "7.4 h/week lesson prep" (OECD) bound the time budget a product could reclaim; a product that removes even 1-2 h/week is meaningful against a 10-hour-over-contract week.
- In Europe the framing is *heterogeneity/inclusion/language* rather than *small-group rotations*: the US centre/rotation workflow is culturally specific (see KQ3), while "three levels of the same task" is universal.

### Gaps
- No K-3-specific breakdown of hours per task (sub plans, morning work, early finishers, report cards, parent comms) was found in any survey; the per-task numbers above are all-grade or special-ed.
- Denmark, Norway, Portugal and Brazil: only qualitative union statements retrieved; no quantitative 2023-26 survey numbers (searches were planned but the budget was exhausted).
- EEF adaptive-teaching evidence and Teacher Tapp UK primary-specific differentiation data not retrieved.

## KQ2. How much do K-3 teachers spend out of pocket per year, and on what?

### Takeaway
US teachers spend **$610-$895/year** out of pocket depending on the survey (AdoptAClassroom 2025: $895, +49% since 2015; DonorsChoose 2025: $655; NCES NTPS: $547 inflation-adjusted, 95% spend something), ~**$3.2 bn/year** nationally — mostly on consumables (paper, pencils, markers, snacks), not curriculum. No European per-teacher spend figure was found.

### Cited Findings
- AdoptAClassroom 2025 (n=3,700 K-12 teachers, all states): **$895 average 2024-25**, up 49% from ~$600 in 2015; 93% have students whose families cannot afford supplies; 97% say school funding doesn't stretch; **62% considered leaving because of own-money spending**. — [AdoptAClassroom 2025 survey](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/2025/06/09/2025-teacher-survey-spending-stats-classroom-needs/)
- DonorsChoose: **$610 (2023-24)** → **$655 (2024-25)** (n>2,500); teachers of colour / Equity Focus Schools spend $15-65 more. — [DonorsChoose 2024 results](https://blog.donorschoose.org/articles/teacher-survey-results-2024); [EdWeek 2025/05](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/as-prices-go-up-and-student-needs-rise-teachers-are-filling-in-the-gaps/2025/05)
- NCES NTPS (2020-21, older): 95% of public-school teachers spend own money; average **$547** unreimbursed (2022 dollars). — [NCES table](https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/ntps/estable/table/ntps/ntps2021_6801_t12ns); [NEA](https://www.nea.org/nea-today/all-news-articles/why-are-educators-still-buying-their-own-school-supplies)
- NEA estimated >$800 for 2023-24 (pre-pandemic ~$500); Chalkbeat's 120-teacher call-out ranged **$65-$6,000**; "most teachers spend most of their money on essential materials such as paper, pencils, and markers", plus crayons, snacks, and "specialised materials for specific learning needs". — [Chalkbeat 2024/10](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2024/10/01/teachers-buying-classroom-supplies-with-their-own-money/); [Chalkbeat 2024/07](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2024/07/17/teachers-spending-money-on-back-to-school-supplies-survey/)
- Aggregate: roughly **$3.2 bn/year** across the profession (search summary; derivation not shown). — [Bored Teachers](https://www.boredteachers.com/post/what-teachers-are-spending-on-students)
- Australia comparator: union finds teachers spend ~$1K/year (AUD) on own classrooms. — [EducationHQ](https://educationhq.com/news/unacceptable-reality-teachers-spend-1k-annually-on-their-own-classrooms-union-finds-204789/)
- 83% of US teachers "struggling to afford essential back-to-school supplies in 2024" (Study.com survey — vendor source). — [Study.com](https://teachinglicense.study.com/featured-insights/teachers-vs-inflation.html)

### Inferences
- US out-of-pocket budgets are real but are dominated by consumables; a paid content product competes for a slice of a ~$650-900 discretionary budget already stretched, and must be priced far below the $69/yr LCS anchor's perceived "supplies" competitor set (TPT single items at $3-8).
- The 62%-considering-leaving statistic means "reduce what I have to pay for myself" is an emotionally loaded value proposition in the US.

### Gaps
- **No European per-teacher out-of-pocket figure** was found for DE/FR/ES/NL/IT/PT/Nordics (searches on this were not run before the budget expired). This is a genuine data hole; European teachers typically have school budgets for Kopiervorlagen/manuels, and TPT-style personal purchase is much smaller.
- No breakdown of the share of US spend going to **curriculum/printables (TPT, memberships)** vs consumables; NTPS/AdoptAClassroom summaries don't separate it.

## KQ3. Which differentiation workflows are unsolved (making 3-4 versions, grouping, tracking mastery)?

### Takeaway
Evidence says teachers currently (1) remix ~90% of the time from internet sources on top of the mandated curriculum, (2) hand-build tiered worksheets — a vendor estimate puts 2-3 extra hours per lesson for multi-level versions — and (3) log mastery on "data sheets / MTSS data collection" they describe as a constantly-dropped ball. German blogs frame the same problem as "dreifach differenzierte Arbeitsblätter" (pictures / words / sentences). Direct forum quotes were NOT retrieved (budget), so forum evidence is a gap.

### Cited Findings
- "Creating differentiated worksheets for varying student levels can take teachers **2-3 extra hours of work per lesson**"; most AI tools require multi-step manual prompting per version (vendor blog — treat as marketing estimate). — [Chalkie.ai](https://chalkie.ai/en/blog/ai-worksheet-generator-tools)
- TES marketplace shows the dominant commercial pattern: worksheets sold in **three versions for low/middle/high ability**. — [TES differentiated resources](https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/christmas-time-worksheets-differentiated-analogue-and-digital-11447443)
- German practice: reading worksheets "dreifach differenziert": **Niveau 1 mit Bildern, Niveau 2 mit Wörtern, Niveau 3 mit Sätzen**; publishers (Kohl, Hase und Igel) sell "Differenzierung/Niveaustufen" as a category; blog counter-narrative says differentiation should not require "ein großer Stapel ausdifferenzierter Arbeitsblätter". — [Hase und Igel](https://www.hase-und-igel.de/blog_hase_und_igel_verlag_lesen_in_der_schule/differenzierung_grundschule/c-236); [Kohl Verlag](https://www.kohlverlag.de/grundschule/methodisch-didaktische-eigenschaften/differenzierung-niveaustufen/); [meinUnterricht](https://www.meinunterricht.de/blog/differenzierung/); [Drachenstübchen blog (2018, older)](https://drachenstuebchen.blogspot.com/2018/03/differenzierung-im-unterricht.html)
- US teachers: ~90% add/mix content from the internet into mandated materials (RAND); new-material adoption adds ~30 min/week planning. — [RAND AIRS 2019-2024](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA134-30.html)
- Tracking: teacher quote "Updating data sheets, MTSS data collection and posting, grading or posting grades" as the perpetual juggling act. — [EdWeek 2025/12](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/constant-juggling-teachers-share-the-job-stressors-that-keep-them-up-at-night/2025/12)
- Small-group model: 90% of US elementary teachers run small groups 3-5 days/week; customisation happens by adjusting content, delivery, scaffolding and intensity per group. — [Scholastic](https://education.scholastic.com/content/dam/education/resources/5252-23_RS_Small-Group_TP_R1_Proofread_Final-INT-singles.pdf)
- Chalkbeat 2024 debate on whether teachers should modify scripted curricula or "stick to the script" — indicating the remix workflow is contested but ubiquitous. — [Chalkbeat 2024/07](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2024/07/23/should-teachers-customize-lessons-or-stick-to-script/)
- Stanford SCALE analysis of real teacher-AI chats (what K-12 educators actually prompt) exists but the summary did not report the breakdown. — [SCALE Initiative](https://scale.stanford.edu/research-in-action/what-k12-educators-are-actually-prompting-to-ai-early-findings-from-teacher-ai-chats)
- Wichita Public Schools uses Copilot Chat to differentiate for IEP students (case, vendor-adjacent). — [EdTech Magazine 2026/03](https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/03/ai-tools-support-personalized-learning-k-12-education)

### Inferences
- The job-to-be-done is "**same skill, same theme, three levels, ready to print, in my language**" — the shape every marketplace already sells piecemeal, but no source offers it systematically across a K-3 scope-and-sequence in 11 languages. LCS's existing per-face variants (base + 5 faces) are adjacent but are *variety* faces, not *level* tiers.
- The tracking half of the workflow (who mastered what) is a software/record problem; teachers are already offloading it to AI/IEP tools; a content product should hand off simple checklists, not build a tracker.

### Gaps
- **No Reddit / Facebook / Lehrerforen / forums-enseignants quotes** were retrieved: Reddit searches returned Goodreads/Scribd noise and the budget expired before site-specific queries could run. Forum evidence for "how do you make 3 versions" remains unverified.
- No survey quantifies how many minutes teachers spend per week specifically on tiering worksheets; the 2-3 h/lesson figure is a vendor estimate.

## KQ4. Which K-3 problems are intensifying (post-pandemic gaps, class size, multilingual/newcomers, phonics mandates, DaZ/Sprachförderung)?

### Takeaway
Three intensifiers are documented across locales: **multilingual/newcomer learners** (US ELs >10% of enrolment, 55% of EL educators say trained staff insufficient; DE VBE "more children with and without migration background struggle with language"; NL TALIS "prepare us for multilingual groups"), **special-needs/inclusion growth outpacing resources** (ES +75% pupils with needs vs +30% resources; IT +25% pupils with disabilities in 10 years, 27% of support teachers untrained; NL behaviour/learning support needs top workload causes), and the **US Science-of-Reading shift** (82% of K-3 teachers trained; only 52% say their instruction reflects SoR; ~30% still don't favour phonics) which is re-routing small-group time to phonics.

### Cited Findings

## KQ5. What do teachers explicitly say they WISH existed?

### Takeaway
Retrieved wish-statements cluster on (1) **differentiation in seconds** ("Differentiation takes so much time. Some AI tools can do so much with that in seconds"), (2) **more prep time / smaller groups / a second adult**, (3) fewer meetings and less admin, and (4) in Europe, **training and tools for multilingual/heterogeneous classes**. AI adoption by EYFS/KS1 teachers doubled in a year (UK) and 57% of US special-ed teachers used AI for IEPs — teachers are already buying time back with tools. ## Ranked pain-point map (synthesis for the strategist)

| Rank | Job-to-be-done | Frequency evidence | Time/money cost evidence | Locale scope | Content-product fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Level-tier the same practice for a wide-ability K-3 class (3 levels + language support)** | DE Grundschule heterogeneity 40% (#1); FR >80% "écarts de niveaux"; ES 9/10; NL >80% "no time for individual attention" + want differentiation training; US 90% remix content | ~5 h/week own-content creation (US); 7.4 h/week prep (OECD); vendor est. 2-3 h/lesson for tiered sheets | Universal | **High** — printable, per-level, per-language |
| 2 | Support multilingual / newcomer / DaZ pupils inside the regular class | US EL >10%, 55% say trained staff insufficient; DE VBE + SWK 2025; NL TALIS | Not quantified | Universal, sharpest DE/NL/US | High — bilingual worksheet pairs |
| 3 | Documentation / data logging (MTSS, IEP, report cards, Dokumentation) | "constant juggling" US; SE documentation driver; IT 56% admin stress; ES 77.5% bureaucracy | Sped ~5 h/week IEP paperwork | Universal | Low (software, not content) |
| 4 | Small-group phonics / guided-reading planning under SoR | 90% of US elementary run small groups 3-5 days/wk; 82% SoR-trained, 52% implementing | Not quantified | US-specific framing (phonics mandates); DE/NL/Nordic have syllable/phonics equivalents | Medium-high (decodable, tiered phonics practice) |
| 5 | Behaviour / classroom management | DE 42% #1 (2026); US > pay (2026); NL top-2 | Not quantified | Universal | Low |
| 6 | Out-of-pocket supplies | US $610-895/yr, 62% consider leaving over it | $3.2 bn/yr US | US-specific magnitude; EU unknown | Medium (price sensitivity) |

**Recommendation from the evidence:** the single problem with the widest, most-cited, most-intensifying demand that a paid content product can solve is **#1 — ready-made, level-tiered (and, where relevant, home-language-paired) practice sets for one K-3 skill**, marketed on "Differentiation takes so much time" and on the reclaimed weekly planning hours.
