## 1. The pitch
"Pack four pockets and one little fun net, then walk the trail: the hot sun asks for water, lunch asks for food, the cold wind asks for a coat, and nightfall asks for a tent to sleep in — and at the campfire, the toy you brought comes out to play."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **The backpack goes on a trip.** After packing, the backpack hops along a dotted trail past four stops. At every stop it stops, its flap opens and the right thing flies out and DOES something: the water bottle cools the blazing sun (the sun's rays soften from orange to yellow and a ring of drops sparkles), lunch lands on a plate on a tree stump, the coat wraps round a drawn peg and the snowflakes stop swirling, and as the moon rises the tent pops up and its door glows warm. Children want to see the trip again and again.
- **The fun net is not a trap.** One small mesh net on the side holds ONE want. At the campfire at the end, that toy jumps out and bounces round the fire. Wants are nice — they just are not what keeps you going. A child who packs a kite gets to watch the kite fly at the campfire.
- **Packing is fiddly in the best way.** Pockets zip open when a thing hovers over them and zip shut on the drop; the bag bulges a little with every item.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** a need is something we must have to live (water, food, shelter, clothing); a want is nice to have. Tell them apart and pack the needs first.
- **Misconceptions and how the trail pushes back:**
  1. *"Anything I really like is a need."* If a want takes a pocket, one need is missing — and at that stop the backpack shakes, the want peeks out of the pocket… and the snowflakes keep swirling (the sun keeps blazing, the plate stays empty, the moon rises over bare ground with nowhere to sleep). The trail waits at that stop. The want does not do the job — that is the definition, shown.
  2. *"Wants are bad."* A want in the fun net is welcomed at the campfire with a show. Words are always "need" and "want" or "nice to have", never "bad", "silly" or "waste".
  3. *"More of one need is enough"* (two foods, no tent). Two foods leave the night stop with nothing to give — "one of each kind" is felt at the stops.
- **Candy is left out** (the K-212 bank files it under wants, but candy IS food, and a K child arguing "it's food!" would be right) — no food item is ever a want here, and no clothing item is ever a want, so every picture has one right answer.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag an item** from the blanket: it lifts 6 px with a shadow (60 ms). Hovering over a pocket: its zip opens (a drawn zip slider runs across, 120 ms). Drop: the item shrinks into the pocket (only its top 30 % shows above the zip line), the zip closes, the bag bulges 2 %. Hovering the net: the mesh stretches; drop: the item sits in the net, visible through the mesh.
- **Pack mode:** any item goes in any pocket — nothing is judged on the drop (the trail judges). A fifth thing for the pockets is refused only because all four are full (the bag gives a little shrug, the item slides back). A second thing for the net slides off (the first stays). Drag out of a pocket/net to unpack.
- **Sort mode:** a need dropped on its own kind's pocket goes in; on another kind's pocket the zip will not open — the item bumps the closed zip and slides back (and its own pocket's icon breathes once); a want dropped on a pocket bumps the zip and slides back, its basket bobbing; any item in the basket is accepted only if it is a want (a need dropped there rolls back out).
- **The trail (Check):** the backpack shrinks to 120 px and hops along the path (each hop 260 ms); at each stop it halts and the flap opens; the matching item flies to the stop and acts (600 ms): water → the sun's rays soften and a ring of drawn droplets sparkles · food → lands on the plate · coat → wraps round a drawn peg, the snowflakes and wind lines fade · tent → pitches under the moon, its door glows warm. Then on to the next stop. At the campfire the net's want jumps out and bounces round the fire (kite: rises on a string; ball: bounces; guitar: three drawn notes float up).
- **Sound (optional):** zips, hops, splash, a tent "fwump", a crackle at the fire; TTS says an item's name when picked up.
- **Free play:** after success the trail can be replayed by tapping the trailhead; items can be swapped and the trip run again to see what each stop does without its need (this exploration is never counted).

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
**Pack** (Check = "Go!", the trail runs):
- **Check with an empty pocket** → hint, no miss, the trail does not start: "Fill all four pockets first." (an empty net is fine.)
- **Miss 1:** the trail runs to the FIRST stop whose need is missing; there the bag shakes, the pocket holding a want (or a second thing of the same kind) opens and the item peeks out and sinks back; the stop keeps going (the snowflakes keep swirling…), and the backpack waits there. One hint per stop: sun → "The hot sun needs something. What do you drink?" · lunch → "Lunch needs something. What do you eat?" · cold → "The cold wind needs something. What keeps you warm?" · night → "Night is coming. Where will you sleep?"
- **Miss 2:** the backpack hops back to the trailhead and the missing need on the blanket lifts and glows; the pocket holding the want gently unzips. Hint: "Swap the {want} for something you need."
- A new move takes the help away until the next Check.
- **Success:** all four stops answered in a row, then the campfire: the want (if any) comes out to play, stars come out over the trail (drawn dots twinkle). "Water, food, a tent and a coat — everything you need! And a {want}, just for fun." (no want: "…everything you need for the trail!")

**Sort** (Check):
- Check with items on the blanket → hint, no miss: "Put every picture in a pocket or the basket."
- (Wrong drops bounce back by themselves — they are not misses.) Since every placement is self-correcting, Sort's Check runs the trail with the sorted needs as the earned show; there is no failing Check once the blanket is empty.
- Miss handling in Sort is per item: an item that has bounced twice gets a soft glow on its right place (help, not a miss). Hint: "A need keeps us alive. A want is nice to have."
- **Success:** the trail runs (one item per stop from each pocket), and every want in the basket comes to the campfire. "Needs in the pockets, wants in the basket."

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a child choose it again?** Yes — a backpack that hops a trail, stops that come alive, a tent that pops up under the moon, and the toy you packed playing by the campfire.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "what would happen without it?" is the needs lesson, and the class sees it happen at the stop; wants are honoured, not shamed.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Library pictures, neutral wording, no shop or money, no score or timer, no food judged as "bad".
4. **The invention:** a trail whose four stops each ask for one need — so a want in a pocket leaves the backpack waiting in the cold or the dark, and a want in the net comes out to play at the campfire.
