## 1 Page concept (base)

**"Butterfly Life Cycle" = the Field-Guide Plate.** One tall flowering plant stands in the middle of the page on a short ground line. The whole life of the butterfly is happening ON it: a speck of an egg on one leaf, a small caterpillar on another, a chrysalis hanging under a third, a butterfly on the flower, each circled by a small coral ring. From each ring a tether runs out to a big lens in a page corner that shows that stage magnified. The egg's lens carries a printed **1**; the other three lenses each have an empty dashed box. Below the plate, a single "loop row": the butterfly lens, a curling coral arrow, one more empty box. The child writes 2, 3, 4 by the lenses, then in the last box writes the number of the picture that comes AFTER the butterfly (the egg: 1).

Why this is the top-quality concept, and why it beats the obvious circle of four arrows (argued fully in §8):
1. **It shows the true biology the circle hides.** Where each stage lives (the egg is laid on the food plant, the caterpillar eats its leaves, the chrysalis hangs, the adult drinks from the flower) and how SMALL the egg really is: the scene draws each stage at real scale (a 5 px speck), the lens magnifies it. Scale honesty is exactly what equal-size cards in a wheel destroy (pedagogy "quality risk": glossy art misleads on scale).
2. **The cycle is ANSWERED, not printed.** In a wheel the return arrow is printed and the child never has to think about it. Here the last box asks "what comes after the butterfly?" and the misconception is visible to the teacher at a glance: a child who thinks life is a line writes **5**; a child who has the cycle writes **1**.
3. **One focal apparatus, pencil first, no words on the body.** Four numerals and one loop numeral are the whole task. The plate is calm (cream page, one teal line plant, four white lenses) with generous air around the stem; nothing is decoration: every element either carries a stage or points at one.
4. **The numbering is a real decision, never a reading order.** Lens positions are drawn from a 7-arrangement whitelist in which neither reading order (row, column, either diagonal sweep) nor either direction round the four corners gives egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly (§4).

**Child's sentence (en source, 139 chars; names only apparatus on the page: boxes, pictures):** "The egg is number 1. Write 2, 3 and 4 to show how it grows. In the last box, write the number of the picture that comes after the butterfly." ⚠ Never "write the number that comes after the butterfly": read literally that asks for the next NUMERAL (5), which would make the misconception the correct reading of the words. Panels keep "the picture that comes after". **Change against the pedagogy base:** answer form numerals-by-the-lens instead of letters-into-a-wheel; `bank`/`decoy` keys drop from the base config, `anchors`/`loop`/`arrangement` replace them (§3); validator rule 9 for the base bans `cut`, `glue`, `tick`, `circle`, `letter`.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- **Nothing on the page states the order.** Lens positions come from a locale-neutral `ARRANGEMENTS` whitelist, computed once and frozen in `data/b5/animal-life-cycles.js` (m, enumerated by node 2026-09-23 over all 24 placements): the adult must sit in a TOP lens (its spot is the flower, which is at the top), and the placement is REJECTED if row-major (TL TR BL BR), column-major (TL BL TR BR), the two mirrored sweeps (TR TL BR BL, TL BL BR TR) or either direction round the corners (TL TR BR BL) starting from the egg reads egg, larva, pupa, adult. Survivors (7): `TL:egg TR:adult BL:pupa BR:larva` · `TL:larva TR:adult BL:egg BR:pupa` · `TL:larva TR:adult BL:pupa BR:egg` · `TL:adult TR:egg BL:larva BR:pupa` · `TL:adult TR:larva BL:egg BR:pupa` · `TL:adult TR:larva BL:pupa BR:egg` · `TL:adult TR:pupa BL:larva BR:egg`. The seed picks one; every locale ships the same one (the tell check therefore runs on the SHIPPED seed once, and it ships x11).
- **Height is not a cue either:** leaf spots are taken from the leaf on the lens's own side and row, so an egg can be high and a chrysalis low. The adult is always high, which points a top-down reader to write 1 on the butterfly: impossible, because 1 is printed on the egg.
- **The child marks:** three numerals (2, 3, 4) in dashed boxes + one numeral in the loop box. Stamps: each lens `data-lcs-lens data-lcs-stage="butterfly.<id>" data-lcs-pos="TL|TR|BL|BR"`; each box `data-lcs-answer="<rank>"` (`blankNumeralBox` answer attribute, never printed); the loop box `data-lcs-answer="1" data-lcs-loop`; root `data-lcs-arrangement="<index>"`, `data-lcs-layout` absent (base byte-identical rule).
- **verify(page):** 4 lenses with 4 distinct stages === `STAGES.butterfly`; ranks re-derived as `STAGES.butterfly.indexOf(stage)+1`, each box's `data-lcs-answer` equals its lens's rank; exactly the `d.anchors` boxes are given and carry their rank as text, every other box is empty; loop box present iff `d.loop`, expects `STAGES[a].indexOf(STAGES[a][(n-1+1) % n]) + 1` = 1 (the modulo IS the cycle, never stored); the placement is in `ARRANGEMENTS`; every tether's end lies within 2 px of its spot ring; no text node in the body other than the given numerals; no `<img>` in the body.
- **A wrong answer is visible to the teacher:** the correct page reads, clockwise from wherever the egg is, as a pattern the teacher checks against the lens pictures in two seconds; the loop box is binary (1 = cycle understood, 5 = "life is a line", anything else = guess).

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **The anchored cycle wheel with a lettered bank (the pedagogy's base, and the genre's standard look).** Four stage slots on a circle, the egg drawn in slot 1, three lettered cards below, letters written into boxes. Honest and buildable, and it IS the page a teacher expects. Rejected for the base because: (a) every competitor page (Twinkl, TPT, Education.com, the harvest's SERP owners) is this circle, so it adds nothing a teacher cannot already print; (b) the letter indirection (find the card, read its letter, carry it to a box) is a working-memory cost at G1 that adds no science; (c) the return arrow is PRINTED, so the cycle is never asked; (d) equal-size cards make the egg as big as the butterfly, the scale error the pedagogy's quality rule forbids; (e) G1-376 is planning a ring for its plant cycle, and two families showing the same ring on one hub read as one family. The ring is not wasted: it becomes F1's pond ring, where cutting and gluing give it a real job.
2. **The butterfly frame** (the four stages inside the four wings of one big outline butterfly, arrows round the body; a well-known craft). Charming from across the room, but the adult stage sits inside its own wing, and a 6-year-old can read the stages as PARTS of the butterfly (the misconception the family exists to kill). Rejected.

**Recommendation: the Field-Guide Plate.** It is the only concept of the three that teaches something the others cannot (where each stage lives, how small the egg is), asks the cycle question outright, keeps one calm focal apparatus with zero words on the body (so it ships identically in 11 locales), and gives the whole family a signature (the lens) that separates it on the hub from G1-203, G1-376 and G3-385. If the critic prefers the wheel for the base, the lens + primitive system transfers unchanged: the wheel's slots become lenses and the loop box can still replace the printed return arrow.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Recognisability of the butterfly egg (the real risk).** At 132 px the egg is 7.9 px tall; at 90 px, 5.4 px (m). It reads as "a leaf with one speck", which is the truth and is unique among the 13 stages (nothing else is a leaf with a single mark), but a child may not know the speck is an egg. Mitigation: the base anchors it (the egg's box is printed 1, and the instruction says "The egg is number 1"); F3 excludes it (pedagogy); F5 uses it only as a CHOICE after the adult butterfly, where the other two choices (caterpillar, chrysalis) are unmistakable. Do NOT enlarge it: the <= 8 % rule is the science.
