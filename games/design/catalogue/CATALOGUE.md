# CATALOGUE — the 200 games

## Preamble: how this table was built

**Subject split — mathematics 110 (55%) · literacy 66 (33%) · science 24 (12%).** Not a division by three. Mathematics carries the most games because it is the most-demanded topic set on every K-3 game site surveyed (F-1: addition within 20 in 14/15 sources, counting 13, subtraction 12, shapes 11, place value 10, tables 10), the most popular wherever a ranking exists (F-2), and it transfers to all eleven languages without content changes (F-7). Literacy is second: letter-sound, rhyme/syllable, blending and sentence conventions are in every one of the twelve curricula (F-22), but the English-specific genres that inflate English-language demand (sight words 1,035 SplashLearn games, silent-e, bossy-r) do not exist in transparent orthographies (F-7, F-24), so literacy games are language-universal by mechanic and per-locale by content (F-217). Science is smallest because it is near-absent as a game genre (F-5: 0-1 of 15 sources) and the curriculum common core at 5-9 is observational (F-23, F-30); its 24 games exist to cover that core in every market, not to chase demand (F-218).

**Band split — 5-6: 64 · 6-8: 98 · 8-9: 38.** The 6-8 band is largest because it is the two years in which all twelve systems establish decoding and +/− to 20 (F-20, F-21) and because the demand table's top topics (addition, subtraction, place value, bonds) sit there (F-1). Every game is levelled by the most conservative system's age for its concept (F-31, F-215): tables, division, fraction notation and clock-to-the-minute are 8-9 everywhere (F-26, F-27, F-28); the 5-6 band only assumes the pre-academic core (F-20 age-5 digest).

**Deliberate repetition.** The load-bearing objectives get 4-8 games across bands and patterns on purpose (F-216, brief §5.2 "practice needs repetition … a decision, not an accident"): counting/cardinality 12, bonds/part-whole 8, +/− to 20 strategies 10, place value 8, shapes 9, letter-sound 10, blending 12. Each repeat differs in pattern OR band OR sub-objective; rows that share (topic, band, pattern) are marked `[deliberate: …]` and the redundancy checker (`_tools/check-redundancy.js`) confirms no un-annotated pair remains. Cross-session spacing is a catalogue property (F-41): a child returning re-practises the skill in a different game.

**Prior art (operator rule, `research/PRIOR-ART.md`).** The site has 204 live free activities covering 142 CCSS codes. A paid game may not be a free activity re-skinned. Rule applied per row: where a free activity already owns the objective, the game must use a different MECHANIC (pattern) and a different presentation (a character goal that IS the task, puzzle/manipulative structure — F-63, F-203) or a different sub-objective; where the free activity already uses the same mechanic (e.g. tap-choice "Which group has more?", ten-frame count, set-the-clock hands, CVC/syllable builders, number-bond find-the-missing-part, sentence builder), the catalogue row avoids that mechanic for that objective. The checker's PRIOR-ART section lists token overlaps for review.

**Cultural neutrality.** No people's names (characters are emoji animals), no national holidays, metric units only, no English-only word play in base content. Money (F-29) and any language-bound list (number words, letters, digraphs, sentences — F-108, F-122, F-124, F-128) live in `LOCALE_DATA`; rows say `[locale]` where that applies.

**Build-first (001-010).** High demand (counting, numerals, ten-frame, bonds, shapes, letters, add-to-20, equals, tables), technically straightforward, and spanning nine of the twelve patterns so pattern problems surface early: P3, P1, P6, P2, P8, P1, P12, P7, P9, P10.

Columns: number · slug · title · subject · topic · band · pattern · objective (one observable behaviour) · description · F-refs.
