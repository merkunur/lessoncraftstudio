## The skill
Doubles (n + n) and halves: a double is a group and its mirror image; a half splits a group into two equal parts.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE HINGED MIRROR. A tray with a hinge line down its middle. Double mode: a numeral card says how many to put on the left; the child taps that many identical library objects onto the left half (one tap, one object) and the hinged mirror swings over and shows the same objects on the right, each reflection joined to its object by a faint thread. The child types the total. Wrong: the reflected side dims away, leaving n on one side so the child sees the double is n and n again; miss 2 numbers the objects 1…2n across both halves. Halve mode: a row of objects lies across the tray and the child drags the hinge along it. Wherever it rests, the left side is reflected over the right as see-through ghosts: off-centre, the ghosts hang past the end of the row or leave real objects uncovered — equal halves are seen as a perfect overlay, never as a running count. When every object has its ghost the child types how many are on each side. An odd row can never overlay: one object always sits on the hinge (the 'can it be shared?' round; the child types how many on each side, and the lonely one stays in view). Right: the mirror flashes and the two halves slide apart into two matching rows.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Doubles 6 as 6 + 1 or 12 + 12; halves by taking "some" off instead of making two EQUAL parts.
- **Pictures:** nouns that read well mirrored: fruits/apple, orange, cherry; bakery/cupcake, doughnut; toys/ball, blocks; flowers/sunflower (identical instances per round, mirrored by CSS)
- **Settings:** Mode: double / halve / mixed · Up to: doubles of 1–5 (totals to 10) / 1–10 (totals to 20)
- **Rounds:** 8+ per mode from distinct n (never 0); keypad answers, so no answer position; in halve mode the row's length and start vary and its true centre is at the stage centre in ≤ 2 rounds (dragging to mid-screen is no shortcut); odd rows ≤ 1 per pass; mixed alternates double/halve.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-247, G1-270, G1-271, G1-286, G1-287, G1-288, G1-296, G1-297, G1-298, G1-299
- **Distinct from:** fractions.partition-equal-shares halves shapes, not quantities; the Twin Pools (#15) make a second group the same COUNT without the doubling sum; dealing-table (below) is about odd vs even, not the double fact.
- **Build risk:** Mirrored library pictures must be symmetric enough not to look wrong (pick nouns that read well flipped).
