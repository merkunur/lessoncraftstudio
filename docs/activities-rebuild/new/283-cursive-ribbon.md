## 1. The pitch
"Each word is sewn with ONE thread: the needle follows the grey stitch holes of the school script, the thread runs on through every join — and if you lift your finger mid-word the thread is cut and a knot shows exactly where the word was broken; the dots and crosses are sewn on last, as separate little stitches."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **A needle and a real-looking thread.** The word waits on cream fabric in a round embroidery hoop as a line of tiny grey stitch holes. The needle sits at the first entry stroke with a coral thread hanging from it. Drag, and the thread follows — a soft twisted line, slightly glossy, *shhhh* — looping through every letter. It feels like sewing, not like a worksheet.
- **One thread, one word.** The thread runs on from letter to letter through the joins; at the end it ties itself off with a tiny bow. Lift your finger halfway and *snip* — the thread is cut, a little knot sits at that spot, and the needle waits there for you. The word now visibly has a break. Nobody needs to be told what "joined" means.
- **Dots and crosses last.** After the bow, the dots above i and j and the cross on t light up as small targets: tap for a French knot (a little round knot, *pop*), drag across for a bar stitch. Try to dot the i while the word is still going and the needle cannot jump there — a dashed jump line flickers and fades: the thread is still busy.
- **Thread colours.** A spool rack of five colours sits by the hoop; tap one to change thread for the next word. Pure toy.
- **The sampler.** Every finished word slides off the hoop into a long **sampler cloth** at the top of the page — over a session the child's own embroidered words fill it, in their chosen colours. A finished sampler can be printed (the tool's print sheet, §12).
- **Earned show per round:** the bow pulls tight, the hoop gives a half-turn wiggle, the embroidered word lifts off with a shimmer and stitches itself onto the sampler. In read mode the chosen picture is "sewn" next to the word as a patch with a running-stitch border. No timer, score, streak or lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (the worksheets):** form the lowercase cursive letters of the locale's school script with the right entry, movement and exit; join two letters (bottom joins, top joins from o b v w r, the national digraphs); write whole words in one movement, adding dots, crosses and accents last; form capitals as the start of a name (joining where the script joins, lifting where it lifts); copy a sentence word by word; read words written in cursive.
- **Misconceptions and the push-back:**
  1. **Lifting the pen between letters** (printing letters and calling it joined): a lift cuts the thread and leaves a knot — the break is a visible object on the fabric, at exactly that place.
  2. **Forming letters separately and joining afterwards** (drawing the join last): the thread only advances forward along the stitch path from the entry stroke; skipping ahead to a later letter is refused (the needle twitches back toward the next hole, a small "this way" chevron pulses at the needle).
  3. **Dotting and crossing mid-word:** dot/cross targets stay grey until the word's thread is tied off; a tap on one mid-word shows the impossible jump (dashed line from the needle) and nothing is sewn.
  4. **Cutting corners / scribbling over the letter shape:** the thread only follows inside a narrow corridor round the centre-line of the letter; outside it, the thread pulls taut and stops (it never draws where the letter isn't).
  5. **Starting a letter in the wrong place / going the wrong way round** (a, d, o, c start at the top right and go anticlockwise): the needle starts at the entry stroke and the holes ahead are the only ones that take the thread — the way round is built into the path.
  6. **Thinking every lift is wrong:** where the school script really lifts (Danish and Norwegian after f g j q y / f g j y z, many capitals before the next letter, between words), the stitch path shows a small dotted hop arc — lifting there leaves no knot.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag from the needle** (the touch must start within 22 px of the needle): the needle follows the finger, constrained to the stitch path: the core projects the finger onto the path and accepts it only if it lies within the **corridor** (12 px at 704 / 9 px at 360 from the centre-line; letters level 16 / 12) AND its path position is between 10 px behind and 26 px ahead of the needle. Accepted → the needle moves there and the thread is drawn up to it (≤ 16 ms per frame, so it answers instantly), each passed stitch hole fills with thread colour, a faint "shhh" while moving.
  - **Outside the corridor:** the needle stops at its last good point; the thread pulls taut toward the finger (a 1 px straight grey "tension" line, 150 ms) and springs back. Nothing is drawn off the letter.
  - **Too far ahead along the path** (skipping): ignored; a small chevron at the needle points the way along the path.
- **Release mid-path:** at a **hop point** (a script lift, a word boundary, a non-joining capital) → no knot; the needle waits at the next entry hole, which pulses. Anywhere else → *snip*: a 6 px knot is drawn at the release point, the thread end frays (two tiny strands), the needle waits there; the next touch near the needle continues — the knot remains as a visible break.
- **End of the word's path:** the thread ties off (a little bow, 300 ms, "tk"). The dot/cross/accent targets turn from grey to the thread colour and pulse once.
  - **Tap a dot / accent target** → a French knot pops in (round 8 px knot). **Drag along a cross target** (≥ 60 % of it) → a bar stitch. The order of marks is free.
  - Tapping a target before the bow → a dashed line flickers from the needle to the target and fades (400 ms); nothing is sewn.
- **Unpick** (a small seam-ripper button, 44 px): pulls the thread back out along its path (animated, 600 ms) to the start of the current word — always available (undo).
- **Spool:** tap a spool → the needle's thread changes colour for the next word (the current word keeps its colour).
- **Read mode:** tap a picture tile → it lifts 6 px (another tap swaps); Check.
- **Keyboard / switch access:** tracing has a keyboard path for children who cannot drag: Space held + Right arrow advances the needle along the path at a steady pace (a "sewing machine pedal"), releasing Space mid-path cuts the thread (same rule); Enter on a target sews a mark. Read mode: Left/Right + Enter.

## 6. The rounds
All content comes from the locale's bank (exported per locale); the core NEVER builds a word path from a font at runtime — paths are precomputed (§12).

**Letters level (8 rounds per locale):** one letter per round, the first 8 letters of the unit's lessons in lesson order — measured from the bank (`data/b6/cursive-writing.js` + `locales/cursive-writing.<loc>.json`, 2026-10-17): en us-trad `i t u w e l b h` · de-va `i u t n m r e l` (de-la `i u t e l b h k`) · es mx `a e i o u c d g` · pt br `a e i o b c d f` · fr-trad `e l b i u t h k` · it-trad `a e i o u c d g` · nl `i u t w e l b h` · da dk-uloopet `i u t n m r v e` · no `i u t n m r v w`. None of them is a lift letter in its unit (da lifts after f g j q y, no after f g j y z — the bank's `unit(…, lift)`; every other shipped unit lifts after none), so every letter chain is ONE thread. Each round has **two passes**: pass 1 traces a joined chain of three ("iii" as one stitch path, as on G2-377); pass 2 traces the chain again with a **fading guide** — holes only at the entry stroke, the turning points and the exit (every hole that is not one of those is removed), the corridor unchanged.
**Joins level (8 rounds; 9–10 where the bank has them, sampled 8 per pass):** the bank's pairs and words — en `ol doll · on moon · br bread · wa wash · oa boat · ve five · ch chair · sh fish`; de `ol Wolke · on Sonne · or Tor · ob Robbe · br Zebra · wa Schwan · ch Buch · sch Tasche · ei Seife · au Maus`; es `ol sol · on mono · br libro · va vaca · ve nieve · ch leche · ña niña · bl blanco · ue huevo`; pt `ol sol · or flor · ob lobo · br cabra · va vaca · ve neve · bo bola · ão pão`; fr `ou loup · on mouton · or porte · br arbre · oi poire · ch chat · bl table · va vache`; it `ol sole · on leone · br libro · va uva · ve neve · or fiore · ch chiave · gn ragno · gl foglia`; nl `oe boek · ou koud · ui huis · ie fiets · br brood · wa water · or worm · eu neus`; da `ol stol · br brød · bo bog · or jord · ve løve · sk skib · st sten · hu hus · ma mand`; no `ol stol · or mor · br brød · va vann · ei vei · sk sko · hu hus · ta tak · år gård`. Round = the pair twice joined ("ol ol" as on G2-385 — two separate threads, a hop between) then the example word (one thread). A pair never starts with a lift letter (the worksheet's join proof: da f g j q y, no f g j y z).
**Words level (8 rounds per pass from the bank's 24–26 pictured words):** ≤ 8 letters; per pass ≥ 3 words carry a dot, cross or accent (en i j t x; de i j t ä ö ü; es i j t á é í ó ú ñ; pt i j t ã õ á é ê ç; fr i j t é è ê à ç; it i j t à è ì ò ù; nl i j t ij ë; da/no i j t æ ø å) and ≥ 2 contain a pair from the joins bank; de nouns keep their capital (cap-row ratio).
**Capitals level (8 rounds):** capital + a first name beginning with it, from the activity's own native-authored name list per locale (8 common first names with distinct initials, chosen by the native panel; the worksheet's `lib/sentence-bank.js` names are the starting point — en M B E L A T + 2). Round = the capital alone (pass 1), then the name (one thread, OR two threads with a hop where the script's capital does not join — e.g. many us-trad and fr-trad capitals; the hop comes from the font's own ink components, §12).
**Sentences level (8 rounds):** the bank's 6 sentences per locale + 2 native-authored (G3-401's bank style: short, no proper nouns beyond the bank's), each word one thread, hops between words; pass 2 = the merged "Joined Line" fading copy (holes every third, entry holes kept), the first pass's sentence lifting up to the sampler as the second copy appears.
**Read mode (8 rounds per pass from the words pool):** one embroidered word + 3 pictures; distractor rule (G2-387's anti-tell): at least one distractor shares the target's first letter or differs in length by ≤ 1, no two pictures a child names the same way (PICTURE-RULINGS: e.g. never duck + duckling), all three from the same locale's pool.
- **Answer position (read mode):** the target picture stands in tiles 1/2/3 with counts 3/3/2 per pass (rotated per pass), never the same tile three rounds running. Writing levels have no position (the child traces).
- **Order:** order-only reshuffle per pass (letters stay in lesson order within a pass only on the first pass; later passes reshuffle).

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Writing levels:** when the word's thread is tied off and every mark is sewn, Check pulses (it can be pressed any time).
  - **Check before the thread reaches the end** → hint "Keep sewing to the end of the word." (no miss). **Marks missing** → "Now sew on the dots and crosses." (no miss).
  - **Miss** = the word is complete but has a knot (a lift where the script does not lift).
    - **Miss 1:** every knot blinks coral twice, then the thread unpicks itself back to the FIRST knot (the good part before it stays sewn); the needle waits there. Hint: "The thread was cut here. Sew on from the needle without lifting your finger."
    - **Miss 2:** in addition, the stretch of path from the needle to the next hop point (or the word's end) shows its holes enlarged and a soft glow travels along it at writing speed, once — the movement to make in one go. Hint: "Follow the glow all the way, then lift."
  - A new stitch clears the help until the next Check.
  - **Success:** the bow, the wiggle, the word joining the sampler (§2). Bubble: letters "Lovely joined {letter}s!" · joins "Yes — {pair} joined, and {word} in one thread!" · words "Yes — {word}: one thread, dots and crosses last!" · capitals "Yes — {name}!" · sentences "Yes — every word in one thread!"
- **Read mode:**
  - **Check with no picture chosen** → hint "Read the word and tap its picture." (no miss).
  - **Miss 1:** the needle re-sews the word slowly in front of the child (a ghost needle, 2.5 s, the letters glowing one after another as the thread passes) — reading by watching the hand move. Hint: "Watch the needle write it. Read it again."
  - **Miss 2:** a small print letter appears under each cursive letter (grey, 16 px, the locale's school print font). Hint: "Read the print letters under it."
  - **Success:** the picture is sewn on beside the word as a patch (a running-stitch border runs round it, 600 ms). Bubble: "Yes — {word}!"

## 11. Distinct from
- **New #218 the Word Ribbon** (a writing line that unrolls under the pencil, space stones between words — PRINT word spacing), **#222 the Pencil Path** (a car drives pre-writing strokes), **#5 the Fading Line** (numeral tracing), **rows 73/74** (the writing carriage; the rewind rail) and **tool letter-studio** (print letter stroke order on lined guides): none joins letters. ## 12. Build plan
- **Core state:** `{locale, unit, level, mode, round, pass, path:{points:[{x,y,s}], hops:[s], joins:[s], marks:[{kind:'dot'|'cross'|'accent', geom}]}, needleS:number, sewnTo:number, knots:[s], marksSewn:Set, tied:bool, pick:null|i, miss, help}`.
- **Core functions:** `project(finger)` → nearest path point within the corridor (spatial grid over the path points) · `advance(finger)` (the window rule −10/+26 px of arclength) · `release()` → hop or knot · `tie()` · `sewMark(i)` (refused before `tied`) · `unpickToFirstKnot()` · `isComplete()` · `check()` · read: `pick(i)`.
- **The centre-line data (the HIGH-risk part — built and gated offline):** a build tool `tools/cursive-centrelines.js` (scratch, under `scripts/worksheet-gen/tools/`):
  1. renders each needed string (every letter chain, pair, word, name, sentence word of the 9 banks) in its vendored Playwrite woff2 in headless Chromium at x-height 200 px;
  2. splits the ink into connected components (the worksheet join proof already measured that each pedagogy pair is ONE component plus its expected diacritic pieces) → main component(s) = the thread path(s); small components = mark targets; extra main components = script lifts (hops);
  3. skeletonises each main component (Zhang–Suen thinning; Playwrite is a monoline school font, so the medial axis is clean) and orders the skeleton into ONE path from the entry stroke (the leftmost-lowest end point) to the exit, choosing at every crossing the branch with the smallest turning angle and honouring a per-letter **stroke recipe** table (26 lowercase + the national letters, shared across units, with per-unit overrides: e.g. `a`, `d`, `g`, `q`, `o`, `c` begin at their top-right and run anticlockwise before the down-stroke; `e`, `l`, `b`, `h`, `k`, `f` loops run upward on the right of the stem in looped scripts);
  4. smooths and resamples the path every 3 px; stores per string `{points, hops, joins (s at letter boundaries), marks}` in `mini tools/cursive-ribbon-data.json` (≈ 220 strings × 9 locales).
  - **Gate (verify-cursive-ribbon-paths.js):** every path point lies inside the rendered ink; stroking the path at the font's stroke width covers ≥ 98 % of the main-component ink; component counts equal the worksheet's measured join-proof counts; the path's direction at each letter's start matches the stroke recipe (sign of the tangent); no path segment jumps > 6 px; every word fits the hoop at its level's x-height at 320 px wide (the measured advance width ≤ 270 px). **Human gate:** a contact sheet ANIMATION per locale (the needle sewing every string) is opened and read by me and by the native reviewer before ship — a wrong ductus is a pedagogical defect the gate's tangent check may miss.
- **Gate's own ground truth (verify-cursive-ribbon-core.js):** synthetic finger traces (on-path, off-corridor, skipping ahead, lifting at a hop, lifting mid-letter) produce exactly: sewn / taut-stop / refused / no knot / knot; marks refused before the bow; unpick restores the state to the first knot; read rounds: one target, distractor rule met (shared initial or length ±1), slot balance; sv/fi produce no rounds (refusal) and the manifest row lists exactly the 9 locales.
- **Mutations:** a mid-word lift leaves no knot · a hop point knots · a mark sewable before the bow · skipping ahead along the path accepted · off-corridor finger draws thread · the path entry at the wrong end (reversed ductus) passes the gate · weather/sun used (no label) · a read round with two pictures of one name · the target always in tile 1 · sv served a round.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — sewing a word with a glossy thread, choosing the colour, tying a bow and watching it join a growing sampler is a craft, not a worksheet.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — it shows the class what "joined" means (one thread), makes a lift visible exactly where it happened, enforces dots and crosses last, follows each country's own school script and ruling, and covers letters, joins, words, capitals, sentences and reading cursive.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — the real school script of their country (or an honest refusal where there is none), calm, no score, no handwriting "recognition" pretending to judge.
4. **The invention, in one sentence:** each word is sewn with one unbroken thread along the school script's stitch holes, so a lift cuts the thread and leaves a knot where the word broke, and the dots and crosses can only be sewn on once the word's thread is tied off.
