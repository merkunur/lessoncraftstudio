## 1. The pitch
"Press Check and a weaving shuttle runs along each row of the picture and then the same row of your copy, left to right — it only passes a row where every square is a twin, and it snags on the exact square that is not."

## 2. Why a child will want it again (the fun)
- **Painting tiles is a toy.** Two fat paint pots; tap a square and it fills with a little "plip" and a 120 ms ink-spread; drag across squares and a run fills like a brush stroke. Tap a painted square again and it lifts off clean. No mess, no wrong colour stays.
- **The finger shadow.** Wherever the finger rests on the empty grid, a soft band lights that row and that column — on the copy *and on the model* — so the child sees exactly which model square they are copying. It feels like the two grids are holding hands.
- **The shuttle.** At Check a fat wooden weaving shuttle (drawn geometry, 40×18, carrying a teal thread) sets off at the top-left. It glides along row 1 of the model, hops the gap and glides along row 1 of the copy — always left to right, the way the child should count — laying its thread across both rows. A twin row: the thread lies flat and taut with a soft "zzip", and the shuttle drops to the next row. A row that is not a twin: the shuttle **snags** with a jolt on the first wrong square of the copy, the thread kinks, and that square and its partner square on the model flicker together. Children watch it like a marble run — will it get through? When it passes the last row, the threads pull tight into a woven cloth, the mosaic goes softly glassy and **the library picture it was drawn from fades in underneath** — the duck, the teapot, the giraffe — then pops forward and hops. Children copy to find out what the picture *really* is.
- **The gallery.** Each finished copy flies (scaled to 56 px) into a gallery strip of nine frames; at the end of the pass the nine library pictures stand in their frames over their mosaics — a wall of things the child copied.
- Free play: between rounds the child can paint anything on the empty grid and send the shuttle through it; nothing is checked.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill.** Copy a pattern square by square: keep the row, keep the column, keep the side; first by looking (6×6, no labels), then by the letter–number of each square (labelled 6×6 and 8×8), then a big grid with no help (G1-416).
- **Misconception 1: losing the row** (counting across and drifting into the row above or below). Pushback: the finger shadow lights the row on both grids while copying; at Check the shuttle compares the copy **one row at a time against the same row of the model** — the exact strategy the child needs — and snags on the first row that drifted, so the child fixes rows top to bottom instead of hunting the whole board.
- **Misconception 2: mirror-copying** (counting columns from the wrong side). Pushback: the shuttle reads both rows in the same direction, from the left edge; a mirrored row puts its tiles on the wrong side, so the shuttle snags at the first square (counted from the left) that is not a twin, with the partner square on the model flickering on the opposite side. Every figure is asymmetric left–right, top–bottom and under a half-turn (gate-measured), so a mirrored or upside-down copy always snags.
- **Misconception 3 (labelled grids): reading the code as row-then-column or counting the label as a square.** Pushback: tapping a model square lights its letter and number on the model's rulers and the same letter and number on the copy's rulers; the label lights are the bridge, and the shadow's crossing point is the square.

## 5. The hand
- **Choose a pot:** tap → raised + ring (2-colour only).
- **Paint:** pointerdown on a copy cell → if empty or a different colour: paint with the pot (ink-spread 120 ms, "plip" 660 Hz 40 ms); if the same colour: clear (lift 100 ms, softer "plup"). The pointerdown decides the brush mode (paint or clear) for the whole drag; dragging across cells applies that mode to each cell entered (so a run never toggles back and forth).
- **Finger shadow** (on in every grid except "8×8, no help", §8): while the pointer is over/pressed on a copy cell, its row and column bands (teal 10 % fill) light on the copy and on the model at the same indices; the crossing cell on the model gets a 2 px teal ring. Updates on every pointermove (< 16 ms).
- **Label lights** (labelled grids): tap or hover a **model** cell → its letter and number glow on the model's rulers and the same letter and number glow on the copy's rulers, and the copy cell at that address shows a dotted ring for 1.5 s. Tap a ruler letter/number → that whole column/row lights on both boards.
- **Keyboard:** arrows move a focus ring on the copy (the shadow follows it), Space paints/clears, 1/2 choose the pot, Enter = Check.
- **Undo:** everything before Check; after a miss the copy returns and stays editable.
- **Free play:** after a right round, the next round waits behind a "next" arrow; meanwhile the child can paint on the cleared copy board and send the shuttle (no hints, no misses) or tap gallery frames to see each picture again.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing painted** → no miss; the copy board wiggles: "Paint the same squares as the picture."
- **Miss 1** → the shuttle runs; every twin row above gets its flat thread; at the **first non-twin row** it snags on the first wrong square, then the whole row's disagreements flicker **in BOTH grids at once** (3 pulses, 900 ms): a tile painted where the model is empty flickers on the copy and its model cell shows a hollow coral ring; a model tile left uncopied flickers on the model and its copy cell shows a hollow ring; a wrong colour flickers as a two-colour split. Rows below the snag are untouched (the shuttle never got there). The snagged squares keep a small dot in their corner until the child touches that cell, and the passed rows keep their thread (help that stays because it is earned: those rows are proven). Hint: "The shuttle got stuck in this row. Find the squares that are not twins."
- **Miss 2** (a snag in the same row again) → the shuttle backs up to the left edge and walks that row **square by square on both grids at once**, a numeral 1, 2, 3 … popping over each square as it passes (counting from the left — the cure for mirror-counting and drift), and stops on the first wrong square with both partner squares ringed; on labelled grids that row's numeral and the square's letter glow on both rulers. Hint: "Count the squares from the left on both grids."
- A new paint clears the marks in that cell (help taken away until the next Check); every Check runs the shuttle from the top again, so a fix in one row can never break a passed row unseen.
- **Success** → the shuttle passes the last row, the threads pull tight into cloth, glassy mosaic, the library picture fades in under it and hops; the copy flies to the gallery. Bubble: "Twins! Look what you copied." and under the revealed picture a caption with its name (`image-vocabulary.js` singular, page locale, no article — a bare caption needs no gender agreement in any locale). Pass end: the nine gallery frames bob in turn.

## 11. Distinct from
- **#229 The Blink Viewer** (another batch) lays one picture over another and colours the differing pixels; this design deliberately does **not** overlay — the check is a row-by-row pass of a shuttle, because the skill being trained is *keeping the row and counting from the left*, and the shuttle performs exactly that procedure in front of the child. **Folding Sheet** tool (colour squares, fold at a crease = symmetry): reflection is its point; here a reflection is exactly what snags. **#284 The Crossing Lights** (G2 grid coordinates: letter/number switches throw beams and a hidden pixel picture appears): finding a square from a code; here the labels are a passive bridge during *copying*, and the reveal is a real library picture under the merged mosaic. **#285 The Fold Press** (wet-paint symmetry completion): reflection again. **Pattern Bench / #226 Bunting Line**: repeating units, not position copying. Registry #1–#22: no grid copying.

## 12. Build plan
- **Core state:** `{ size, labels, colours, guide, rounds, r, model:Set<"c,r:k">, copy:Map, pot, misses, marks, gallery }`. Functions: `paint(c,r)`, `clear(c,r)`, `stroke(cells, mode)`, `diff()` → `{extra:[], missing:[], wrongColour:[]}`, `rowTwin(r)`, `shuttleRun()` → `{passed, snagRow, snagCol, rowDiff}` (rows top to bottom, each row left to right), `check()`, `nextRound()`, `shadowFor(c,r)`, `addressOf(c,r)` → "B2".
- **Gate's own truth (`verify-tile-twin-core.js`):** re-parses every figure from its own copy of the tables; asserts tile counts in range, connectivity, asymmetry under the three flips (shape-only), ≥ 4 rows and ≥ 4 columns used; `check()` accepts only the exact set (and colours); every single-cell edit of the right copy fails; the left–right mirror, top–bottom mirror, half-turn and every one-row/one-column shift of the right copy fail and report the expected flicker sets; `shuttleRun().snagRow` = the topmost row of the diff and `snagCol` its leftmost wrong square; no row below the snag is reported; the miss-2 counting walk stops on `snagCol`; labelled addresses letter-then-number; reveal pictures exist in colour themes.
- **Mutations:** accept a mirrored copy · ignore colour in 2-colour mode · flicker only on one grid · shuttle passes a non-twin row · shuttle scans right to left · shuttle reports rows below the snag · miss-2 count starts from the right · address as number-then-letter · drag toggles each cell (mode not latched) · a symmetric figure admitted · shadow lights a different row on the model.
- **local-test:** real pointer painting of every figure in every grid size at 7 widths; one deliberate mirrored copy per grid size must snag at the expected row and square; 320 px 8×8 cells ≥ 34 px.
- **Pictures:** 18 reveal pictures seen on `b4-tiletwin.png` (all read clearly; orientation of each mosaic matches its picture). **Nothing blocked.**

## 13. The four questions
1. **Choose it again?** Yes — painting tiles, a row-and-column shadow that follows the finger, a shuttle to cheer through every row, and every right copy turns into the real picture.
2. **Teacher on the board?** Yes — it is the "reproduction sur quadrillage / Gitterbild" exercise with the two classic errors (row drift, mirror copy) caught by a shuttle that checks the way a teacher checks — row by row, from the left — and the label setting builds the coordinate bridge.
3. **Parent trust?** No words, no score, library pictures as the reward, works on a phone with a big copy grid.
4. **The invention:** a weaving shuttle runs each row of the picture and then the same row of the copy, from the left — it only passes twin rows and snags on the exact square that is not.
