## 1. The pitch
"Tilly's ribbons live on shelves far apart and can't be brought together — so you carry the same cord to each one, and the cord leaves a little tag saying 'shorter than me', 'just as long as me' or 'longer than me'."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- The cord is a toy: a soft teal cord with a brass clip, dragged across the shop; it **lays itself along** a ribbon with a wiggle, as if measuring were something it loves doing.
- The shop is a real puzzle for the eyes: one ribbon hangs down from a hook, one lies along a high shelf, one lies on the low counter, all starting at different places — and the hanging one **looks** longer than it is (the vertical–horizontal illusion is real for adults too). Children are genuinely surprised when the cord says otherwise.
- Every visit leaves a **tag** on the ribbon's hook: a little picture of the cord against the ribbon. After three visits the shop is labelled, and the answer is something the child has found out.
- The earned show: Tilly (library cat) jumps down, gathers the three ribbons and lays them **side by side on the counter from one edge** — the direct comparison the shelves made impossible — and they line up exactly as the cord said. The cord coils itself up.
- Free play: the cord can be carried to anything on the board (even Tilly) and laid along it; the tag game works on everything.
- No score, no timer, no lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (1.MD.A.1): when two objects cannot be put side by side, compare each with a **third object** that stays the same (the cord), and reason: red is longer than the cord, the cord is longer than blue, so red is longer than blue. Order three objects by length (K-033/034/036, G1-140).
- Misconceptions and push-back:
  1. **Judging by eye across distance and direction** (the hanging ribbon "looks longest"; the one further right "looks longer") → the ribbons are deliberately in different places and directions, so the eye is unreliable; the cord gives the same answer wherever it is laid.
  2. **Comparing only the far ends** (ignoring where each starts) → the ribbons start at different points; the cord's clip always fastens at a ribbon's start pin, and the ribbon's end is read against the cord's end — the start is part of the act.
  3. **Thinking the reference changes** ("the cord is longer now") → the cord is visibly the same object every time (same colour, same clip, same knot at its end), never stretches, and every round uses it at the same length.
  4. **Transitivity not used** → in the order rounds there is one shorter, one as long, one longer than the cord, so the three tags alone put the ribbons in order — and Tilly's side-by-side reveal confirms it.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag the cord** out of its basket by the clip → it straightens and follows (the knot trails behind on a soft spring, 80 ms). Drop the clip **near a ribbon's start pin** (within 40 px; snapping is generous) → the cord **lays itself along the ribbon** (360 ms, the cord flows into place, a gentle zip sound):
  - ribbon shorter: the ribbon ends, the cord carries on — its spare end lifts and wiggles twice;
  - ribbon as long: both ends meet, a soft double click (1.2 kHz), the ends glint;
  - ribbon longer: the cord's knot stops, the ribbon's extra glows faintly for 600 ms.
  Then the tag flips onto the place's tag hook (200 ms) and the cord springs back to the basket (300 ms). Measuring the same ribbon again just re-shows it.
- Dropped elsewhere (not near a start pin) → the cord glides home; if dropped on a ribbon's far end, the clip slides along to the start pin first (a small showing that measuring starts at the start).
- **Tap a ribbon** → it is chosen (a teal outline 3 px, lifted 2 px). Tap another → the choice moves. **Check** (shell) confirms.
- **Order rounds**: drag a ribbon swatch (a 64 × 24 copy that peels off the ribbon when dragged) onto an order slot; swatches can be swapped; Check when three are placed.
- **Undo**: tags can be removed by tapping them (they fly back to the cord); choices can be changed until Check.
- **Free play**: the cord can be laid along Tilly (clip at her paws — she sits still and purrs), along the shelf, along the counter; tags only appear on ribbon places. After a solved round all three ribbons stay on the counter side by side and the cord can be laid along each.
- Optional TTS: long-press a ribbon → its colour word.

- **Answer position**: at every showing the three ribbons are dealt to the three places (hook / shelf / counter, or stool / box / shelf) and their start pins are offset by a random 0–2 u. Gated over 300 showings per level: the answer is in each place 28–39 % (so the hanging ribbon is the answer about a third of the time, and never always in the illusion rounds' favour); the answer is the object that sits furthest right in ≤ 40 %; for tall things the object whose TOP is highest is the answer in ≤ 50 % (stands do this). Honest note (critic): drawn to scale, the longest ribbon always has the largest on-screen extent, so "looks longest" cannot be balanced away — what the design does instead is make the eye UNRELIABLE: in every question kind three of the seven rounds are **close calls** (the answer and its nearest rival differ by exactly 1 u and lie in different directions — L3, L5, L7 · S4, S5, S6 · C4, C5, C6), where the vertical–horizontal illusion makes the hanging one look longer than it is. Close-call rounds are shuffled like the illusion rounds are kept: the place shuffle may move them, but always keeps one of the two close rivals on the hook (hanging) and the other lying flat. Gated: each kind has ≥ 3 close calls and in 100 % of their showings the two rivals lie in different directions. Rounds flagged "illusion" keep their default place (it is the lesson).
- **Order**: order-only reshuffle per pass (`nextTask`), never the same sequence twice; Mixed draws 3 + 3 + 3 per pass with the question kind never three times in a row.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing chosen** → bubble "Carry the cord to each ribbon first, then tap one." (no miss) · order with empty slots → "Put all three ribbons on the rail." (no miss)
- **Miss 1** → the cord **travels by itself** to every ribbon that has no tag yet and to the ribbon the child chose (1.1 s each), laying itself along each, and leaves all three tags. The chosen ribbon's tag pulses. Bubble (longest): "Look at the tags: only one ribbon is longer than the cord." · (shortest) "…only one ribbon is shorter than the cord." · (as long) "…only one ribbon ends exactly where the cord ends." · (order) "Look at the tags: which is shorter than the cord, which is just as long, which is longer?"
- **Miss 2** → the three **tags slide off their hooks and line up** in the bubble column, in the order shorter / as long / longer, each with a thread back to its ribbon (the cord in the middle of each tag aligned, so the coloured bars visibly step up). The child still taps (or orders). Bubble: "The cord is the same every time — so the tags tell you the order."
- A new tap or drag takes the help away until the next Check.
- **Success** → the chosen ribbon glows; then Tilly hops down, and the three ribbons **fly to the counter and lie side by side from the same start pin**, shortest at the front (500 ms, staggered 120 ms); the cord lays itself along the back as a fourth line, and its knot sits exactly where the tags said. Tilly does a slow blink. Bubble:
  - longest: "Yes! {colour} is longer than the cord — and longer than the others."
  - shortest: "Yes! {colour} is shorter than the cord — and shorter than the others."
  - as long: "Yes! {colour} is just as long as the cord."
  - order: "{colour1}, {colour2}, {colour3} — from shortest to longest!"
  - tall: "tallest / shortest / just as tall as the cord" and "from shortest to tallest!"

## 13. The four questions (answered, honestly)
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a cord that lays itself along things, a shop where the eyes are tricked, tags that pile up, and Tilly's side-by-side reveal that proves the cord right.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — it is 1.MD.A.1's indirect-comparison clause done honestly: the objects truly cannot be compared directly, the third object never changes, and the transitivity ("longer than the cord, the cord is longer than blue") is visible in the tags.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — calm, no numbers or words on the board, a library cat, nothing timed or sold.
4. **The invention in one sentence:** the same cord travels to ribbons that can never meet and leaves a tag on each — shorter, as long, or longer than me — so the tags put the ribbons in order.
