## 1. The pitch
"Drop two cards in the twin stand: each one pours its hundreds, its tens and its ones into its own three glass tubes — a numeral pours digit by digit, a word card pours word by word, an expanded card pours each part — and the two cards only clip together when every pair of tubes stands level: 305 and 350 fill the same hundreds tube but not the same tens."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **Pouring is irresistible.** The twin stand is a drawn wooden rack with two card cradles and six glass tubes (three under each cradle: hundreds teal, tens coral, ones gold), each tube marked 1 to 9 with faint lines. Drop a card in a cradle and it tips like a jug: its liquid runs into the tubes with a gurgle, one place at a time, the level rising mark by mark (80 ms per mark). An empty place pours nothing — the tube stays dry, and a dry tube next to a full one is impossible to miss.
- **Words pour in their own order.** "three hundred forty-seven" pours the hundreds, then the tens, then the ones. German *dreihundert­sieben­und­vierzig* pours hundreds, then **ones**, then tens — the child watches the seven arrive before the forty. Danish *tohundrede og syvogfyrre* does the same. The language's quirks become something you can see.
- **The clip.** When all three pairs are level, the tubes chime in a rising triad, the two cards slide together and clip into one double card, which flies to the found shelf. The third card of that number is then dropped against the double card — one more pour, one more chime, and the number's three cards fan out like a hand of cards.
- **The end-of-round show:** the three found numbers stand on the shelf as fans; their tubes pour one last time side by side, and the round's digit-sharing becomes visible: three hundreds tubes at the same height, the tens and ones all different.
- **Free play:** any two cards may be compared at any time — the stand never scolds; levels just are or are not level.
- **⭐ FROSTED GLASS — the stand never reads the hard card FOR the child (critic, 2026-10-17).** If every card poured into clear glass, the stand would translate "four hundred twenty-seven" into digits on the first try and a child could pour pairs at random until two stand level without ever reading a number word. So the card that carries the round's skill pours into **frosted tubes**: the **word card** in "three ways", the **bright-digit numeral** in "digit values". Frosted glass shows the liquid ARRIVING, place by place in the card's own order (the German seven still clouds the ones tube before the forty clouds the tens), but not its HEIGHT. Numerals, expanded cards and value cards pour into clear glass (they are already digits). On a clip the frost melts away in a shimmer and the levels stand revealed side by side — the earned show. Before the first miss of a round a non-level pour only wobbles; the help ladder (§7) then rings the place that differs (miss 1) and finally splits the word over its tubes (miss 2) — so reading the word is always the child's job first.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** read and write numbers to 1000 as base-ten numerals, number names and expanded form, and match them (2.NBT.A.3); know the value of a digit by its place (G2-225).
- **Misconception 1 — the place of the zero** (305 = 350 = 3005; "three hundred five" written 3005). **Pushback:** 305 pours a full hundreds tube to 3, *nothing* into tens, 5 into ones; 350 pours 3, 5, nothing. Side by side the tens and ones tubes are mirror opposites. No card can produce "3005" — there is no fourth tube.
- **Misconception 2 — reading the digits as a list, not as places** (matching 247 with "two hundred seventy-four" because the same digits appear). **Pushback:** every round puts permutations of the same digits together (247, 274, 427), so only the tubes' *positions* separate them: the tens tubes are 4 against 7.
- **Misconception 3 — the digit's face value vs its value** (in 472 the 7 "is 7"). **Pushback (digit values setting):** a numeral card with one lit digit pours only that digit — and it runs into the tube of its place: the 7 of 721 into hundreds, the 7 of 172 into tens, the 7 of 217 into ones. Same digit, three different tubes.

## 5. The hand
- **Into a cradle:** drag a card into a cradle, or tap it (it flies to the first empty cradle, 200 ms). Tap a card in a cradle to send it back.
- **Pour:** when both cradles are full, both cards tip and pour, place by place in the card's own order (numerals and expanded: hundreds, tens, ones; words: in the order the words name the places), 80 ms per mark, a gurgle per place. The first liquid moves within 100 ms of the second drop.
- **Level:** all three pairs level → chime, the frost melts, clip, fly to the shelf (500 ms). **Not level:** the liquid wobbles in all six tubes (no place is named yet); the cards stay; swap either one.
- **Complete the triple:** drop the remaining card of a found number into a cradle and the double card sits in the other cradle automatically (it can be tapped there from the shelf too).
- **Keyboard:** Tab through cards, Enter puts one in the next cradle, Backspace empties the last cradle.
- **Sound** (bonus): gurgle per place (pitch by place), the triad chime on a clip; TTS reads a word card on tap where a voice exists.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **The pour is the Check.** One card in a cradle and nothing else → the empty cradle pulses; **"Put a second card in the stand."** (no miss).
- **Miss 1 — the uneven place is named:** after the first non-level pour in a round, the uneven tube pair gets a coral ring and the CLEAR card's level shows its number beside its tube in 16 px ("4"); the frosted tube keeps its height hidden ("?"); EN **"The {place} are not the same. This card has {a} {place}."** ({place} = "hundreds" / "tens" / "ones"; when both cards are clear, e.g. numeral against expanded, both numbers show: **"The {place} are different: {a} and {b}."**). ⚠ {a} can be 1 or 0 (106, 160, 305 …): the frame is authored per count — en "1 hundred" / "4 hundreds" / "no tens", fi partitive after numbers (*4 kymmentä*), fr *aucune dizaine*, de *keine Zehner* — never "1 hundreds" or "0 tens".
- **Miss 2 — the card shows how it pours:** after a second non-level pour, the word or expanded card in the stand splits into its parts above its tubes, each part over the tube it fills ("three hundred" over hundreds, "forty" over tens, "seven" over ones; "300" · "40" · "7"); EN **"Each part goes in its own tube. Look for the card that fills the same tubes."**
- A new card in a cradle takes the help away until the next pour.
- **Success (each clip):** chime, clip, fly to the shelf; EN **"{numeral} — {word} — {expanded}!"** when a triple completes (e.g. "305 — three hundred five — 300 + 5!"); digit values **"The {d} in {numeral} is worth {value}!"** After the third number: the shelf show (§2).
