## The skill

## The apparatus and its invention: THE JAR OF TEN
- A glass jar packed with library bugs of one kind (ladybug, bee, butterfly, snail, caterpillar, ant — contact sheet: wasp doubles the bee, spider/mosquito are not jar friends, the long bugs do not pack), heaped so it cannot be counted. Beside it a jar with exactly ten of the same bugs (five up to ten). Both jars are packed at one density, so the fuller jar really holds more: a fair yardstick.
- The child guesses: fewer than ten, or more? (changeable until the pour; a setting turns the guess off). Pour → the bugs fly to a 4 × 4 tray (its size never tells) in rows of four, an array, a ring, a line (up to 10 only) or a scatter (≤ 10).
- A tap ticks a bug (never a number); Clear the ticks.
- The label: − and +, starting at the yardstick, so "ten and four more" is a way there; untouched it is never graded (the yardstick is never the answer).
- Check: at the jar → guess / pour hints; untouched label → set it (no miss). Wrong → miss; unticked bugs → "tick each one", else too big / too small. Miss 2 → bugs numbered 1..n−1 in reading order with "?" on the last (for that Check only): the child names the last number. Right → numbered in tick order, last gold, the count held against the guess ("more than 10, just as you guessed" / "more than you guessed") — a guess is never wrong.

## Why it differs
The premium Estimation Jar owns the class number-line estimate and the auto-reveal in tens; #1 Counting Table owns numbered tags to 10; #14 Treat Box owns hiding. Here: a benchmark estimate against a known ten, then the child counts past ten and reports through a label that invites counting on.
