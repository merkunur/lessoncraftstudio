## The skill
Split run-together words into sentences, end each with . or ?, and start each new sentence with a capital.

## The apparatus and its invention
A word train runs along a track: "the dog ran home it was hungry". The child drops a GATE (a full stop or a question mark) between two words; the train splits into two sentences at the gate, and the first car of the new train automatically flips its first letter to a capital. A gate in the wrong place makes two broken sentences that the child can read and see are wrong ("the dog ran. home it…"); a ? gate on a telling sentence or . on an asking one is the second check: the question sentence starts with a question word or flipped verb, and the child chooses the gate shape.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Putting a full stop at the end of the line instead of where the thought ends; using . for questions; leaving the second sentence lowercase.
- **Pictures:** none (word cars are text content); one library picture per item as a meaning cue
- **Settings:** Task: add the end mark / choose . or ? / split two sentences · Capitals: flip automatically / child flips them
- **Rounds:** ≥ 8 items per task, native-written per locale; the split point falls at different word positions (never always the middle); . and ? balanced in choose mode.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-274, G2-281, G2-282, G2-307, G2-311
- **Distinct from:** Wally's Capital Crane capitalises NAMES; Dr. Plume's Sentence Clinic repairs grammar; Wiggles' Sentence Builder orders words. Sentence boundaries are not taught.
- **Build risk:** es: a question takes ¿ … ? — the ? gate in es places a ¿ at the start of its sentence too (the child chooses the shape, the pair is automatic). fr: U+202F before ? and !. de: nouns are capitalised anyway — the flipped first letter is shown by its colour change, and de rounds avoid a noun at the split point. Native-written items ×11.
