## 1 Page concept (base)

**"The drawing desk": a spine of four step cards on the left, one big white sheet on the right.** From across the room a teacher sees a tall narrow column of four small cats growing (orange lines turning black down the column) and one large empty dashed sheet beside it that fills the rest of the page. Why it is top quality: (1) the practice sheet is the hero, 471 × 535 px for the cat (m: 0.88 bbox ratio), 20 % more area than a strip-over-box page can give the same unit at the same budget, because the strip no longer spends the width-bound row; (2) the eye moves SIDEWAYS from a step to the matching height of the drawing (the outline card sits level with the top of the sheet, the detail card level with its bottom), the "model beside the paper" posture an art teacher sets up, not "look up, look down"; (3) a right-handed child's drawing hand rests on the sheet and never covers the reference; (4) at the one-line-chrome stack the column gains air, never a bigger panel (`justify-content:center` on the desk, fixed sizes), so the page is calm at every title length; (5) nothing is printed in the body but lines from the library drawing and apparatus on the tokens: no words, no numerals, no arrows except four small teal chevrons in the column gaps that say "then" to a pre-reader. The cumulative construction (each card = the previous card's lines in ink + this step's lines in coral, the last card all ink) is itself the greyscale channel: on a B&W printer the coral prints at luma 151 against ink 54 (a 97-point gap, the same gap as ink to inkSoft), and even with no colour at all the new lines of card k are exactly the lines that are not on card k−1.
