## The skill
Know a multiplication table: each rung is one group more than the one below, and unknown facts come from known ones.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE TABLE LADDER. A ladder of ten rungs for the table of n; rung k holds k × n as a bar of k equal groups of n. The anchor rungs (×1, ×2, ×5, ×10) are filled and lit. The task asks for one rung (7 × 6). The child may climb from any lit rung: dragging the '+6' group bar up (or the '−6' bar down) one rung fills that rung's bar and shows its bracket (+6), so 7 × 6 is built from 5 × 6 = 30 in two climbs. At any moment the child types the answer on the keypad. Wrong: the rung does not fill; the bracket from the nearest lit rung to the asked one appears (+6 +6), so the next try is derived, not guessed; miss 2: the asked rung's bar counts its groups by n. Right: the rung fills, lights and stays lit for the rest of the pass, so the ladder fills up over the session. Fluency setting: anchors start dim and climbing is off until a miss.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Thinking each fact must be memorised alone (or counted from 1); off-by-one-group errors (7×6 = 36).
- **Pictures:** none (ladder drawn tool-grade)
- **Settings:** Table: 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 / 6, 7, 8, 9 / mixed · Anchors: shown and climbable / dim (fluency)
- **Rounds:** All ten rungs of the chosen table(s), asked in reshuffled order, ≥ 10 rounds per pass; answers typed.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-358, G3-364, G3-365, G3-366, G3-367, G3-368, G3-402, G3-403, G3-404, G3-405
- **Distinct from:** Echo Grove interprets products; skip-rod shows multiplication on a line. The ladder is the fluency instrument and its feedback is the neighbouring fact, not a red X.
- **Build risk:** Low; needs a big readable keypad (shared shell keypad is fine).
