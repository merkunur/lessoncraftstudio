## The skill
Find one half, one third or one fourth of a set of 4–24 objects by dealing it into equal shares, and say how many one share is.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE DEALING TRAY. A tray with 2, 3 or 4 equal compartments under a strip of lids, and a pile of identical library objects. Each tap deals the next object into the next compartment in turn (round-robin, like dealing cards), so the shares stay equal by construction — the tray cannot take objects any other way. When the pile is empty the task line asks for one third (or 2/3, 3/4 in the several-shares setting): the child lifts the lids of that many compartments and types how many objects are under the open lids. Miss 1 (typed the number of shares, 3 for one third of 12): the closed compartments shake and the open share's objects number themselves; miss 2: every compartment shows its count. Right: the open share slides out onto a plate beside its fraction card (1/3) while the rest stays in the tray, so the share is seen as a part of the whole set.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** "One third of 12 is 3" (the denominator taken as the answer): the tray makes 3 the NUMBER OF SHARES, and the answer is what is in one share.
- **Pictures:** fruits / toys / vegetables (identical instances)
- **Settings:** Fraction: halves / thirds / fourths / mixed · Take: one share / several shares (2/3, 3/4 — absorbed Fence Pens)
- **Rounds:** ≥ 8 rounds per setting: set sizes that are multiples of the denominator, 4–24, never a set equal to the denominator twice in a row; the compartment to lift is chosen at random (all hold the same, so the reading cannot be guessed by position).
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-233, G3-320
- **Distinct from:** Maple's Bakery shares cookies onto plates (division answer = per plate); the tray names the share as a FRACTION of the whole set and asks for one share only, the bridge to 3.NF.A.1. Not the Treat Box or Fruit Crates.
- **Build risk:** Low.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 3 "The Fence Pens" (fence-pens): A fraction of a set: split the group into b equal pens and take a of them. — THE FENCE PENS: a meadow holds N identical animals. The child drags fence posts across the meadow to split it into b pens; the animals walk into the pens one at a time (round-robin), so unequal pens are impossible to leave unnoticed — a pen with one more animal sways. Then the child taps a pens to open their gates, and the animals that come out are counted into a barn readout: that number is a/b of N. Miss 1 shows the count in each pen; miss 2 rings one pen as 1/b.
