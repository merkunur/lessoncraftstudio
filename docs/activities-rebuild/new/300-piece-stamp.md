## The skill
Given one unit-fraction piece, build the whole: 1/b repeated b times.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE PIECE STAMP. A single coloured bar labelled 1/3 is a rubber stamp; an empty track runs off to the right with no end mark. The child stamps it end to end along the track; each print adds one piece and a counter under the track reads 1/3, 2/3, 3/3 … The child presses Done when the track holds what they think is the whole. Then the whole bar (labelled 1) slides in under the track: if the prints match it exactly it clicks and glows; too few leave a gap, too many hang over its end. Non-unit setting: the given piece is 2/3 (or 3/4); the child first splits it with a cut tool into its equal unit pieces (the cut only divides into as many equal parts as the numerator), then stamps one unit piece. Miss 1: the gap or the overhang flashes and the counter stays; miss 2: each print is labelled 1/3 and the target 3/3 appears beside the counter. Right: the prints and the whole merge into one bar labelled 1.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Choosing the longest bar as 'the whole' regardless of piece size, or thinking the whole is always b pieces of any size.
- **Pictures:** none (bars drawn tool-grade)
- **Settings:** Pieces: 1/2 to 1/4 / 1/2 to 1/8 · Ask: build the whole / build a non-unit amount (a/b from 1/b)
- **Rounds:** Generated b 2–8, ≥ 8 rounds, no b twice running; the piece's screen length varies from round to round (a 1/2 piece can be shorter than another round's 1/4), so 'the longest bar' is never a cue; the child builds, so there is no answer position.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-321
- **Distinct from:** Fox Forge builds a/b inside a given whole; the stamp works backwards from the piece to the whole. Distinct from folding-strip and cutting-board.
- **Build risk:** Low.
