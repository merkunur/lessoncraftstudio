## The skill
The equal sign means both sides are the same amount; find the missing number when both sides hold an expression.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE EVEN BEAM: a balance beam whose two pans hold number blocks whose height is their value (8+4 on the left, a block 5 and an empty socket on the right). The child drags a block from the tray into the socket; the beam tilts by the difference and only levels when the sides are equal. A wrong block (12, the answer 'that comes next') tips the beam far to the right. Miss 2 shows both sides' totals as stacked bars.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Reading '=' as 'the answer comes next' (8+4 = _ + 5 → 12), or adding all the numbers shown.
- **Pictures:** none (beam and blocks drawn tool-grade)
- **Settings:** Sides: × and ÷ on both sides (default; 3.OA.A.4) / + and − on both sides · Numbers: tables to 5 / to 10 (sums within 100)
- **Rounds:** ≥ 9 per setting: the unknown on either side and in either slot; ≥ 3 rounds where the 'answer comes next' value (12 for 8 + 4 = ▢ + 5, or 24 for 4 × 6 = ▢ × 3) is a tray distractor; tray order shuffled every round; exactly one block levels the beam.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-350
- **Distinct from:** Numbers Court judges given equations (grade 1, within 20); the beam builds a balance with expressions on both sides. Not the Friendship Bridge (balances groups of objects).
- **Build risk:** Low.
