## 1. The pitch
"Pour counters into the top tray; nothing can reach the bottom tray until the top one is full — so fourteen is literally a full ten that overflowed, plus four."

## 2. Why a child will want it again (the fun)
- **The spout.** A drawn jug spout sits above the top tray. Hold it and counters pour — clack, clack, clack — each rolling into the next empty cell in reading order (4 per second, slow enough to stop on purpose). Tap it for exactly one. It feels like pouring sweets.
- **The overflow.** When the 10th cell fills, the top tray gives a little shiver, its rim flashes gold, and a tiny chute at its bottom-right corner opens with a "clunk"; the 11th counter rolls down the chute into the bottom tray. The moment a teen number is born is a physical event.
- **The split numeral.** On success the big numeral card (say **14**) breaks in two: the **1** floats down and settles as a badge on the full top tray, the **4** floats onto the four counters below. The number's two digits land on the two things they mean.
- **Number of the day:** past 20, every time the open tray fills it snaps shut, slides up onto the tens shelf as a sealed "full tray" with a soft whoosh, and a fresh empty tray rolls in. **The tray stack (critic: pouring 85 counters one by one would teach counting by ones, the opposite of place value):** beside the spout stands a stack of ready, sealed full trays. A tap on the stack slides ONE whole sealed tray onto the shelf with a thud — a ten arrives only as a full tray, never as loose counters. So 85 is eight taps on the stack (a tower of eight trays, like pancakes) and five pours from the spout. Pouring ten loose counters still works and still seals into a tray by itself, so a child who pours ten discovers that the tray stack is the same thing.
- **The day card** on the side fills, face by face, as each step is proved: tens and ones, the number word, ten more, ten less, and (rounds 3, 6, 9) one more or one less — the faces of the G1-243 worksheet family; a tally fence appears only on teen numbers (G1-255), as on the worksheet, where the tally card gives way to the more/less card for bigger numbers. A whole card of one number, made by hand.
- **Free play ("Just pour"):** pour and empty freely up to 99; the day card follows live (in free play the readout is on — exploring is not a task).

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (K.NBT.A.1):** compose numbers 11–19 as ten ones and some further ones; see 14 as 10 + 4. Number of the day (grade 1 worksheets): show one number many ways — tens and ones, ten-frames, tally, word, ten more, ten less.
- **Misconception 1: the "1" in 14 means one.** A child who reads 14 as "1 and 4" pours five counters. **Push back:** nothing can enter the bottom tray until the top is full, so "the 1" cannot be a single counter in the bottom; at miss 1 the target's own shape appears on the trays as dotted cells (a full dotted top tray + 4 dotted cells below) — the child sees ten empty cells they have not filled.
- **Misconception 2: teen numbers are counted, not structured** (counting all 14 one by one without seeing the ten). **Push back:** the overflow event marks the ten; the split numeral puts the "1" on the ten.
- **Misconception 3 (number of the day): ten more changes the ones.** **Push back:** pouring ten more in a "ten more" step always ends with the open tray showing the same ones as before and one more sealed tray on the shelf — the child watches the ones come back to where they were.

## 5. The hand
- **Press and hold the spout:** within 50 ms it tilts and the first counter drops; one counter every 250 ms (teen level), each rolling along a short arc into the next empty cell (180 ms) with a clack (wood, pitch rising a semitone per cell in the tray). Release = stop instantly (the counter in flight finishes).
- **Tap the spout:** exactly one counter.
- **Overflow (cell 10 filled):** the top tray shivers (±1.5 px, 200 ms), its rim flashes gold, the chute flap opens (clunk). Counters 11+ roll through the chute into the bottom tray.
- **Take back:** tap any counter in the bottom tray (or the top tray if the bottom is empty) — the LAST counter poured rises and flies back into the spout (220 ms). One tap, one counter. (Removing from the top tray while the bottom has counters is impossible: the top must stay full while it has overflowed — tapping a top counter then gently shakes the top tray: "it's full; take from the bottom".)
- **Drag down from the spout** (alternative for touch users who struggle with hold): each 40 px dragged pours one.
- **Day level — the seal:** when the open tray fills (10th counter), the tray lid snaps shut (120 ms), the tray slides up to the shelf (420 ms whoosh) and becomes a sealed bar; a new empty tray rolls in from the right (300 ms). The pour speed stays 4 per second (always stoppable); tens come from the **tray stack**: tap it → one sealed full tray slides onto the shelf (350 ms, thud). Up to 9 trays on the shelf; a 10th tap makes the stack wiggle (100 is beyond the card).
- **Day level — un-seal:** tap a sealed bar on the shelf: it slides back down, opens, and its ten counters pour back into the spout (one sweeping 600 ms stream) — "ten less" by hand. Only possible when the open tray is empty or as the "ten less" step (see §6); otherwise the bar wiggles.
- **Break a ten (one less from a round ten):** when the open tray is empty and a take-back is asked, the top sealed bar slides down and OPENS IN PLACE as a full open tray of ten counters (a crack and a shiver, 400 ms), then the last counter flies back to the spout — 30 becomes 2 trays and 9.
- **Undo all:** a small circle-arrow under the trays empties everything back into the spout (one stream, 500 ms).
- **Free play:** "Just pour" — readout visible on the day card, no target.

## 6. The rounds
**Level "Teen numbers" (default) — 9 rounds:** make 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 (the frozen targets), one round each, order-only reshuffled per pass. One right state: top tray full (10) + bottom tray holds target − 10.

**Level "Number of the day" — 9 rounds, each with three steps on the same number (four in rounds 3, 6, 9):**
1. **Build it:** pour the number of the day (the open tray fills and seals as needed).
2. **Ten more:** pour ten more onto it (the open tray keeps its ones once the new ten seals).
3. **Ten less:** from the ORIGINAL number (the build snaps back to it with a quick rewind, 500 ms), take ten away — un-seal one bar.
4. **One more / one less** (rounds 3, 6, 9 only; again from the original): round 3 asks one more (49 → 50: the open tray's tenth counter seals it — the ones roll over to a new ten), round 6 one less (72 → 71), round 9 one less (30 → 29: the child must break a ten).

| # | number of the day | ten more | ten less | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 23 | 33 | 13 | ten less is a teen |
| 2 | 37 | 47 | 27 | |
| 3 | 49 | 59 | 39 | + one more → 50 (the tray seals) |
| 4 | 56 | 66 | 46 | |
| 5 | 64 | 74 | 54 | |
| 6 | 72 | 82 | 62 | + one less → 71 |
| 7 | 18 | 28 | 8 | teen; ten less leaves no full ten |
| 8 | 85 | 95 | 75 | |
| 9 | 30 | 40 | 20 | no extra ones — the open tray is empty; + one less → 29 (break a ten) |

(All within 1–99; no step asks for 0 or for 100; the gate asserts both.)

**Answer position:** these are build tasks — there is no option to guess between; the guessing risk is a visible count, so **no count readout is shown during a task** (the day card faces fill only after a Check proves the build). The gate asserts the readout is hidden in task mode.

**Order:** order-only reshuffle per pass; never the same order twice.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with no counters:** no miss. The spout bobs; bubble: **"Hold the spout to pour. Stop at {n}."**
- **Success (teen):** the numeral card splits: the tens digit floats onto the full top tray as a gold badge, the ones digit onto the bottom tray's counters (each 500 ms). The top tray's ten counters ripple left-to-right (a 30 ms wave). The day card fills tens 1 / ones 4 and the word. Bubble: **"14 is a full ten and 4 more."**
- **Miss 1 (too few or too many):** the target's shape appears as dotted cells over the trays — every cell the target needs, filled or not, gets a dotted ring; extra counters beyond the target get a faint coral halo. The child sees the gap or the excess, without a number. Bubble (too few): **"Look at the dotted places. Fill them all."** (too many): **"Some counters are outside the dotted places. Take them back."**
- **Miss 2:** the dotted cells number themselves along the pour order (1…14, 14 px numerals inside the dotted rings) and the top tray's ten dotted rings flash together once as one block; bubble: **"{n} needs a full ten on top, then {ones} more."**
- **A new pour or take-back takes the help away** until the next Check.
- **Day level steps:** step 1 success → tens/ones, word (and, for a teen, the tally) faces fill (each with a 200 ms pop); bubble **"37 is 3 full tens and 7 more."** Step 2 success → the "ten more" box fills: bubble **"Ten more: 47. The ones stayed 7."** — the ones digit in both numbers flashes together. Step 3 success → "ten less" fills: **"Ten less: 27. Only the tens changed."** Step 4: **"One more: 50. Ten ones made a new ten!"** / **"One less: 71."** / **"One less: 29. You opened a ten."** Round end: the whole day card glows once and slides a little "done" tick (no stars, no score).
- Day misses use the same dotted-shape showing; for step 2/3 the dotted shape is the target state (e.g. 4 sealed bar outlines + 7 dotted cells).

## 8. Settings
1. **Level:** Teen numbers / Number of the day (default Teen numbers) — at rest the stage shows two trays and a numeral card, or the tens shelf and the full day card.
2. **Ten-frame help:** Dotted shape only after a miss / Dotted shape always (default after a miss) — "always" shows the target's dotted cells at rest (a support for children who need it; the task becomes filling the shape).

**Locale traps:**
- **Teen number words reverse the digits in many languages:** de "vierzehn", nl "veertien", sv "fjorton", da "fjorten", no "fjorten", fi "neljätoista" (four-of-the-second), en "fourteen" all say the ONES first; fr/es/it/pt 11–16 are opaque words ("quatorze", "catorce", "quattordici", "quatorze"), 17–19 say ten first ("dix-sept", "diecisiete", "diciassette", "dezessete"). The split-numeral moment shows the written order (1 then 4) regardless; the word is shown below as content and never relied on to teach structure.
- **Two-digit words (day level)** with ones-first order in de/nl/da ("siebenunddreißig", "zevenendertig", "syvogtredive") and vigesimal Danish (50 "halvtreds", 70 "halvfjerds", 90 "halvfems") and French 70–99 ("soixante-douze", "quatre-vingt-cinq"). The word face reads `scripts/worksheet-gen/lib/number-words.js` — the same reviewed 1–99 tables the Number-of-the-Day worksheets use (copied into the activity's data at build; the gate asserts byte-equality), so page and screen can never disagree; never generated by rule at runtime.
- **Tally marks:** the five-bar gate (four strokes + a diagonal) is common in en/de/nl/sv/da/no/fi; fr/es/pt/it classrooms also use it, though some Latin-American schools draw a square with a diagonal. The design uses the five-bar gate (it matches the G1-243 worksheet's tally face); the reviewer confirms per locale.
- "ten more / ten less" phrasing: de "zehn mehr / zehn weniger", fr "dix de plus / dix de moins", fi "kymmenen enemmän / vähemmän" — natural in all; the success frame keeps numbers as numerals.
- Plural agreement: "{tens} full tens" needs plural forms per locale (fi partitive after numerals: "3 täyttä kymppiä"); frames written per locale with the numeral and the noun in the right case.

## 10. Sound off, access, phones
- Legible silent: the numeral target, the dotted shape, the overflow flash and the split numeral carry everything.
- Keyboard: Space on the focused spout pours one; holding Space pours continuously; Backspace takes one back; S (day level) un-seals the top bar when allowed.
- aria: spout "Pour one counter"; trays "Top tray, full" / "Bottom tray, {k} counters" (announced only after Check in task mode, so the count is not read out during the task); live region reads the success sentence.
- Taps: spout grip 64 px; counters 46 px; sealed bars 116 × 44 hit area.
- Phones: §4; Check above both folds.
- Reduced motion: counters appear in their cells without the rolling arc; the seal swaps instantly.

## 11. Distinct from
- **place-value-lab (tool):** ten ones snap into a ten on a workmat — the child groups loose ones. **counting-cups (tool):** scoop a pile into cups of ten. **exchange-machine (tool):** regroup by tapping a column. The Overflow Trays' invention is **order by gravity**: the child controls how far to pour, and the second tray can only receive after the first is full — the ten is forced first, and the stop is the child's reading of the numeral.
- **#2 Ten-Shelf** (count by tens with egg cartons) — counting tens, not composing a teen. **#31 Ten-Tank** (this batch) — finds the ten inside a loose scattered bubble pile and names the number (quantity → numeral); here the child goes numeral → quantity by pouring.
- **ten-frame (tool):** a free ten-frame with draggable counters; no overflow, no target.

## 12. Build plan
- **Core state:** `{ level, order[], idx, step:1|2|3, target, base, poured:int, sealed:int, open:int, helpAlways, misses }` — `poured = sealed*10 + open` in the day level; in the teen level `top = min(poured,10)`, `bottom = poured − top`.
- **Functions:** `pour(k)`, `takeBack()`, `seal()`, `unseal()`, `check()` → `{ok, kind:'few'|'many', dotted:[cells]}`, `stepTarget(round, step)`, `nextTask(index)`.
- **Gate `verify-overflow-trays-core.js` (own oracle):** (1) the bottom tray is empty whenever the top has < 10 (exhaustive over pour/take sequences up to 20); (2) take-back always removes the last poured; (3) teen targets exactly 11–19, one each; (4) day table: ten more = n + 10 ≤ 99, ten less = n − 10 ≥ 1, never 0 or 100; (5) the readout is hidden in task mode; (6) the split puts the tens digit on the top tray and the ones digit on the bottom; (7) success sentences' numbers equal the build; (8) the day-card faces equal the build after each Check; the number word equals the reviewed table.
- **Mutations:** let the bottom tray fill before the top is full; take back the first counter instead of the last; show the readout during a task; accept target ± 1; swap the split digits; ten more adds to the ones; ten less from the step-2 state instead of the original; seal at 9; let the spout pour faster than 4/s; let the tray stack deliver loose counters instead of a sealed tray; let one-less from 30 go negative instead of breaking a ten; show the tally face for n > 20; write "0" as a target.
- **Risks:** the number-word face (tables reused from `lib/number-words.js`, already reviewed); the pour speed must stay stoppable on a slow phone (rate is time-based, not frame-based). Nothing BLOCKED.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — pouring clacking counters, the chute clunking open, watching sealed trays stack into a tower: it is a toy.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "Pour fourteen. Stop when you think you're there." The class sees the ten fill and overflow, then the 1 land on the ten. Number of the day runs as the morning routine.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Calm, no score, no characters, no readout giving the answer away; the number words are correct for their language.
4. **The invention:** the top tray must be full before anything can overflow into the bottom one, so a teen number is a full ten that spilled over plus some more.
