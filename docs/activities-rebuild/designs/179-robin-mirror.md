## 1. The pitch
"Every pronoun has a face in Robin's mirror — one person, one thing, a group, me, you — and a word like *herself* points back at whoever did the action, so the mirror only clears when the face it shows is the doer's own face."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- Robin's mirror is a real hand mirror on a stand (drawn frame). Put a word in it and the glass mists, then shows that word's **face**: a single dot (one person), a square (one thing), two dots (more than one), a gold star (me), a star with a dot (me and others), a ring (you). The doer of the sentence wears the same kind of face on a little badge. Matching faces = the glass clears with a bright sweep.
- The surprise on success: the mirror shows the doer's NAME in **mirror writing** — and then flips it the right way round with a little spin. Children love reading backwards.
- Pronouns become a matching game of faces children can see and argue about ("*themselves* is two dots, but Mia is one dot!").
- The earned show: the sentence reads with the word in place, the doer's badge and the mirror face glow together, and a thin bright line of light runs from the mirror back to the doer — "it points back".

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill: a pronoun stands for a name and must match it (person and number); a reflexive pronoun (*myself, herself, themselves*) points back to the doer of the same sentence; a possessive (*her, his, its, their*) points to the owner; a repeated name can be replaced by a pronoun.
- Misconceptions and the push-back:
  - *"themselves / ourselves are just fancy 'self' words — any will do"* → each word's mirror face is fixed by its grammar (person + number); a two-dot face cannot clear against Mia's one-dot badge — you can SEE one ≠ two.
  - *"myself sounds polite, use it everywhere"* → *myself* shows the gold star (me); the doer *Ben* has a dot.
  - *"it/he/she refers to the nearest noun"* (L1) → the child must link the pronoun to its name; the mirror compares faces: *It* (square) can only clear against a thing, *She* (dot) against a person.
  - *"their / its are interchangeable"* (L3) → the owner's badge decides: one thing → square → *its*; the twins → two dots → *their*.

## 5. The hand
- **L2/L3**: drag a chip into the gap (or tap chip → it flies in, 160 ms). The mirror mists (300 ms) — nothing more until Check. Another chip replaces it; drag it out to empty.
- **L1**: the pronoun in sentence 2 is coloured coral and already sits in the mirror (its face mists, unrevealed). The child taps a candidate name card in sentence 1 → a light thread runs from the pronoun to that card (180 ms). Tap another card to move the thread.
- **L3 replace rounds** (G2-355): the repeated name is underlined coral (*Ben washed **Ben's** bike.*); tap it → a ring of 3 pronoun chips opens around it; tap one → it replaces the name in the sentence and in the mirror.
- **Check** → the mirror reveals the face (see §7).
- **Free play**: after a cure, tap any pronoun chip in the tray → the mirror shows its face (a reference toy: what face does *ourselves* have?). Tap the doer's badge → the mirror shows the doer's name in mirror writing again.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with an empty gap / no thread** → bubble **"Put a word in Robin's mirror first."** (L1 **"Tap the name the coloured word stands for."**) No miss.
- **Miss 1** → the mirror clears and shows the chosen word's face, and the doer's badge (or chosen candidate's badge) lifts beside it; the two faces stay side by side with a small `≠` drawn between them. Bubble **"The mirror shows {face}. {Doer} is {face}. Find the word with the same face."** — faces said as words: "one person", "one thing", "more than one", "me", "me and others", "you".
- **Miss 2** → every chip shows its face as a small badge on its corner; L1: every candidate card's badge pulses. Bubble **"Match the faces."**
- A new move clears the faces until the next Check.
- **Success** → the glass clears with a bright sweep, shows the doer's name in mirror writing, spins it the right way round, and a line of light runs back to the doer. Bubble L2 **"{Word} points back to {doer}: “{sentence}”"** · L1 **"{Pronoun} means {name}."** · L3 **"Whose? {Owner}'s — “{sentence}”"**.

- Locale traps (rebuild per locale; never translate the en sentences):
  - **de**: reflexives are mostly obligatory with reflexive verbs (sich waschen) — keep the old de rounds; *sich* is the face for ALL third persons (• ■ ••): the de mirror shows a combined "not me, not you" face (dot-square-dots trio) so *sich* clears against any third-person doer. Possessive *ihr* = her AND their → a de L3 chip set never contains *ihr* with both meanings live; agreement with the possessed noun (sein Hut, seine Mütze) is authored per round.
  - **fr / es / it / pt**: the reflexive clitic goes BEFORE the verb (je me lave) — the gap sits before the verb, as in the old rounds; *se/si* cover all third persons (combined face like de). fr possessive son/sa/ses = his OR her (agrees with the THING, not the owner) → fr L3 contrasts owner NUMBER: son/sa/ses vs leur/leurs. es *su/sus* = his/her/their/your-formal → es L3 contrasts PERSON (mi · tu · su), not number. pt-BR *seu/sua* is ambiguous in speech → pt L3 uses *dele/dela/deles* after the noun ("o chapéu dela").
  - **sv / da / no**: the real grade-2 trap is the reflexive possessive **sin/sitt/sina** (da *sin/sit/sine*; no bokmål *sin/si/sitt/sine*) vs **hans/hennes** (da/no *hans/hendes/hennes*) — "Mia tvättar **sin** cykel" (her own) vs "Mia tvättar **hennes** cykel" (someone else's). The mirror is made for this: *sin* points back to the doer, *hennes* points away (its face shows an arrow-out mark). sv L3 is built around it; sv also has the gender-neutral *hen*. ⚠ **One right answer needs care: "Mia tvättar ___ cykel." takes BOTH *sin* and *hennes* (two different bikes, both grammatical).** So a *sin*-round always carries *egen/eget/egna* (da/no *egen/eget/egne*): "Mia tvättar ___ egen cykel." → only *sin*; and a *hennes*-round has NO third-person doer that could own the thing: "Det här är Ellas cykel. Det är ___ cykel." → only *hennes* (*sin* needs a subject to point back to). The gate checks each sv/da/no L3 round against both readings.
  - **fi**: no gender at all (*hän*); reflexive *itse* with a possessive suffix (itseni, itsesi, itsensä, itsemme, itsenne, itsensä) and possessive suffixes on the noun (pyöränsä) — the fi faces are person/number only and the L3 chips are suffix forms.
  - **nl**: *zich* for third persons; *zijn/haar/hun* possessives (hun = their).
  - Names per locale from the sentence bank's `names` list (fi Aino, Eeli…).

## 13. The four questions
1. **Choose it again?** Yes — the mirror's mist-then-reveal and the backwards name are irresistible, and matching faces is a game children can win by thinking.
2. **Teacher on the board?** Yes — "a pronoun must match its name; a reflexive points back" is shown as shapes the whole class can compare.
3. **Parent trust?** No gender guessing, no people pictures, no scores, calm.
4. **The invention:** the Mirror That Shows Who — every pronoun shows its face (one, thing, more than one, me, us, you) in Robin's mirror, and a reflexive only clears when it shows the doer's own face.
