# Content graph — what the two reviewers said about the teaching points themselves

Recorded 2026-11-11 with the authored order and prerequisites (`frontend/content/teaching-graph/order.json`, `prerequisites.json`). The reviewers were asked to order the points and to list, without acting on it, anything a teacher would call wrong in the data. Nothing below has been changed in the points: a point's grade is where its worksheets sit, and the source (`docs/activities-rebuild/coverage/coverage-map.json`) belongs to the activities programme. **Each line is an input for a later part:** Part 2 places every point in each country's own grade (so a US code that is one grade off stops mattering there); Part 4 must not put two points that are one skill into the same unit as two lessons, and must know which descriptions fit English only.

## Mathematics (158 points)

- K:number-bonds-20 is grade-1 content (its own code is 1.OA.C.6); in kindergarten only the teen numbers as 10 + n are expected.
- K:fact-fluency-colour-code: K.OA.A.5 is fluency within 5 only; the 'within 10 / 20' sheets are grade 1 (1.OA.C.6), and the point is a worksheet format, not one skill.
- K:odd-even-pairing and 1:odd-even-pairs are the same skill (pairing up, one left over) at two grades, both under the grade-2 code 2.OA.C.3; 2:odd-even repeats it again as its first part.
- 1:make-10 is the same skill as the 'make ten' part of K:number-bonds-k (K.OA.A.4).
- 1:days-months-seasons and 1:days-months-seasons~season-wheel have the identical skill description; as written they are one point.
- 2:bundle-ten-ones (1.NBT.B.2.a) comes BEFORE 1:tens-and-ones in any progression; it sits one grade too high.
- 1:time-set-hands includes quarter hours and five minutes, which is grade 2 (2.MD.C.7) and overlaps 2:time-5-min-quarter; only o'clock and half past belong in grade 1.
- 1:bonds-to-100 'any two numbers' (e.g. 37 + 63) is grade 2 (2.NBT.B.5); only the tens partners are grade-1 work. 1:coins is grade 2 in the Common Core (2.MD.C.8).
- 2:flat-or-solid (K.G.A.3) and 2:solids-in-real-life are kindergarten / grade-1 content placed in grade 2.
- 2:2d-attributes 'sort 2D shapes by number of sides' is the same skill as K:count-sides (K.G.B.4); 2.G.A.1 asks for more (angles, naming pentagons and hexagons).
- 2:fraction-of-a-set and 3:fraction-of-set are the same skill; 2:round-to-ten is the first half of 3:rounding and carries a grade-3 code (3.NBT.A.1).
- 3:time-12-24 is coded 2.MD.C.7, which is wrong: 24-hour time is not in the Common Core at all (it is grade 3-4 content in European curricula).
- 3:division-remainder (4.NBT.B.6), 3:compare-4-digit (4.NBT.A.2), 3:right-angles and 3:symmetry (4.G) are grade 4 in the Common Core, and 3:volume-cubes (5.MD.C.4, hidden cubes) is grade 5; fine for KMK/Nordic grade 3 except the volume point, which is high everywhere.
- 3:balance-equations: 'an operation on both sides' goes beyond its code 3.OA.A.4 (one unknown in a single multiplication or division equation); 3:estimate-by-rounding is coded 3.OA.D.8 (two-step word problems) but only rounds two addends.
- K:numeral-formation says '0–9 and 11–20' and leaves out 10; 2:read-jugs reads to 2000 ml while grade-2 numbers stop at 1000; 2:number-line-not-from-zero is a number (NBT) skill filed under Measurement.

## Literacy (71 points) and thinking skills (19 points)

- 2:synonyms and 2:synonyms~doing-stage are the same skill with the same description (literacy); likewise 3:two-attribute-sort and 3:two-attribute-sort~logic-cabinet (thinking). Each pair should be one point or get descriptions that say what differs.
- K:grid-copy and 1:grid-copy have the same description, and 2:logic-grid and 3:logic-grids nearly the same; nothing says what makes the higher grade harder (grid size, number of clues), so a teacher cannot tell them apart.
- K:segment-phonemes and 1:phoneme-boxes are nearly the same skill (sound boxes); the K point also asks for 'two letters for one sound share one box', which needs digraph knowledge that is only taught later (K:digraphs-k, 1:digraphs) - that part belongs in grade 1.
- 2:digraphs (find and count sh in sentences) is easier than 1:digraphs (hear, write, read and match the letter team) and carries a grade 1 code (RF.1.3.a); it reads as a grade 1 review, not a grade 2 skill.
- K:digraphs-k, 1:digraphs and 2:digraphs are described only with English letter teams (sh / ch / th); other languages have other teams (de sch/ch, fr ch/ou, nl ch/ng/oe) and Finnish has almost none - the description should say 'the letter teams of the language'.
- 2:spelling-rules gives only English rules (ck/k, tch/ch, -dge/-ge, ai/ay, -ies); every language has its own position rules (de ck/tz/ie, nl open and closed syllables, double consonants in sv/da/no/fi). Its plural part (-s/-es/-ies) overlaps 2:plural-nouns.
- K:sight-words-reading: 'sight words' is an English idea (irregular words learned whole). In languages with regular spelling (fi, it, es, de) these are high-frequency words that children decode; the description should say 'common words read quickly'.
- K:word-sentence-tracing asks for copying long words and whole sentences with spaces under the letter-level code L.K.1.a; this is end-of-kindergarten to grade 1 work and sits above K:letter-formation in difficulty.
- K:articles and 1:articles are close to the same skill, and the K point carries a grade 1 code (L.1.1.h). English a / an needs the first sound and vowels versus consonants (a phonics choice), while der / die / das or le / la is word knowledge (gender) - two different skills under one description.
- 1:alphabetical-order includes ordering by the second letter, which is grade 2 dictionary work in most curricula; its 'hop the letters in order' part is the kindergarten skill K:alphabet-order.
- 1:syllables-split is filed under Reading: Foundational Skills, but clapping and counting syllables is phonological awareness and repeats K:count-syllables; only the written splitting and syllable cards are reading work.
- 1:word-families-syllable-reading mixes two skills (word families by rime, and reading words syllable by syllable), and 'carpet' is not a word-family example. Rime families are an English teaching route; syllable reading is the route in es/it/pt/fr/fi.
- Codes below the grade of the point: 2:question-words (L.K.1.d), 2:pronouns (L.1.1.d), 1:rhyming (RF.K.2.a), 2:sentence-boundaries (L.1.2.b first). Either the points are review or the codes should be the grade's own.
- Literacy grade 3 has only 4 points (cursive and vocabulary): no reading comprehension, no grammar, no spelling, no writing. A grade 3 year cannot be built from these.
- Thinking points that need literacy or maths: K:mazes (ABC, lowercase, numbers 1-20, beginning-sound routes), 2:find-differences (write a sentence about each difference, W.2.2), 1:secret-code (letters and numbers). Their real prerequisites lie in another subject and cannot be shown here.

## What follows for the plan

- **Literacy grade 3 holds four points** (cursive and vocabulary). A grade 3 literacy year cannot be assembled from the catalogue as it stands; Part 4 builds literacy units for K to 2 first and reports grade 3 as a content gap.
- **Same skill at two grades** (odd and even, make ten, fraction of a set, rounding to ten, digraphs, articles, grid copy, logic grids): the unit builder treats the later point as review of the earlier one, never as a new lesson.
- **English-only descriptions** (sight words, digraphs, spelling rules, word families, a / an): the skill text shown to teachers in other languages comes from that language's own worksheet titles and, from Part 2 on, from its national objective, not from a translation of the English description.
- **Thinking-skill pages that need letters or numbers** (ABC mazes, secret codes, write-about-the-difference): their real prerequisites lie in another subject; the graph keeps prerequisites inside a subject, so these carry none.
