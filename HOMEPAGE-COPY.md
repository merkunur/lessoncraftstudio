## Section 3: Language proof

## Where most worksheets break down

Generic worksheets translated by software get the words mostly right and the language wrong. A masculine noun marked feminine. A missing umlaut. A plural that doesn't exist. Small errors a child can't catch but a teacher can't unsee.

Two examples, side by side.

### German

[Implementer: render two side-by-side worksheet excerpts. Left: a typical auto-translated K-3 vocabulary worksheet rendered with "*der schule*" — wrong article AND lowercase — and a plural rendered as "*schules*" with English -s pluralization. Right: the LessonCraftStudio version, with "*die Schule*" capitalized and gendered correctly, plural "*die Schulen*" with the proper -n ending. Annotations point from each correction on the right side.]

**Annotation 1**
Every German noun is capitalized. *Schule*, never *schule*.

**Annotation 2**
*Schule* is feminine: *die Schule*. Not *der*, not *das*.

**Annotation 3**
The plural is *Schulen* — -n ending, not English -s. *Schules* isn't a German word.

### French

[Implementer: same structure. Left: auto-translated K-3 worksheet with "*la école*" un-elided, "*à le école*" un-contracted, and "*ecole*" missing its accent. Right: corrected to "*l'école*", "*à l'école*", and "*école*" with the acute accent. Annotations point from each correction on the right side.]

**Annotation 1**
*École* starts with a vowel, so *la* elides to *l'*. *L'école*, not *la école*.

**Annotation 2**
*À* + *le* contracts to *au*, but before a vowel it's *à l'*. *À l'école*, not *à le école*.

**Annotation 3**
Accents are part of the spelling. *École* takes an acute on the É; *ecole* is wrong.

### Closing line

Errors like these compound across a vocabulary set. We built the vocabulary system first. The illustrations came after.

One click from any deck. The PDF mirrors the on-screen worksheet — same illustrations, same language, same vocabulary. Take it home, photocopy it for the class, leave it for a substitute teacher.

Paste an embed code into your class blog, school site, or learning-platform page. Students play the deck where they already are. The embed is fully free and stays free.

One link, anywhere — email, parent newsletter, classroom chat, your LMS. Students open the link and play in the browser. No login, no account, no friction.

## Notes for the implementer

**Anchor IDs.** Suggested anchor slugs for the sections, in case anything elsewhere on the site needs to deep-link: `#breadth`, `#language-proof`, `#free`, `#subscription`. The hero and footer don't need anchors.

**Headings hierarchy.** The H1 is the hero. Sections 2 through 6 are H2. Sub-headings inside Sections 4 and 5 are H3. The annotation labels in Section 3 are not headings — render them as captioned annotations next to the worksheet excerpts, not as document structure.

**Bold, italics, formatting.** Italic is used in Section 3 to mark German and French words being discussed (e.g., *Schule*, *l'école*). Outside Section 3, italics are not used. Bold is used for the price line in Section 5 and for the CTA button labels. No other bolding in body copy.

**No body-copy emoji.** Section 2's deck thumbnails have language flags (per HOMEPAGE-SAVE-STATE.md) — those are not emoji used as decoration, they're functional labels on a thumbnail. Outside the thumbnails, no emoji anywhere.

**Locale of this copy.** This file is the English-language home page copy. Per CLAUDE.md the platform serves 11 languages; localized home page copy is downstream work, not in this draft. The translation effort should preserve the rhetorical structure (hero claim, breadth grid, language-proof comparison with locale-appropriate examples, free experience, subscription, structural footer) rather than translate phrase-for-phrase. Section 3 in particular needs locally-relevant examples: the German/French worked examples are the right choice for an English-speaking visitor; for a German visitor's home page, the comparison should be redrawn with examples that demonstrate the platform's quality from a German speaker's perspective.

**What this draft does not include.** Pricing-page copy (separate page; this is the home page only). FAQ copy (separate page if the operator wants one). About-page copy (separate page). Per-section microcopy for forms, signup flow, account dashboard — none of which lives on the home page.
