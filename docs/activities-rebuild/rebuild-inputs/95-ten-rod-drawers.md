## The skill
Compare and order two-digit numbers by looking at the tens first, then the ones; write it with > < =.

## The apparatus and its invention
NUMBER CARDS WITH DRAWERS. Each numeral card has two drawers: tens (left) and ones (right). The child drags cards onto the comparing podium; when two cards stand there their TENS drawers open first and the rods slide out side by side, so the longer row of tens is plain. Only if the tens tie do the ONES drawers open. Compare task: a hinged wedge lies between the cards; once the drawers have spoken it becomes draggable and the child turns it so its open mouth faces the bigger number (> or <; a flat '=' bar in the one equal round). Order task: three cards and three podium steps (smallest-first or greatest-first, shown by a step icon as at the Creek Map); a card dropped on a wrong step opens its tens drawer against its neighbour's and slides back. Miss 1: the deciding drawer pair glows (the tens unless they tie); miss 2: the rods count themselves in tens under each card. Right: the wedge clicks onto its pin and the bubble reads the sentence ('42 is greater than 24'). Groups setting (to 20): ten-packs and loose library objects stand in the drawers instead of rods and cubes.

- **Misconception:** Compares the ones digit first (29 > 41 because 9 > 1), or reads > as "is less".
- **Pictures:** none for numerals; fruits / crayons in ten-packs for the groups-within-20 setting
- **Settings:** Numbers: groups to 20 / two-digit to 99 / to 120 · Task: compare two with a symbol / order three (smallest first or greatest first).
- **Rounds:** 9+ rounds; at least 3 per pass are digit-swaps (24 vs 42) and 2 are equal-tens (37 vs 34), never equal numbers except in one "=" round per pass; the greatest card stands in each place of the row across a pass; symbol answer side balanced 35-65%.
- **Worksheet types it must cover:** G1-114, G1-135, G1-136, G1-137
- **Build risk:** Symbol reading must stay one-right-answer: the wedge only becomes draggable after both drawers have shown.
