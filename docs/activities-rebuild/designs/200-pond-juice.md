## 1. The pitch
"When Pippa serves your juice, the jug pours out into a tray: one big carafe for every numbered line it passes and one little tasting glass for every small mark after that — so the tray counts on for you, 600… 650… 700… 750, and the last glass either lands on your number or it doesn't."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **The tap.** A fat brass tap over the jug: press and hold and juice glugs in with a rising pitch; let go and it settles with a little slosh onto the nearest mark. A tiny drain valve at the bottom lets one mark back out per tap. Children fiddle with this between rounds — it is the most satisfying thing on the screen.
- **The frost.** In the "by feel" rounds the jug is frosted glass: you only see a pale shape rising. When you let go, the frost wipes away from the top down in a 600 ms sweep — "how much did I make?" — and the scale appears.
- **The tasting tray (the show).** Every round ends with Pippa tipping the jug: the juice runs into a row of **carafes** (one per numbered gap, each tagged with its numeral) and then **tasting glasses** (one per small mark, each tagged as it fills: 650, 700, 750). The pieces are drawn with widths in proportion to what they hold, so the tray is also a little bar of juice. The customer's order flag is planted where the row should end. When the last glass lands under your flag, the customer picture hops (a 240 ms bounce) and the glasses chime in a rising scale.
- **Two jugs, one truth.** In twin rounds the shorter-looking jug often holds more. Both pour out into the same-size glasses side by side, and the longer row wins — every time a child is "fooled" by height, the tray shows why.
- **One rule for every gauge (the lab's signature).** Whatever Pippa measures, the reading cashes out onto the same tray the same way — **one big piece for every numbered gap, one little piece for every mark after it**: the jug pours **carafes + tasting glasses**; the thermometer's red column breaks off into **big red beads (one per numbered gap) + little red beads (one per mark)** that roll onto the tray; the kitchen scale's pointer travel lands as **brass blocks (one per numbered gap) + small brass discs (one per mark)**. A child who has learned the tray on the jug already knows how to check the thermometer.
- **Free play:** between rounds the tap and drain stay live; pour any amount and press Serve to watch the tray count it out (no grading).

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (3.MD.A.2 + the worksheets):** read the amount on a measuring jug (to 500, 1000 and 2000 ml), a hanging kitchen scale and a balance (g), and a thermometer (°C) whose marks are worth 2, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100 or 250 while numerals are printed only every 10, 100, 200 or 500; pour to a given amount (the scale read backwards); write the amount with its unit; estimate a liquid volume, then measure it.
- **Misconception 1 — every small mark is 1** (750 on a jug numbered every 200 with 50-ml marks read as 603). **Pushback:** the tray tags each tasting glass with the running amount — 650, 700, 750 — so a child who typed 603 sees their flag planted far back, under the first glass's left edge, while three glasses stand beyond it. The jug never says "wrong"; the glasses simply keep coming.
- **Misconception 2 — read the nearest printed number** (750 read as 800 or 600). **Pushback:** the flag stands one glass too far (800: an empty dashed glass outline under the flag) or two glasses short.
- **Misconception 3 — higher juice means more juice** (two jugs with different scales). **Pushback:** both jugs pour into equal glasses on one tray; the row length is the amount, whatever the jug looked like.
- **Misconception 4 — pouring to an amount by guessing the height** (asked for 1750 on a 2000 jug, stopping at "most of the way"). **Pushback:** in exact-pour rounds the scale is visible and the tray shows exactly how far over or under the order flag the pour landed — in glasses of the jug's own mark size.

## 5. The hand
- **Pour (tap):** pointerdown on the tap starts the stream within 16 ms; the level rises at a constant 1/7 of the jug's height per second (full 0–max in 7 s), with a soft glug tone every 120 ms whose pitch rises with the level. Pointerup → the level eases to the nearest mark (180 ms, a 3 px slosh). A quick tap (< 150 ms) adds exactly one mark ("a splash"). Keyboard: Space held = pour; ↑ = one mark up.
- **Drain:** a tap removes one mark (a little gurgle, 150 ms); hold = drain continuously at the pour rate. Keyboard ↓.
- **Frost wipe** (by-feel rounds): on pointerup the frost clears top-down in 600 ms; the tap locks for this round (you made what you made); the drain locks too.
- **Read:** type the amount on the keypad (≤ 4 digits); ⌫ erases.
- **Twin rounds:** tap one of the two jugs (each jug is a 160 × 300 hit target) → it lifts 6 px with a teal rim; tap again to put it down.
- **Serve** = the Check button (labelled Check, as everywhere): the jug tips (400 ms), the juice runs into the tray piece by piece (carafes 260 ms each, glasses 200 ms each), each piece's tag pops as it fills.
- **Thermometer and scale modes** have no tap: the apparatus is already showing its reading; the child reads and types. In scale mode the fruit can be lifted off the hook and hung back (free play: the pointer springs to 0 and back — a 500 ms damped bounce). **Check cashes the gauge out onto the tray** exactly like the jug: the thermometer's column snaps off from the bulb up into beads (big bead 220 ms per numbered gap, tagged 10, 20; little bead 160 ms per mark, tagged 22, 24, 26) that roll down onto the tray; the scale's pointer slides back to 0 and its travel drops onto the tray as brass blocks (tagged 100, 200) and discs (tagged 220, 240…). Balance rounds: the weights slide off the pan onto the tray, biggest first, each tagged with the running sum.
- **Undo:** keypad ⌫; in pour rounds drain/pour freely until Check.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing typed** → the answer box pulses; bubble **"Read the scale, then type the amount."** (no miss). **E round, nothing poured** → the tap glows; **"Hold the tap to pour."** **T round, no jug chosen** → both jugs wobble once; **"Tap the jug you think holds more."**
- **F rounds** never miss on the estimate; only the typed reading is graded. The tray shows the order flag and the child's pour side by side: bubble **"You made {made} ml — the order was about {order} ml."** (no right/wrong colour).
- **Miss 1 (R, F reading, E)** — the tray pours out **with tags on** and the child's teal flag planted at their number: the glasses keep coming past it, or it stands over an empty dashed glass. Bubble R/F: **"Look where the last glass stops."** E: **"Your pour stopped at {made} ml. Pour to {order} ml."** The juice flows back into the jug; the level stays where the child left it.
- **Miss 1, thermometer / scale** — the same cash-out: the beads (or brass blocks and discs) roll onto the tray with their tags and the child's teal flag stands at the typed number on the tray's track — beads run past it, or it stands over an empty dashed bead. Bubble **"Look where the last bead stops."** / **"Look where the last disc stops."**
- **Miss 2** — the gauge's scale lights the marks one by one from the last numeral up to the level (or the pointer), each lit mark showing its amount beside it (600 → 650 → 700 → 750), 300 ms per mark. Bubble: **"Each little mark is {step} {unit}. Count on from {numeral}."**
- **T miss 1** — both jugs pour into equal glasses on two shelves of the tray; the longer row glows. Bubble **"Count the glasses: the scales on the jugs are different."** T is then answered (the child taps the jug again to confirm; no second miss needed).
- A new key, pour or drain takes the help away until the next Check.
- **Success** — the tray fills and the last glass lands exactly under the teal flag, which turns into a gold star pennant; the customer bounces; Pippa (frog picture) appears on the tray's end at 64/96 px. EN: jug **"{amount} ml — exactly right!"** · thermometer **"It is {amount} °C."** · scale/balance **"It weighs {amount} g."** · twin **"This one has more — {a} ml and {b} ml."** (warmer: **"{a} °C is warmer than {b} °C."**; heavier: **"{a} g is heavier than {b} g."**). End of pass: the nine served drinks stand on the counter as their trays, longest at the left — no score.
