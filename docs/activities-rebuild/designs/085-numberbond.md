## 1. The pitch
"The first number sits in a ten-rack with a hole in it — snap the second number's stick so one piece plugs the hole exactly, and the rack turns into a golden ten with the rest waiting beside it."

## 2. Why a child will want it again (the fun)
- **Linking cubes you can snap.** The second number is a stick of chunky linking cubes. Tapping a joint snaps it apart with a crisp click and a tiny jolt, like real cubes. Children love snapping.
- **The plug.** Drag a piece to the rack's hole. Too long: it bumps the rack's end wall, tilts, wobbles and slides back out. Too short: it drops in and leaves a dark empty slot. Just right: it slides in with a satisfying "thunk", and the whole rack lights up gold, cube by cube in a ripple, with a rising ten-note scale.
- **The golden ten.** A gold rack is a ten you can lift: after the plug fits, the gold rack slides up to the "tens" shelf and the leftover piece hops beside it. 10 and 3 sit there looking like the number 13.
- **Free play ("Cube bench"):** any two sticks (dial each 1–9), snap and join freely, plug the rack, chain golden tens onto the shelf.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (1.OA.C.6):** add within 20 using the make-a-ten strategy — decompose the second addend so that one part makes ten with the first (8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13). Make 10 level: know the partner that makes 10 (7 and 3). In-my-head mode: fluent mental addition within 20 (the sheets' mental-math pages), with the plug as the help.
- **Misconception 1 — breaking the wrong amount:** the child snaps the 5 into 1 and 4, or 3 and 2 and offers the 3. **Push back:** a piece that is too long cannot enter — it hits the rack's end wall and slides out; a piece too short leaves an empty slot that glows; only the exact partner of the first number fits.
- **Misconception 2 — adding the whole second number after making ten (8 + 5 → 10 + 5 = 15):** **Push back:** after the plug the stick is physically gone — only the 3-piece is left beside the gold ten. On a miss with 15 the ones area shows 5 ghost slots with only 3 cubes in them; the 2 empty ghost slots glow and a thin gold thread runs from them into the rack: those 2 are inside the ten now.
- **Misconception 3 — the first number must be the one in the rack:** for 6 + 8 the child may tap "swap" so the 8 sits in the rack and the 6 is snapped (2 + 4); both ways fit and both are right — the rack shows that either number can be made into the ten.

## 5. The hand
- **Tap a joint in the stick** (or Enter on a focused joint): within 40 ms the joint flashes, the stick snaps apart into two pieces with a 10 px gap and a click; the bond's two empty circles fill with the two pieces' lengths (left piece → left circle). Tapping another joint of a piece snaps it again (three pieces are allowed — the bond then shows the first snap only; the miss logic handles any piece).
- **Join pieces back:** drag one piece end-to-end against another → they link with a lower click (undo); the bond clears.
- **Drag a piece to the rack's hole:** it lifts (scale 1.05, shadow) and follows the finger within 30 ms.
  - **exact length:** released over the hole it slides in (220 ms, "thunk"); the ten cubes light gold left to right (30 ms apart) with a rising scale; the piece's bond leg turns gold; the equation strip extends.
  - **too long:** it slides in until its front cube meets the end wall, tilts up 12° with its extra cubes over the wall, wobbles (2 × 120 ms) and slides back to the mat.
  - **too short:** it drops in at the left of the hole, the remaining slots glow dashed amber; the rack stays wood-coloured. The piece can be dragged out again.
- **After the plug:** the gold rack rises onto the tens shelf (400 ms), the leftover piece hops onto the ones shelf; the keypad wakes (in "Snap first" mode).
- **Swap** (chip): the rack's cubes and the stick trade places (450 ms arc); the equation strip reads the turned-around sum "8 + 6"; the bond moves to the new stick.
- **Keypad:** digits into the answer box; backspace removes.
- **Free play "Cube bench":** two dials (1–9) make the rack and the stick; everything above works; golden tens collect on the shelf.

**Level "Make 10" — 8 rounds** (G1-106 / G1-427): the rack holds k coral cubes; the stick is a full ten-stick (10 teal cubes); the child snaps off the piece that fills the hole and types the missing partner: the equation strip reads "k + □ = 10". k = 7, 4, 9, 2, 6, 5, 8, 3 → answers 3, 6, 1, 8, 4, 5, 2, 7. (The ten-stick snapped at the right joint makes two pieces — the partner and k itself — and k only fits when k = 5, the one round where both pieces are the same length.)

**One right answer each; never 0;** every addend ≥ 1; every total 11–17 in the default level (11, 12, 14, 15, 16 twice each, 13 three times — the three old 13-sums are kept; the new rounds were chosen so that no total dominates) (every sum really crosses ten — no round can be done without making a ten).
**Answer position:** the total is typed; the plug is found by physics, not chosen from options. Measured: (a) the misconception-2 total "10 + second number" never equals the true total (the first number is never 10), so that error always misses (gate asserts for all 14); (b) no two adjacent rounds share a total after the shuffle (the gate asserts the shuffle never puts equal totals side by side); (c) the plug piece is not always the shorter piece of the snapped stick — without swapping it is shorter in 7 rounds (1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 13), longer in 5 (6, 9, 11, 12, 14) and equal in 2 (3, 7), so "drag the small piece" fails; the gate recomputes the split per round and asserts all three cases occur.
**Order:** order-only reshuffle per pass; never the same order twice.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check before the plug fits (Snap first mode):** no miss; the stick's joints glint once in turn; bubble: **"Snap the stick so one piece fills the hole in the ten."**
- **Check with an empty answer box:** no miss; bubble: **"A ten and {left} more — type the total."**
- **Miss 1:** the shelves show the child's number as ghost structure: a ghost ten on the tens shelf and ghost slots on the ones shelf for (typed − 10). If too many ghost slots (the "10 + the whole second number" error), the extra slots glow amber and a gold thread runs from them into the rack; if too few, real cubes stand outside ghost slots. Bubble: **"The ten is full. Count what is left beside it."**
- **Miss 2:** the leftover cubes get numerals 11, 12, … counting on from a big "10" printed on the gold rack's end. Bubble: **"Count on from 10."**
- **In-my-head mode miss 1 (no plug yet):** the stick snaps itself at the right joint and the plug slides in (the strategy shown), then the child types again; miss 2 as above.
- **A new move or keypress takes the help away** until the next Check.
- **Success:** the total lands in the answer box with a gold pulse; the gold ten and the leftover piece slide together into one "number" on the shelf and give a little bounce. Bubble: **"{a} + {b} = {a} + {plug} + {left} = 10 + {left} = {total}"** (Make 10: **"{k} and {p} make 10!"**).

- **Gate `verify-ten-plug-core.js` (own oracle):** (1) for every round a + b ∈ 11..17 and a, b ∈ 1..9 (default level); hole = 10 − a; strategy split = (hole, b − hole) with both parts ≥ 1; (2) `tryPlug` returns fit iff len = hole, long iff > hole, short iff < hole, for every piece length 1..b; (3) swap rounds: after swap the plug split is (10 − b, a − (10 − b)) and the total is unchanged; (4) typed = 10 + b never passes (misconception 2), typed = a + b passes; (5) Make 10: k + partner = 10, the ten-stick snapped at joint (10 − k) yields pieces (10 − k, k) and only 10 − k fits (k = 5 both fit — asserted as the single exception); (6) totals never adjacent equal over 10 000 seeds; (7) the keypad is asleep until the plug fits in Snap first mode and awake at rest in In my head; (8) no total, addend or partner is 0.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — snapping cubes and plugging a hole is physical fun, and the gold ripple when the plug fits is a real "click" of success.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "how many does 8 need to be a ten?" is answered by a piece that fits or bounces, and the shelf shows 10 and 3 as 13; the 10 + 5 error is caught by showing where the missing 2 went.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Wordless apparatus, no score, the total is typed, the help shows the structure of ten-and-ones rather than the answer.
4. **The invention:** the first number is a ten-rack with a hole, and only the exact piece snapped from the second number plugs it.
