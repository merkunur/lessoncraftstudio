## The skill
Quadrilaterals share attributes in nested categories: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE SHAPE SIEVE. A chute drops a shape onto a first sieve that tests 'four straight sides' (each side taps once as it is counted); shapes that fail stay on the top tray. Below, the chute forks into two side-by-side tests: a set-square swings into every corner (four square corners) and a ruler measures every side (four equal sides). Passing the corner test drops it into the RECTANGLE tray, passing the side test into the RHOMBUS tray; a shape that passes both drops through both into the SQUARE tray, which sits under both forks, so the child sees a square land as a rectangle and a rhombus at once. Before the drop the child taps the tray where they predict it will land; then the tests animate. Trays are marked by icons (right-angle mark, equal-side ticks, both); names appear in the bubble. Sort mode: the shape stays on the table and the child drags it onto every name tag that fits (quadrilateral, rectangle, rhombus, square); a fitting tag left empty pulses when Done is tapped. Miss 1: the failing test replays (the gap under the set-square, the ruler's two different lengths); miss 2: the shape's sides and corners get marks. Right: the shape settles in its tray and the bubble lists every name it carries.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** A square is not a rectangle; a tilted square is not a square; a shape belongs to only one category.
- **Pictures:** none (shapes and sieves drawn tool-grade)
- **Settings:** Shapes: rectangles, squares, rhombuses / also trapezoids and kites · Mode: predict the tray / tap every name
- **Rounds:** Generated shapes at varied sizes and rotations, ≥ 9 rounds; the deepest tray balanced across top / rectangle / rhombus / square; squares appear turned (diamond-like) in ≥ 2 rounds and non-square rhombuses in ≥ 2.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-340
- **Distinct from:** Sort by Sides counts sides only; Pip's Museum names shapes in any orientation at K. The sieve teaches the inclusion hierarchy.
- **Build risk:** Low.
