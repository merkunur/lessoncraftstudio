## The skill
Build words from a root with suffixes (bake -> baker, baking, baked) and use the form a sentence needs.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE WORD PRESS: a root word block (bake) sits in a little printing press; suffix stamps (-er, -ing, -ed, -s, and native equivalents) hang above. The child pulls a stamp down; the press prints the new word, applying the spelling change visibly (the e drops, the n doubles in runner) and shows what the word now names: a person picture from the occupations theme for -er, the action for -ing. Person mode: the child prints the person word for a picture. Sentence mode: a sentence with a gap shaped like its job (a person slot, an action slot) waits below; the child prints the form and drags it in — a wrong form does not seat.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Adding the suffix without the spelling change (bakeer, runer); choosing the root form regardless of sentence role.
- **Pictures:** occupations theme (baker, painter, teacher, farmer, singer, swimmer), food for 'bake'
- **Settings:** Mode: person words / root family in sentences · Spelling changes: none / with e-drop and doubling
- **Rounds:** Native root banks per locale (agent-noun suffixes differ: -er, -eur/-euse, -ero/-era, -dor...), ≥ 8 rounds, which suffix is right balanced.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-398, G3-399
- **Distinct from:** Sage's Root Garden recognises root families and Marigold's machine applies prefixes/suffixes for meaning; the press builds the form and seats it by sentence role. The two settings are separate round sets (two skills).
- **Build risk:** Medium: agent-noun morphology differs sharply across the 11 locales (gender in fr/es/it/pt/de); native panel must rebuild each bank.
