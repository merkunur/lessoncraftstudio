## The skill
Sort finished things into the right recycling bin (paper, plastic, glass, metal, food waste).

## The apparatus and its invention
THE BIN WITH A WINDOW. Three to five bins (apparatus) in the locale's bin colours with a material icon on the lid and a see-through window low down showing what each already holds (two library things of its material). All slots are the same size, so shape is never a tell. The child drags a finished thing to a bin. Right: it drops through and appears in the window lying with its own material. Miss 1: it taps the lid and stays out; the lid icon of the right bin lights. Miss 2: the window of the right bin glows. Success: all things in, the bins' lids close one after another.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** All rubbish goes in one bin; a greasy pizza box is paper; a plastic toy goes in plastic recycling.
- **Pictures:** kitchen-tools (jar, glass, bottle), at-the-supermarket (can, water bottle, bag), post-office (envelope, letter, package), classroom (notebook). MISSING: food-waste items (banana peel, apple core, egg shell) — the food bin is refused until they exist.
- **Settings:** Bins: 3 (paper · glass · plastic & metal) / per-locale full set (minus food) · Window: shows contents / empty
- **Rounds:** 9 things per pass, bins balanced, each bin the answer 2–4 times; one right bin per locale table (the K-357 bank).
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-357, K-366, K-367
- **Distinct from:** No recycling activity exists.
- **Build risk:** Bin colours and groupings differ strongly per country (de Gelber Sack, sv FTI, fi, nl, it) — per-locale bin sets from the K-357 banks, one reviewer. Glass jar vs plastic bottle must be visually distinct pictures.
