## 1. The pitch
"A story is a book that breaks and gets mended: the trouble page cracks open, and only the page that really fixes THAT trouble can send its bandage over the crack."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- The book is a real object: a hard cover, a spine with a ribbon bookmark, cream pages that **turn** with a page-curl. A fat gold stud on the spine makes the book read itself, page after page, like a parent at bedtime.
- Every story has a **shape you can see**: the start page is whole; the trouble page splits with a coral zigzag crack, a jolt and a dry "tock"; the fix page sends a bandage (the library `hospital/bandage`) spinning across to seal the crack with a soft two-note chime. A story literally breaks and gets mended — and children want to break the next one.
- The wrong guesses are funny and fair: a calm page asked to be the trouble just lies there, perfectly whole — "nothing went wrong!"; a page that fixes *something else* (an umbrella for a lost kite) sends its bandage over, it doesn't fit the crack, peels up and flutters to the floor.
- Earned show per round: a mended book closes, its cover shows the three pages as a tiny strip, and it slides onto a bookshelf rail above the stage; after a pass the shelf holds 9 spines, each in its story's colour. No score — the shelf simply fills.
- Free play: between rounds the child can open any shelved book and press the stud to watch it crack and mend again; the ribbon bookmark swings when tugged.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** RL.K.3 — identify the characters, the setting and the major events of a story, structured as **start** (who and where), **trouble** (what goes wrong), **fix** (how it gets better); and the sequencing the six worksheets ask for, read through that structure: beginning / middle / end (G1-401), first / next / last and numbering the pictures (K-379, K-381 — the bound pages carry the numerals 1, 2, 3), what happens next (G1-400), sentences to pictures (G1-402), the retell with starters (G2-378 — the success read).
- **Misconception 1 — "the trouble is any sad or busy picture" / "the first picture is the problem".** **Pushback:** a page tabbed TROUBLE has to crack when the book reads it; a calm page cannot — it lies flat and whole, and the reading stops. The loose pages are always scrambled, so place never tells the part.
- **Misconception 2 — "any happy ending will do" (G1-400 answered by mood).** **Pushback:** the bandage only seals a crack its fix really mends. A page where something nice happens that has nothing to do with the trouble (the fox gets an umbrella; her kite is still stuck in the tree) sends its bandage, it does not fit the crack, peels up and falls. The fix must answer the trouble — that is what makes it the fix.
- **Misconception 3 — "a story is just things that happen" (no trouble in the middle).** **Pushback (Find the trouble):** choose a middle page where nothing goes wrong and there is no crack for the fix to mend — the bandage slides across a whole page and drops: a fix with nothing to fix.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag a page or a tab** (it lifts 6 px with a shadow within 50 ms and follows the finger). Drop on a slot / a page → it snaps (120 ms); drop elsewhere → glides home in 180 ms. Tap-tap alternative everywhere (tap to lift — it wobbles; tap the target). Everything can be moved again until the stud is pressed; nothing reacts on drop, so no part can be found by trying.
- **The stud (= Check):** the book reads. Each turn is a 600 ms page-curl (on phones the next row unfolds downward); the caption is highlighted word by word and read by TTS where available.
  - **START page:** the green pennant plants itself on the page corner and flutters.
  - **TROUBLE page:** a coral zigzag (3 px) tears the frame top to bottom in 250 ms, the halves part 4 px, a dry "tock" — and the trouble's **thing** (the nest, the carrot, the kite…) gets a thin coral ring: this is what went wrong.
  - **FIX page:** the bandage lifts from the page corner, spins once (300 ms) and lies across the crack; the halves close; the thing's coral ring turns teal — it is back, or safe. A rising two-note chime.
- **A wrong page at the stud:** a calm page in the trouble slot does not tear — it lies flat, the reading stops and the turn holds half-open; a page in the fix slot that does not mend THIS trouble sends its bandage, the bandage meets the crack, curls up at both ends (200 ms), peels off and flutters to the floor; a page with nothing changed (the kite still in the tree) sends nothing — the bandage stays on its corner and the crack stays open.
- **Name-the-parts tabs:** a tab dropped on a loose page clips to its top edge; dropping a second tab on the same page swaps them. At the stud the tabbed pages fly into the book in tab order (START slot 1, TROUBLE slot 2, FIX slot 3) and it reads as above.
- **Strips (Match):** drag a sentence strip to a page's caption slot; it snaps in 120 ms; strips swap like pages. At the stud each strip is read under its picture.
- **Free play:** shelved books open on tap and read on the stud; the ribbon swings when tugged.

### The candidate pages (for Find the trouble and Find the fix) — the same actor every time, so the actor never gives it away
For each story two wrong **trouble** pages and two wrong **fix** pages, composed from the same library pictures:
- **T-calm** (nothing goes wrong — it cannot crack): the start scene a moment later (the actor still has the thing; caption e.g. "Bear held his balloon.").
- **T-other** (a real trouble, but not the one the fix mends): rain falls on the actor, who still has the thing (weather/rainy, caption "Then it rained."). In S3, where rain IS the trouble, T-other is birds-2/crow taking the basket ("A bird took the basket!" — the umbrella does not bring it back); in S8 T-other is rain with the boat still beside the frog (the duck has nothing to push back).
- **F-other** (something nice that does not mend THIS trouble): the actor with a different thing — S1 robin with spring/worm; S2 mouse with toys/ball; S3 the friends with toys/kite, still under rain; S4 fox under weather/umbrella, kite still in the tree; S5 bear with toys/ball; S6 pig under weather/umbrella, hat still in the lake; S7 dog with toys/kite; S8 frog with toys/ball; S9 mouse with toys/ball, key still on the ground.
- **F-same** (nothing changed): the trouble scene a moment later — the thing still lost (caption e.g. "The kite was still in the tree.").
Every candidate carries its own authored caption (shown with Words: shown).

### Tasks (setting 1)
- **Name the parts** (RL.K.3, G1-401, K-379/K-381) — 9 rounds: the story's three pages lie loose on the table in a **derangement** of story order (over a pass each table position holds the START page 3×, the TROUBLE page 3×, the FIX page 3×; never the same arrangement twice running); tab order in the tray cycles through its 6 permutations. The child tabs each page; the stud binds them in tab order (the numerals 1, 2, 3 appear on the slots — first, next, last) and reads.
- **Find the trouble** (G1-401 "draw the middle") — 9 rounds: START bound in slot 1, FIX bound in slot 3, slot 2 empty; three candidates on the table: the true trouble, T-calm, T-other. The child puts one in the middle; the stud reads.
- **Find the fix** (G1-400 "what happens next") — 9 rounds: START and TROUBLE bound, slot 3 empty; three candidates: the true fix, F-other, F-same.
- **Match the sentences** (G1-402) — 9 rounds: the three pages bound in true order without captions; three caption strips dealt in a derangement under the book. The child places each strip; the stud reads each strip under its picture.

**Answer position:** Trouble/Fix — the true candidate's table slot is balanced 3/3/3 over a pass; the gate fails if any slot exceeds 40 % or repeats three times running. Name — as above. Match — strip order is a fresh derangement.
**Order of rounds:** order-only reshuffle per pass; never the previous pass's order; never the same story twice in a row across a pass boundary.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Nothing done:** Name with no tab placed → "Put a tab on a page." · Trouble/Fix with the slot empty → "Put a page in the empty place." · Match with strips still dealt → "Put a sentence under each picture." (no miss)
- **Name, miss 1:** the book reads to the first wrong page and stops there (calm page in the trouble slot: no crack; non-fix in the fix slot: the bandage peels off; a non-start page in slot 1: the pennant tips over and slides off). The pages drop back onto the table, tabs still on. Hint by the first wrong tab: START "START is where the story begins: who and where." · TROUBLE "TROUBLE is when something goes wrong." · FIX "FIX is how the trouble gets better." **Miss 2:** as miss 1, then the page that belongs under the wrong tab shows that part's mark faintly (pale pennant / pale zigzag / pale bandage outline).
- **Trouble, miss 1:** T-calm → no crack, the bandage slides across a whole page and drops; hint "Nothing goes wrong on that page." · T-other → it cracks, but the bandage peels off; hint "Something goes wrong — but the fix doesn't mend it. Look at the fix page: what does it fix?" **Miss 2:** the fix page's thing gets a teal ring and the same thing glows on the true trouble candidate. Hint: "Find the page where the {thing} goes wrong."
- **Fix, miss 1:** F-other → the bandage doesn't fit and peels off; hint "That makes something else better. What went wrong?" · F-same → no bandage leaves the page; hint "The trouble is still there on that page." **Miss 2:** the trouble's thing gets its coral ring on page 2 and glows on the true fix candidate. Hint: "Find the page where the {thing} is back."
- **Match, miss 1:** the book reads; under a wrong strip its key word (marked in the data per locale: "nest", "carrot", "rained"…) is underlined and a dotted line searches the picture above and ends in a small empty circle — the picture does not show it. Hint: "Read that sentence again. Does the picture show it?" **Miss 2:** the dotted line jumps to the page that DOES show it and that page's frame flashes.
- A new move clears the showing until the next stud press.
- **Success:** a clean read — pennant, crack, mend. The ribbon lies flat, the book closes and slides onto the shelf. Bubble: the **retell** with starters, read from the three captions: "First, {retell1} Then, {retell2} At the end, {retell3}" (each `retell` string authored per locale — §9), then "Start, trouble, fix — that's a story!"

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a book that reads itself, cracks and mends, a bandage that peels off when it doesn't fit, and a shelf that fills with the child's own books.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "the story breaks at the trouble and the fix mends THAT trouble" is a sentence a teacher will reuse, and the class can argue whether the umbrella really fixes the kite.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Library pictures only, calm, no score, works on a phone.
4. **The invention:** a story is a book that cracks at the trouble — and only the page that really fixes that trouble can seal the crack with its bandage.
