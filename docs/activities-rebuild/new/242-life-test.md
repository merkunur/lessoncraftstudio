## The skill
Tell living things from non-living things by what living things do: they grow, they need food and water, they make young.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE THREE LAMPS. A test bench with three lamps (apparatus icons): GROWS · NEEDS FOOD AND WATER · MAKES YOUNG. The child drags a library thing onto the bench and it is tested: a living thing lights the lamps one by one with evidence — grows: its young picture swaps to its grown picture (chick → hen, bud → flower); food and water: a water drop is drawn in; makes young: a small copy (the young picture) appears beside it. A non-living thing lights nothing, even if it moves (the car drives across the bench and the lamps stay dark; the clock's hands turn and the lamps stay dark). Then the child sorts it into LIVING or NOT LIVING. Miss 1: the basket refuses and the lamps re-run. Miss 2: the lamps stay lit over the right basket. LAMPS-AFTER setting: sort first, then the lamps test it.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Moving = alive (a car, the wind, a clock), and plants are not alive.
- **Pictures:** Living: farm-animals (hen, chick, cow, calf, sheep, lamb, horse, foal), forest-creatures (caterpillar), insects-and-bugs (butterfly), spring (bud), flowers (tulip, sunflower), tree (apple tree), vegetables. Not living: vehicles (car, train), around-the-house (clock), toys (robot, ball, kite), beach (rock), camping (log → refused: once living).
- **Settings:** Things: obvious / tricky (cars, clocks, robots, plants, seeds) · Lamps: shown first / test after sorting
- **Rounds:** 9 things per pass, living and not living balanced, ≥ 3 movers among the not-living (car, clock, robot, kite) in the tricky setting; one right basket.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-201
- **Distinct from:** No life-science activity exists.
- **Build risk:** Dead natural things (log, leaf, shell) refused — "was alive" is beyond K. Plants without a young picture use bud → flower only.
