## 1. The pitch
"Pour one part into the basket and the cloth drops over it with its number written on top — now the other part's things go in one at a time, and the only way to know the total is to count on from the number on the cloth."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **The pour.** The child drags a whole bowl over the basket and tips it: the things tumble in with a soft wooden patter (one sound per object, 60 ms apart), then the checked cloth floats down and tucks itself in, and a little paper tag pins itself to the cloth with the number. It is a magic trick: the things vanish, the number stays.
- **The posting.** The second part goes in one by one through the slot in the cloth: each thing is tapped (or dragged) and drops through with a *plip*; the cloth bulges a little higher every time. Children count along out loud — "eight… nine, ten, eleven!".
- **Which bowl first?** The child chooses. Pour the 8 and post the 3 = three posts; pour the 3 and post the 8 = eight posts. Nobody says so; children discover that pouring the big one first is quicker and start doing it — the counting-on-from-the-larger strategy, found by the hand.
- **The lift.** When the total is right, the cloth whips off: everything inside has arranged itself into ten-frame rows, two colours, and the bond's whole circle fills with the number.
- **Free play:** after a round the basket stays: the child can pour and post any bowl again, lift the cloth, pour back out.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** add two parts within 20 by putting them together (1.OA.A.1), using counting on as the efficient strategy (1.OA.C.5); in the bond faces, decompose numbers to 10 (and 20) and find the whole or the missing part (K.OA.A.3–A.5).
- **Misconception 1 — counting all from 1, losing track:** the classic K–1 error is recounting both sets from one and double-counting or skipping. **Pushback by physics:** the first part is hidden. It cannot be recounted. Its number is on the cloth, and the child must start from it.
- **Misconception 2 — counting the cloth number as the first post** ("8 … 8, 9, 10" → 10 for 8 + 3): **Pushback:** the tag never changes while posting (so the child must keep the count), and at miss 1 the posted things are numbered *from* the tag — 9, 10, 11 — so the child sees the first thing posted is one more than 8, not 8 again.
- **Misconception 3 — the whole is the bigger part:** in "Find the whole" bonds, typing 8 for 8 + 3. **Pushback:** the basket's bulge is visibly higher than the 8-bowl's heap when lifted; at miss 2 the lifted cloth shows 8 + 3 in ten-frame rows.

## 5. The hand
- **Pour:** drag a bowl over the basket (the bowl tilts 30° as it crosses the rim, ≤ 100 ms response) and release — or just tap a bowl, it flies over and tips. The things tumble in one by one (120 ms each) and the basket counts them aloud on the tag as they fall — 1, 2, 3 … 8 — so the child watches the first part being counted; then the cloth drops (240 ms) and the tag pins on with the final number. The poured bowl turns upside down beside the basket (empty). From now on that part is a number, not a heap.
- **Post:** the other bowl's things can now be tapped one by one (or dragged) — each flies to the slot and drops through (*plip*, 140 ms); the cloth bulge rises 4 px. They go in the order the child taps, and every one leaves an empty dimple in the bowl so the child can see what is still to post.
- **Order choice:** either bowl may be poured; the other becomes the posting bowl automatically. Tapping a *second* bowl to pour when one is already under the cloth → the second bowl tips and its things *post themselves one by one* anyway (the slot only takes one at a time) — pouring is only possible into an empty basket.
- **Peek (sound-off safe, costs nothing):** a long-press on the cloth lifts one corner for 600 ms — just a glimpse of jumbled things, not countable (they are piled, not in rows), so peeking cannot replace counting on.
- **Write:** tap the whole circle or the keypad → total.
- **Undo:** "Empty the basket" (a small curved-arrow button under the basket) pours everything back into the bowls (300 ms) and the round starts again; no miss is counted.

Eight different answers per pass. Over-posting is impossible: the basket is full at the whole — an extra post sits on top of the cloth and rolls off back to the tray (physics, not a message), and that roll-off is how the child knows they have reached the whole. - **Order:** order-only reshuffle per pass; never the same order twice; never 0 (no part is ever 0 — the bonds-of-n faces leave out n + 0).

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check before anything is poured** → bubble "Pour one bowl into the basket first." (no miss).
- **Check with things still in the posting bowl** → the remaining things bounce once; bubble "Some are still waiting to go in." (no miss).
- **Miss 1:** the cloth tag stays, and a ghost numeral rises over every posted thing *in the order they went in*, starting one after the tag (8 → 9, 10, 11), shown as a little arc of numerals over the slot. EN **"Start at {first} on the cloth and count on: {first+1}, {first+2} …"**
- **Miss 2:** the cloth lifts: inside, the things lie in ten-frame rows, the poured part in its colour, the posted part in the other; the whole circle shows a pencil outline of the right total. EN **"{a} and {b} make {total}."**
- A new move takes the help away until the next Check.
- **Success:** the cloth whips off (300 ms), the things hop into ten-frame rows (two colours), the whole circle fills with the total and the two bond lines glow toward it; a soft rising three-note chime. EN **"8 and 3 make 11 — you counted on from 8!"** (if the child poured the smaller part first, the line is the same — no judgement — but the *next* round's bubble at the pour step offers, once per pass: **"Try pouring the bigger bowl first."**). Make-the-whole: **"7 and 3 make 10."**

Nothing blocked.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Choose it again?** Yes — a basket that swallows things and keeps their number on a tag, a slot to post through, a cloth that whips off.
2. **Teacher on the board?** Yes — "hide the first addend and count on" is exactly the move teachers make with a cup over counters; here it is built in, and pouring the bigger part first is discovered, not drilled.
3. **Parent trust?** Library pictures, calm sounds, nothing timed or scored.
4. **Invention:** the first part goes under a cloth with its number pinned on top, so the total can only be found by counting on as the second part is posted in.
