## The four questions

1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** A fruit stand: an order slip shows a number, two market trays wait with ten cups each. Tap a cup — the fruit tumbles in, cup after cup, up to the one you tapped. Tap a fruit and the fruit from there on goes back. Find where 17 ends, press Check, and the stand counts its fruit out loud.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** K.CC.B.4 to 20, the second half of the cluster after the Animal Train (to 10): one fruit per cup, filled in counting order (B.4a); the child decides where the count ENDS — the last number tells how many (B.4b); the next cup is one more, the last fruit taken back is one fewer (B.4c). The two trays of ten make the teens visible: 17 is a full tray and 7 more, and the two slips teachers see at the teens are named — only the 7 of 17, or only the full tray.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** No mascot, no score, no timer; real fruit pictures; nothing is counted for the child before Check; after a wrong Check the stand numbers its fruit, after a second it marks the extra fruit or the cups still waiting. The help never moves a fruit.
4. **The invention: FILL TO HERE.** The child does not tap seventeen times; they find the seventeenth cup. Counting becomes a search for where a number ends on the double ten-frame — by ones, or by tens and fives. Teacher setting "one at a time" turns it back into one fruit per tap.

## Rounds

To 20 (default): 12 · 17 · 9 · 15 · 20 · 11 · 14 · 6 · 18 · 13. Up to 10 (one tray): 4 · 7 · 9 · 3 · 10 · 6 · 8 · 5. A different fruit every round. Guessers: "tap the last cup" is right only where the order is the whole stand (20 once in ten, 10 once in eight); no order is 0.

## Layout

## Refuse-list

No mascot, no drawn fruit, no running count while filling, no help that moves fruit, no timer or score, no 0, no words on the apparatus (the order slip carries a numeral only).
