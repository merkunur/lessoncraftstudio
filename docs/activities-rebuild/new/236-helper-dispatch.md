## 1. The pitch
"A window on the street calls for help; you send a helper, their tool flies to the window and does its OWN job — a whisk whisks, a hose sprays, a hammer taps — so only the right helper's tool can fix what is wrong."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **The street fills up.** Three little houses, three windows each: nine windows, nine calls per pass. Every fixed window stays lit and shows what the helper did (the car drives back clean, the vase holds tulips, the board says 1 2 3). At the end of a pass the whole street glows window by window, like a town at dusk. It is a picture of the town the child helped — never a score.
- **The tools are funny when they are wrong.** Send the chef to a smoking car and the whisk whirls busily in front of it… and the car keeps smoking. Send the firefighter to the empty vase and the hose sprays it full of water — still no flowers. Children send the wrong helper on purpose to see what the tool does, and in doing so they learn what every tool is FOR.
- **The helpers walk.** A helper card slides down the road to the door with a small bob and a shadow, and walks back if the job was not theirs.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** name community helpers, the jobs they do and the tools they use; match a problem to the helper whose job it is (dispatch), and a helper to their tool (K-213).
- **Misconceptions and how the street pushes back:**
  1. *"Any grown-up helper can fix anything."* A tool only does its own job: the stethoscope listens (rings pulse), the screwdriver turns, the mop wipes. On the wrong problem the action plays out in full and changes nothing — the trouble ring keeps turning. The mismatch is visible as physics, not as a cross.
  2. *"A helper is their uniform/building, not their job."* The request is a THING with a problem (a smoking car, a puddle, a leaning fence), so the child must reason from the job, not match a picture of a hospital to a picture of a doctor.
  3. *Gender assumptions about jobs.* **No round's answer ever depends on gender** — every request has exactly one helper whose JOB fixes it, and the station never holds two helpers who differ only by sex (there are no such pairs: chef and cook are kept apart, doctor and nurse never meet in dispatch). No string uses a pronoun for a helper. The library's occupation pictures are stereotyped (the vet, florist, teacher, nurse, librarian are women; the mechanic, firefighter, carpenter, baker, janitor, gardener, farmer are men), so the activity does not lean on them for a lesson about gender — it simply never lets sex carry information. The stereotype is recorded honestly in §12 as an art need, and the hint and celebration frames never imply that a job "belongs" to anyone.

## 5. The hand
- **Dispatch:** press a helper (with their pegged tool) → the pair lifts 6 px with a shadow (60 ms) and follows the finger. Drop on the calling house: the pair slides along the road to the door (500 ms, ±3° bob), the tool flies from the peg to the window (250 ms) and performs its OWN action (600 ms):
  - screwdriver: turns three times · stethoscope: its disc taps the request, three teal heartbeat rings pulse out · hose: a drawn blue water arc sprays · mop: wipes left–right twice · tulips: three tulips slide in from above · marker: draws three strokes "1 2 3" in teal · whisk: spins fast · rake: sweeps in an arc · hammer: three taps with a small dust puff.
  - **Right job:** the trouble resolves — puffs vanish and the car rolls out of the frame and back; the dog hops once and a drawn heart floats up; the fire shrinks to nothing and three grey puffs drift off; the puddle shrinks to nothing; the tulips stand in the vase; the board shows 1 2 3; a cake (bakery/cake) rises onto the plate; the leaves sweep into one corner and fade; the fence turns upright. Ring turns teal; the window settles warm.
  - **Wrong job:** the action plays in full, nothing resolves (hose on a car: water droplets run off the car — still smoking; tulips bounce off a car and fall back to the peg), the trouble ring keeps turning, the tool flies back and the helper walks back to the station (400 ms). No sound of failure — a soft "boop".
  - Tap-tap works too (tap a helper, tap the house).
- **Tools mode:** drag a tool from the rack to the hook at the helper's hand; it hangs and swings (it can be swapped). Nothing is judged until Check.
- **Sound (optional):** each tool has a tiny sound (whirr, splash, tap-tap, strum); TTS says the helper's job name when tapped.
- **Free play:** after the last window, all nine windows stay tappable — tapping a fixed window replays its helper's action; the station stays live and any helper can be sent to any fixed window to watch their tool do its job.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Dispatch has no Check button:** sending a helper IS the check. A wrong helper costs a miss (counted, never shown as a score).
  - **Miss 1** (first wrong helper): after the wrong tool's action, the calling window's trouble cue pulses bigger once (the puffs swell, the fence wobbles) — the problem is shown again, not the answer. Hint: "That tool did its own job. What does this window need?"
  - **Miss 2:** the right helper's tool on its peg glows and does a tiny preview of its action (the mop twitches, the rake sweeps a little) at the station. Hint: "Look at the tools. Which one can fix it?"
  - Success: the window resolves (§5) and the bubble says "The {helper} fixed it with the {tool}!" After the ninth window: the street lights up window by window (90 ms each) — "Thank you, helpers! The whole street is fixed."
- **Tools mode:** Check with no tool hung → hint, no miss: "Drag a tool to the hook."
  - **Miss 1:** the hung tool performs its own action beside the helper — and the helper's work picture appears in a small drawn bubble frame over the doorway for 1 s (the farmer's field = vegetables/corn, the chef's plate = bakery/cake…) so the child sees what the job needs; the tool swings back to the rack. Hint: "That tool does a different job. What does a {helper} do?"
  - **Miss 2:** the work picture stays, and the right tool on the rack does its preview twitch. Hint: "Which tool helps with that job?"
  - **Success:** the tool clicks onto the hook, a teal ring joins helper and tool, and the tool does its action. "A {helper} uses a {tool}."
- A new move takes the help away until the next Check/dispatch.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a child choose it again?** Yes — a street that lights up window by window, helpers that walk, and tools that do comically honest things when sent to the wrong job.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "who can help?" is the K community-helpers conversation; the class can predict what the whisk will do to the car before sending it.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Library pictures, no pronouns, no scary scenes (the syringe is out; the fire is a small bonfire), no score or timer.
4. **The invention:** every tool always does its own job, wherever it is sent — so the problem is only fixed when the right helper's tool arrives.
