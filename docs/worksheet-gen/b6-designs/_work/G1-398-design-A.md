## 1 Page concept (base: "Animal Habitats: match the animal", G1)

**"Four plates of a field guide, eight loose specimen tags."** The top of the page is a row of four habitat plates, each a drawn emblem tile of a place (no animal inside it) under a teal tab lettered A, B, C, D, with the habitat's name printed below the tile. The lower two thirds are eight specimen tags in two rows of four, each carrying one animal picture and an empty tab-shaped box. The child reads a tag, looks up to the plates, finds where that animal lives, and copies the plate's tab letter into the tag's tab. The tabs are the only bridge, so the page cannot be done without looking at the places.

Why top quality: ONE focal apparatus (the plate row, 639 x 210, drawn in one line language), eight calm tags with large pictures (92 px, twice the G1 floor), pencil-first (the answer is a single capital letter a first grader can write), generous whitespace (594 of 722 px used; the slack goes to the gaps, never to a bigger box). The tile is an EMBLEM (a place reduced to its signature shapes: the flat-topped acacia, the dark cattail heads, the jagged iceberg), so the child learns what makes each place that place, which is the content of the habitat lesson itself. The stepped tab row is the family's identity: every face reuses plates and/or tags, so six pages read as six pages of one guide.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- **Base.** Plates print the place and the letter; tags print only the picture and an EMPTY tab box stamped `data-lcs-answer=<letter>`. No animal name, no line, no colour grouping. Uniqueness: the bank `data/b6/habitats.js` stores per animal `{home:<tileId>, also:[tileIds]}`; a page is legal iff for every tag `home ∈ shown` and `also ∩ shown = ∅`. verify() re-derives each letter from `data-lcs-vocab` + the plate order (`data-hb-tab` on each plate, `data-lcs-habitat` on its tile). Wrong is visible: the teacher sees "A" under a whale while plate A is a forest.
- **Tells gated (per face, all 11 locales, pooled over 400 seeds; the sample is raised, never the threshold):** (1) share of tags whose answer equals the plate directly above their column: expected 25 %, gate asserts it is not the modal outcome and records the measured value in the build record; (2) no tag row reads A B C D or D C B A; (3) the two tags of one habitat never sit in the same column; (4) each letter appears exactly twice by construction (elimination is legitimate reasoning, not a tell, and is recorded as such).
- **F2 (homes).** Both columns printed, no line drawn; one home per animal; right column a derangement (not identity, not reversal). **F3.** Row = plate + 4 tags, none crossed; exactly one tag's `home` is in the row habitat's `farFrom` list; residents' `also` never contains a habitat that could make the stranger belong. **F4.** Feature cards + tags, no line; each feature is TRUE for exactly one animal on the page (bank `trueFor` lists every pool animal it is true for, not only the target). **F5.** Each animal row: 6 chips, none circled; exactly 3 are needs (its food, water, its shelter), 3 are human-made objects. **F6** is open (no verify(), layout lints only).

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **"A world of windows"** (each habitat a round porthole lens, animals dropped in by number). Rejected: a circle wastes 21 % of a 147 px square exactly where the emblem's signature shapes live (the acacia crown and the cattails sit at the edges); round lenses are the G1-377 life-cycle signature, blurring a live family's look; and a window invites a SCENE (looking out at one place with things in it), which pulls toward composing animals inside the tile, the thing the lock forbids.
2. **"A nature map"** (one invented land with forest / pond / sea regions, write each animal's number in its region). Rejected: the map representation is owned by `maps` G1-379 (plan view, neat line, key); a region map must mix a top-view land with side-view library animals, the exact projection error the maps family teaches against; and region AREA becomes a tell (the big sea swallows more answers), which no seed rule removes cleanly.
3. Rejected inside F5: drawing the shelter with `animalHome` and the food as a library picture in the same chip strip (two art sources in one set, cross-panel ruling 4).

**Recommendation: the field guide.** Plates and tags are two object types the child can name ("the places" and "the animals"), the tab letter is the smallest possible pencil answer at G1, the stepped tabs are a visual signature no other family has, and the same two objects carry all six faces, so the family reads as one book.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Long locales:** plate names are the only text on the base; gate `scrollHeight <= 44` per pill in all 11 locales; F4 sentences capped at 140 chars per locale and measured (card `scrollHeight <= 72`); the instruction is budgeted at three lines by the 722 stack.
- **Greyscale print:** tiles carry no coral and are distinguished by SHAPE first (dark cattails, dark flat crown, white berg, corner leaves) and value second; the tile gate measures both on the render. Coral dashed tab boxes print as mid-grey DASHED outlines: the dash, not the hue, marks "write here". Tabs are teal fill with white letters (strong contrast in B&W). The only coral-vs-teal pairing on any page is dashed-box vs solid-tab, which differ in stroke pattern.
- **Pencil space:** tab box 56 x 44 for one capital; F6 lanes glyphH 24 on school lines (`rulingBlock`, starters none).
- **Cut lines:** none; nothing on this family is cut out.
- **9 px floor:** smallest text 15 px; the smallest drawn detail (web spiral, 1.2 units at 64 px = 0.77 px) is decorative, never a label; the gate asserts no `<text>` in either primitive.
- **Science truth:** penguin and walrus may share the single "polar" tile at G1 (the lock allows one polar tile at K-1), but no text anywhere says they live together; F4 copy never names the Arctic or Antarctica generically. Camel humps store FAT. Whale and dolphin never carry "gills". Farm animals and pets never appear.
- **What QA lints catch:** overflow, footer intrusion, off-token hex, 9 px floor, blank page (`data-ws-content`). **What only the family gate catches:** tells, uniqueness (`also` / `farFrom` / `trueFor`), name-pill fit, picture floors. **What only a human eye catches:** whether a tile reads as its place at 110 px in a photocopy, whether the duckling reads as "duck" and the nest chicks as a bird's nest in that country, whether the panel's habitat word is the school word. The native landing panels are briefed to AUDIT the render for exactly these.
