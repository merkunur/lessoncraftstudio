# Premium Tools v5 — the final twenty

**Ideas only, for you to read before we design anything.**

## The anatomy — read the two examples, not a theory

**Learning Clock** and **Money Mat** define the standard, and both failed batches missed it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A curriculum topic the teacher must teach and dreads** | telling time · money — not a maths structure (batch 1's mistake), not a pedagogy technique (batch 2's mistake) |
| **A concrete apparatus whose NAME says the topic** | a teacher scanning the shelf knows what it teaches before clicking |
| **A superpower the physical version cannot have** | geared hands + "halb drei" in your language · your country's coins + no wrong tap can exist |
| **Fun that lives in the mechanism, not in decoration** | gears, not stickers — the research is explicit that cute decorated materials *hurt* learning |

The two failures (Say It Board, Feelings Check-In) are both **non-curriculum**. So: every idea below
is a curriculum topic, and the batch was built topic-first — I mapped all 47 live tools by topic,
listed the uncovered dreaded topics, ran a fresh web pass per topic (best existing tool, its gap, the
beloved analog teacher trick to steal), and ran a creative panel (children's-museum exhibit designer +
K-3 teacher + toy inventor) to punch every apparatus up until it has a gasp. Fence = **the 47 live
tools only**, as instructed.

**The dread ranking from teacher forums:** 1. subtraction with regrouping ("the hardest math skill
taught in second grade") · 2. word problems · 3. telling time ✓shipped · 4. money ✓shipped ·
5. place value · 6. times tables · 7. explaining your reasoning. **#3 and #4 are the two tools you
called good — the standard is calibrated. This batch takes the rest of the list.**

# THE TWENTY

## A · Arithmetic and number (10)

### 1. THE EXCHANGE MACHINE · *Die Wechselmaschine* — subtraction with regrouping
**The dread:** #1 on the list. *"I write the little 1 and I can see half the room decide maths is a
set of arbitrary marks."*
**What it is:** the written vertical sum and the base-ten blocks are ONE object. Cross out the tens
digit and a ten **shatters** into ten ones in the same instant, in the same place; run it backwards
and ten ones fuse into a ten with a click — carrying and borrowing revealed as one machine in two
directions. A slow-motion crank lets the class watch the shatter frame by frame. Write the algorithm
and the blocks obey; move the blocks and the algorithm writes itself.
**The superpower nobody has:** every free block tool (Number Pieces, MathsBot, Brainingcamp) is a
blocks-only sandbox — the algorithm stays on the teacher's whiteboard and the child must weld the two
mentally. And **nobody localises the notation**: Germany writes the Übertrag *below* the answer line,
NL/US write it *above*, and German states split between *Abziehen* and *Ergänzen*. A US-shaped tool
is silently wrong in a German classroom. **This is the "halb drei" moment for arithmetic.**
**The fun:** the shatter. Every teacher who has taught regrouping forwards that six-second clip.

### 2. THE BAKING TRAY · *Das Backblech* — what multiplication IS
**The dread:** multiplication arrives as chanting; the meaning never lands.
**What it is:** trays of buns in equal rows. Push 3 trays of 4 together → a 3×4 array. **Rotate the
tray** and the same buns read 4×3. Then the real invention: the tray **cracks like a chocolate bar**
along any gridline — 7×6, the fact every eight-year-old fears, cracks into 5×6 and 2×6, two facts
they already own. Same buns throughout; the count never changes, only the story.
**The gap:** ictgames and Visnos show arrays *already abstracted into labelled dots*; no tool anywhere
does groups → array → rotate → crack as one continuous object. The crack turns a demo into a
fact-*strategy* engine, which is what the teacher actually needs on Tuesday.
**The fun:** cracking chocolate. Distributivity as a physical snap.

### 3. THE FOLDING WALL · *Die Faltwand* — the hundred facts that are really twenty
**The dread:** the child sees a 10×10 wall of facts and their face closes before you start.
**What it is:** the whole times-table grid stands like a wall. Tap ×1 — the column folds away with a
hinge-slam. ×10, ×2, ×5 fold. Then each commutative twin **folds onto its partner like a mirror
closing** — 7×3 lands on 3×7. The diagonal of squares stays as the wall's glowing spine. End state:
~20 tiles floating in a field of folded hinges.
**The gap:** the entire tables field is timers, diplomas and scores — the exact things banned here
(and the Dutch tafeldiploma culture is the extreme case). "You only need half the table" exists as a
throwaway line on posters; **no tool anywhere performs the fold-away as a visible whole-class event.**
The constraint (no timer, no score) is the whole product.
**The fun:** watching a mountain collapse into a molehill, once per year, with a gasp.

### 4. THE COUNTING CUPS · *Die Zählbecher* — why we group in tens
**The dread:** place value — *"grasping that 23 is two tens and three ones"* — named the Grade-1
sticking point everywhere.
**What it is:** two hundred buttons spill gloriously across the screen — far too many to count in
ones. The number display shows only **"?" — it refuses to know** until you group. Drag buttons into
cups: each cup **snaps shut at ten with a click and refuses an eleventh** (the Money-Mat purse
refusal); ten sealed cups auto-stack into a crate; and only when a container closes does a digit
advance. Digits literally ARE closed containers.
**The gap:** Counting Collections is the routine with the best research pedigree in early maths
(Stanford DREME, Franke/Kazemi) and **no digital version exists** — everything digital caps at ~20
pre-arranged objects, which deletes the entire problem.
**The fun:** the great spill, and the display that refuses to know.

### 5. THE NUMBER HOTEL · *Das Zahlenhotel* — the hundred chart with a body
**The dread:** children colour hundred charts for years and never own the structure; +10 stays a
counting chore.
**What it is:** rooms 1-100 in a ten-storey hotel. **+1 walks the corridor; +10 takes the lift one
floor.** Corridors physically END at room 9 of each floor — you cannot walk from 49 to 50; the
stairwell takes you up, which is *why* 49+1 changes the tens digit. The lift shaft ticks the tens
digit as you rise. Zoom out at the end: a second tower next door — the hundreds. Place value as a
skyline. Austere: numbered doors and a brass lift, no bellhops.
**The gap:** hundred-chart tools are everywhere and every one is a flat **colouring surface**; none
gives the movement a body, and "number hotel" returns nothing anywhere.
**The fun:** "Room 47, one floor up" — a five-year-old can narrate it.

### 6. THE NUMBER DRUM · *Die Zahlentrommel* — counting across the boundary
**The dread:** the K-1 wall: …38, 39, "forty-ten?" Decade boundaries, and later 99→100.
**What it is:** a geared rolling drum counter — the Learning Clock's own DNA. Crank forward and the
ones-drum **forces** the tens-drum at every full turn: 39 rolls to 40 because the gears leave it
nowhere else to go. Crank *backwards* through 100 → 99 and watch two drums surrender at once. Wrong
states are not corrected; they are unreachable.
**The gap:** ICT Games' ancient Flip Counter shows digits flipping; nothing lets a child CRANK it,
feel the gearing, or run it backwards slowly through the boundary.
**The fun:** the crank, and the double-surrender at 100.

### 7. THE LANDING STRIP · *Die Landebahn* — where numbers live
**The dread:** *"Is 71 nearer 20 or nearer 90?" gets silence* — and magnitude sense is the single
best predictor of later arithmetic in the research literature.
**What it is:** an empty strip, 0 at one end, 100 at the other. A number flies in as a small glider;
the class points where it should land; it lands where they said, and the true position is revealed —
and **every placement the class has ever made stays as a ghost**, so the room sees its own drift
(everyone crams 60-90 into the last inch). Then the second superpower: **pinch, and the space between
40 and 50 stretches to become its own empty strip.** The number line is the same all the way down.
**The gap:** the sticky-note number-line ritual is beloved and analog (NCTM, Marilyn Burns); the
accumulating class-guess cloud has never been digitised.
**The fun:** the reveal of the class's own collective bias, ghosted on the strip.

### 8. THE ROUNDING HILL · *Der Rundungshügel* — rounding as gravity
**The dread:** rounding is memorised as an arbitrary rule and understood by nobody.
**What it is:** the number line bent into hills — every ten is a valley, every five a ridge. Place 47
on the slope and let go: it **rolls** to 50. A ball on exactly 45 teeters on the ridge, wobbling —
and the country's convention arrives as a visible **puff of wind** that tips it (the arbitrary rule
shown *as* arbitrary, which is honest). Then pour a whole bucket of numbered balls and watch them
sort themselves into valleys — the class has just rounded a data set, and a histogram assembled
itself out of physics.
**The gap:** the "rounding roller-coaster" is a beloved metaphor that exists only on laminated
cardstock; the digital field is text-heavy US calculators.
**The fun:** the teeter on the ridge, and the bucket pour.

### 9. THE PAIR GATE · *Das Paartor* — odd and even
**The dread:** low dread, shallow learning — children memorise last digits and understand nothing.
**What it is:** counters march to an archway that admits them strictly **two abreast**. The odd one
is stopped at the gate, standing alone. Then the theorem: two stopped loners from two odd parades
**find each other and march through together** — odd + odd = even, proved by two lonely counters
pairing up.
**The gap:** every odd/even game tests the label; **nothing anywhere animates the pairing**, and the
odd+odd=even reveal exists nowhere.
**The fun:** quietly moving, and cheap to build — the highest delight-per-euro on the list.

### 10. THE DOUBLING MIRROR · *Der Doppelspiegel* — doubles, halves, and near-doubles
**The dread:** doubles are the load-bearing fact strategy of Grade 1, taught as flashcards.
**What it is:** put 6 counters before a mirror: you see 12. Near-doubles: one extra counter stands
**outside the glass** — 6+7 is "double 6 and the one who wouldn't fit." Halving: slide the mirror
across a set hunting for the fold line — for 9, the line **cannot rest**; one counter straddles it
forever. Odd numbers are the numbers the mirror can't split. Mirror-of-mirror: 3 → 6 → 12 → 24.
**The gap:** digitally nothing but flashcards; no tool links doubling and halving as inverse motions
of one apparatus. **Paid mode:** the hinged double mirror (the German *Spiegelbuch*) — close the
angle and 4, 6, 8 copies appear.
**The fun:** the mirror that hunts for 9's fold line and cannot rest.

## B · Word problems — the #2 dread (2)

### 11. THE MISSING QUESTION — *already approved by you; unchanged*
A wordless situation plays and freezes: five birds on a wire, two lifting off. **No question
appears.** The class argues about what this picture could be asked — it can be asked several things —
and only then writes a number sentence. The tool generates the problem structures no teacher can
generate by hand; the questions the class produces are captured and printable.

### 12. THE CURTAIN · *Der Vorhang* — the problems nobody sets
**The dread:** change-unknown problems (5 + ? = 9) are the documented second rung of the difficulty
ladder, and teachers never set them because writing them is a nightmare — so children believe maths
always runs left to right.
**What it is:** five ducks. The curtain closes. Silent, visible evidence — shadows bulge, footprints
walk in under the hem. The curtain opens: nine ducks. **What happened in the dark?** The killer
mechanic: a child drags ducks into the hidden region to state their theory, and the curtain
**replays the whole scene with their answer inside it** — if they said 3, the replay shows 5, then 3
arriving, then 8 — and reality visibly disagrees with the 9 they witnessed. A second mode replays
three silent versions (join, take-away, comparison) and the class picks which one is the number
sentence on the wall.
**The gap:** word-problem software is 100% text (dead in 11 languages, dead for 5-year-olds), and
every product uses result-unknown problems because they are trivial to generate. **Nothing wordless
exists. Nothing change-unknown exists.**
**The fun:** detective work, and the machine acting out YOUR theory so reality can disagree.

## C · Shape and space (4)

### 13. THE SHAPE STRETCHER · *Der Formenstrecker* — what makes a triangle a triangle
**The dread:** *"Every triangle I draw points up, every square sits flat — then they call a tilted
square a diamond, and I caused it myself."* (The prototype effect, documented from age 3.)
**What it is:** every shape wears its defining properties as **brass clasps** — three corners, four
equal sides, right angles. Grab it, stretch it needle-thin, tilt it, spin it: watch which clasps
**hold** and which **pop off with a snap**. Stretch a square: the right-angle clasps hold, the
equal-sides clasp pops — you are holding a rectangle, and the apparatus just showed you why. Tilt
anything: no clasp ever pops. The diamond myth dies by unbreakable clasps, not by assertion.
**The gap:** real dynamic geometry (GeoGebra) is built for secondary; every K-3 tool shows canonical
upright shapes — which research says *causes* the misconception.
**The fun:** the pop.

### 14. THE HINGE · *Das Scharnier* — angle as amount of turn
**The dread:** the documented misconception since Piaget: *the angle with the longer arms is bigger.*
**What it is:** a barn door and a cupboard door on one screen, both open 30° — one enormous, one
tiny; drag either and both numerals move together. Drag an arm LONGER — buttery, satisfying, and
**the numeral refuses to move.** Only turning speaks to the number. Keep turning: 90 clicks, 180,
270, and at 360 the door closes on its own shadow.
**The gap:** every existing angle tool is a scored degree-estimation game with a protractor (Alien
Angles, Banana Hunt) — and **not one varies arm length**, which is the entire misconception.
**The fun:** dragging the arm longer, longer, longer — and the number simply will not move.

### 15. THE SHEEP PEN · *Der Schafpferch* — fence and grass
**The dread:** the canonical Grade-3 confusion: perimeter and area collapse into one idea.
**What it is:** a pen built from **24 countable fence rails** — the perimeter conserved before your
eyes; no rail ever appears or vanishes. Each sheep needs one square of grass. Squash the pen long and
thin and the sheep get visibly, comically **cramped**, filing shoulder-to-shoulder; open it toward a
square and they spread out. Same 24 rails. The class discovers, by squashing, that the square is best
— a genuine theorem, reached by sheep.
**The gap:** every existing area/perimeter product is a scored game with fail states ("the animals
will escape!") or a grid-counting sandbox; **the fixed-fence deformation — the one demonstration that
fixes the confusion — exists nowhere.**
**The fun:** the cramped sheep.

### 16. THE STAIRCASE · *Die Treppe* — the leap to step ten
**The dread:** early algebra: children extend patterns step by step and never make the jump to "step
10 without building 4 through 9" — the leap from recursive to functional thinking.
**What it is:** build steps 1, 2, 3 from tiles. Then an empty bay: **build your prediction of step
10, brick by brick, from the rule alone.** The staircase then grows beneath it in a fast-forward
blur — and either arrives **flush under your step with a click**, or arrives visibly short, leaving
**a gap you would trip on**. The error is not a red X; it is a trip hazard.
**The gap:** visualpatterns.org is grade 6-8; nothing lets a 7-year-old make the jump-the-middle
move, which is absent from every K-3 product.
**The fun:** the flush click — or the trip-gap, which is the conversation.

## D · New territory (4)

### 17. THE ICE-CREAM STAND · *Die Eisdiele* — how many different ways?
**The topic:** Kombinatorik — explicit in the German curriculum from Klasse 2 ("Welche und wie viele
Möglichkeiten gibt es?"), present in NL and Brazil (BNCC), near-invisible in US K-3 — which is
exactly why no English-language tool exists.
**What it is:** 3 flavours, 2 cones. Build every different ice cream. Ones you have already made
**grey out and line up on the counter** — the hard part on paper is knowing when you have found them
all, and the counter makes it visible. The gasp: there are exactly 6, not 5. Add a flavour and the
row grows by exactly two. (The tree diagram grows alongside as the paid layer for Klasse 3.)
**The gap:** the entire field is paper cut-outs of ice-cream scoops (Frau Locke, Grundschulkönig).
Systematic enumeration — the actual skill — is invisible on paper and is a pure software superpower.
**The fun:** the ice cream, and the argument about whether chocolate-then-vanilla counts twice.

### 18. THE SHADOW STICK · *Der Schattenstab* — the day, compressed
**The topic:** shadows and the sun — core Sachunterricht/science in every market, and *logistically*
dreaded: it needs a sunny day, three trips outside, and a whole school day, so teachers skip it.
**What it is:** a stick in a yard; drag the sun across the sky and the shadow swings and stretches.
**Plant a flag where you think the shadow will point at lunchtime** — then run the day and watch.
Set it to YOUR sky: an Oslo December and a São Paulo December are different worlds, which turns the
11-country problem into the product's best feature.
**The gap:** existing shadow sims are login-gated, US-curriculum, upper-elementary and word-heavy;
none is wordless, projector-first, or latitude-true.
**The fun:** the flag, and running a whole day in ten seconds.

### 19. THE QUEUE · *Die Warteschlange* — first, second, third
**The topic:** ordinal numbers — a real K standard with no tool anywhere, taught with a line of
children and pure chaos.
**What it is:** animals queue at a bus stop. Who is third? The bus arrives at the OTHER end — now who
is third? The apparatus makes the one hard idea physical: **ordinal position depends on where you
count from**, and "third from the front" and "third from the back" name different animals. Board the
first two and everyone's ordinal changes at once.
**The gap:** ordinals exist digitally only as worksheets; the direction-dependence — the actual
teaching point — is nowhere.
**The fun:** the bus arriving at the wrong end.

### 20. NUMBER STUDIO · *Das Zahlenstudio* — digits on your country's paper
**The topic:** numeral formation 0-9 — the daily complaint (reversed 3s and 5s persist for years),
and the platform ships this for LETTERS already; digits are simply missing.
**What it is:** the shipped Letter Studio engine pointed at digits: one huge digit on your country's
actual lined paper (German ZL-Lineatur, French seyès…), the guided start-dot path — the pen simply
will not flow from a wrong start (refusal, never exposure: you never show the reversed digit you are
preventing).
**The gap:** LetterSchool and Writing Wizard are single-child touchscreen apps; and the digits
themselves differ by country — **the barred 7 (mandatory in DE/FR, absent in US/UK), the open vs
closed 4, the serifed 1.** Nobody owns that. Cheapest build in the batch; the engine exists.
**The fun:** none needed — it buys its place with certainty, and teachers ask for it by name.

## Cut, and why — so these do not come back
- **The crocodile for < >** — educators actively reject it ("replaces one symbol with a gimmick");
  and the honest apparatus for comparison is a balance, **which you already ship** (`number-balance`).
- **The equal-sign balance, cube buildings/Baupläne, a thermometer for negatives, class vote towers,
  a sharing/remainder table, a broken ruler** — each already IS a live tool (`number-balance`,
  `build-plan`, `cold-line`, `class-graph`, `lids`, `ruler` — the broken-ruler and mirror-book ideas
  are noted as upgrade modes for their owners instead).
- **3D nets that fold** — Polypad and LivePhysics already do the whole spec well, free.
- **A plant growing over weeks** — the real bean on the real windowsill is better and free; time-lapse
  is a video, not an apparatus.
- **A flat hundred chart** — crowded to death; only the Hotel's building-with-a-lift survives it.

## The three heroes
**The Exchange Machine · The Number Hotel · The Sheep Pen** — the shatter, the lift, the cramped
sheep. Three six-second clips, each owning a top-five dread, each legible from its name alone.
