# Reviewers' notes: registry `fr`

Written by the reviewers of the crosswalk (`scripts/curricula/apply-review.js`). 306 teaching points carry a reviewer's row; the reason for each is in `frontend/content/curricula/fr/crosswalk-authored.json`.

## author-math

- PART 2 looks incomplete for cycle 2 and should be checked against the Bulletin officiel: it holds no mass or capacity objective at all (so the grams, jugs and g/kg, mL/L sheets have no row), no time objective for CE1 (clock reading jumps from whole hours in CP to CE2), and for CE2 no whole-number objectives (numbers beyond 1000), no mental-calculation objectives, no data objectives and no space objectives. A French teacher expects all of these in the 2024 programme.
- CE1 in PART 2 also has no mental-calculation section and no objective on the meaning of multiplication or division, so several grade-2 points could only be tied to CP objectives or to the column method.
- The wording of M.CE1.16 is damaged in the data (« les fractions 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 et 1. 2 3 4 5 6 8 10 »): the stacked fractions 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6, 1/8 and 1/10 were flattened when the text was taken from the PDF.
- Fractions come a year earlier in France than on the site: unit fractions, fractions up to 1 and comparing with the same denominator are CE1 objectives, so most grade-3 fraction points map to CE1; only equal fractions, the number line and general comparison are CE2.
- Multiplication is spread differently: its meaning is a CP objective, the × sign, commutativity and the tables start in CE1, and division with ÷ is CE2. The grade-3 'equal groups' and 'commutative' sheets are CE1 content here.
- Parity is a CE1 objective; the K and grade-1 odd/even sheets can only prepare it. Symmetry is CE2: the two grade-2 symmetry points teach CE2 objectives.
- Written sums with + − = and addition facts are CP content; the grande section works with composing and decomposing quantities, without the signs. The K colour-by-code fact sheets and bonds to 20 are therefore CP material for a French teacher.
- Clock: CP is whole hours only (reading and setting hands). Half hours, quarters and five minutes on the grade-1 and grade-2 sheets could only be tied to the CE2 objectives.
- Money: CP works in whole euros only; euros with cents start in CE1 and written amounts with a comma are CE1–CE2. Coin sheets must show euro coins and notes to count for these objectives.
- Area and volume are not cycle 2 magnitudes (only length with perimeter, money and time are listed); rounding, estimating a result, input/output rules and line plots are not named anywhere in PART 2.
- Not part of the French school: inches and feet (2:choose-units), a.m./p.m. (3:time-12-24; French uses the 24-hour count in everyday life), and probably the wording of the number words: French 70–99 (soixante-dix, quatre-vingts, quatre-vingt-dix) need their own treatment in 1:number-words.
- Days, months, seasons and the calendar are not mathematics in France (they sit in exploring / questioning the world, which this registry part does not hold); position words for the grande section are likewise outside the maternelle mathematics programme and were tied to the CP vocabulary objective.
- Objectives of these years that no teaching point covers and that teachers will look for: tracing with the ruler and checking alignments (CP), the set square and the compass, circles and right-angled triangles (CE1–CE2), drawing a segment of a given length, metres / kilometres / decimetres / millimetres and their relations, column multiplication (CE2), adding and subtracting fractions, mixed two- and three-step problems, multiplicative comparison and Cartesian-product problems (CE2), coded moves on a plan, nets of the cube, ordinals to 100, and in the grande section the board-game position problems and the ordering of three masses.
- Skill lines too vague to place with confidence: K:number-bonds-20 (which bonds), 1:sort-three-groups (what is sorted), 1:number-walls and 3:magic-squares (number range), 1:coins (which currency and whether cents appear).
- Strand names: the graph and table activities shown under 'Measurement & Data' belong in France to « Organisation et gestion de données », a heading of its own beside « Grandeurs et mesures »; if the site can split that strand, data activities should carry that name.

## author-rest

- The maternelle list in PART 2 (ML.M4, ML.M5) holds only oral language and the handwriting gesture. Nothing is listed on sounds, syllables, rhymes, letter names or the alphabetic principle, although every grande section class does this work daily; this is why all the K sound and letter points only 'prepare' CP objectives. Please check whether a part of the 2024 maternelle programme is missing from the registry.
- The CP list has no 'Grammaire et orthographe' part at all, and the CE1 list has only 'Se repérer dans la phrase simple' (4 objectives) where CE2 has 9: no CE1 objective on conjugation, on agreement as grammar or on end punctuation. Teachers do teach the present tense, the verb and the plural in CE1, so the rows for 2:verb-forms, 2:sentence-boundaries and 1:articles point to CE2 or to the dictation objective F.CE1.22 only because of this gap. Worth checking against the source.
- The history-geography themes are titles only, so almost every HG row is 'prepares' or 'extends'. Seasons, day and night, moon, family generations, map key, directions and continents should be read again when the full text of the 2026 programme is in the registry.
- Subjects with no objective here: arts (the three drawing points), enseignement moral et civique (feelings, wants and needs), road safety (three points), the five senses (two points), recycling and waste, and astronomy (planets; moon and day-and-night only hang on a CP history title). The maternelle domain 'Explorer le monde' is not held, so every K science point can only 'prepare' the cycle 2 objectives.
- Placed in another year by the French programme: reading words, sight words and building words are CP, not the grande section; cursive starts in the grande section and CP (French children never learn to write lowercase print letters), so cursive in grade 2 and 3 is consolidation; synonyms, opposites and alphabetical order are CP objectives; continents and oceans sit under a CP theme while the worksheets are in grades 2 and 3; the water cycle, the parts of a flower and the planets are usually cycle 3 (ages 9 to 11).
- Skill lines that are English in content and need a French design rather than a translation: digraphs (French: ch, ou, on, an, in, oi, eu, gn, ph; no sh / th), CVC words (French first words are mostly consonant-vowel syllables: vélo, moto, lama), the spelling rules (French: m before m / b / p, s or ss, c or ç, g / gu / ge), a / an (French: un / une, le / la / l'), compound words with a joining letter (none in French), possessive pronouns (mon, ton, son are determiners in French grammar), irregular past forms (the French CE1 child meets the present of être, avoir, aller, faire first), person words (French needs the feminine too: boulanger / boulangère).
- 1:food-groups: 'four food groups' is not what French teachers use; the national nutrition guidance sorts foods into seven families (fruits and vegetables, starchy foods, dairy, meat-fish-eggs, fats, sweet products, drinks). The French sheets should follow it or teachers will find them wrong.
- Content of these years that no teaching point covers and that teachers will look for: silent final letters (chat / chaton) and the accents; the sounds of s, c and g; negative and exclamative sentences; subject and verb of the sentence; registers of language and literal / figurative meaning; dictionary work; dictation and copying of several lines; reading fluency (words per minute). In science: the states of water and changes of state as such, air, simple electric circuits, technical objects, growth of the body and the teeth, sleep and physical activity. In history-geography: the great periods of history (CE1), prehistory, Roman Gaul and the kingdom of France (CE2), and the geography of France (CE2).
- Small things in the data: F.CP.36 and F.CP.38 carry the same wording (start and end of year); F.CP.11 repeats the speed figures of F.CP.7 in a way that reads oddly ('30 mots par minute au minimum sans préparation, 50 après préparation' for a text read after preparation); the science document is the 2020 one, which the note says will stand beside a new 2026 programme that could not be read, so the science rows may need a second pass.
- Strand names not changed but worth knowing: the 2024 programme calls the vocabulary part 'Vocabulaire' and the grammar part 'Grammaire et orthographe'; 'Le lexique' and 'Étude de la langue' are the older names that teachers still use, so they stay.

## second-reading

- Answered by the new objectives: mass (2:mass-grams now has a home at CE1; g or kg in 2:choose-units; K:heavy-light leads to CP), half hours and quarter hours on the clock (CE1: 1:time-read, 1:time-set-hands, 2:time-5-min-quarter), the CE2 whole numbers to ten thousand (3:compare-4-digit, 3:rounding, 3:arithmetic-patterns) and the CE2 data objectives (3:scaled-graphs, the year-3 logic grids).
- Answered in French: the CP grammar objectives (sentence with capital and end mark, masculine and feminine, singular and plural, word classes) and CE1 conjugation give exact homes to 1:unscramble-sentence, 1:articles, 1:word-classes, K:singular-plural, K:articles and 2:verb-forms; the maternelle phonology and letter objectives give 'teaches' rows to the rhyme, syllable, first-sound, sound-position and letter-case points, which before could only 'prepare' CP or CE1.
- Capacity is only half answered: the text names litre, décilitre and centilitre, at CE2 only. The millilitre is not a cycle 2 unit, so 2:read-jugs (ml to 1000 and 2000) can only 'extend' M.CE2.52; a French class would want the same jug sheets in cL and dL.
- Still without any home in the official text held here: rounding and estimating a result (3:estimate-by-rounding, the rounding points only 'extend' the word encadrer), area (3:same-area), input/output rules, road safety, recycling, the five senses, wants and needs, the planets, and all drawing. As I know the curriculum these sit in documents the registry does not hold: the other maternelle domains (explorer le monde, activités artistiques, vivre ensemble), enseignement moral et civique, arts plastiques, the attestation de première éducation à la route and éducation au développement durable; area and the millilitre come in cycle 3.
- K:feelings now has one 'prepares' row to a story-comprehension objective (ML.M5.26); that is a stopgap, its real home is enseignement moral et civique and the maternelle 'vivre ensemble', which are not in the registry.
- Not changed because it is not a new objective, but worth a look: K:days-of-week, 1:days-months-seasons (both parts) and 2:calendar have no row, while the history list holds the CP theme HG.CP.H2 « Les représentations humaines du temps », whose title suggests the week, the months and the calendar. Only the theme titles of histoire-géographie are in the list, so I could not decide it from the wording.
- The section « S’éveiller à la diversité linguistique » has objectives for 4-year-olds only (ML.M4.20, ML.M4.21); none is listed for 5-year-olds, the year of K:bilingual-cards. Check whether the official text has them for the grande section.
- M.CE1.16 (the fractions 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 ...) is given as new in the brief, yet five points already carried it (2:equal-shares-shapes, 2:fraction-of-a-set, 3:partition-equal-areas, 3:whole-from-unit, 3:fraction-of-set). I left those rows as they are; someone should confirm that the id means the same objective as when the first reviewers used it.
- Skills France places in another year than the worksheets: half hours and quarter hours are CE1 and five-minute reading CE2 (sheets in years 1 and 2); morning and afternoon hours (12 / 24) are CE1 (sheets in year 3); unit fractions and comparing them are CE1 (many sheets in year 3); odd and even is CE1 (sheets in K and year 1); rhymes and syllable work are grande section (sheets also in year 1); splitting a whole word into phonemes is CP work (sheets in K); symmetry is CE2 (sheets in year 2); capacity starts at CE2 (sheets in year 2).
- Many new objectives got no worksheet because they are oral, shared-reading or classroom practice (voice and sound games, stories read by the teacher, dictée à l’adulte, kinds of everyday writing), or because the site has no sheet for them: the CE1 and CE2 mental-calculation procedures (add 9, 19, 29; multiply by 10, by 4 or 8; a teen number times a digit by splitting it), kilogram-gram relations and reference masses, durations between two clock times at CE1, the negative and exclamative forms, and stem and ending of a first-group verb.
