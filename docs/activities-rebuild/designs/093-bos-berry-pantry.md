## 1. The pitch
"The number on Bo's pantry door can be pulled apart: drag the 3 of 34 away and a hidden 0 comes out from behind the 4 — 30 and 4 — and each part slides over the shelf it counts, while the crates crack open: 10, 20, 30, and the berries: 31, 32, 33, 34."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **A slingshot.** Bo sits beside a wooden Y-sling. Load a crate or a berry, pull the pouch back, let go — it arcs up and lands on the pantry shelf with a thump (crate) or a plop (berry). Pulling a sling back is irresistible.
- **The stretching tag.** On Check the brass tag on the pantry door stretches like toffee: the tens digit slides left, a 0 rolls out from behind the ones digit (it was there all along), and "30" and "4" hang over the crates and the berries.
- **Crack-open count.** Then the crates crack their lids one after another and fan their ten berry beads out like a peacock tail for a moment — 10 … 20 … 30 — and the loose berries hop — 31 … 34. The count lands exactly on the tag, and Bo's pantry window lights up warm.
- **The winter pantry fills.** Each right shelf slides up out of sight and an empty one comes down; over a pass Bo's pantry wall fills with stocked shelves (a growing picture of the work, not a score).
- No timer, score, streak or lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill (1.NBT.B.2):** the two digits of a two-digit number stand for amounts of tens and ones; build a number from tens and ones, write the number for tens and ones, write it in expanded form (34 = 30 + 4), see that ten ones make a ten (2.a), that 13–19 are a ten and some ones (2.b), and that decades are tens with no ones (2.c).
- **Misconceptions and the push-back:**
  - *Reversal* (34 built as 4 crates and 3 berries): the tag stretches to 30 + 4 and the crack-count runs 10, 20, 30, **40** — the fourth crate cracks open beyond the 30-plaque and glows; the berry count stops at 33 with an empty dimple under the 4-plaque.
  - *Face value* (the 3 in 34 "is three"): the stretch shows that the 3 drags a 0 with it — it is 30 — and three sealed crates crack into thirty berries.
  - *A crate counted as one* (3 crates and 4 berries read as 7): the crack-count counts each crate as ten berries, every time; a tag set to 7 meets a count of 34.
  - *Expanded form as 3 + 4*: on the plaque hooks, a "3" hung over the crates shows a plaque that says 3 above crates that crack into 30.
  - *Ten loose ones not seen as a ten* (2 crates and 13 berries "is 2 tens and 13"): the bowl only has nine dimples; berries beyond nine spill onto the shelf lip, and the ones wheel only goes to 9 — there is no 13 to write. Bo packs ten of them into a crate at Check, and the count still lands on 33.

## 5. The hand
- **Load:** tap the crate stack or the basket → a crate / a berry pops into the sling pouch (180 ms). Tapping the other ammo swaps it.
- **Sling:** drag the pouch back (down-left; the band stretches, a creak rising in pitch); release → the piece arcs to the shelf (500 ms): a crate always lands on the crate deck in the next free place (from the left); a berry always lands in the next free dimple of the bowl (row by row). A crate can never land in the bowl (it bounces off the bowl's rim and drops onto the deck — shown once, the first time a child aims "into the bowl"); berries beyond nine bounce off the full bowl and roll onto the shelf's front lip (the **spill**, up to 12 lie there, visibly untidy). Response to the finger < 30 ms. **Quick path:** double-tap the crate stack / basket flings one directly (same flight); keyboard: C = fling a crate, B = fling a berry.
- **Take back:** tap the last crate on the deck / the last berry in the bowl or on the lip → it hops back into the stack/basket (no gaps ever).
- **Wheels (label rounds):** ▲ ▼ flip the digit (0–9; the ones wheel stops at 9 with a bump — it cannot show 10 or more).
- **Plaques (stretch rounds):** drag a plaque from the rack onto a hook (crate hook = left, bowl hook = right); it swings and settles; drag it back or onto the other hook to change.
- **Stretch it yourself (free play, any time):** drag the door tag's tens digit sideways → the tag stretches and the hidden 0 rolls out; let go and it snaps back together. (In label rounds the tag is the child's wheels, so this works on whatever the child wrote.)
- **Free play after success:** the shelf stays slingable; the tag rewrites itself live as pieces land (in free play only), so the child can sling and watch 3 crates + 4 berries become 34, the tag stretching on every change.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with an empty shelf / wheels at 0 · 0 / a hook empty** → hint "Sling crates and berries onto the shelf." / "Turn the wheels to write the number." / "Hang a plaque on each hook." (no miss).
- **The stretch-and-count (every Check):** (1) the tag stretches (450 ms): tens digit + its rolling 0 over the crate deck, ones digit over the bowl — in stock rounds the tag is the order; in label rounds the tag is the child's wheels; in stretch rounds the plaques slide together first (the 0 tucks behind the ones plaque) and then apart again; (2) the crack-count (each crate 250 ms): crates crack, fan ten berries for a beat and close, a counter numeral above the deck running 10, 20, 30 …; then the berries hop, the counter running on 31, 32 …; (3) each part's count lands on (or misses) its plaque.
- **Spill on a right total** (stock: 2 crates and 14 berries for 34): not a miss — Bo hops over, packs ten berries from the bowl and lip into an empty crate (snap!), the shelf becomes canonical, and the round counts as right with the bubble "Bo packed ten berries into a crate — a crate is ten!". (Building the smart way is taught by showing, not by refusing.)
- **Miss 1 — what the apparatus shows:** the crack-count stops on the first part that disagrees: extra crates beyond the tens plaque stay open and glow coral; missing crates show as dotted crate outlines; missing / extra berries as empty dimples / coral berries. Label rounds: the count runs over the delivered shelf, and its result is written in grey under the child's wheels (e.g. wheels "5 2", grey "25"). Hint by case: reversal "The left number is the crates — each crate is ten." · face value "The 3 in 34 means 30 — three crates." · crates as ones "Each crate holds ten berries. Count the crates by tens." · overflow "Ten loose berries make a crate."
- **Miss 2:** the crates crack open and STAY open, each with its ten berries in a column and "10" under it; the loose berries carry 1, 2, 3 …; the tag's two parts get the colours of their shelf parts (tens teal, ones coral). Hint "Count the crates by tens, then the berries by ones."
- Any new fling, take-back, wheel turn or plaque move clears the showing.
- **Success:** the count lands on both plaques with a chime, the tag snaps back together, Bo's window lights, the shelf slides up into the pantry wall and an empty shelf comes down. Bubble: "{tens} crates and {ones} berries: {tensValue} + {ones} = {n}!" (e.g. "3 crates and 4 berries: 30 + 4 = 34!"); decades "{tens} crates and no berries: {n}!"; overflow "Bo packed ten berries into a crate: {n}!"

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a slingshot, crates that crack open and fan their berries, a door tag that stretches like toffee.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "where does the 0 of 30 hide?" is the expanded-form lesson in one gesture, and reversals are corrected by counting, not by a red cross.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — library bunny and berries, calm wooden pantry, the same numbers as the worksheet, no score.
4. **The invention, in one sentence:** Bo's door tag stretches apart into tens and ones — the hidden 0 rolls out behind the ones — and each part hangs over the crates or berries it counts while the crates crack open by tens.
