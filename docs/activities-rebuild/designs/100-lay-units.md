## 1. The pitch
"Inchie can only hop exactly one helper long — so when you tap the helpers to count, she hops along them, and every gap, overlap, wrong-size helper or crooked start makes her land in the wrong place, where you can see it."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- The helpers are soft green capsules that the child drags out of a flower-pot and lays under the object; they click when they touch end to end (a tiny "tk") and sit a little crooked when they don't.
- Counting is a game: each tap makes **Inchie** (the library worm) hop onto the next helper, leaving a little footprint — hop, hop, hop — and the child counts the hops.
- When something is wrong, Inchie's hop shows it, and it is funny: she plops **into the gap** and pops out with soil on her nose (a soil puff), or lands on the **bump** where two helpers overlap and wobbles, or her hop lands in the **middle** of a too-big helper and she looks around.
- The earned show: when the count is written on the seed label, Inchie does one long happy wiggle along the whole object and the object's label flips over to show "[pencil] = 4 [helper]" as a picture sum of helpers (four capsules in a row), and a sprout grows out of the flower-pot.
- Free play: helpers can be laid anywhere on the path, and Inchie can be sent hopping at any time — children make bridges with gaps just to watch her fall in.
- No score, no timer, no lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (1.MD.A.2; G1-139): lay identical units end to end from one end of the object to the other, with no gaps and no overlaps, then count them: the length IS the number of units. G1-140: measure three objects this way and order them.
- Misconceptions and push-back (each one is a hop that goes wrong — the hop length is fixed at one helper, so it is a physical ruler the child never sees as a ruler):
  1. **Gaps** → Inchie hops one helper-length from the end of a helper and lands in the gap; the soil shows through. The count of hops no longer matches the helpers.
  2. **Overlaps** → the second helper lies raised on the first (drawn as a bump); Inchie lands on the bump and wobbles.
  3. **Mixed sizes** → a too-big helper (1.5 ×) or too-small one (0.5 ×) from the "decoy" pot: Inchie's hop lands in its middle or beyond its end.
  4. **Not starting at the end** → Inchie always starts on the start stone under the object's tip; her first hop lands one helper-length from the tip, which is not where a shifted first helper ends.
  5. **Counting the spaces or the object's length by eye** → the count is made of hops, one per tap, and written by the child afterwards — the number is the child's, the hops are the evidence.
  6. **Bigger units, bigger number** (inverse rounds) → measured twice, with big and with small helpers, the two numbers sit side by side on the label: big helpers 2, small helpers 4.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag a helper** out of the pot → it lifts (80 ms) and follows the finger; over the path it shows a ghost where it will lie. Release on the path → it lies there, **snapping to quarter-unit steps** (so gaps and overlaps of ¼, ½, ¾ are possible). If its start edge is within ⅛ u of a neighbour's end, it clicks flush (tk, 1.4 kHz, 60 ms) — the only automatic help, matching "end to end" when the child aims well. Dropped onto a neighbour by ≥ ¼ u, it rides up on it (drawn raised by 6 px, slight tilt) — an overlap is visible.
- **Drag a laid helper** along the path to move it (same snapping); drag it off the path (back towards the pot) to remove it.
- **Tap to count**: each tap on **the next helper** makes Inchie hop one u to the right (arc 260 ms, a soft "boop" rising by one semitone per hop), leaving a small footprint dot on the helper she lands on.
  - Lands exactly on a seam (or the object's end) → the helper she left glows faintly — counted.
  - Lands in a gap (soil under her) → she sinks halfway with a soil puff (220 ms), the gap's soil glows coral; the count stops there.
  - Lands on an overlap bump → she wobbles (±8°, 3 ×), both overlapping helpers outline coral.
  - Lands inside a helper (wrong size, or because the first helper did not start at the stone) → she stops mid-helper and looks back (a 15° turn); that helper outlines coral.
  - Hops past the object's end onto a helper beyond it → that helper outlines coral ("past the end").
  - A tap on any helper other than the next → the next helper pulses (no penalty).
  - After any fault, a tap on Inchie sends her back to the stone (footprints clear); fixing a helper also resets her.
- When Inchie reaches the object's end exactly on the last seam, she **sits up** and the keypad opens; the child types the number of hops into the label box (⌫ to change). **Check** confirms the typed number.
- **Judge rounds**: one object, three pre-laid helper tracks under it, each with its own start stone; tap a stone and Inchie walks over and hops along that track; then tap the track that was right (Check).
- **Free play**: always available — lay any helpers, send Inchie; after a solved round the object can be swapped by dragging another picture card onto the bed (from a small 3-card drawer of the round's objects).
- Optional TTS: each hop speaks its count in the 5 TTS locales ("one, two, three…") — **never needed**: the footprints carry the count.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with no number typed** → if Inchie has not reached the end: bubble "Tap the helpers so Inchie can hop to the end." (no miss); if she has: "Write how many hops it took." (no miss).
- **Miss 1** (wrong number typed, helpers correct) → Inchie hops the whole way again **slowly**, and every footprint now shows a small **numeral above it** (1, 2, 3, …) as she lands; the typed number in the label pulses. Bubble: "Count Inchie's hops again — one hop for each helper."
- **Miss 1** (a fault still in the helpers, e.g. the child typed a number after a faulty run that never reached the end cleanly — the core does not allow typing then, so this happens only in judge rounds) → the chosen row's Inchie hops and stops at its fault; bubble "Watch where Inchie lands — that row has a {gap|bump|wrong-size helper|crooked start}."
- **Miss 2** → the helpers each show their numeral permanently, the last one ringed; the keypad key with that number is outlined (the child still presses it). Bubble: "The last helper says how long it is."
- Fault hints (shown when Inchie stops at a fault, not counted as misses): gap "Oops — a gap! Push the helpers together so they touch." · overlap "Two helpers are on top of each other. Give them room." · wrong size "That helper is a different size. Use only the same helpers." · start "Start the first helper right at the stone." · past the end "That helper is past the end of the {object}."
- A new drag or tap takes the numerals away until the next Check.
- **Success** → the label locks with the number, Inchie wiggles along the whole object (600 ms), the label flips to show the picture sum "[object] = N × [capsule]" (N capsules drawn in a row under the picture), a sprout grows from the helper pot. Bubble: "The {object} is {N} helpers long!" · order: "{object1}, {object2}, {object3} — from shortest to longest!" · big and small: "Big helpers: {B}. Small helpers: {S}. Smaller helpers — you need more of them!"

## 13. The four questions (answered, honestly)
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — laying capsules that click, then a worm that hops along them and falls into every gap with a puff of soil.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — every 1.MD.A.2 rule (end to end, no gaps, no overlaps, same size, start at the end) is tested by one fixed-length hop, the length is the number of hops the child counted and wrote, and the big/small rounds show the inverse relation.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — library pictures, no ruler tricks, calm feedback, nothing timed or sold.
4. **The invention in one sentence:** Inchie can only hop exactly one helper long, so counting the helpers by tapping makes her hop along them — and every gap, overlap, wrong size or crooked start makes her land where you can see it.
