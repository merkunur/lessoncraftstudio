## The skill
Compare 3-digit numbers place by place starting at the hundreds, record with < > =, and order four numbers.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE THREE-DOOR TUNNEL. Two number carts, each three digit plates long (hundreds, tens, ones), stand before a tunnel with a door per place (four doors in the 4-digit setting). First the child taps the door where the race will be decided. Then the carts roll: at each door the two digits rise side by side as stacks of that many blocks; equal stacks let both carts through, and at the first unequal door the door drops in front of the smaller cart. The child then drags the < > = sign between the two numerals. Order mode: four carts race door by door and park on a four-step podium; the child places each cart as soon as its door decides it. Wrong door predicted: the doors passed with equal stacks stay lit, showing why that door could not decide (or which earlier door already did); wrong sign: the sign's mouth swings to face the taller stack and flashes; miss 2 names the deciding place in the bubble. Right: the winning cart's deciding digit glows and the sentence is read in the bubble.

- **Misconception:** Comparing by the ones or by "more digits that look big" (e.g. 389 > 412 because 8 and 9 are big): the tunnel only lets the hundreds door decide first.
- **Pictures:** none (drawn carts and doors; digits are the content)
- **Settings:** Mode: compare two / order four · Numbers: hundreds differ / same hundreds, tens differ / same hundreds and tens · Size: 3-digit / 4-digit (grade 3, absorbed Place Gates)
- **Rounds:** ≥ 9 per mode and setting; fresh numbers each round with designed ties at the hundreds (and tens); the deciding door is each place equally often across a pass; the bigger number on the left 40–60 %; equal pairs ≤ 15 % of compare rounds; no leading 0; the same digits swapped (389 vs 398) in ≥ 2 rounds.
- **Worksheet types it must cover:** G2-219, G2-220, G3-325
- **Build risk:** Low.

## Merged in
- grade 3 "The Place Gates" (place-gates): Compare multi-digit numbers by looking at the highest place first. — THE PLACE GATES: two four-digit numbers march as trains of digit carriages (thousands carriage first) toward a row of gates, one gate per place. At each gate the two carriages are weighed by height (a digit is a stack of that many bricks); if equal, both pass on; at the first gate where they differ the gate drops in front of the smaller train. The child predicts by tapping the gate where the race will be decided, then the trains run; then the child taps <, = or > between the numerals.
