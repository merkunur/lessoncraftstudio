## 1 Page concept (base)

**"Rail and Paper".** The page is a drawing-book spread, not a worksheet. A teal rail runs DOWN the left edge of the body; four square step cards hang on it, badge 1 at the top, badge 4 at the bottom, the fourth card the finished cat in ink. The rest of the body, 449 px wide, is the paper: one white box with a 2.5 px dashed coral frame, top edge level with card 1, proportioned like the cat (0.88), and under it an empty hand-rest. From across the room a teacher sees a tall column of small cats growing on the left and one big empty sheet on the right: a drawing lesson, nothing to fill in, nothing to read.

Why the ladder and not the strip-over-box: (1) the eye starts top-left, and the ladder puts step 1 exactly there and ends at the finished drawing, at the same height where the pencil starts; (2) a right-handed child rests the heel of the hand bottom-right, on the air under the paper, never on the steps (a strip above the box is covered by nothing, but a bottom strip or a right-hand column would be); (3) seven of the eleven pinned units are PORTRAIT (m: cat 0.88, dog 0.67, rabbit 0.59, pony 0.90, owl 0.74, bear 0.73; only dinosaur, fish, frog, hummingbird, butterfly are landscape), and a portrait animal's steps are tall: at 150 px wide with `viewBox:'bbox'` the dog card is 150 × 216, which pushes a top strip to 216 tall and starves the paper (677 − 216 − 16 = 445 tall, 299 wide at 0.67). Beside the ladder the same dog gets a 442 × 660 paper. The landscape units (fish, frog, hummingbird, butterfly) use the same rail laid flat (§7) because their steps are short and wide. **Orientation is a resolved config key `orient: 'ladder' | 'strip'`**, set per face in the bank (default: `bbox` ratio < 1 → ladder; ≥ 1 → strip), keyed on the unit, never on the level.

Top quality = one apparatus (rail + cards), one hero (the paper, 228 k px² of white), pencil-first (the only marks the child makes are drawn, not written), digits only in the body, and whitespace as a hand-rest, not as leftover.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

There is no answer on the base: the task is a drawing. What the page must NOT do is pre-draw it: the paper holds 0 paths at d2 (`guideShapes:false`), and coral appears only inside `htd-step` cards. The child marks nothing but the drawing; a teacher reads success off the paper against card 4 beside it (same orientation, 3 : 1 scale). Uniqueness: `data-lcs-htd-unit` pins the slug; the four cards' path sets are the bank's `steps[0..k]` by string equality; card 4's visible ink equals `full` (the engine's `stats.missing = extra = dup = 0`, m: 0/0/0 on every pinned unit). ## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

**Alt 1: strip-over-box (the drawing-book frieze).** Four cards across the top, the paper below. Handedness-neutral and the simplest build. Loses on portrait animals (the dog's strip is 216 tall and its paper 299 wide) and the eye ends the strip at the top-RIGHT, then has to come back down-left to start drawing. Kept as the family's second orientation for landscape and paired faces, never as the base.

**Alt 2: the paper as the page.** One 639 × 660 paper filling the body, the four steps printed small (118) in a column INSIDE its left margin, the cards' frames dropped so the steps float on the sheet. Beautiful and the biggest paper of all, but the steps then sit where a left-handed child's hand rests, the lint cannot tell "inside the box" from "a pre-drawn mark in the box" (every face's "paper empty" rule would need an exclusion zone), and small floating steps read as decoration, not a lesson.

**Recommendation: Rail and Paper (ladder for portrait, strip for landscape).** It is the only concept where the step order, the finished model and the pencil's start are on one straight eye-path, where the hand-rest is designed rather than leftover, and where every portrait animal (7 of 11) gets a paper ≥ 440 px on its short side. One component, two orientations, zero engine changes.
