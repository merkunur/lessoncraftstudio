## The skill
Match baby animals to their parents and notice how young animals are like and unlike the grown-ups.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE MAGNIFIER OF LIKENESS. Four grown-up library animals stand in a meadow; a baby waits at the gate. The child drags the baby to a parent; it trots there. A round magnifier then slides between the two and lights the features they share at hand-set points (beak and webbed feet; hooves and mane; woolly coat; spots). Right: several features light in pairs, the baby nuzzles in. Miss 1: one or no feature lights, the baby looks round and walks back to the gate. Miss 2: the true parent lifts its head. CHANGE round (caterpillar → butterfly): few features light even though it is right, and the magnifier shows the two side by side with "changes a lot" shown by an arrow between them. REVERSE mode: a parent stands alone and three babies wait — the child brings its own baby.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Babies always look like small copies of their parents.
- **Pictures:** farm-animals (hen/chick, cow/calf, horse/foal, sheep/lamb, duck/duckling), forest-creatures (caterpillar), insects-and-bugs (butterfly); distractors from the same family (goose for duckling, goat for lamb, donkey for foal, rooster/turkey for chick, ox for calf).
- **Settings:** Mode: find the parent / is it alike? · Change rounds: off / on
- **Rounds:** 12 rounds per pass (6 baby→parent + 6 parent→baby), parent positions shuffled with the right one in each slot ≤ 30 %; every round has a same-family distractor; one right parent.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-205
- **Distinct from:** Home Finder is about where animals live; this is about offspring.
- **Build risk:** Only six pairs; more pairs (kitten, puppy, piglet, tadpole) would widen it. Feature coordinates are hand-set per picture and checked by the reviewer.
