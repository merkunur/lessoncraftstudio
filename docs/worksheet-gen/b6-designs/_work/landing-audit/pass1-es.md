# Landing audit pass 1 — es

1. 1. K-379 / K-381 / G1-400 (beach-walk story): the footprints are drawn as solid black ovals that a Mexican preescolar child will name 'piedritas' (or seeds); the towel is a small striped rectangle and the shell a teal half-disc; the walker is a neutral dark silhouette although the es sentence bank makes it 'una niña'. Without the teacher naming the scene it reads as 'un señor tirando piedras'. These faces are picture-only, so no word rescues it.

6. 6. G1-403: card 3 (toothbrush under the running tap, bubbles falling) has a second defensible answer — many Mexican families wet the brush BEFORE the paste (antes) — while the intended answer is después (rinse the brush); only the foam cue separates them. Cards 4 and 5 (brush on the lower arch) are near-duplicates, both durante.
7. 7. G1-404: title names only 'tose en el codo' (row 1 of 4); the page also covers soap vs water-only, own cup vs shared cup, tissue in the bin vs on the floor. Row 2's wrong picture (hands under running water, no soap) is itself hand-washing — a child who believes water alone cleans will circle both; the only cue is the foam.
8. 8. G2-379: bank reason 'No nos quemamos en un día de mucho calor' is scientifically off — sunburn comes from the sun's rays, not heat; a Mexican teacher will correct it to 'en un día de mucho sol'. Fix data/b6 healthy-habits.es reasons['sun-protect']. One picture/sentence pair is straight across (sleep, row 2).
9. 9. G1-405: title 'higiene, ejercicio y descanso' omits one of the five rows (tomar agua); the rows are cepillarse los dientes, lavarse las manos, hacer ejercicio, tomar agua, dormir. The week starts on domingo (dom…sáb), correct for the Mexican calendar.
10. 10. G1-398: the 8 animals split 3 mar (pez payaso, delfín, pulpo) / 3 bosque (ardilla, tejón, venado) / 1 lago (libélula) / 1 selva (gorila) — lake and jungle each get a single animal. The black-and-white badger will be named 'zorrillo' by most Mexican children (same habitat, different name); the only selva animal is an African gorilla, not a selva mexicana animal.
11. 11. K-383: title names 3 homes (nido, colmena, madriguera) but the page shows 6 (telaraña, casa del castor, nido, madriguera, colmena, hormiguero). The es bank calls two different homes 'madriguera' (rabbit burrow = madriguera; beaver lodge = 'madriguera de castor') — a lodge of sticks on the water is not a madriguera to a Mexican teacher (casa / presa del castor). The hive is drawn inside a hollow trunk, not the hanging panal Mexican children know.
12. 12. G1-406: correct; intruders gorila / pulpo / ardilla / cebra at positions 3-2-3-1. Beaver (lake row) and badger (forest row) are unfamiliar fauna for most Mexican classrooms — solvable, noted.
