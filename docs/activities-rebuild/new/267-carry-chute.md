## The skill
Add and subtract 2- and 3-digit numbers in written columns, ones first, recording the carried ten above the tens and the borrowed ten in the ones.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE ONE-DIGIT BOX. The written sum on squared paper with digits aligned in H/T/O columns; under each column an answer box that holds exactly ONE digit. Only the ones box is open first, so the child must work right to left. Addition: if the ones make 13, the child types 13 and the box keeps the 3 while the extra ten pops out as a small '1' tag the child drags up to the carry hat on top of the tens column; the tens box opens only when the hat is filled. Subtraction: when the top ones digit is too small the ones box refuses (no negative fits); the child taps the tens digit above, which ticks down by one while a '1' hops in front of the ones digit (5 becomes 15). Across a zero: tapping a 0 in the tens does nothing; the tap must start at the hundreds digit, which ticks down, the tens 0 becomes 10, then ticks to 9 as the ten moves on. Miss 1: the column lights and its two digits are read in the bubble; miss 2: a ten-frame of that column's ones appears beside it. Counter help (absorbed Carry Chute): every digit is also a stack of counters in its lane; ten counters slide down a chute into the next lane as one, and a borrowed counter breaks into ten on landing. Right: the finished answer is read and the column digits flash once.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Writing 13 in the ones place (or forgetting the carried ten); starting at the left; subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger regardless of position (52 − 27 = 35).
- **Pictures:** none (paper, digits and boxes are the content)
- **Settings:** Operation: add / subtract / mixed · Size: 2-digit / 3-digit · Regrouping: none / one place / two places (incl. across a zero) · Help: digits only / counters in the lanes
- **Rounds:** ≥ 8 rounds per setting, generated to the setting (no-regroup rounds never need a carry, one-place rounds exactly one); answers never repeated in a pass; mixed mode balances + and −.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-251, G2-255, G2-256, G2-257, G2-258, G2-259, G3-357, G3-359, G3-360, G3-361, G3-362, G3-363
- **Distinct from:** Tuck (place-value-regroup) regroups BLOCKS and Exchange Machine (tool #45) co-locates material and digits; this is the written algorithm alone — the step after the blocks, which every column worksheet drills.
- **Build risk:** Low.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 3 "The Carry Chute" (carry-chute): Add and subtract within 1000 in columns: carry when a column passes 9 and borrow across a zero, writing the digits. — THE CARRY CHUTE: a column board with ones, tens and hundreds lanes; the problem is written at the top in digits and each digit is also a little stack of counters in its lane. The child works one column at a time from the right: tapping the ones column adds (or takes) its counters; if there are ten or more, ten counters slide into a chute and drop into the tens lane as one counter while a small '1' is written above the tens column — the carry is the counter. For subtraction the child drags one counter down from the next column; it breaks into ten on landing, and when that column is empty (a zero) the drag must start further left and break twice. The child types each answer digit under its column.
