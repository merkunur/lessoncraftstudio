## 1 `plants` - parts of a plant, what plants need, plant life cycle (G1 base; K/G2 faces) - Pedagogy 5

**Teaching point.** Germanic: en **K-LS1-1** (what plants need to survive, verbatim NGSS), **1-LS1-1** (external parts, G1), **2-LS2-1** (plants need water and light, G2); no CCSS (science) → strand needs a science literal; de Sachunterricht 1./2. Klasse "Pflanzen: Wurzel, Stängel, Blatt, Blüte", "Die Bohne keimt", "Frühblüher" (Perspektive Natur); nl groep 3-4 "delen van een plant, zaaien en kiemen" (Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld, kerndoel 40-41). Romance: es-MX 1º-2º "partes de la planta, qué necesitan las plantas" (Conocimiento del Medio / Exploración de la naturaleza); pt **BNCC EF02CI06 verbatim** "identificar as principais partes de uma planta (raiz, caule, folhas, flores e frutos) e a função desempenhada por cada uma delas" + **EF02CI05** (água e luz) - the cleanest code in the longlist; fr CP-CE1 "le vivant: les parties d'une plante, la germination, les besoins des végétaux" (Questionner le monde); it prima-seconda "le parti della pianta, la germinazione" (Scienze). Nordic: sv åk 1-2 (Lgr22 NO åk 1-3 plant life cycles and plant needs; panel quotes the wording, never in metadata); da 1.-2. kl natur/teknologi "planter: rod, stængel, blad, blomst"; no 2.-3. trinn naturfag "planter, frø og spiring". fi 1.-2. lk ympäristöoppi "kasvin osat: juuri, varsi, lehti, kukka".
**Boundary.** Never fruit/veg classification (K-202), living/non-living (K-201), natural/man-made (K-214), seasons (K-322 spring). The family owns: the PLANT DIAGRAM (new primitive), plant NEEDS, the seed→plant SEQUENCE, and "which part of the plant do we eat". It must not become a second `fruits-vs-vegetables`: the eat-face asks for the PART (root/stem/leaf/flower/seed/fruit), never fruit-or-vegetable.
**Faces.**
- base **label the plant** (G1; 4-6 word chips → anchors on the plant primitive; closed; genuine).
- **what does a plant need?** (K; circle the 3-4 needs among 6 picture chips: water (raindrop), light (sun primitive, `space/sun` is BLOCKED), air (primitive), soil (primitive) vs distractors e.g. toys; closed; distinct: a needs judgement, not naming). ⚠ K-LS1-1 lists water+light (+air); soil is NOT a strict need (hydroponics) → panel decides per locale whether "soil" is a correct chip; closed faces never make a contested item the discriminator.
- **order the life cycle** (G1; seed → sprout → plant → flower (→ seed/fruit) as 4 primitive stages, write 1-4; closed; distinct: temporal sequence; the bean cycle lives HERE, not in `life-cycles`).
- **which part do we eat?** (G1/G2; 6 vegetable pictures → one of root/stem/leaf/flower/seed/fruit; closed; genuine classic). Items restricted to textbook-unambiguous ones (carrot/radish/beetroot = root; lettuce/spinach/cabbage = leaf; broccoli/cauliflower = flower; celery/asparagus = stem; corn/peas = seed; tomato/cucumber/pepper = fruit). **Excluded outright:** potato (tuber = stem, contradicts intuition, K-3 books disagree), onion/garlic/leek (bulb), mushroom (not a plant), `vegetables/turnip` (= beetroot art), rhubarb.
- **what does each part do?** (G2; match part → job sentence "takes up water", "makes food", "holds the plant up"; closed; distinct: function, the EF02CI06 second clause).
- **plant diary / grow a bean** (G1/G2; open observation template, days 1-10 with a drawing box + height line; no verify; the classic de/fr/nl "Bohnentagebuch / carnet d'observation" routine).
**Per-locale refusals / re-targets.** None required. Eat-face: pt/es-MX item lists are the panel's (es-MX "elote"=corn, "jitomate"=tomato); fi/sv/no/da may lack peas/corn pictures in vocab → measured, not assumed.
**Traps.** Part terms: de Stängel (herbaceous) vs Stamm (tree) - never mix; fr tige, racine, feuille, fleur, graine; es tallo raíz hoja flor semilla fruto; pt caule raiz folha flor semente fruto (pt "fruto" botanical vs "fruta" food - the chip is **fruto**); it fusto/stelo (stelo for flowers) - panel chooses one; nl stengel wortel blad bloem zaad; sv stjälk rot blad blomma frö; da stængel rod blad blomst frø; no stilk rot blad blomst frø; fi varsi juuri lehti kukka siemen. Botanical "fruit" (tomato) contradicts kitchen "vegetable" in every locale → the eat-face instruction says "which PART of the plant", and the landing explains it. Nordic definite forms never printed (sv roten).
**Quality risk.** Plant diagrams are usually a clip-art tulip with leader lines crossing the stem. Rule: ONE `primitives/plant-figure.js` (flowering herb, visible roots below a soil line, 2 leaves, 1 flower, seed/fruit optional anchor), labels 3 per side, leaders never cross; every stage figure of the sequence drawn in the same primitive style.
**Assets.** NEW `primitives/plant-figure.js` (anchors root/stem/leaf/flower/fruit/seed; stage variants seed / sprout / young plant / flowering plant) + `primitives/sun.js` (disc + rays, palette only; `space/sun` stays blocked). Vegetables from `vegetables/`; raindrop from `weather/`.

**Boundary.** Never the chicken sequence (G1-203, owns egg→chick→hen with LIBRARY pictures), never baby-animal pairs (K-205, adult↔young of mammals/birds), never plants (candidate 1 owns seed→plant). **Faces.**

- **same or different from the parent?** (G1; circle whether the young looks like the adult: puppy-yes vs tadpole-no, using K-205-style library adults + primitive young; closed; 1-LS3-1; distinct: the metamorphosis CONCEPT).

**Per-locale refusals / re-targets.** None. Ladybird (en-US "ladybug") is well known everywhere but its larva is unfamiliar → ladybird may be a d3/G2 animal only.

Ladybird larva in fi "leppäkertun toukka".

A biologist-level check of every stage drawing (eggs on a leaf underside, tadpole with external-then-no gills omitted at K-3, froglet with tail stub) signed in the design file.
**Assets.** NEW `primitives/life-stage.js` (12 stage drawings: butterfly egg-on-leaf, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly; frogspawn, tadpole, froglet, frog; ladybird eggs, larva, pupa, adult) + a `cycle-wheel` layout (4 slots on a circle with arrows).

## 3 `materials` - materials and their properties (G1 base; K-G2 faces) - Pedagogy 5

**Teaching point.** Germanic: en **2-PS1-1** verbatim ("describe and classify different kinds of materials by their observable properties") and **2-PS1-2** (which material suits a purpose), G2; G1 pages readiness; de Sachunterricht 1./2. Klasse "Stoffe und ihre Eigenschaften", "Magnete: Was zieht der Magnet an?" (a canonical Kl 1-2 experiment); nl groep 3-4 "materialen: hout, metaal, glas, plastic; magnetisch". Romance: es-MX 1º-2º "los materiales y sus propiedades" (duro/blando, flexible, transparente); pt **BNCC EF01CI01 verbatim** ("comparar características de diferentes materiais presentes em objetos de uso cotidiano") G1; fr GS-CE1 "la matière / objets et matériaux; les aimants" (Explorer le monde → Questionner le monde); it prima-terza "oggetti e materiali: proprietà" (Indicazioni). Nordic: sv åk 1-3 (Lgr22 NO: sorting materials by properties incl. **magnetism** - panel quotes); da 1.-2. kl "materialer og deres egenskaber"; no 2.-3. trinn "materialer og egenskaper"; fi 1.-2. lk "materiaalit ja niiden ominaisuudet, magneetti".
**Boundary.** ⚠ Hardest boundary in the batch. `recycling` already ships **G1-364 "What Is It Made Of?"** (identify the material), **G2-348 odd one out by material**, **K-366 which bin**; `science-sort` ships **G1-204 sink/float** and **K-214 natural/man-made**. So `materials` must **never ask "what is it made of" as the task**. It owns PROPERTIES (hard/soft, bendy/stiff, see-through/not, waterproof/absorbent, magnetic/not) and SUITABILITY (why is a window glass, not wood). The material may be GIVEN as a label; the property is the answer.
**Faces.**
- base **property pairs** (G1; each picture → circle one of two property words, hard/soft, bendy/stiff, see-through/not; closed; genuine).
- **magnetic or not?** (G1; predict-then-sort into two bins; closed; distinct: a single property with an experiment; `sv` has it verbatim). Items only where the depicted metal is unambiguous iron/steel (scissors, nail as spike, fork, spoon, key? NO, see traps).
- **the wrong material** (G2; silly objects drawn as named "a chocolate teapot / a paper umbrella / a glass pillow": circle why it would not work, choose the property from 3; closed; 2-PS1-2 suitability; distinct: reasoning from property to purpose). Needs a text frame + a library picture, no recolouring (the material is NAMED, not drawn).
- **best material for the job** (G2; job + 3 material chips, pick one: "a window: glass / wood / cloth"; closed; distinct: choice, the inverse of the previous face).
- **waterproof or soaks up water?** (G1/G2; two-bin property sort, deliberately TWO bins (a third "neither" bin makes items two-valid); closed; distinct from the base: one property across many objects, sorted, not a per-item pair choice).
- **test it: my material investigation** (G2; open recording sheet: object, prediction, result for magnet / water / bend; no verify; the fr/de/sv experiment genre).
**Per-locale refusals / re-targets.** None. Property words authored per locale (not in vocab).
**Traps.** ⚠⚠ **Magnetism:** keys are usually brass (not magnetic) → excluded; drink cans are aluminium, food cans steel → excluded; coins vary (euro 1-5 c are copper-plated STEEL and magnetic; 10-50 c Nordic gold not; US coins not; MX, BR vary) → coins never; "all metals are magnetic" is THE misconception → the face must include at least one non-magnetic metal ONLY if the picture makes the metal certain (aluminium foil? not in library → then include none, and the landing states the rule). `tools/nail` = a spike: still steel, usable. **Property relativity:** "hard/soft" must be extreme pairs (rock vs pillow), never a sponge vs a ball. Words: de biegsam/starr, durchsichtig, wasserdicht vs saugfähig; fr souple/rigide, transparent/opaque, imperméable/absorbant; es flexible/rígido; pt flexível/rígido, transparente/opaco (pt-BR); it flessibile/rigido; nl buigzaam/stijf, doorzichtig; sv böjlig/stel, genomskinlig, vattentät; da bøjelig/stiv, gennemsigtig; no bøyelig/stiv, gjennomsiktig; fi taipuisa/jäykkä, läpinäkyvä, vedenpitävä/imukykyinen. Adjective agreement with the object noun (es/fr/it/pt, de, sv/da/no neuter) → property words print as chips in citation form, never in a sentence about the noun; the suitability frames are panel literals.
**Quality risk.** Material worksheets fail on ambiguous objects (a chair: wood, metal or plastic?). Rule: `data/b5/materials.json` stores per item the property values fixed by OPENING the picture, `picOpened:true`, and composite items (umbrella = fabric + metal) are excluded from any face where a component would change the answer.
**Assets.** Library only (kitchen tools, around the house, classroom, clothing, toys). New: a small magnet primitive (horseshoe, palette teal/coral) for the magnet face header.
