# ASSUMPTIONS — judgement calls made without asking

Per brief §11: anything unclear, wrong, or in tension with the research is recorded here with the decision taken and why. Entries are numbered A-nn; FINAL-REPORT.md reports how each resolved. Entries added during research and spec-writing are appended at the bottom with the date.

## Written before starting (2026-09-05)

**A-1 · The brief contradicts itself on approvals.** The introduction says "stop at each checkpoint for my approval"; §11 says "Run all three phases straight through without stopping … Do not ask for approval between phases." §11 is later, explicit and repeated → I run straight through. The single approval taken was the implementation plan itself (plan mode, 2026-09-05), where the operator also ruled: output root `C:\Users\rkgen\lessoncraftstudio\games\`; parallel agents allowed for research and for spec batches after exemplars; avoid duplicating the site's 204 live free activities; add `makeTile()` to the shared library.

**A-2 · The project's own doctrine said games were cancelled.** `CLAUDE.md` and the memory files record an operator ruling (2026-07-03) that "there will never be games". This brief re-opens the program and the operator confirmed in-session that earlier decisions do not bind ("No decision is absolute"). This brief governs; the old triggers ("let's start building the games") and the old premium-games catalog are not used as inputs.

**A-3 · Stage geometry.** The brief says "never assume a fixed viewport" and Phaser needs a logical stage. Decision: logical stage 720 × 560, `Scale.FIT` + horizontal centring (the site pins tool iframes near 704 px; `THEME.size.cardMaxWidth` = 720). Consequence: tap floors are stated in logical px with headroom — 56 for ages 6-9 (44 px real down to a 565-px iframe) and 80 for ages 5-6 (64 px real at the same scale). Below ~560 px iframe width targets shrink under the accessibility floor; the brief scopes tablets and desktops, so that is accepted and noted as a risk. `Scale.RESIZE` with per-game reflow was rejected: it would put layout decisions into 200 build-model files.

**A-4 · The shared library gained four helpers.** The brief's "use the shared libraries" assumed they covered every need; `game-core.js` only made keyboard-operable *buttons* at a fixed 220 × 72, so answer tiles and grid cells had no keyboard path. Added (additive, tested in `_test/run-tests.js`, poison-tested): `makeTile(scene,x,y,w,h,opts)` (any-size keyboard-registered tap target; operator-approved), `playAnim(scene,target,spec)` (runs one ANIM entry), `tone(name)` (four Web-Audio tones, silent under `?sound=off`), `setSoundEnabled(v)`. The last three were my call: without them 200 specs would each re-describe a tween wrapper and an oscillator, and the build model would produce 200 slightly different ones. The operator may strike them; the fallback is a verbatim snippet in BUILD-CONVENTIONS.

**A-5 · "50 common strings" is the real key set, not a promise.** `ui-strings.js` carries exactly the keys listed in BUILD-CONVENTIONS §9. Specs use only those through `t()`; everything else is a game-specific string. The linter fails a spec that calls a non-existent key.

**A-6 · Emoji rendering is not uniform.** Older tablets lack glyphs newer than Unicode 12 (2019); an unsupported emoji renders as a box. Rule: prefer ≤ Unicode 12 emoji; a newer glyph (e.g. 🪹 nest, U+1FAB9, Unicode 13) is allowed only with a `fallback` in the same ART entry. The brief's own example uses 🪹; the rule keeps it usable.

**A-7 · PROGRESS.md is written on verification, not on drafting.** The brief says "after every spec, append one line". With batches of specs written by agents, an agent appending concurrently would interleave lines. Decision: a spec is written to disk the moment it is finished (resumability preserved); the PROGRESS line is appended by me only after the spec passes `_tools/lint-specs.js`. One line per spec, in order, still holds; a file on disk without a PROGRESS line means "drafted, not yet verified" on restart.

**A-8 · Locale-varying content.** The brief forbids national currency and imperial units. Metric everywhere. Money games are pedagogically load-bearing (every curriculum), so instead of dropping them a money game declares `LOCALE_DATA` per language (BUILD-CONVENTIONS §14: en→£, de/fr/it/es/nl/fi→€, pt→R$, sv/da/no→kr) and uses only coin values that exist in that currency. "en" is mapped to England (the brief lists England as the English-language curriculum), not the US.

**A-9 · The build-first ten also serve as my exemplars.** I write specs 001-010 myself before any writer agent starts, so the house voice, the level of detail and the lint-clean shape are demonstrated on real specs, and the first games the human builds are the ones I checked most carefully.

**A-10 · Specs cite research by ID.** The brief demands traceability ("if you later specify a game that your own research doesn't support, that is a defect"). Mechanism: FINDINGS.md numbers every finding `F-nn`; every spec's Curriculum links and Common misconceptions cite at least one; the linter fails a spec with no citation or a citation that does not exist.

**A-11 · The subject split is decided by the research, but "science" is interpreted broadly.** The brief lists mathematics, literacy and science. Several curricula fold "science" for ages 5-9 into world-knowledge / sachunterricht / questionner le monde (nature, materials, seasons, the body, time and space). Catalogue rows in that space are tagged `science` with the sub-topic named; "logic / spatial" games that no curriculum owns as a subject are tagged under mathematics (geometry / patterns) only when a curriculum names them; otherwise they are not built.

**A-12 · "Design to the lower edge of the band" is enforced structurally.** For the 5-6 band a spec may not rely on any instruction sentence: the prompt is an ART element plus at most three words, the first item is solvable by tapping any tile and watching, and the linter's font floor is 28 px. A game whose objective cannot survive that is banded 6-8, not "5-6 with instructions".

**A-13 · Line-count ceiling.** Brief: 400-800 lines. Spec estimates must fall in 300-800 (linted); the lower bound is relaxed to 300 because a simple tap-one-of-N game with the shared library genuinely fits in ~350 lines and padding it would be waste.

**A-14 · The build model never chooses tile positions.** BUILD-CONVENTIONS §7.1 gives the formula for centring N tiles, so a spec can say "3 tiles, 80 × 80, gap 24" and the positions are determined.

## Added during research / catalogue / specs
(appended below with dates)

**A-15 (2026-09-05, from research) · The literacy misconception catalogue is English-derived.** Reversals, letter-name interference (y/w/h), short-vowel confusions, three-cueing, silent-e are documented for English readers; the equivalent confusable sets for de/fr/es/it/pt/nl/sv/da/no/fi were not systematically researched (the learning-science source names the traps it found: per-locale digraph inventories, V2 word order, inverted number words in de/nl/da, Nordic/German half-hour idiom, southern-hemisphere seasons). Decision: literacy specs carry the English content in `## Content`, keep every language-bound list inside `LOCALE_DATA` or declare it "en pilot; other locales need a native word list", and cite F-121…F-129 for the error CLASS, which is language-general even where the instances are not.

**A-16 (2026-09-05, from research) · Two demand sources could not be fetched.** Google Trends (HTTP 429) and Reddit teacher threads (never surfaced by search). Demand is therefore established from 15 game sites' inventories and rankings, SplashLearn's per-topic content counts, Mumsnet threads and app-store editorial lists (F-1…F-9). The ranking is robust at the top (addition, counting, subtraction, shapes, place value, tables) and weaker in the mid-tier, so mid-tier topic counts in the catalogue are set by curriculum commonality (F-21…F-23) rather than by demand.

**A-17 (2026-09-05, from research) · The within-session re-queue schedule is transferred, not tested.** Expanding lags (1 → 2-3 → 5-7 intervening items) come from preschool word-learning studies, not from a 5-7-minute digital game. Adopted as the BUILD-CONVENTIONS §8 default because the alternative (no re-queue) is known to be worse (F-41); the adaptation thresholds (3-up / 2-down, later relaxed to 2-up in §8 for short sessions) are design choices consistent with the 80-90% success target (F-46), not measured algorithms.

**A-18 (2026-09-05, from research) · "Grade" labels are never shown; content is keyed to AGE.** Grade 1 means age 6 in ES/BR/IT/NO/US/England but age 7 in SE/DK (bridge years) and Finland starts at 7. Every spec states the age band; the curriculum links name the country's year for that AGE (F-20).

**A-19 (2026-09-05, from research) · Money games are Romance-market + England games with a neutral core.** SE/DK/NO do not teach currency before age 10 (F-29). Decision: money games (values 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50) ship with `LOCALE_DATA` for every currency that exists (£, €, R$, kr) using only real denominations, and the value structure (not the coin picture) is the objective; a Nordic child can still play (kr coins exist: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20) — the curriculum simply does not require it, so those rows are not counted toward Nordic coverage.

**A-20 (2026-09-05, from research) · Times-table games target 8-9 with a 6-8 "groups-of" on-ramp.** Tables to 10 are required by 8 only in Italy; Brazil sequences 2-3-4-5-10 by 8; Spain 8-10; the Nordics list no tables at 5-9 (F-26). Demand for tables is the strongest signal on the web (F-2). Decision: tables games are banded 8-9 (their lower edge is grade 3 everywhere) and are built on arrays/groups (F-110, F-203) so they teach the concept the Nordic curricula DO require, with retrieval fluency as the upper level.

**A-21 (2026-09-05, catalogue) · Band outcome 64 / 98 / 38, not the 63 / 89 / 48 first targeted.** After levelling every row by the most conservative system's age (F-31) and re-scoping ~35 rows that duplicated a free activity's objective AND mechanic (`research/PRIOR-ART.md`), the 8-9 share settled at 38. Kept as-is: 8-9 objectives are the market-conditional ones (tables, division, fraction notation, minute-level time — F-26…F-28), and the free catalogue already covers much grade-3 CCSS ground, so paid 8-9 games have the least room to be distinct. Mid-tier 6-8 rose to 98 because that is where all twelve systems converge (F-20, F-21). Subject split landed exactly on the justified 110 / 66 / 24.

**A-22 (2026-09-05, catalogue) · "Not a free activity re-skinned" is applied as: different MECHANIC (pattern) and a different cognition or sub-objective.** Where the free activity owns both the objective and the natural mechanic (ten-frame counting, set-the-clock hands, bond fill-the-missing-part, CVC/syllable builders, partition halves, count-out coins, sharing/grouping deals), the game row moved to a predict-then-reveal, sort-into-bins, pair-matching, judge-among-three or trace-a-path form of the same skill. Lexical overlaps that remain in the checker's PRIOR-ART list were each read and kept only where the mechanic differs.

**A-23 (2026-09-05, specs) · The spec-length floor measures characters, not lines.** The first floor (150 lines) failed three complete exemplar specs at 140-148 lines because they pack 12-14k characters into long table rows and bullets. Changing the threshold to pass would have been the forbidden move; changing WHAT is measured is the right one: the floor is now 9,000 characters of spec body (a thin spec of the kind the floor exists to catch is 3-5k). The poison fixtures were re-run: the clean one passes, the broken one still fails on all nine injected defects.

**A-24 (2026-09-05, specs) · The batch gate's "needs a fallback" emoji rule is Unicode 13+ by code point, not a broad range.** The first version flagged whole blocks (1FA70-1FAFF etc.) and refused five literacy specs for Unicode-12 glyphs (chair, kite, lamp, yo-yo). Corrected to the Unicode 13-15 code points and poison-checked in both directions (🪑 passes; 🪹 and 🦫 are flagged). Same lesson as A-23: fix what is measured, never the threshold.
