## 5 Quality verdict

Yes, I would print the base and every face for my class, and I would print the base first: four big windows I can hold up and ask "what do you see under the water?", eight animals a child can name, one letter each. What would have embarrassed me, and is now designed out, is the kind of mistake parents and colleagues find in a week: a "leopard" my student sends to the jungle while the key says savanna, a teddy bear filed under forest, two identical yellow parrots on one page, a "worm" that is a smiling snake, a honeycomb hanging from a branch that any beekeeper would call a wasp nest, penguins on the same ice as walruses, a camel whose hump "stores water", and home words that are the same word for a burrow and a beaver lodge in Spanish and Italian. What still worries me is small and named: the duckling some children will call a chick, and whether the pond reads as a pond rather than a puddle when the photocopier is tired; both are on the print check and the native panels' list. The weakest page is the needs face, because four or five animals is a short pool and the page will feel familiar after two copies; it is honest and it teaches the real idea (this animal's food, not any food), which is more than the toy-and-television versions did.
