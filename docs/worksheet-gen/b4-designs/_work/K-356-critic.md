## 5 Quality verdict

I would print this for a K-1 class, and the first page is the one I would pin to the wall: six honest weather badges in a ring, the words across a clear 213 px run, nothing on the page that is not the task. The thermometer page is the one that needed the most surgery and is now the one I am proudest of: no numeral anywhere, a key the child reads like a legend, four instruments with no picture on them, and the scarf / T-shirt / sunglasses chips that a five-year-old actually reasons about ("it is low, so it is cold, so I wear the scarf") instead of matching a snowflake to a snowflake. The water-cycle page is a proper grade-3 diagram with its arrows numbered the way every textbook does it, and the forecast page finally makes the child READ a question rather than scan for a picture. What would embarrass me: a grey pink cloud on a mono copier (ruled out by choosing `cloudy`), a sun whose rays vanish (ruled out by `sunny`), a Portuguese forecast row whose day pills spill off the lane (measured, not assumed, before the wave), and a diary that overflows under a four-line Finnish title (re-ruled to 676). The two honest floors (four labels, three questions) are the genre, not laziness, and every landing says so. The one thing no gate will catch is whether a K child accepts a scarf as "cold" without being told; the key row tells them, and the base and F1 have already taught the six words by the time the sheet is handed out.
