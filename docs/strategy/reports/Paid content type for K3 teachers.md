# Sell the Three-Level Set, Not the Download

**Bottom line:** LessonCraftStudio should build and sell **Level Sets** — one K-3 skill, one theme, the same exercise at three calibrated difficulty levels (support / core / stretch), printable and playable, sequenced week-by-week to each locale's national curriculum, with a one-page placement check and class record sheet — and move the paywall from "3 downloads a month" to "the single worksheet is free, the set is paid". Conversion is low today for a structural reason, not a traffic reason: the Teacher plan charges for *more of the same* standalone worksheets plus sharing features that Boom, Kahoot, Wordwall and Blooket give away free, while every durable K-3 paid product sells a *sequence* or a *record* ([freemium note, KQ3-KQ5](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/freemium_conversion.md)). The single teacher problem that is top-3 in every locale with data and is solvable by content is producing level-differentiated practice for one wide-ability class — **40% of German Grundschule teachers name heterogeneity as their #1 burden**, **more than 8 in 10 French teachers cite "écarts de niveaux"**, **9 in 10 Spanish teachers say ratios prevent attending to diversity**, and **over 80% of Dutch teachers say they lack time for individual attention** ([Bosch Schulbarometer 2025](https://www.bosch-stiftung.de/sites/default/files/documents/2025-06/Deutsches%20Schulbarometer_Lehrkr%C3%A4fte_2025.pdf); [DEPP/PRAESCO](https://www.ih2ef.gouv.fr/pratiques-denseignement-du-francais-praesco-en-classe-de-cm2); [STEs-i](https://elmirondesoria.es/nacional/sociedad/stes-i-apunta-en-encuesta-problemas-que-sufren-maestros-y-profesores-en-espana); [AOb 2024](https://www.aob.nl/assets/Brieven-AOb/Position-Paper-AOb-Passend-onderwijs-tbv-debat-29-05-2024-en-bijlage.pdf)). Level Sets fit the site's existing machinery almost exactly: the generators already parametrise number range and difficulty, the catalog already ships an identical 11-locale corpus with self-checking interactive decks, and the recent "base + 5 faces" batches already produce *variants* — they just vary theme rather than level. Two alternates are worth keeping in reserve: a year-long sequenced daily-review programme (the most proven US price band, $15-72 per kindergarten bundle) and a home-language parallel sheet with a parent note (the only thing an 11-language identical catalog can do that nobody else can, but with no proof yet that teachers pay for it). The evidence base is real but uneven: every external fact came from search-result summaries because page fetching was blocked, the researchers' budget ran out before Spain, Italy, Brazil, Denmark, Norway and Finland were covered, and several key price points (Worksheet Crafter, Twinkl tiers, Heggerty) were never captured.

## The meter charges for the thing that brought the visitor, and the paid tier sells what rivals give away

The site's own code confirms the offer exactly as the operator describes it: `PLAYS_PER_DAY = 10` for anonymous and free accounts, `DOWNLOADS_PER_MONTH = 3` with a free account and zero without one, and a Teacher plan at $69/year or $8.99/month that removes the limits and adds hosted worksheets, collections, play links, activity share links, topic-pack ZIPs and "full depth" in about 50 of 62 classroom tools ([frontend/lib/quota.ts](../../../frontend/lib/quota.ts); [site audit §2](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). Everything on the pricing page is genuinely built and live; payments were proven end-to-end in July 2026 ([pricing page](https://www.lessoncraftstudio.com/en/pricing); [site audit §4](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). So the problem is not a broken checkout. It is what is on either side of the wall.

Start with the arithmetic. No teacher-content company publishes a visitor-to-subscriber rate, but the best proxies say EdTech is the *worst-converting* freemium category at roughly **2.6% of registered free accounts** (not visitors), against 3-5% for "good" self-serve freemium generally ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)). A Google visitor landing on one worksheet first has to become a registered free user, and on content sites that step is typically single-digit percent. At ~300 clicks a day (~9,000 visits a month) the funnel would yield somewhere between single-digit and low-double-digit new subscribers a month *if it performed at benchmark*. "A handful, total" is therefore well below benchmark — the offer is underperforming, not only the traffic. The winners in this category also run enormous funnels: Twinkl has **1.1 million individual subscribers** on £98.9m turnover, Kahoot had **450,000 education paid subscriptions** in 2023 ([Insider Media](https://www.insidermedia.com/news/yorkshire/milestone-year-for-educational-publisher-as-turnover-nears-100m-disney-partnership-set-to-support-further-growth); [Kahoot Q1 2023](https://kahoot.com/files/2023/05/1Q23_Kahoot_quarterly_report.pdf)). Traffic alone will not fix this; the site is an order of magnitude below any comparable that reached viability, and even at that scale the *shape* of the offer would still be wrong.

The shape is wrong in three specific ways. First, the meter is a **consume cap on the entry content**. The 3-downloads-a-month shape is Education.com's, and Education.com has had to tighten its free quota from 10 to 3 to reportedly 2 per month — the signature of a wall that does not convert at a generous setting — while its own Premium pitch has drifted toward a progress tracker rather than "more downloads" ([Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/18496630-education-com-2-free-pre-k-through-8th-grade-printable-educational-worksheets-per-month-math-english-science-social-studies-foreign-language); [Education.com blog](https://blog.education.com/2020/11/12/the-benefits-of-a-premium-education-com-membership/)). The meters that survive without backlash are **create-or-own caps**: Wordwall lets teachers *play* unlimited community activities free and caps *creation* at five; Kahoot, Blooket and Quizizz cap live players, not plays ([Wordwall help](https://wordwall.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360015608178--Is-Wordwall-Free); [Kahoot plans](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/)). Consume caps are the ones with the complaint threads — Quizlet's 2022 paywall "alienated its loyal user base", Kahoot's 2023 report paywall drew "no warning" complaints, Twinkl's Trustpilot page cites "hidden download limits in fine print" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@maxtan0626/navigating-quizlets-controversial-changes-afeb97aafd1e); [Kahoot community](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/20896401687827-Reports-Now-require-a-paid-subscription); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/twinkl.co.uk)). A 10-plays-a-day cap is also unlikely to be *felt* by the one-worksheet Google visitor at all; it bites the returning heavy user, who is exactly the person most annoyed by it.

Second, **the paid tier has nothing that the free tier lacks in kind, only in quantity**. Hosted links, play links and QR codes correspond to free-tier features elsewhere — Boom's Fast Pins, Kahoot's game codes, Wordwall's community play ([Krafty in Kinder](https://kraftyinkinder.com/how-to-use-the-free-boom-learning-account/)). What those companies actually charge for is persistence (Boom's free pins expire in 14 days), the per-child *record* (Boom, Kahoot, Quizizz, Freckle, Education.com all draw the free/paid line at "the child can do it free; the teacher can see what each child did, paid"), and unlimited *creation* ([Freckle help](https://freckle-uk-help.renaissance.com/hc/en-gb/articles/13774935728923-What-features-of-Freckle-are-free-What-do-I-get-with-Freckle-Premium-Edition); [Boom help](https://helpcenter.boomlearning.com/assigning-un-assigning-and-playing-boom-cards)). The site records nothing per child by operator ruling, and the "saved setups" advertised on the homepage persist only in the browser's localStorage — the notes themselves call a server-side tool-state store "the #1 unbuilt shared piece" ([site audit §4](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). Third, **the offer is invisible until the wall**. Landing pages, activity pages, tool pages and maker landings carry no upsell; topic hubs and maker landings still say "free to print… no signup"; 28 tools still call the plan "Premium" though it is sold as "Teacher" ([site audit §3](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). A visitor who never returns never learns there is anything to buy, and the visitor who does return meets a surprise limit on the very thing they came for — the two documented trust-breakers in this market.

One more structural point matters for what to build next. Every content-exclusive premium the site has tried — teaching packages (killed for "generic filler" quality), the storybook library and Story Studio (ruled dead in full), the premium games (cancelled, re-opened, still undeployed) — was killed before reaching paying users; the only premium that survived to market is limit-and-feature based ([site audit §7](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). The lesson is not "content premiums fail". It is that a content premium here must be derivable from the catalog machinery (so quality is template-guaranteed, not hand-authored filler) and must solve a problem teachers already name, not one the operator finds interesting.

## The one problem teachers name everywhere: one class, three levels, no time

The pain-point research covered eleven school systems, and the same two burdens top the list wherever numbers exist: adapting instruction to a widening ability spread, and administrative load. Only the first is a content problem. The second-order evidence is consistent with the headline surveys: **90% of US public-school teachers add or remix internet content** on top of their mandated curriculum and spend roughly **5 extra hours a week creating their own materials** ([RAND AIRS](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA134-30.html); [Fordham](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/commentary/case-curriculum-about-reducing-teachers-workload)); OECD TALIS 2024 puts lesson preparation at **7.4 hours a week** across systems, with Dutch teachers specifically asking to be trained "for teaching multilingual groups and differentiating in class" ([OECD TALIS 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/results-from-talis-2024_90df6235-en/full-report/the-demands-of-teaching_0e941e2f.html); [Rijksoverheid TALIS NL](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/rapporten/2025/10/07/nationaal-rapport-talis-2024-de-werk-en-leeromgeving-op-school-in-beeld)). The Swedish union finds **36% of teachers stressed by too little planning time** and Finnish teachers put "adequately small group size that considers learners needing support" at the top of their wish list ([Vi Lärare](https://www.vilarare.se/nyheter/arbetsbelastning/varannan-larare-svarar-vi-kan-inte-utfora-vart-jobb/); [OAJ 2024](https://www.oaj.fi/contentassets/476cfb03fec74aa0a9feea4166f4dba7/opetusalan_tyoolobarometri_2024.pdf)). The single most quotable line the researchers retrieved is from a US educator's AI wish list: "Differentiation takes so much time" ([EdSurge](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2025-06-20-an-ai-wish-list-from-teachers-what-they-actually-want-it-to-do)).

The workflow this produces is remarkably uniform across countries even though the vocabulary differs. German publishers sell "dreifach differenziert" reading sheets — level 1 with pictures, level 2 with words, level 3 with sentences — and list "Differenzierung / Niveaustufen" as a shop category; UK marketplaces sell worksheets in low/middle/high versions; a vendor estimate puts hand-building multi-level versions at **2-3 extra hours per lesson** ([Hase und Igel](https://www.hase-und-igel.de/blog_hase_und_igel_verlag_lesen_in_der_schule/differenzierung_grundschule/c-236); [Kohl Verlag](https://www.kohlverlag.de/grundschule/methodisch-didaktische-eigenschaften/differenzierung-niveaustufen/); [TES](https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/christmas-time-worksheets-differentiated-analogue-and-digital-11447443); [Chalkie.ai](https://chalkie.ai/en/blog/ai-worksheet-generator-tools)). In Germany the dominant *paid* teacher tool is not a content library at all but Worksheet Crafter, whose strength "lies particularly in Grundschule, Förderschule and Inklusion, where teachers can design… variants" — i.e. teachers already pay for a differentiation generator ([Lernmarktplatz](https://lernmarktplatz.de/products/worksheet-crafter)). The problem is intensifying rather than fading: in Spain pupils with specific support needs grew **75% while resources grew 30%**; Italy counts **359,000 pupils with disabilities, up 25% in ten years**; in Germany, the Netherlands and the US the multilingual-learner share keeps rising ([STEs-i](https://elmirondesoria.es/nacional/sociedad/stes-i-apunta-en-encuesta-problemas-que-sufren-maestros-y-profesores-en-espana); [ISTAT 2025](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alunni-con-disabilita-as-23-24.pdf); [EdWeek 2025](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/teachers-say-they-need-more-support-for-english-learners-survey-finds/2025/09)).

What the demand-side evidence adds is the *format* teachers pay for. The candidate-type research scored twelve content types on verified willingness to pay and resistance to free AI. The two that scored highest were year-long sequenced daily review (kindergarten morning-work bundles at **$15-72**, a first-grade number-talks yearlong at **$210**) and differentiated small-group/intervention kits (**$19-63** phonics bundles, a "growing bundle" priced to reach $165) ([TPT morning work](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Kindergarten-Morning-Work-Year-Long-Bundle-749474); [TPT number talks](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/First-Grade-Number-Talks-DIGITAL-and-Printable-A-Yearlong-Program-1883045); [TPT phonics bundle](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Kindergarten-Phonics-Activities-Bundle-Centers-Small-Groups-Interventions-5525055)). The freemium research reaches the same conclusion from the subscription side: every K-3 product with a per-classroom-year price that teachers pay without complaint — Heggerty's same-shaped daily lesson in a 35-week progression, Raz-Plus's levelled ladder, Twinkl's PlanIt schemes, Zaubereinmaleins' Materialpakete — organises content as **a ladder or a routine** and attaches evidence of the child's place on it ([ReadFlare on Heggerty](https://readflare.com/articles/phonics-and-decoding/heggerty-phonics); [Raz-Plus](https://www.raz-plus.com/); [Twinkl PlanIt](https://twinkl.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360006840038-Twinkl-PlanIt)). A flat library priced by volume is the shape of the products with the complaint threads.

## The recommendation: Level Sets, sequenced to the national curriculum

The primary content type this evidence supports is the **Level Set**: one skill (say, addition within 10), one theme (farm animals), rendered as three versions of the same exercise at calibrated levels — a *support* version (smaller range, more pictures, fewer items), a *core* version (the grade-standard task), and a *stretch* version (larger range, fewer scaffolds, a reasoning twist) — each available as a printable PDF and as a self-checking interactive deck, sharing one layout and one answer-key convention so the teacher can hand out all three in one lesson without three explanations. Sets are not standalone: they sit on a **week-by-week scope and sequence per locale** (Lgr22 for Sweden, Lehrplan for Germany, BNCC for Brazil, SEP for Mexico, and so on), so a teacher can pull "week 7 of Kindergarten number sense" and get the whole ladder for that week. Each set carries a **one-page placement check** (six to eight quick items that tell the teacher which child gets which level) and a **class record sheet** (a printable grid: child × skill × level reached). That record is deliberately paper and teacher-held: it gives the teacher the "who mastered what" artefact that the comparables charge for, without the student accounts or progress tracking the operator has ruled out.

This is the synthesis of the two top-scoring candidates. It takes the *differentiation* content from the intervention-kit category (the one that answers the universally-cited pain) and the *sequence* structure from the morning-work category (the one with the most proven price points and the lowest AI-kill risk), and it packages both in the one artefact the German market already proves teachers value — the three-level set. The pain-point researchers' own recommendation was exactly this: "ready-made, level-tiered (and, where relevant, home-language-paired) practice sets for one K-3 skill, marketed on 'Differentiation takes so much time' and on the reclaimed weekly planning hours" ([teacher pain points, ranked map](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/teacher_pain_points.md)).

Why teachers would pay for this instead of prompting an AI or downloading it free comes down to four things that text generation cannot supply. **Pictures and pre-reader design**: Diffit, Brisk and MagicSchool level *text* for free, but none produces a picture-led K-1 task at three calibrated levels, and their output is "an editable starting point" rather than a finished, self-checking page ([TeachTools 2026](https://teachtools.co/blog/best-free-ai-worksheet-generators-2026); [MagicSchool](https://www.magicschool.ai/tools/worksheet-generator)). **Native language with correct grammar**: the site's 1,200-noun vocabulary carries singular, plural and gender in all 11 languages, which is precisely what a machine-translated or prompted sheet gets wrong, and which native panels have been auditing batch by batch. **Calibrated levels, not random variants**: an AI gives one page per prompt; a level ladder needs someone to have decided what "support" means for addition-within-10 in Grade 1 under Lgr22, and to have checked that the stretch version does not silently cross into Grade 2 — the kind of quantity-ceiling discipline the landing-page programme already enforces per coordinate. **Sequence**: the 2026 AI generator round-ups describe single-worksheet output; none is described as producing a coherent 36-week progression, which is the property the $210 yearlong and the $57 mega-bundle are actually selling ([TeachTools 2026](https://teachtools.co/blog/best-free-ai-worksheet-generators-2026)). Against free sites, the argument is the same one Twinkl uses against free blogs — everything in one place, visibly labelled to the curriculum on each page, in the teacher's language — but with a level ladder that Twinkl's flat resource pages do not offer and that the free national portals (Skolverket, éduscol, LOWAN, OPH) do not offer for regular-class maths and literacy at all ([Twinkl blog](https://www.twinkl.com/blog/why-subscribe-to-twinkl-usa)).

Price and positioning should stay inside the personal-wallet band the market has already set. Teacher-paid subscriptions cluster at **€5-11 / $7-16 a month, roughly $45-130 a year** (Twinkl DE ≈ €5/month annual, Wordwall €45-67.50/year, Education.com $60-120/year, Starfall teacher $70/year); anything above ~$150 is packaged as a classroom licence with student caps and a quote path ([TwinklCares](https://twinkl.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/19162663770525-How-Much-Is-a-Twinkl-Subscription); [Wordwall plans](https://wordwall.net/price-plans); [Starfall](https://store.starfall.com/membership)). The current $69/$8.99 already matches the market's two-months-free annual structure; the pricing shape is not the problem. Germany is the one caution: teacher-paid there is tiny one-off contributions (Zaubereinmaleins €15-20 for a year) or per-download purchases on eduki, and Worksheet Crafter sells to schools in seat packs, so a €60-a-year German subscriber is a harder sell than a US one and the German pitch should lean on "Wochenplan-ready three-level sets", not "morning work" ([Zaubereinmaleins](https://intern.zaubereinmaleins.de/home/info.html); [Worksheet Crafter](https://worksheetcrafter.com/de/preise)). Out-of-pocket budgets exist everywhere the numbers were found — US teachers **$655-895 a year**, French primary teachers **€297-410**, German teachers roughly **€585**, Austrian **€550** — but they are spent on things the teacher can see working in front of the class, which is why the placement check and record sheet belong in the set: they make the value visible on the first Monday ([AdoptAClassroom 2025](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/2025/06/09/2025-teacher-survey-spending-stats-classroom-needs/); [Scolinfo](https://www.scolinfo.net/92-des-enseignants-depensent-plus-de-300-euros-par-an-dargent-personnel-pour-leur-classe/); [Lehrerfreund](https://www.lehrerfreund.de/schule/1s/lehrer-ausgaben-unterrichtsmaterial/4435); [kosmo.at](https://www.kosmo.at/550-euro-privat-so-viel-zahlen-oesterreichs-lehrer-fuer-den-unterricht/)).

## What has to be built, and how the paywall should change around it

Level Sets are unusually cheap for this site to make because almost every component already exists in some form; the work is mostly *calibration and packaging*, not new generators. The 29 interactive apps already expose the parameters a level ladder needs (number range, item count, picture-versus-word modes, distractor counts), the headless printable generator already produces "base + 5 faces" per type in 11 locales, every deck already plays and prints with a baked answer key and action strip, the taxonomy already carries five educational levels and 140 exercise-type keys, and the topic-pack ZIP already bundles selected PDFs client-side ([site audit §1, §4](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). What is missing is captured in the table below, ordered by how much it blocks a first sellable set.

| Component | Exists today | What must be built | Why it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level ladder per skill | Faces vary *theme*; generators vary range/count | A per-(type, locale, grade) "level recipe": what support/core/stretch mean, with a quantity-ceiling check so stretch never crosses the grade band | This is the product; without calibrated levels it is three random variants |
| Scope and sequence per locale | Educational-level axis; national framework names in copy | A week-by-week K-2 sequence per locale (36 weeks × skills), authored once with a native panel, stored as data | Sequence is what the $57-210 products sell; also the renewal reason |
| Set page | Landing per deck; topic hubs | One page per set: the three PDFs, three play links, the placement check, the record sheet, the curriculum label | The unit of purchase and of SEO ("addition within 10, three levels, Grade 1") |
| Placement check + record sheet | Nothing | Two templated printables per set (11 locales, derived from the set's items, no per-item authoring) | The visible-value artefact; the teacher-held "record" without student data |
| Paywall shape | Consume meter (3 PDF/month, 10 plays/day) | Single worksheets and single plays **free and unmetered**; the *set* (all three levels + check + record + sequence navigation) **paid**; keep hosted/collection features as paid conveniences | Moves from the backlash-prone consume cap to the create/own cap the survivors use |
| Trial | "The catalog is the trial" | A time-boxed full-access trial (e.g. 30 days) timed to August/September and January, easy cancel, first-year discount | ESGI (60 days) and Twinkl (monthly) convert on time, not on counts |
| Trust surface | No per-page upsell; "Premium" naming residue | Curriculum label and a visible "get the three-level set" strip on every landing; fix the plan name in the 28 tools | Teachers trust other teachers (81%) and visible standards, and distrust hidden limits |

Two of these deserve a word for a non-technical reader. The **level recipe** is a judgement task, not a coding task: for each of the roughly 140 exercise types someone must decide, per locale and grade, what "easier" and "harder" legitimately mean and where the grade boundary lies — the same discipline the landing programme already applies when it excludes a coordinate whose sums exceed 20 from a Grade-1 page. That decision should be made by the native curriculum panels the site already convenes per batch, and it should be made *once per type*, because the generators then produce the three versions for every theme automatically. The **paywall change** is the part most likely to feel risky, because it gives away the download that is currently metered. The evidence says the download was never converting: Education.com's tightening to 2 free per month and the "handful" of subscribers here point the same way, and the single-worksheet click is the acquisition channel, not the product. Wordwall's 600,000 subscribed educators came from letting everyone play free and charging for creation ([Wordwall plans](https://wordwall.net/price-plans)). Charging for the set, not the sheet, means the wall appears exactly at the moment the teacher wants something the free page visibly does not have — the other two levels and the record — instead of at the eleventh play of something they already had.

Sequencing the build matters more than its size. The first sellable slice is one subject, one grade band, one locale, with real demand evidence: Kindergarten and Grade 1 number sense and addition/subtraction in English and German (the site's proven converting locales are sv/nl/da/de, and en is the international-school entry point), roughly 20-30 sets covering a semester, each with the three levels, the check and the record. That slice tests the two unknowns that no amount of desk research can settle — whether teachers at this traffic level buy a level ladder at $69, and whether the placement-check-plus-record artefact is the thing they mention when they say why. Only after that should the sequence be extended to literacy (where locale-specific phonics conventions make the level recipe harder) and to the remaining nine locales.

## Two alternates, and where the evidence is thin

The **first alternate** is a year-long sequenced daily-review programme — "morning work" in US terms, "Wochenplan material" in German — a 180-day spiral of one page a day per grade, printable and playable. It scores highest of any candidate on proven price points (**$15-72 per kindergarten year**, repeat sellers, an all-access K-3 mega bundle sold direct) and lowest on AI risk, because sequencing is exactly what single-prompt generators do not do; and for a generator platform with 140 types across 11 locales, sequencing is a data job rather than a content job ([TPT spiral review](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Kindergarten-Morning-Work-for-Daily-Spiral-Review-Worksheets-2455633); [Second Story Window](https://www.secondstorywindow.net/product/morning-work-all-access-mega-bundle-k-3-math-ela-spiral-review/)). It is an alternate rather than the primary because its demand evidence is almost entirely US, "Tagesplan" means the schedule card in Germany rather than a review page, and — critically — it does nothing about the level spread in the class, which is the pain every locale names. If Level Sets are built on a week-by-week sequence, the daily-review programme becomes a natural second product on the same spine rather than a competing one.

The **second alternate** is the one only this site can make: a **home-language parallel sheet** — the same task, same layout, same answer key, rendered in the school language and the child's home language — with a one-paragraph "how to help with this" parent note on the back in the family's language. Nothing found in any locale does this at catalog scale across Finnish, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Dutch, Portuguese and Italian; Twinkl's dual-language packs are vocabulary aids and TPT's bilingual market is Spanish-English only ([Twinkl dual language](https://www.twinkl.com/resources/eal-inclusion-teaching-resources/new-starter-eal-inclusion-teaching-resources/dual-language-inclusion-teaching-resources); [TPT bilingual](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse?search=bilingual+kindergarten)). Messaging apps translate free text but not the attached task — Seesaw's translation is explicitly invisible to students, and TalkingPoints' new at-home-learning product suggests the vendors see the gap ([Seesaw help](https://help.seesaw.me/hc/en-us/articles/42560159628941-How-do-family-members-translate-Messages-into-different-languages); [TalkingPoints](https://talkingpts.org/bts-25/)). The segment is large — **23.4% of English state-primary pupils use EAL**, **29% of Swedish grundskola pupils are entitled to mother-tongue tuition**, **13.7% of Italian primary pupils are non-citizens** ([Bell Foundation](https://www.bell-foundation.org.uk/app/uploads/2025/11/EAL-attainment-2025-Oxford-report.pdf); [Skolverket](https://www.skolverket.se/download/18.1526584f195b2462c74d69/1742542679927/pdf13226.pdf); [Tecnica della Scuola](https://www.tecnicadellascuola.it/qual-e-la-regione-con-piu-alunni-stranieri-dati-mim-2024-25-scarica-il-focus-pdf)). But two facts keep it an alternate. First, no evidence was found that *teachers* pay for this today: the money in the multilingual niche flows to school-licensed diagnostic platforms, municipality-licensed symbol tools and district-paid messaging, and European teachers expect newcomer material to be free from the state agency ([multilingual niche note, headline verdict](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/multilingual_niche.md)). Second, the home languages of the largest newcomer cohorts — Arabic, Somali, Ukrainian, Turkish, Dari, Tigrinya, Polish, Romanian — are not among the site's eleven, so the pairing serves bilingual-European and international-school homes, not the refugee-driven cohorts that dominate the statistics. The right use of this idea is as a **bundled feature of the Level Set** (a "print the support level in the home language" toggle and the parent note), which raises retention and gives international schools a compliance argument, rather than as a separate paid product.

Now the honesty about evidence. Every external fact in this report came from search-result summaries, because page fetching was blocked for every domain during the research; prices and quotes were read from a search engine's description of a page, not the page itself, and should be spot-checked before being used in marketing copy. The researchers' search budget was exhausted mid-way in all five workstreams, which leaves specific holes. **Spain, Italy, Portugal/Brazil, Denmark, Norway and Finland** have essentially no country-specific evidence on what teachers pay for or on paid differentiation products; for those locales the report relies on the pain-point surveys (which do exist for ES, IT, FI, and partially DK/NO) and on inference from the German and Swedish patterns. Brazil is a particular gap given the notes' own forecast that pt-BR and es would carry ~40% of new-batch clicks: the only Brazilian signal is that Nova Escola sells post-pandemic "recomposição" materials, which is consistent with demand for levelled practice but is one data point ([Nova Escola](https://novaescola.org.br/fichas-de-recomposicao-de-aprendizagem)). **Several price points were never captured**: Worksheet Crafter's individual licence, Twinkl's per-tier prices outside the US and Germany, Heggerty's list price, Grundschulmaterial.de, Gynzy — so the "teacher wallet band" is inferred from clustering, not from a stated survey threshold. **No verbatim teacher-forum quotes** were retrieved for differentiation ("how do you make three versions") from Reddit, Lehrerforen or forums-enseignants; the "differentiation takes so much time" line is a single interview quote, and the 2-3 hours per lesson figure is a vendor estimate. **No conversion data** exists for any specific meter setting, for sequenced-versus-standalone content at equal price, or for the September-versus-January lift; the recommendation that sequence and record convert rests on which products *survive and are priced per classroom-year*, not on a controlled comparison. Finally, the site's own recorded numbers stop in July 2026: the operator's "~300 clicks a day, a few paid subscribers" exists nowhere in the repository, no wall-hit or pricing-page-visit counts were ever exported, and the last recorded proportion — 78% of clicks landing on topic hubs and almost none on the 30,000-plus landings — predates both the paywall and the September batches ([site audit §6](../research_notes/Paid%20content%20type%20for%20K3%20teachers/site_audit.md)). Before committing the build, the cheapest and most valuable piece of evidence the operator can add is a Search Console export by page type and locale for August-September 2026 and the Umami counts for `meter-wall-shown` and pricing-page visits; together they would say which locales and which surfaces the first Level Sets should target, and whether anyone is currently reaching the wall at all.

## Conclusion

The deeper finding is that the site has been selling *access* in a market that pays for *arrangement*. Fifty thousand worksheets in eleven languages is a genuine asset, but as a flat library it competes with free AI on the one axis where free AI is now adequate — a single generic page — and it hides the two things that make the catalog unusual: that every item can be regenerated at a chosen difficulty, and that every item exists identically in eleven curricula. Level Sets turn both into something a teacher can see on a Monday morning: the three sheets on the desk, the check that told her who gets which, and the grid she fills in afterwards. That is the same "it gives me my time back" justification that ESGI, Boom and the no-prep bundle sellers all trade on, delivered without student accounts and without hand-authored filler, because the generators and the native panels the site already runs produce the sets.

The build is smaller than the site's recent batches — twenty to thirty sets in two locales is a semester's slice — but it changes the business more than any batch has, because it moves the wall from the eleventh play to the second level and gives the returning teacher a reason to renew (next week's set) rather than a reason to complain. The evidence supports that direction firmly and the specifics loosely; the holes in Spain, Italy, Brazil and the Nordics, and the missing funnel numbers, are exactly the questions a first small slice will answer with real purchases instead of search summaries.
