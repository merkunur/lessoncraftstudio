## The skill
Compare two fractions of the same whole by size of the pieces (same numerator) or count of pieces (same denominator).

## The apparatus and its invention
THE CUTTING BOARD. Two identical bars (one whole, visibly the same length) lie on one board. The task gives two fractions. For each bar the child slides a knife-count slider to the denominator — the bar is cut into that many equal pieces, and the pieces visibly shrink as the number grows — and taps the numerator's worth of pieces to lift them. With both amounts lifted, the child taps the bigger fraction's numeral (a prediction). Then the lifted pieces slide down onto a comparison rail, both rows starting at one line, so the longer row is the bigger amount. Right: the longer row glows and the bubble reads '3/4 is more than 2/4'. Wrong: the rail shows the other one longer; the child taps again, and miss 2 labels each piece with its unit fraction. Different-wholes setting: one bar is shorter; the rail now shows 1/2 of the small bar coming out shorter than 1/4 of the big one, and the round asks the child whether that was a fair comparison (same-whole / not-the-same-whole icons).

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Bigger denominator means bigger fraction (1/8 > 1/4); comparing fractions of different wholes.
- **Pictures:** none — drawn fraction bars (apparatus blocks); no library food, whose uneven shape would contradict 'equal pieces'
- **Settings:** Compare: same denominator / same numerator / mixed · Wholes: always the same / sometimes different
- **Rounds:** Generated pairs with b in 2-8, never equal, ≥ 9 rounds; the bigger fraction is on the left in 40-60% of rounds; same-numerator rounds ≥ 3 so the 'bigger number wins' strategy fails.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G3-319
- **Distinct from:** fraction-equiv finds equal amounts only; Twin Pools / Friendship Bridge compare whole-number groups. The beam is a new comparator, not the Friendship Bridge (which balances counts).
- **Build risk:** Low.
