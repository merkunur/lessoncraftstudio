# Multilingual K-3 niche (international schools, dual-language, DaZ/EAL/newcomer classrooms, heritage-language homes): is there a real, distinct, PAID problem an 11-language same-content site could own?

> Method note: WebFetch was blocked for every domain in this environment; all evidence below comes from WebSearch result summaries (≈50 queries in EN/DE/FR/ES/NL/SV/FI/IT/NO/DA) and is therefore **summary-only** unless marked otherwise. Facts that could not be confirmed are listed under Gaps rather than asserted. The search budget was exhausted before the last ~15 planned queries (Danish/Finnish home-language shares, Snap Core First, Toddle/Seesaw pricing, TPT dual-language seller revenue, keyword volumes) — those are recorded as gaps.

## Headline verdict (for the report-writer)

- **The segment is real and large, but the *paid* problem is not "the same worksheet in two languages".** Across every country searched, the newcomer/EAL/DaZ *content* market is dominated by free public-sector material (Skolverket, LOWAN, éduscol/CASNAV, OPH, Bell Foundation, Mercator) plus a low-cost generalist (Twinkl ≈ £/$/€ 5–11 per month) that already ships ~22-language dual-language packs. Paid products that survive in this niche are **school-paid diagnostics/proficiency platforms** (FlashAcademy, Learning Village £25/mo per 5 pupils, EAL Hub EAL STAR £139/20 licences), **school-paid symbol/visual-support tools** (Widgit Online — municipal licences in Sweden), and **district-paid parent-messaging translation** (TalkingPoints, ClassDojo, Seesaw — free to the individual teacher).
- **What is genuinely unsolved and where an 11-language *identical* catalog is structurally unique:** (a) a *single* task rendered in the school language AND the child's home language with identical layout and the same answer key (Twinkl's dual-language packs are vocabulary/visual aids, not parallel exercise sets; TPT dual-language sellers cover ES/EN only); (b) "how to help at home" / homework instruction sheets in the parent's language that match the *exact* worksheet sent home (messaging apps translate free text, not the attached task); (c) language-light or home-language-rendered *placement* tasks for newcomers (Skolverket's mandatory Step 2 mapping needs an interpreter/mother-tongue teacher — a real bottleneck). All three are plausible but **no direct search-volume or sales evidence was found that teachers *pay* for them today** — the willingness-to-pay evidence is for school-level licences, not individual subscriptions.
- **Purchasing behaviour:** in the US, >90% of teachers spend own money (~$655–$895/yr, 2025) and ~half have bought individual online lessons/units — this is the only segment with proven teacher-paid content buying at scale, and its multilingual sub-segment is overwhelmingly **Spanish–English dual-language** (3,600+ DLI programmes). In Europe the buyer for DaZ/EAL/NT2/S2 material is almost always the **school/municipality** (publisher packs from Finken/Mildenberger/Cornelsen; Widgit municipal licences), and teachers expect newcomer material to be free from the state agency.

## KQ1 — How large is the segment?

### Takeaway
Large and growing everywhere: ~15,000 international schools / 7.4M pupils / 714k staff (ISC 2025); 23.4% of English state-primary pupils use EAL (Jan 2025); ~20% of German under-16s speak mainly a non-German language at home, 38.5% of third-graders do not speak *only* German at home; 29% of Swedish grundskola pupils are entitled to mother-tongue tuition; 13.7% of Italian primary pupils are non-citizens; 12.9% of Spanish pupils are foreign nationals; France schools 88,500 newly-arrived allophones; US has 3,600+ dual-language immersion programmes; Canada ~11.6% of pupils in French immersion. But the *K-3 teacher* headcount within these is not published anywhere I could find.

### Inferences
- Rough order of magnitude of multilingual K-3 classrooms in the 11 target languages: England alone ≈ 1 in 4 primary pupils EAL; DE/SE/NL/IT/ES each 12–30% of primary cohorts have a non-school home language; so the *teachers who face this weekly* number in the hundreds of thousands across the target locales — far larger than the international-school teacher pool (~714k staff worldwide, of which primary/early-years is a fraction).
- The Ukrainian inflow (2022–24) produced a visible step-change in NO/NL/DE newcomer numbers and in Twinkl's Ukrainian dual-language catalogue — demand spikes are refugee-wave-driven, which favours *fast, broad language coverage* over deep curriculum alignment.

### Gaps
- No source gives the number of K-3 (ages 5–9) teachers in international schools; ISC breaks down by staff not by phase. Danish and Finnish home-language shares for primary were not retrieved (budget). Dutch primary-level home-language share (only 15-year-olds found). US DLI programme count post-2021 not updated (one source says "1,000–2,000" — conflicts with 3,600; treat 3,600 as the canvass figure).

## KQ2 — What do DaZ/EAL/newcomer teachers say they lack?

### Takeaway
The documented gaps are (1) training/qualification and standards, (2) diagnostic instruments and differentiated support material, (3) practical ways to *use* pupils' home languages in class, and (4) the mother-tongue interpreter bottleneck for initial mapping. Explicit demand for "the same task in L1 and L2" is implied (Mercator's *Mehrsprachige Unterrichtselemente*, NALDIC dual-text advice, Skolverket Step 2 needing an L1 speaker) but nowhere quantified.

### Cited Findings
- Germany: Mercator finds no nationwide DaZ standards; teachers need "framework specifications, diagnostic instruments and support materials"; SWK recommendations published 29 Jan 2025 — [Deutsches Schulportal / Mercator](https://deutsches-schulportal.de/bildungswesen/mercator-institut-fuer-sprachfoerderung-und-deutsch-als-zweitsprache-daz-unterricht-es-fehlen-qualitaetsstandards-und-ausbildungsangebote/). - Parents: US survey — more than half of teachers concerned about language barriers with ESL parents — [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/survey-more-than-half-of-us-teachers-concerned-about-language-barriers-wit/544191/); 2024 qualitative study of Vietnamese/Burmese parents in Australian primaries: homework/home learning "at times problematic" — [Teaching and Teacher Education 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0742051X24003172); 2025 review: parents facing language barriers cannot follow up homework — [Int. J. Inclusive Education 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13603116.2025.2593562).

### Inferences
- The single most concrete, recurring institutional need is **assessment/placement independent of the school language** (Skolverket Step 2, Bell Home Language Assessment, DaZ diagnostic instruments) — the one place where "same task in 11 languages" is a *structural* advantage rather than a nice-to-have.
- Teachers' stated pain is *time and differentiation*, not "no worksheets exist" — any offer must present as "one click gives me the L1+L2 versions of what I was already going to hand out", not as another library to browse.

### Gaps
- No quantified survey found that asks teachers specifically whether they want L1/L2 parallel task sheets. BiSS, ISB (Bavaria), EAL Academy positions not retrieved. LOWAN newcomer counts for 2024 not found.

## KQ3 — What products serve them today and who pays?

### Takeaway
Content is cheap or free (Twinkl ≈ $9–11/month individually, 22-language dual-language packs; publisher DaZ packs school-bought; state agencies free). Paid, sticky spend sits at **school level** for proficiency platforms (Learning Village from £25/mo per 5 pupils; FlashAcademy quote-only; EAL Hub £29/teacher/yr, EAL STAR £139/20 licences), symbol tools (Widgit Online annual invoice, municipal licences), and messaging translation (TalkingPoints/ClassDojo/Seesaw free to teachers, districts pay). Lalilo killed its free tier (Nov 2025) — a data point that classroom-licence pricing works for individual-teacher-initiated purchases.

## Consolidated answer to the objective

**Is there a REAL, distinct, PAID problem?** Real and distinct: yes — every locale searched documents (a) a large and growing share of K-3 pupils whose home language differs from the school language, (b) teachers who lack differentiated material, diagnostics and ways to use L1, and (c) parents who cannot help with homework in the school language. **Paid: only partly proven.** Money currently flows to (i) school-licensed EAL proficiency/diagnostic platforms and symbol tools, (ii) district-licensed messaging translation, (iii) US teacher out-of-pocket ES–EN dual-language printables. It does *not* visibly flow to European bilingual worksheets, which teachers expect free from Skolverket/LOWAN/éduscol/OPH/Mercator/Twinkl.

**What an 11-language identical-content site could do better than anyone (evidence-backed):**
1. **One task, two languages, same layout, same answer key** — nothing found in any locale does this at catalog scale across FI/SV/DA/NO/NL/PT/IT (Twinkl's dual-language sets are vocabulary aids; TPT is ES–EN).
2. **Newcomer placement / "what does the child already know" tasks that are language-light or rendered in L1** — the Skolverket Step 2 interpreter bottleneck and Bell's Home Language Assessment show institutional demand; buyer = school/municipality.
3. **Parent-facing per-worksheet instructions and "how to help" notes in the home language** — the gap messaging apps leave; buyer = teacher (feature) or school (compliance).

**Where the 11 languages help least:** the home languages of the biggest newcomer cohorts (Arabic, Somali, Ukrainian, Turkish, Dari, Tigrinya, Polish, Romanian) are *not* among the 11 — so the site's pairing covers *bilingual-European/international* homes (e.g. Swedish school × Finnish/Portuguese/German home; international school × any of the 11), not the refugee-driven DaZ/nyanlända/UPE2A cohorts that dominate the statistics above. This is the single most important caveat for the strategy.
