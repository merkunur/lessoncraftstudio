## The skill
Use informational clues one at a time to narrow down and identify a mystery animal.

## The apparatus and its invention
Nine library animals stand on a stage. A curtain hides the mystery animal. Clue cards turn over ONE at a time; after each clue the child taps every animal the clue rules out, and those animals step behind a screen. Only after the field is narrowed can the child pull the curtain cord on the last one. Ruling out an animal the clue does not rule out makes it shake its head (it fits the clue); keeping one that does not fit lets the next clue fail visibly.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Guessing from the first clue, or ignoring a clue (keeping a fish after "it has fur").
- **Pictures:** animals / zoo animals / farm animals / ocean life / birds / reptiles and Amphibians
- **Settings:** Clues: 3 / 5 · Field: 6 / 9 animals
- **Rounds:** ≥ 8 mysteries per setting with clue sets that leave exactly one animal and each clue removing ≥ 1; field positions shuffled.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-343
- **Distinct from:** The Clue Board is a grid deduction about people and things; the curtain is elimination using animal knowledge. No activity does either.
- **Build risk:** Low–medium: clue sentences native per locale.
