So every face that shows people shows them as **arch-topped portrait frames hanging in one soft tree**: a tealSoft crown behind the older rows, a creamDeep trunk standing on a teal ground line under the child's row, and crisp teal relationship lines drawn ON TOP of the tree. The tree is decoration you can read from across the room; the lines are the fact. Portraits are head-and-shoulders busts, never full bodies, so the page carries no height at all.

## 1 Page concept (base)

**"Mia's family tree: find each person, write the number."** One tree fills the upper two thirds: three rows of arch-topped portrait frames (grandparents in the crown, parents on the branches, the child row on the trunk), joined by the conventional couple bar and descent lines. Mia's frame (the ego) is the only one with a name: a coral frame, a coral star, a name plate. Every other frame carries a teal number badge 1..5 in an arbitrary order. Under the tree, five kin-word chips (mom, dad, grandma, grandpa, brother), each with an empty numeral box; the child writes the number of the person the word names. From across the room a teacher sees one tree with six calm faces in it and five words below: one apparatus, one move (word → relation → person), repeated five times. Top quality because: the portraits are big (104 × 120 frames, K floor 56), identical in size (no size cue to confuse with age), and legible without colour (age = hair tone + proportions, sex = hair reach, both measured by the gate); the only coral on the page marks the ego and the answer boxes, so the eye goes Mia → lines → person; the page is pencil-first (five numerals, 56 px boxes) and 60+ px of paper stays empty.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- **No kin word anywhere in the stage.** The only text inside `[data-ws-content].family-stage` is the ego's name plate and the badge numerals (gate: no literal from `words[loc]` inside the stage; poison: a word printed under a frame must FAIL).
- **The answer lives only in stamps:** chip row `data-lcs-kin="<wordKey>"`, box `data-lcs-answer="<badge n>"`; frames `data-lcs-person data-lcs-path data-lcs-sex data-lcs-age data-lcs-badge`. The gate re-derives each answer from the GRAPH (`persons.filter(p => kin[loc][p.path] === wordKey)` → exactly one → its badge), never from the stamp it is checking.
- **Relations are drawn, not implied by position:** the "no person stands above someone they do not descend to" rule (§5 `familyTree`) means a child cannot read "the one above mom is her mother" by column; they must follow the line. Badge numbers are assigned by `rng.shuffle` and re-drawn until the badge sequence read top-down, left-right is NOT monotonic in generation.
- **Visibly wrong to the teacher:** the teacher reads the number in each box against the badge in the tree; a box with the grandpa's number next to "dad" is wrong at a glance, and the two most common errors (mom ↔ grandma, confusing generation; mom ↔ dad, confusing sex) point to frames that differ in hair tone or hair reach, both of which survive a mono copy.
- **Two same-sex parents** (if the operator enables them in `allowShapes`): the figures are drawn, the word `mom`/`dad` is simply never asked on that page (pedagogy rule 4); nothing on the page marks the family as different.

**NEW `primitives/family-tree.js`** (layout + connectors + the tree skin; the only place geometry is derived):

`familyTree({ persons, frame:{w, h}, labelH = 0, coupleGap = 28, sibGap = 24, sepGap = 16, rowGap = 32, skin:'tree'|'none', w = 675 }) → { svg, nodes:[{id, x, y, w, h}], width, height, trunkX }`

- **Rows** = generation from the path (grandparents 0, parents and their siblings 1, ego row 2). Node box = `frame.w` × (`frame.h` + `labelH`) (F3 adds its 44 px name box under each frame, node width = max(frame.w, 130)).
- **Placement rules (in order):** (1) ego row centred under its parents' couple midpoint at pitch `node.w + sibGap`; a single parent's children centred under that parent. (2) a parent's siblings (aunt/uncle) stand on the OUTWARD side of that parent at pitch `node.w + sibGap`; their children (cousins) centred under them in row 2. (3) a grandparent pair's INNER member stands directly above its child (or above the midpoint of the sibship when the child has siblings); the pair extends outward. (4) **no person stands above a person they do not descend to:** a spouse from outside the lineage is pushed outward until its frame's x-range clears every ancestor frame above it by `sepGap`. (5) the whole bbox is centred in `w`; throws if it exceeds `w`.
- **Connectors (teal 3 px, round caps, drawn AFTER the skin, BEFORE the frames):** couple bar horizontal at frame mid-height between the two inner frame edges; descent line from the couple-bar midpoint (or a single parent's frame bottom centre) down to a sibling bar 16 px above the children's frame tops; drops from the sibling bar into each child's frame top centre. A single-parent family draws a plain descent: no empty partner slot, no dangling bar, ever. `grandparent-home` draws the grand pair's descent directly to the ego row (the parents are not drawn and no gap marks their absence).
- **Tree skin (`skin:'tree'`, behind everything, no stroke on the crown):** crown = the union of circles of radius 64 centred on every row-0 and row-1 node centre plus a rounded rect spanning their bbox inflated by 20, rx 60, fill `tealSoft`; trunk = rect 56 wide centred on `trunkX` (the ego row's parent-couple midpoint, or the grand pair midpoint for `grandparent-home`) from the crown's lowest point to the ground line, fill `creamDeep`, teal 2 px outline, two 1.5 px bark strokes; ground line teal 3 px across the stage width + a 7 px `creamDeep` band. Row 2 frames stand in front of the trunk. `skin:'none'` (not used by any face; the gate's control).

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **The plain pedigree chart** (rectangles joined by lines, full-body figures standing in each box, no tree). Rejected: full bodies at three rows cannot keep the K 56 px floor for faces (a 120 px full figure has a ~22 px head), bodies bring HEIGHT onto the page (a grandparent shorter than a parent is correct and reads as "younger"; the comparing-sizes fence), and it answers the "template" query with a chart where every competitor and every harvest string expects a tree.
2. **One family photograph** (all six people standing together in one group picture, each numbered). Rejected: it removes the relationship lines, so "who is Mia's grandma" becomes a guess from age cues (an old woman) instead of a reading of relations, and it cannot carry the Nordic lineage point at all (mormor vs farmor is invisible in a photo). A third idea, a house with three floors, was dropped at once: it implies who lives with whom.

**Chosen: the tree the word names.** It costs one extra background path over the pedigree chart, keeps every relation drawn, gives F5 the shape teachers search for, and makes all six faces one family look (tree / tree key / tree with names / Mia's frame alone / empty tree) without sharing a layout.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Sex legibility (the answer-critical risk for mom ↔ dad, grandma ↔ grandpa, aunt ↔ uncle):** made a measured rule (hair reach vs jaw line, beard only on `m`), not a style choice; plus a **human read-test before the build ships**: print the d2 base in mono at 100 %, three adults each name every frame; any look misread by one reader is removed from `LOOKS`. Only a human eye can pass this; no gate can.
- **Baby sex:** not drawable honestly; resolved by the one-baby-per-page rule (validator 16) so it is never the cue.
- **Greyscale (measured from token hexes, 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B):** ink 54 · teal 80 · coral 150 · grid 192 · tealSoft 230 · coralSoft 233 · creamDeep 234 · white 255. Age cue hair ink 54 vs grid 192 survives any printer. Garment tints (230-255) carry nothing and are allowed to vanish. The crown (230) behind white frames (255) goes faint in mono; the frames keep their teal outline, and the relationship lines (teal 80) stay the strongest marks on the page, which is correct: the lines are the fact. Ego coral frame (150) vs teal (80) differs in tone AND carries a star and the only name plate.
- **Overflow in long locales:** every stack quoted at 722 and 677 (base 659, F1 624, F2 520, F3 530, F4 650 de/fi, F5 684 with the strip dropped at 677); chips and plates measured by the validator in a real render; fi title capped by the shared 3-line rule.
- **Pencil space:** base numeral boxes 64 × 56; F1 48 × 44; F3 name boxes 130 × 44 with school lines; F4 slots 150 × 36; F5 frames 132 × 124 draw area. Nothing to cut; no cut lines.
- **9 px floor:** nothing under 16 px of text anywhere; badge numerals 20 px.
- **Tree clutter at K:** the crown has no leaves, no texture and no outline; if the critic's mono print shows it competing with the lines, the fallback is `skin:'none'` on the base only (a one-flag change, the gate's control already renders it).
- **What QA lint catches:** overflow, footer intrusion, font < 9, off-palette hex, a blank page (stamps `data-ws-content`). **What only the type gates catch:** kin word inside the stage, answer re-derived from the graph AND from the drawn lines, frame overlap, the ancestor-column rule, hair-reach per look, one baby. **What only a human eye catches:** whether a five-year-old reads the busts as the right sex and generation, whether the arch frames read as "pictures of people" and not windows, whether the crown reads as a tree.
