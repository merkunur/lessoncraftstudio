## Boundary

This page is NOT G1-202 `where-animals-live` (a word-bin sort into land / water / AIR; never three bins named land/water/air, never its titles), NOT G2-318 animal-fact-file (+ G2-339..343: no fact card, no riddle, no compare-two-animals), NOT K-209 / K-204 (farm / pet vs wild), NOT `what-animals-eat.json` (plant eater vs meat eater), NOT the animal-life-cycles family G1-377 (+ faces), NOT plants G1-376 (plant needs), NOT maps G1-379 (top-view plan symbols; my windows are SIDE CROSS-SECTIONS, the opposite projection). It owns three things: ECOLOGICAL PLACES (ocean, forest, pond/lake, polar, savanna, rainforest, meadow, mountain; per-locale set), animal HOMES (nest, hive, web, burrow, anthill, lodge) and ADAPTATION. **Visual signature: every place and every home is drawn as a museum diorama seen through a window, cut open along a surface line so the child sees above AND below it** (the sea under the waves, the pond bowl under the reeds, the soil and roots under the forest floor, the ice under the waterline, the tunnels under the anthill, the dry chamber inside the beaver lodge). No other family draws a cross-section of a place.

## 1 Page concept (base)

**"The Habitat Museum."** From across the room a teacher sees a wall of four framed diorama windows (2 x 2), each with a coral letter disc in its corner and a name plaque on its bottom rail like a museum label, and under the wall a "specimen drawer" of eight animal cards, each over an empty letter box. The windows are EMPTY places (no animal drawn inside, per the pedagogy panel); the animals wait in the drawer to be sent home. The child looks into a window, reads the place (waterline, ice, acacia, giant leaves), finds each animal's place and writes that window's letter under it.

Why this is top quality: ONE focal apparatus (the wall), the rest is quiet cards; the cross-section makes each place legible from its structure, not from a word, so pre-readers can do it and readers get the vocabulary from the plaque; generous white sky in every window (half of each diorama is paper) keeps the page calm and saves toner; pencil-first (one letter per card, big dashed box); coral is reserved for the four letter discs, so the only colour on the wall is the thing the child writes.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- The page prints the places (windows + plaques + letters) and the animals; the letter under each animal lives ONLY in `data-lcs-answer` of an empty `blankNumeralBox` (m: stamps the attribute, renders empty). Nothing inside a window depicts an animal (the primitive draws no animal and no home; the tile gate asserts this by a whitelist of part ids).
- **Uniqueness by the bank, not by the picture:** `data/b6/habitats.js` stores per animal `{theme, noun, place:'<id>', alsoIn:['<id>'…], picOpened:true}`. An animal is eligible on a page IFF exactly ONE of `[place, ...alsoIn]` is among the page's windows. Multi-place animals (fox, owl, bat, duck, seal, frog, otter, walrus-with-ocean, narwhal-with-ocean, hedgehog-with-meadow) are tagged `alsoIn` so the composer can only use them when their other places are absent.
- Each window receives exactly `perWindow` animals; `verify(page)` re-derives every box's answer from the bank (`place` of `data-lcs-noun` → the letter of the window stamped `data-lcs-habitat=<place>`), asserts the eligibility rule for every card, `perWindow` per letter, the drawer-order rules, the absence of text / `<image>` inside every window svg, and every box empty.
- A wrong answer is visibly wrong to the teacher: the key is a four-way letter; a penguin with "C" (savanna) is wrong at a glance because the teacher sees the C window's acacia.
- Pooled tell check (the nt10-E lesson): over 400 seeds per locale the gate measures the share of each letter per drawer POSITION (must sit within 25 +- 8 %) and the share of "same letter as the card above" (must be <= 30 %).

## 6 Locale slot structure

- **Habitat set = data** (`data/b6/locales/habitats.<loc>.json`): `places:[id…]` in the order the native panel wants (own country first), `plaque:{id: literal}`. - **Plaques:** Baloo 2 700 17 ink, one line, max 280 px (+40 % reserve built in: the en longest "rainforest" is 10 chars ~ 95 px). Place names are nouns in citation form (de keeps the capital: Wald; fi nominative: metsä) printed ALONE on a plaque, so no case or article is ever computed.

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **Emblem tile grid (the pedagogy panel's literal page):** four small square emblem tiles A-D + eight animals. Rejected as the visual concept: an emblem (waves, a tree) is a SYMBOL of a place, and two emblems (waves vs pond ripples, tree vs big leaf) collapse into each other at 100 px in greyscale; the cross-section carries the difference STRUCTURALLY (a water band vs a bowl in land; a light scalloped crown vs a dark leaf wall) and teaches that a habitat has an above and a below, which is exactly what F6 then asks the child to fill.
2. **One panoramic transect** (mountain → forest → meadow → pond → beach → ocean in one continuous 639 x 300 cross-section, zones lettered): the most "museum" of all and a single focal object, but zones TOUCH, so every edge animal (heron, frog, otter, seal) becomes a second right answer, polar and rainforest cannot sit in one transect, and the per-locale sets would need one transect drawing each. Kept only as a future poster, not a worksheet.

**Recommendation: the Habitat Museum (windows + drawer).** It keeps the panel's move (4 places, 8 animals, write the letter) and its uniqueness rules, while giving the family one drawing language (every place and every home is a cut-away) that is legible in greyscale, locale-neutral (no words inside any drawing), reusable at three sizes (300 / 196 / 150 and 639 for the report) and extensible to the Nordic mountain / meadow set without new code.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Two right answers (the biggest risk):** multi-place animals are tagged `alsoIn` in the bank and are only eligible when their other places are absent; near pairs capped at one per page; F3 strangers only from the `far` list; the polar tile NEVER claims penguins and walruses share a place (plaque says "polar" / the panel's word; G2+ copy names Arctic / Antarctica per animal); whales never classified as fish (ocean is only a place). The native panel re-checks every `place` per country (a Nordic child's "sjö" animals).
- **Picture named differently:** the duckling (`birds 2/duck`) may be called "chick"; the seal pup is white; the gazelle and antelope are the same drawing (only one in the bank). Each is a panel check with the picture open.
- **Overflow in long locales:** plaques 280 px one line (15 px fallback once, then re-author); home names 144 px, 2 lines; F4 sentences 3 lines at 405 px; F6 labels one line at 639. Every stack summed at 677.
- **Greyscale:** the tiles carry identity by outline first and value second (gate measures the near pairs); coral appears only in letter discs (white letter on coral, the plant-tag precedent) and in the child's marks; the dashed coral boxes print as mid-grey dashes. Rainforest is intentionally the darkest window; its letter disc sits on a tealSoft sky and gets a 2 px white halo so it survives toner.
- **Pencil space:** letter boxes 64 x 44 (base), 56 x 44 (F4); F3 crosses on 96 px cards; F5 circles around 96 px chips with 10 px gaps (a circle may overlap a neighbour; acceptable, the teacher reads the centre); F6 lanes glyphH 24 (G2 floor).
- **No cut lines** (nothing is cut on any face).
- **9 px floor:** smallest text 15 px.
- **What the QA lint catches:** overflow, footer intrusion, font floor, palette, blank page. **What the family gates catch:** eligibility / uniqueness, position and letter tells (pooled), tile and home near-pair separation on the render, no animal / text inside a drawing, feature-span count, open boxes empty. **What only a human eye catches:** whether a six-year-old reads the pond as a pond and not a puddle at 196 px, whether the acacia reads as a savanna tree, whether each opened picture is named the same way in each country, the plaque word register (Teich vs Weiher), and the B&W print of the rainforest letter disc (engineer prints base in en and fi, F2, F3, F4 on a mono laser at 100 %).
