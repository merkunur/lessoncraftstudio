## 5 Quality verdict

I would print this for my class, and the base and the sort page would be the ones I reach for in week one of a senses unit: five big objects, five pink organs, one line each, and a ten-picture sort whose bins carry both the verb and the organ so my non-readers are not locked out. What would embarrass me is the nose: at chip size it is a red cone, and if a child circles the ear for a rose because the nose did not read, the sheet, not the child, failed; the fixed strip order and the 46 px chip are the honest mitigation, and I want to see one mono print before the wave. The second embarrassment was avoided only by measuring: the "I can smell ..." page the design drew would have shipped a 327 px "Ich kann schmecken:" across a 430 px line with a drawing box beside it, and a Grade 1 child would have had 100 px left to write in; the bare present on a 560 row is plainer and correct. I am content that every printed word on the type is one of ten literals per locale, that cheese, sun and snowflake can never appear here by construction, and that the 45 pictures were opened by name; I am less content that the taste pool leans on sweets (candy, lollipop, cookie, cake, cupcake, chocolate) and a national panel may want a lemon-and-honey balance the composer does not enforce. Nothing on any face prints an answer, no page pairs two flowers, no stack exceeds the fi budget, and the six titles change the child's act each time; the type is buildable in all 11 locales with 66 hub rows.
