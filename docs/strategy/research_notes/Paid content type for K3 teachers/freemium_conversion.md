# Freemium conversion in teacher-content sites (2022-2026) — evidence note

**Scope / method note.** WebFetch was blocked for every external domain; all evidence below comes from WebSearch result summaries (≈30 queries; the session's search budget was then exhausted). Every fact is therefore a **search-summary-only fact** unless the source is a primary document whose text was quoted in the summary (Kahoot quarterly PDFs, Wordwall/Kahoot/Lalilo/Quizizz help-centre articles, Twinkl/Heggerty/ESGI pricing pages). Older-than-2022 facts are labelled. Numbers without a source are marked *unknown*.

**The case being judged:** ~300 Google clicks/day landing mostly on individual worksheet pages; free tier = 3 PDF downloads/month + 10 plays/day; Teacher tier $69/yr or $8.99/mo (unlimited play + downloads, "save your own interactive worksheet to a hosted link", collections, play links); only a handful of paying subscribers.

## KQ1 — Realistic visitor→subscriber conversion rates for teacher content sites, and the traffic level at which they become viable

### Takeaway
No teacher-content company publishes a visitor→subscriber rate. The best available proxies are (a) generic freemium benchmarks, where **EdTech is the worst-converting category at ~2.6% of *free accounts* (not visitors)** and "good" self-serve freemium is 3-5%, and (b) scale data from the winners (Twinkl 1.1M individual subscribers on £99M revenue; Kahoot 450K education paid subs), which show that these businesses only work at very large free bases. At 300 clicks/day (~9k visits/month) a metered worksheet site is far below the traffic at which comparable products became viable, and "a handful" of subscribers is consistent with a visitor→paid rate an order of magnitude below even EdTech's poor benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Freemium self-serve: 3-5% free-to-paid is "GOOD", 6-8% "GREAT" (Canva named as fitting this model); freemium with sales-assist 5-7% good / 10-15% great; data from 1,000+ products (Lenny Rachitsky + Kyle Poyar/OpenView) — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion) *(summary-only)*
- "Education/EdTech has the lowest average [freemium→paid] at 2.6% among major SaaS categories"; range runs 2.6% (EdTech) to 5.8% (RegTech); EdTech *free-trial*→paid is much higher at ~24.8% — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/); [Userpilot trial benchmarks](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/) *(summary-only; these are vendor blog compilations, not audited data)*
- An alternate compilation gives EdTech freemium 5-8% and trials "as high as 22%" — [Geneo](https://geneo.app/query-reports/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks) *(summary-only; conflicts with Userpilot's 2.6% — treat both as rough)*
- Twinkl: subscriber base "grew by more than 20 per cent to 1.1 million individual users and approximately 17,000 schools in 2024/25"; parent Wild Peak Holdings turnover £98.9m (year to 30 Apr 2025) vs £79m in 2023/24; "triple-digit" subscriber growth in emerging markets — [Insider Media](https://www.insidermedia.com/news/yorkshire/milestone-year-for-educational-publisher-as-turnover-nears-100m-disney-partnership-set-to-support-further-growth) *(summary-only)*
- Twinkl Ltd 2024 revenue £78.6m, operating profit £23.8m; Companies House no. 07201458 — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/legal-entities/united-kingdom/twinkl-ltd/__waC1Zr7rEycnpTv1MiqMCIv_jHo6q1qgYGG7zqb9F2Q); [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07201458) *(summary-only)*
- Twinkl claims "more than six million customers" (free + paid, not a subscriber count) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinkl) *(summary-only)*
- Kahoot Q1 2023: 1,345,000 paid subscriptions total, of which Education 450,000, Commercial 595,000, Consumer 300,000; Q2 2023 revenue >$41M, +14% YoY — [Kahoot Q1 2023 report](https://kahoot.com/files/2023/05/1Q23_Kahoot_quarterly_report.pdf); [Kahoot Q2 2023 report](https://kahoot.com/files/2023/08/2Q23_Kahoot_quarterly_report.pdf) *(primary PDF, quoted via summary)*
- Kahoot was taken private (Goldman Sachs AM, General Atlantic, KIRKBI) and delisted from Oslo in January 2024, so 2024+ subscription data is not public — [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/strategy-operations/k-12-dealmaking-kahoot-acquired-by-investor-group-that-includes-goldman-sachs/2023/07) *(summary-only)*
- Education.com (at IXL acquisition, Nov 2019 — **older than window**): "more than 23 million teachers, parents and homeschoolers", 30,000+ resources — [Education.com press release](https://www.education.com/about/press/ixl-acquisition-2019/) *(summary-only)*
- Teach Starter: acquired by Tes Global 1 Feb 2023; no public subscriber/revenue data found — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teach_Starter) *(summary-only)*
- Boom Learning: "over 500,000 decks" created by independent authors; "more than 12 million students" have used Boom Cards; no member/revenue data — [Boom help centre](https://helpcenter.boomlearning.com/what-is-boom-learning) *(summary-only)*
- TPT (**pre-window context, still relevant**): 185,000+ sellers made a sale in the last 12 months; 300+ sellers have cleared $1M lifetime; acquired by IXL 2 Mar 2023 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_Pay_Teachers); [EdSurge 2022](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2022-11-28-why-did-we-stop-hearing-about-the-teachers-making-millions-on-teachers-pay-teachers) *(summary-only)*

### Inferences
- The published benchmarks measure **registered free users → paid**, not Google visitors → paid. A worksheet-page landing visitor first has to become a registered free user (rate *unknown*; on content sites it is typically single-digit %), so a visitor→paid rate for a metered worksheet site would plausibly sit at 0.1-0.3% even if the account→paid step performed at EdTech's 2.6% benchmark. At ~9,000 visits/month that is roughly 9-27 new subscribers/month *if* the funnel performed at benchmark. "A handful" total therefore signals a funnel that is well below benchmark, not merely a traffic problem — though traffic is also an order of magnitude below any comparable that reached viability.
- Twinkl's ratio (≈1.1M paying individuals out of ≥6M "customers") is ~18% if "customers" = registered users, which would be far above generic benchmarks; it more likely reflects that Twinkl counts only engaged accounts. Treat as indicative that the winners run *very* large funnels rather than as a benchmark.
- Every comparable that publishes numbers monetises at a scale of hundreds of thousands to millions of registered users; none started from single-worksheet SEO landings with a hard meter.

### Gaps
- No primary visitor→subscriber conversion rate was found for Twinkl, Education.com, Teach Starter, Wordwall, Kahoot, Canva, Boom, Nearpod or Quizizz/Wayground; all are private or stopped disclosing.
- Twinkl's Companies House filings themselves were not readable (fetch blocked); figures above are press/aggregator summaries of them.
- No source was found for the traffic level at which any of these products "became viable".

## KQ2 — Metered paywalls in edtech: do "N per period" meters convert; Education.com, IXL post-acquisition, Wordwall's 5-resource cap, Quizlet's 2022-23 paywall, Kahoot's licence shift; what teachers say

### Takeaway
Meters are common but the evidence on them is almost entirely qualitative and negative-toned: they generate complaints (Quizlet, Kahoot reports, Twinkl "hidden download limits", Education.com reducing its free quota), and the products that keep growing pair the meter with something else (Twinkl's PlanIt/scheme depth, Wordwall's *create* cap that still lets teachers *play* unlimited community content, Kahoot/Quizizz's move of the real value to school licences). No source reports a metered download wall converting well on its own; Education.com's free quota has been cut over time (10 → 3 → reportedly 2/month), which suggests the meter was tightened to force conversion rather than that it converted at a generous setting.

### Cited Findings
- **Education.com meter**: free members restricted to "three resources per month"; Premium "$15.99 per month, or $9.99 per month if you pay annually"; teacher membership "supports up to 35 students"; Premium includes a Progress Tracker that "shows your students' proficiency in core skills"; "Most Premium members report saving 1-2 hours on planning per week" (company claim) — [Education.com blog 2020](https://blog.education.com/2020/11/12/the-benefits-of-a-premium-education-com-membership/) (**pre-window**); [Learnopoly review 2025](https://learnopoly.com/education-com-review/); [Teach Simple review](https://teachsimple.com/blog/reviews/education-com/) *(summary-only)*
- Education.com's free quota has been tightened over time: an older source says "up to 10 free printable worksheets each month"; a Slickdeals thread reports "a maximum of 2 downloads per month with a free account" — [Jill Cataldo](https://jillcataldo.com/free-downloadable-worksheets-from-education-com/) (older); [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/18496630-education-com-2-free-pre-k-through-8th-grade-printable-educational-worksheets-per-month-math-english-science-social-studies-foreign-language) *(summary-only; dates of the changes not established)*
- IXL acquired Education.com 18 Nov 2019; no public statement on post-acquisition premium changes was found — [IXL press PDF](https://www.ixl.com/assets/company/IXL_Acquires_Education_com.pdf); [EdSurge](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2019-11-18-ixl-learning-buys-education-com-to-add-more-learning-games-and-activities) *(summary-only; pre-window)*
- **Wordwall meter**: Basic (free) accounts can create "up to five activities", edited unlimited times; deleted activities still count ("lifetime creation limit"); teachers "can play and use hundreds of games shared by other teachers on the Community Page for free"; Standard/Pro give unlimited creation — [Wordwall help: Is Wordwall Free?](https://wordwall.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360015608178--Is-Wordwall-Free); [Wordwall help: Why can I not create more activities?](https://wordwall.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015616137-Why-can-I-not-create-more-activities); [Wordwall price plans](https://wordwall.net/price-plans) *(primary help-centre text via summary)*
- **Quizlet paywall**: on 1 Aug 2022 Learn and Test modes went behind Quizlet Plus after "five free practice rounds in learn mode and one practice test"; Plus $35.99/yr; the paywall "alienated its loyal user base and hurt its reputation"; teachers with Quizlet Teacher can give their students full features free — [Medium/Maxwell Tan](https://medium.com/@maxtan0626/navigating-quizlets-controversial-changes-afeb97aafd1e); student-paper opinion pieces: [Bloomington South Optimist](https://www.bloomingtonsouthoptimist.org/17407/opinion/quizlets-paywall-hurts-the-students-it-claims-to-help/), [NT Daily 2024](https://www.ntdaily.com/opinion/quizlet-s-paywalls-place-priority-on-profits-over-pupils/article_848d54c6-4eca-11ef-b6bd-bba8eff900d3.html) *(summary-only; opinion sources)*
- **Kahoot reports paywall (Sept 2023)**: teachers "denied access to reports they had previously been able to see", now requiring an upgrade; complaints of "no warning", of already-paid packages being changed; one teacher: "really ridiculous to have to pay to see the positions and results of all the participants" — [Kahoot community thread](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/20896401687827-Reports-Now-require-a-paid-subscription) *(primary forum via summary)*; BBB reviewers "misled by the free trial terms", features "locked behind paid plans without clear disclosure" — [BBB reviews](https://www.bbb.org/us/tx/austin/profile/games/kahoot-edu-inc-0825-1000192927/customer-reviews) *(summary-only)*
- **Kahoot licence structure**: free "Kahoot! Go" for teachers with a player cap; school/district Standard $15/teacher/mo (min 3 licences, annual) up to 800 players, Pro $25/teacher/mo up to 2,000 — [Kahoot school plans](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/); [Wooclap pricing overview](https://www.wooclap.com/en/blog/kahoot-pricing/) *(summary-only)*; in 2021 "the most paid subscriptions of Kahoot were from businesses rather than schools" — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-kahoot-make-money/) *(summary-only)*
- **Quizizz/Wayground**: free Basic caps live sessions at 30 participants (per session, not per day), 3 question types, no standards-mastery reports; from 15 July 2025 all "Free-For-Schools" users moved to the Starter plan; rebranded Wayground late 2024 — [Quizizz help: Free-For-Schools is evolving](https://support.quizizz.com/hc/en-us/articles/29068288320409-Free-For-Schools); [VidQuiz](https://vidquiz.com/blog/is-quizizz-free-quizizz-pricing-explained); [Makerstations](https://www.makerstations.io/quizizz-pricing/) *(summary-only)*
- **Nearpod**: free Silver = 40 students/session, 100 MB; Gold $159/yr (75 students, ~1 GB); Platinum $397/yr (90 students) — [Nearpod blog](https://nearpod.com/blog/why-is-nearpod-free/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/122875/Nearpod/) *(summary-only)*
- **Twinkl complaints** on Trustpilot cite "misleading advertising about resources and hidden download limits in fine print", plus a convoluted cancellation flow and post-cancellation charging (Aug 2025 → Jan 2026 example) — [Trustpilot twinkl.co.uk](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/twinkl.co.uk) *(summary-only)*
- Twinkl pricing (US): monthly from $10.00; annual $89.98 vs $108 for 12 monthly payments; PlanIt requires the "Extra" tier — [TwinklCares](https://twinkl.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/19162663770525-How-Much-Is-a-Twinkl-Subscription); [Twinkl PlanIt help](https://twinkl.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360006840038-Twinkl-PlanIt) *(summary-only)*

### Inferences
- The meters that survive are **create/host caps** (Wordwall: 5 creations; Kahoot/Quizizz/Nearpod: live-player caps) rather than **consume caps**: they let a teacher *use* the product with a class unlimited, and charge when the teacher wants to *own* or *scale* something. A "3 downloads/month + 10 plays/day" meter is a consume cap on the very thing that brought the visitor (a single worksheet), and comparable consume caps (Education.com, Quizlet) are the ones with the loudest backlash.
- Education.com tightening from 10 → 3 → 2 free downloads is the signature of a wall that is not converting at a generous setting; its Premium pitch has also shifted toward *Progress Tracker/assignment* features rather than volume, i.e. even the archetype of the metered worksheet library sells a workflow, not more downloads.
- A 10-plays/day meter is unlikely to be *felt* at all by a teacher who lands on one worksheet from Google — the meter only bites the returning heavy user, who is precisely the user most annoyed by it (Kahoot/Quizlet pattern).

### Gaps
- No quantitative conversion data for any meter setting (Education.com 3/month, Wordwall 5, Quizlet 5 rounds) was found.
- No Reddit threads were surfaced by search for "education.com premium worth it" or "wordwall free limit" — results were vendor/affiliate reviews; the teacher-voice evidence is from Kahoot's own forum, Trustpilot and student op-eds.
- IXL's post-acquisition changes to Education.com's meter/pricing: *unknown* beyond the quota reports above.

## KQ3 — "Depth over breadth": do teachers pay more for sequenced/curated/organised content than for large libraries of standalone items?

### Takeaway
The products with the strongest paid pull in K-3 sell **a sequence the teacher runs every day/week** (Heggerty: same-shaped daily lesson in a year-long progression; Raz-Plus: a levelled ladder with books at every level; Twinkl PlanIt: full-lesson packs gated to the higher "Extra" tier; Zaubereinmaleins: ~€20 Materialpakete per topic) and are priced per classroom-year, not per download. TPT's own marketplace guidance names bundles as one of three growth accelerators. Direct A/B evidence (sequenced vs. standalone at equal price) was not found; the inference rests on what the durable paid products are.

### Cited Findings
- Heggerty: teacher guides "$35 to $45 each"; schools buy class sets/site licences; separate K and Grade-1 guides with "progressively harder tasks", "many schools running both levels in sequence"; "A Heggerty lesson follows the same shape every day, which is part of why teachers stick with it"; no consumable workbooks — [ReadFlare](https://readflare.com/articles/phonics-and-decoding/heggerty-phonics); [Heggerty shop](https://shop.heggerty.org/products/kindergarten-phonemic-awareness-curriculum) *(summary-only)*
- Raz-Plus: retail $228-$299 per 1-year classroom subscription (1 teacher, up to 36 students); 3,000+ levelled books, 15,000+ ancillary lesson plans/worksheets/quizzes tied to each book, plus "online data-driven reports that show individual student and class-wide activity and progress" — [The Learning Standard review](https://thelearningstandard.org/apps/raz-plus); [Raz-Plus](https://www.raz-plus.com/); [About Raz-Plus](https://www.readinga-z.com/about-raz-plus/) *(summary-only)*
- Twinkl PlanIt: "everything needed to teach a lesson in one place", full scheme of work (presentation, differentiated activities, home learning, assessment); requires the Extra membership; Twinkl's own pitch is time saved and curriculum alignment shown "on each resource page" — [Twinkl PlanIt help](https://twinkl.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360006840038-Twinkl-PlanIt); [Twinkl blog: worth it for a trainee/ECT?](https://www.twinkl.com/blog/is-a-twinkl-subscription-worth-the-money-for-a-trainee-teacher-or-ect) *(summary-only, vendor copy)*
- Zaubereinmaleins (DE): a Materialpaket "costs around 20€"; a Lehrerforen poster calls the membership fee "almost laughably reasonable" measured against time saved; individual packages now also sold without the intern membership — [lehrerforen.de thread](https://www.lehrerforen.de/thread/28766-zaubereinmaleins/); [Zaubereinmaleins shop](https://www.zaubereinmaleins-shop.de/produkt-schlagwort/lehrer/) *(summary-only; forum date not established)*
- MatematikFessor (DK): a school price of "10,000 kr. per year" is cited; sold as tasks/lessons/courses that let teachers "differentiate and evaluate" and gain "insight into students' mathematical understanding"; a Folkeskolen critique argues teachers could have done the instruction themselves — [Folkeskolen](https://www.folkeskolen.dk/karakterer-laeremidler-matematik/matematikfessor-vores-undervisning-kunne-laerere-selv-have-klaret/814872); [Alinea product page](https://www.alinea.dk/matematik/grundsystemer-og-portal/produkt/matematikfessor) *(summary-only; price source/date unclear)*
- TPT: "the biggest accelerators for growth are a tight niche + strong previews + bundles"; bundles selling for "$150 or more"; income distribution extremely top-heavy ("top ~1% earn more than the bottom 99% combined"; most sellers $0-100/month) — [SEOLumina 2026](https://seolumina.com/blog/how-much-do-tpt-sellers-make-in-2026-real-data-income-breakdown) *(summary-only; secondary blog)*; a seller's income report cites growth to $5,000+/month — [Lindsay Bowden](https://lindsaybowden.com/tpt-income-report/) *(summary-only)*
- Education.com's Premium pitch itself leans on the Progress Tracker ("pinpoint where they need extra help… recommend games and exercises you can assign") rather than on library size — [Education.com blog](https://blog.education.com/2020/11/12/the-benefits-of-a-premium-education-com-membership/) *(pre-window, summary-only)*

### Inferences
- Every K-3 product with a per-classroom-year price point that teachers or schools pay without complaint organises its content as **a ladder or a routine** (levels, daily lessons, a scheme) and attaches **evidence of the child's place on it**. A large flat library priced by volume (downloads/plays) is the shape of the products with the complaint threads.
- The site under review has a flat library of standalone worksheets; nothing on the paid tier is a sequence, a level, a weekly plan or an intervention pathway. That is the structural gap the comparables point to.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence (same content, sequenced vs. standalone) was found; no Teach Starter unit-pricing or Bridge to Reading cost data (search budget ran out before those queries returned).
- TPT bundle economics are anecdotal seller blogs; TPT/IXL publish no category-level sales data.

## KQ4 — Workflow lock-in: which products became indispensable by holding the teacher's data, how they monetise, and is the "record" what turns a free user into a payer?

### Takeaway
Across Boom, Kahoot, Quizizz, Freckle, Prodigy, Education.com and ESGI, **the record/report is consistently the line between free and paid** — free tiers let the child do the activity, paid tiers let the teacher *see what each child did*. The strongest teacher-paid case found is ESGI ($259/yr for K assessment records). But the monetisation direction is mixed: several data-holding products charge **schools** (Seesaw removed individual teacher plans in 2024; Lalilo removed its free tier in Nov 2025) or **parents** (ClassDojo Plus, Prodigy Premium), not teachers. The record is what creates dependence; who pays for it depends on who owns the budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Boom Cards**: Fast Pins (free) give login-free access but "you will not receive student data" and links "expire in 14 days"; paid membership lets teachers "monitor student activity and track performance"; Essential membership $25/yr (unlimited FastPlay, 17 classrooms, 150 students, 5 self-made decks) — [Krafty in Kinder](https://kraftyinkinder.com/how-to-use-the-free-boom-learning-account/); [Boom help: Assigning](https://helpcenter.boomlearning.com/assigning-un-assigning-and-playing-boom-cards); [Your Thrifty Co-Teacher](https://yourthriftycoteacher.com/what-are-boom-cards/) *(summary-only)*
- **Kahoot**: reports moved behind a paid plan in Sept 2023 (see KQ2) — [Kahoot community](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/20896401687827-Reports-Now-require-a-paid-subscription)
- **Quizizz/Wayground**: free reports "won't show standards mastery" — [Makerstations](https://www.makerstations.io/quizizz-pricing/) *(summary-only)*
- **Freckle**: "Many of Freckle's features are and will remain completely free for teachers"; Premium Edition adds "unlimited access to all content and reporting" — [Freckle UK help](https://freckle-uk-help.renaissance.com/hc/en-gb/articles/13774935728923-What-features-of-Freckle-are-free-What-do-I-get-with-Freckle-Premium-Edition) *(primary help text via summary)*
- **Lalilo**: "Since November 12th 2025, there is no free version of Lalilo anymore"; access now Basic / classroom licence / school-district licence (district licence ties placement to Star assessments) — [Lalilo FAQ](https://intercom.help/lalilo/en/articles/5676123-lalilo-differences-and-pricing); [Lalilo help: classroom licence](https://lalilo-help.renaissance.com/hc/en-us/articles/28705366416667-Classroom-license-what-is-it-and-how-to-get-it) *(primary help text via summary)*
- **Prodigy**: free for teachers ("over 800,000 teachers use Prodigy annually at no cost"), full teacher dashboard free; revenue from optional parent Premium memberships, which add in-game content plus parent-side reports (Curriculum Progress report, monthly Report Card) — [Prodigy teachers](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/teachers); [Prodigy blog: premium membership](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/how-to-use-your-premium-membership) *(summary-only, vendor)*
- **ClassDojo**: teacher side "fully free"; ClassDojo Plus is a family subscription ($15.49/mo or $109.99/yr announced Jan 2024) and "does NOT give teachers extra features" — [BeeNet blog](https://beenet.app/blog/is-the-classdojo-app-really-free-what-it-costs-in-2026/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/classdojo) *(summary-only)*
- **Seesaw**: 2024 "removal of individual teacher subscriptions, requiring district-level purchase instead"; free Starter capped at 10 students, a cited frustration — [Capterra Seesaw reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/204060/Seesaw/reviews); [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/web-seesaw/pricing) *(summary-only)*
- **ESGI** (K assessment record): $259/yr, 60-day free trial with "$60 off your first year", 2,500 pre-loaded tests, parent-communication reports — [ESGI pricing](https://esgisoftware.com/pricing/); [ESGI kindergarten](https://esgisoftware.com/how-we-help/for-teachers/kindergarten/) *(summary-only)*
- **Education.com Premium** includes a Progress Tracker of student proficiency (see KQ3) — [Education.com blog](https://blog.education.com/2020/11/12/the-benefits-of-a-premium-education-com-membership/) *(pre-window)*
- Gimkit Pro ($59.88/yr) adds *assignments* "so students can work through question sets on their own time"; group licence at 50 teachers ≈ $20 each — [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/gimkit-pricing-cost); [Gimkit group pricing](https://www.gimkit.com/group/pricing) *(summary-only)*

### Inferences
- The consistent free/paid line is **activity free, record paid**. A play-link product with no per-child record has nothing on the paid side that the free side lacks in kind — only in quantity — which is the weakest possible upgrade proposition.
- Individual-teacher paid tiers for data-holding products are shrinking (Seesaw, Lalilo, Kahoot's minimum-3-licence school plans); the durable individual-teacher price points are small ($25 Boom Essential, $59.88 Gimkit, $69-90 Twinkl) and either give the record or give ownership/creation.
- $259 ESGI shows K-teachers will pay real money individually when the product *is* their assessment record; nothing in the comparables shows them paying $69 for unlimited access to worksheets they can otherwise get 3/month.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the share of upgrades attributable to reports vs. volume in any product.
- Boom membership counts, Seesaw's stated rationale for dropping teacher plans, and any Freckle Premium uptake figures were not found.

## KQ5 — Student-facing use: does letting the teacher assign to students (class codes, no student accounts) raise willingness to pay?

### Takeaway
Class-code / no-account play is the free layer everywhere (Kahoot Go, Blooket free ≤60 players, Quizizz ≤30, Boom Fast Pins, Gimkit Basic) — it drives adoption, not payment. What the same products charge for is the layer *above* free play: larger player caps, **assignments/solo links that persist**, and per-student results. So student-facing use raises willingness to pay only when it is coupled with persistence and a record; on its own it is the acquisition engine that these companies deliberately give away.

### Cited Findings
- Blooket: free account, unlimited question sets, up to 60 live participants; Plus ($2.99/mo billed annually per one source; "$119.88/year" per another — sources conflict) raises live cap to 300, adds analytics and "direct solo-practice links… without needing a game code"; students never pay — [Plisio review](https://plisio.net/education/blooket); [Engleish guide](https://engleish.com/blooket-complete-guide-for-teachers-students-game-modes-join-codes-learning-benefits/) *(summary-only; pricing conflict unresolved)*
- Gimkit: Basic rotates 3 free game modes; Pro opens all 13 at full player count and adds assignments; "the mode catalogue is Gimkit's moat… the reason teachers tolerate the price" — [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/gimkit-pricing-cost); [Gimkit Info](https://gimkitinfo.com/gimkit-pro/) *(summary-only)*
- Kahoot Go free with player cap; paid tiers scale players to 800/2,000 — [Kahoot school plans](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/) *(summary-only)*
- Quizizz free: 30 live participants per session; assignments "not capped the same way" — [VidQuiz](https://vidquiz.com/blog/is-quizizz-free-quizizz-pricing-explained) *(summary-only)*

## KQ6 — Trust and positioning for a small unknown brand

### Takeaway
Teachers' most trusted source is other teachers (81%), far above review sites (39%) or evidence reports (18%); the market leaders back this with crowdsourced ratings (TPT), free samples/trials (Raz-Plus samples, ESGI's 60-day trial), and explicit curriculum-alignment labels per resource (Twinkl). Negative trust signals that are documented in the same market are hidden limits and hard cancellation (Twinkl, Kahoot). No source was found on money-back guarantees or school-invoice options for small vendors.

### Cited Findings

## KQ8 — Applying the evidence: what the case site is missing and which structures convert

### Takeaway
Read against the comparables, the site is charging for the wrong layer: its paid tier is *more of the same standalone content* plus features (play links, hosted links) that the market gives away free, while it lacks every structure the paying comparables share — a sequence/level ladder, a per-child record, and a time-based trial. Its 300 clicks/day of single-worksheet SEO traffic is an acquisition channel of the right kind but at least an order of magnitude too small to monetise via a consume meter at benchmark rates.

### Cited Findings
- (Synthesis of KQ1-KQ7 above; each underlying claim is cited there.)

### Inferences
- **Traffic arithmetic.** ~9,000 visits/month × (visitor→free account, *unknown*, plausibly low single digits %) × (account→paid, EdTech benchmark 2.6% per Userpilot) ≈ single-digit to low-double-digit new subscribers/month *at benchmark*. A handful total implies the account→paid step is far below 2.6% — i.e. the offer, not only the traffic.
- **Meter shape.** "3 downloads/month + 10 plays/day" is a consume cap on the entry content (the Education.com/Quizlet shape that draws backlash and that Education.com has had to tighten repeatedly), not a create/own cap (Wordwall's 5 creations, Kahoot/Blooket player caps) that leaves classroom use free and charges for ownership/scale.
- **Nothing to own.** The paid tier's "save your own interactive worksheet to a hosted link / collections / play links" corresponds to free-tier features at Boom (Fast Pins), Kahoot/Blooket (game codes) and Wordwall (community play). The comparables charge for (a) unlimited *creation*, (b) *persistence* (Boom pins expire in 14 days free; Gimkit/Blooket assignments and solo links are paid), (c) *the record* (Boom/Kahoot/Quizizz/Freckle/Education.com reports; ESGI's whole product).
- **No ladder.** None of the durable K-3 paid products is a flat library; Heggerty, Raz-Plus, PlanIt, Zaubereinmaleins Materialpakete sell a per-classroom-year sequence. The convertible structures the evidence points to are: a levelled/sequenced set per skill (e.g., a 30-week K-1 plan or an intervention pathway) sold as one thing; class-level assignment with a per-child completion/mastery record behind the paywall; and a September time-boxed trial with first-year discount and an easy cancel.
- **Trust.** For a brand found via one search, the comparables' minimum is playable samples (already present), a visible curriculum label per item, peer testimonials/ratings, and no hidden limits — the two most-documented complaints in this market (Twinkl, Kahoot) are surprise walls and hard cancellation.

### Gaps
- All of the above is inferred from comparables; no comparable at exactly this traffic level (~300 clicks/day) publishes its economics, and no source quantifies how much conversion each missing structure would add.
