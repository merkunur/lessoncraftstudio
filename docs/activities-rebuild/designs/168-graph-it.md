## 1. The pitch
"Pip's fence has a spirit-level rail hinged to the number post: lay its free end on a picket's top and slide the post end until the bubble sits in the middle — now the rail is level, its pointer reads that picket's number on the post, and whatever pokes up above the rail is 'how many more'."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **A real spirit level, used the real way.** A long clear rail with a green bubble vial hangs from a brass slider on the number post. Drop its free end onto any picket top and the rail tilts down or up to it — the bubble runs to the low side. Slide the post end up or down (it clicks from tick to tick): the moment the slider is at exactly that picket's height the rail lies **level, the bubble settles in the middle with a soft click and the picket's top board glints**; the pointer on the post reads the number. A rail laid level is honest physics — it tilts at every wrong height. Children lay it on every picket top just to make the bubble settle.
- **Building is pulling planks up.** Each picket rises board by board as you drag its top up (a wooden *tok* per board), like pulling a telescope; in picture rounds each step reveals a library picture (or half of one in the scaled level).
- **Stack and take apart.** Tap one picket, then another, and the first picket's boards **lift off and ride over to stand on top of the second**, the post growing taller to fit — put together, literally. Drag boards off a picket's top and they tumble into Pip's basket — take apart, literally.
- **The earned show**: when a round is right, Pip (library squirrel) scampers along the top rail of the fence from left to right, hopping onto each picket top in turn; the picket he lands on rings its number. No score, timer, streak or lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (2.MD.D.10 + the graph sheets): build a bar graph (and a picture graph) from a tally, a picture list or a data table, one unit per board; read a bar's value against the scale; compare two bars (how many more / fewer), put two together (how many altogether), take some away (how many are left); find the tallest/most; in the scaled level read and build bars where one step means 2, 5 or 10 and pictures where one picture means 2 or 5 (half a picture = half the key).
- Misconceptions and the push-back:
  1. **Reading a bar against the wrong line** (the eye drifts down to the scale and lands a line too high or low) → the rail is a straightedge that can only lie level when its post end is at exactly the picket's height: one line too high or too low and it visibly tilts, the bubble off-centre.
  2. **"How many more" = the taller bar's value** (answering 9 instead of 3) → with the rail resting on the shorter picket, the taller picket's part above the rail turns striped, its boards outlined: only the overhang is "more".
  3. **"Altogether" read off the taller bar, or the bars compared instead of joined** → stacking moves one picket onto the other; the total is read with the rail on the new tower.
  4. **Taking away from the wrong bar / subtracting the wrong way** → boards are dragged off the named picket's top into the basket; the rail then reads what is left.
  5. **Scaled graphs read as one per step** (a bar reaching the 4th line read as 4 when each line is 5) → the post counts by the scale (0, 5, 10, 15 …); in picture graphs the key card ("one picture = 2") sits beside the fence and miss 2 counts the pictures by the key (2, 4, 6, 7 for three and a half).
  6. **Building with gaps or starting a bar above the ground** → pickets grow only from the ground, one board at a time.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag a picket's top** up/down (build rounds): the picket grows/shrinks one plank per unit of drag (snap), a *tok* per plank; in picture fences each unit reveals one picture (scaled level: half a picture, then the other half). Tapping a picket's top adds one plank; long-press removes one.
- **Lay the rail** (drag its free end onto a picket, or tap a picket's cap while the rail is selected): the free end drops onto that top (200 ms) and the rail tilts to it. **Drag the slider** up/down the post: it snaps to every post tick (half-ticks in the by-2 scale) with a tick sound; the rail re-tilts live; at the picket's exact height the bubble centres, a soft click, the top plank glints and the pointer's numeral brightens. With the rail level, parts of taller pickets above it are striped with their planks outlined. A rail left tilted shows no stripes.
- **Stack** (put-together rounds): tap picket A's cap, then picket B → A's planks lift (each 60 ms after the last) and stack onto B as a combined tower (A's planks keep their own label colour band so both parts stay visible); tap the tower to send them home.
- **Take apart** (take-apart rounds): drag planks off the named picket's top into the basket (one plank per drag; they tumble in with a hollow *knock*); tap the basket to put the last one back.
- **Answer**: dial wheels (tap ▲/▼ or drag), True/False tags, or tap a picket (most rounds).
- **Free play**: between rounds the fence is live: the child can build any pickets, stack them, and rail them.
- **Keyboard**: Tab: pickets (↑/↓ add/remove a plank), rail (Enter lays it on the focused picket; ↑/↓ move the slider one tick), stack mode (S then two pickets), basket, dial, tags.
- Sound (optional): tok per plank, click when the bubble settles, knock into the basket, a two-note chime per picket in the success show. TTS (optional): tap a category picture to hear its name.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing done** → the source card glows (build) or the rail's slider pulses (read); bubble: "Pull each picket up to match the card." / "Lay the rail on a picket and make it level." (no miss)
- **Build rounds**
  - miss 1 → each wrong picket wobbles and the rail lays itself level on that picket's top, its pointer showing the number the picket says now beside the card's number (e.g. "6 / 7"), coral; bubble "The acorn picket says 6. The card says 7."
  - miss 2 → the card's tally/picture marks for that category light up one by one and a ghost plank grows on the picket with each, so the child sees one mark = one plank; bubble "One plank for each mark: 1, 2 … 7."
- **Read rounds**
  - miss 1 → the apparatus SHOWS the tool, not the answer: compare rounds — the rail lays itself level on the shorter picket, the overhang stripes appear (planks outlined, unnumbered); bubble "Lay the rail level on the {b} picket. Count the planks that stick up." · total — the two pickets wiggle in turn; "Stack the {a} picket on the {b} picket, then read the top." · take-apart — the basket opens and the named picket's top glows; "Take {k} planks off the {a} picket, then read what is left." · most — the rail lays itself level on the shortest picket, then on the next taller, and so on (600 ms each), stripes showing what still pokes up each time; "Lay the rail level on a picket. If nothing pokes up above it, that is the most." · true/false — the rail lays itself level on the shorter picket; "Count what sticks up above the rail. Is it {by}?"
  - miss 2 → the overhang planks (or the tower's planks, or the planks left) number themselves 1, 2, 3 … in small tags; for the scaled level the tags count by the scale/key (5, 10, 15 … or 2, 4, 6, 7). The answer itself is not shown; bubble "Count them: {list}." / "Count by {scale}s: {list}."
- A new move removes the tags and stripes until the next Check.
- **Success** → Pip scampers across the picket tops, each ringing; bubble: "**There are 2 more leaves than raspberries.**" · "**10 leaves and raspberries altogether.**" · "**5 acorns are left.**" · "**There are most leaves.**" · "**False — there are 3 more acorns, not 2.**" · build "**Your fence matches the tally!**" · scaled "**Each picture is 2, so 1½ pictures are 3 pinecones.**"

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a spirit level that clicks onto picket tops, planks that ride over to stack and tumble into a basket, and Pip running along the fence are toys.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — the rail is exactly the straightedge teachers ask children to use; "how many more" is the overhang, "altogether" is a stack, "left" is planks into the basket — the three problem types of 2.MD.D.10 become three physical moves; scaled graphs bridge to grade 3.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — library pictures instead of drawn icons, calm, no answer in the accessibility tree before reading, works silently on a phone.
4. **The invention in one sentence:** a spirit-level rail that only rests level on a picket's top — it reads the bar's number on the post, and whatever sticks up above it is how many more.

## 14. Critic review (2026-10-17)
Scores before → after: invention 5→5 · fun 5→5 · completeness 5→5 · correctness 4→5 · distinctness 5→5 · reality 4→5
What changed:
