# Landing audit pass 1 — de

1. G2-381: title says 'Forscherblatt: Lebensraum Wald' but the page draws a POND labelled 'Teich' (cattails, water lily). The report layout now picks the first set member with a true word pair (fix round 1) = pond, while the title string is hard-coded to Wald. Landing describes the Teich; fix the title (or make it follow the chosen habitat).
2. G2-381: instruction says 'kreise in jedem Paar das richtige Wort ein' (plural), but the render has ONE pair (nass / trocken); the row config asks chipPairs: 2 but pond has no true hot/cold value so the temperature pair is dropped. The pair also carries no question telling the child what nass/trocken refers to.
3. G2-384, G2-386, G2-387: rendered TITLE says 'in Vereinfachter Ausgangsschrift' but the script tag on the same page says 'Lateinische Ausgangsschrift' and the letterforms are the LA ones (bank exemplarByMode: capitals/words/read = de-la). The {U} title token resolves from the unit axis (de-va), not from exemplarByMode, so one page names two different scripts. Landings name LA (what the body shows); fix the {U} resolution so the title follows exemplarByMode.
4. G2-377/G2-384/G2-385/G2-386/G2-387/G3-401 (face table): the strings table titles still carry the raw token '{U}' ('Großbuchstaben in {U}'); the renders resolve it, so this is only a trap for anything reading titles from the face table.
5. G2-377: the lowercase t in the model ribbon and in the traced 'ttt' row does not look like the Vereinfachte Ausgangsschrift t - it reads as a looped form with the cross-stroke near the baseline, almost like a script A. A German teacher would not recognise it as the VA t; please check the VA font's t glyph against the official model.
6. G2-377: in the i row the traced joined 'iii' groups put three dots close together above linked strokes; at print size they read like 'üi' (umlaut dots) and look very similar to the ü row directly below. The two rows are hard to tell apart.
7. G1-404: title 'Keime stoppen: in die Armbeuge husten' names one habit, but the page has four rows (cough into elbow; soap vs water only; shared cup vs own cup; tissue on the floor vs into the bin) and only row 1 is about coughing. The title undersells the page; suggest 'Keime stoppen: Was hält Keime auf?'.
8. G1-404: row 2 (hands with soap foam vs hands under water only) is the weakest pair - rinsing with water alone also removes some germs, so a child circling it is not simply wrong; the only visible difference is foam bubbles.
9. G1-407: header line reads 'Jedes Tier braucht Nahrung, Wasser und ein Zuhause', but there is no water anywhere on the page - only two headed columns (Nahrung / Zuhause), no water choice. Named apparatus not drawn.
10. G1-407: the correct home sits in the first home slot (position 4) in 3 of 4 rows (beaver lodge, spider web, rabbit burrow); only the bird row has it second - a mild position tell.
11. G1-407 and K-383: the rabbit is drawn as a long-eared orange 'Hase' that most German children will name Hase; a Feldhase does not dig a Bau (it lies in a Sasse) - a classic Sachunterricht trap, and here it is the intended answer (Kaninchenbau). The bank accepts both names. Either draw a clearly smaller short-eared Kaninchen or keep the landing's caveat (it now carries one).
12. G1-406: every animal to cross out is an exotic zoo animal (Zebra in the sea, Löwe in the forest, Papagei at the pond, Affe on the meadow). A child can solve all four rows by 'not a German animal' without reasoning about the habitat; consider at least one native misfit (e.g. Maulwurf at the pond, Fisch in the forest).
13. G1-406 (also G2-380, G2-386, G2-387): the duck is always drawn as a yellow duckling, which German children call 'Küken' - a chicken chick (farm, not pond). On G1-406 this invites a second answer in the pond row. The bank lists Küken as an accepted duck name, which covers naming but not the habitat logic.
14. G1-398: the Biber is filed under Teich; beavers live at streams, rivers and lakes and in German material they are a Bach/Fluss animal - 'Teich' is defensible but thin. The beaver art (furry, no flat scaly tail) also reads as Murmeltier/Hamster to children. The habitat spread is uneven on this draw: Meer 3, Wald 2, Teich 2, Wiese 1 (grasshopper only).
15. G1-403: first card (hand turning the toothpaste cap, curved arrow) can be read as opening (vorher) or closing (danach) - two defensible answers. The three brushing cards are exactly KAI (Kauflächen, Außen, Innen) - good; the landing spells KAI out.
16. G1-403: title says 'vorher, dabei und danach' but the answer chips read 'vorher / beim Putzen / danach' - small wording mismatch.
