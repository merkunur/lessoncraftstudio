# The art brief: every new drawing (operator rulings 2026-11-07)

Read this before drawing. These are the owner's rulings, in their words where quoted.

- "each one of the images you will create should be a masterpiece because they are the moat of the product."
- "they should be simple cartoon images. Adding more details doesn't make them better. It is about the design, style."
- "cute doesn't mean to draw a face to all the objects. It is so stupid and cheap."
- "the most terrible drawings are human and animal drawings. You should put even more effort to make them top quality."
- "Each image you will recreate should get a thorough visual analysis and review."

The 12 trial drawings in `scripts/worksheet-gen/art-src/drawn/` are the accepted standard: "good. This is the standard." Match their
hand, and beat them where you can. The owner's library (`scripts/worksheet-gen/cache/themes-512/<theme>/<noun>@3x.webp`) is the
other reference: study it, never trace or copy it. Its simplest drawings show the target best: bear_2, dog, cat, eggplant, airplane, fish.

## What a good drawing is

A simple, professional cartoon. Few confident lines, strong and appealing shape design, a clear silhouette, big readable forms, one
idea per drawing. Quality is design, not detail: if a line does not help the design, remove it. A child should want to color it, name
it at once, and a teacher should remember it.

## Faces

- Only living creatures and people have faces. An object never does: no eyes, smile or cheeks on food, furniture, vehicles, plants, tools,
  toys that have no face in real life, or on the sun, moon, stars, clouds and weather.
- A thing that really has a face in the world keeps it: a teddy bear, a doll, a snowman, a scarecrow, a jack-o'-lantern, a ghost, a robot, a
  rubber duck.
- The one exception: the 11 Shape Friends keep faces, and each gets its own personality.
- A noun ending in `_friend` is only a file name. `door_friend` is a door, with no face. `robot_friend` is a robot.

## People and animals: the most effort

- Effort goes into proportion, pose, silhouette and expression, never into extra lines.
- Every animal is drawn from that species' real silhouette, in a pose it would really take, with a face that belongs to it. No shared body.
- Every person is a distinct character: their own build, hair, pose and clothes. Two people must never read as one figure in a different hat.
- **Child friendly and cute, always.** Every creature is warm, happy and lovable, the way the library's animals are. Big, soft, friendly eyes with a
  highlight are the house style, and the library's cat, dog, cow and bear show them. Vary their shape, size and spacing to suit the species, but never
  draw grumpy, scowling, sleepy, startled, bulging, slit-pupil or staring eyes, or any expression a five-year-old would not smile back at.
- Keep eyes at the library's scale: lively and friendly, not huge round discs. Do not reuse one ringed-iris eye on every animal; the library's goat, pony and
  cat each have eyes of their own that are still warm and happy.
- **A face obeys the view of the head.** A head in profile shows ONE eye, on the side we see. A head facing us shows two eyes, one each side of the nose or beak. In a
  three-quarter view the far eye is smaller, narrower and tucked behind the bridge of the nose. Two full eyes side by side on a profile head, both on one side of a
  beak or snout, is a terrible face: the owner called it out on the T. rex and the chicken. A muzzle drawn pointing sideways makes the head a profile, so it shows ONE eye; two eyes need a muzzle coming towards us, on the middle of the face. Hanging two eyes beside a side-on muzzle gives a head with no real perspective: the owner said the skunk's eyes "look very strange". When in doubt, draw a clean profile or a clean front view, and avoid three-quarter heads. A neck is as wide as a real neck, never a stick under a big head.
- Simple natural poses come first: standing, sitting, walking, waving, the way the library draws them. A gimmick pose does not make a better drawing.

## Construction: how a body holds together

The owner found obvious faults in drawings that looked fine as a whole: "pig, beaver, mole and moose have very obvious discrepancies." Check every one of these, part by part:

- **The head sits on a neck or on shoulders.** Front legs grow from the shoulders and chest, never straight out from under the head. A head facing us on a side-on body needs its neck and
  shoulder drawn so the two connect. The bear and pig had legs growing out of the head.
- **Each part belongs to its species.** A pig has small soft ears, not a fox's. A moose's antlers grow from the top of the skull, not from behind an ear. Ears, tails, hooves, paws and claws
  are the right kind and join the body at the right place.
- **Every line starts and ends on purpose.** No floating arcs, no stray ticks beside a smile, no line that stops in empty space or runs through another shape. A body contour never stops at
  one side of an object and restarts at the other.
- **What a creature holds, it really holds.** The object passes in front of the body, and the paws close round it. Nothing cuts a body in two.
- **Things rest on what holds them.** Eggs sit down inside a basket, cut off by its rim, and nothing hovers: the owner saw two eggs floating at the edges of the egg basket. Every object has weight, and touches what supports it.
- **The two sides agree.** On a head facing us both eyes match. Legs on one side match in thickness and shape. Feet stand on one ground line.
- **Keep it simple.** Leave out what you cannot construct well. A gentle closed smile is safer and cuter than a big open mouth.

## What the owner sees as "strikingly unnatural and ugly"

Owner, on 13 drawings that had passed review: "when they hold an object the z order is wrong and the object is just behind the hand. Generally arms and legs are unproportional or drawn wrong, some has only one ear". Each of these is a failure:

- **A hand that holds wraps round the object.** Draw in this order: palm, then the object, then the fingers over it, so the object visibly passes through the hand. A lump placed on top of an object, with the object just appearing either side, is the fault the owner named. If you cannot show the grip, change the pose so nothing is held.
- **Two ears, always.** Every animal that has ears shows both. In profile the far ear shows behind the near one. Horns and antlers likewise.
- **Skeleton first.** Before any outline, set the head, spine, shoulders, hips, elbows, knees, wrists and ankles as points. Paired limbs have equal lengths. Then flesh out the body round those points. A limb is an upper and a lower part with a joint, the same thickness on both sides, never a sausage, a stick or a loop.
- **Heads are not discs.** A head is shaped to its species and joins the body through a neck or shoulders. A circle sitting on a body is the look the owner rejects.
- **Bodies have form.** Chest, belly and haunch are shaped, not one plain bag or bell.
- **Match the library's proportions.** Open the library's picture of that animal or a similar person and compare head size, leg length, body length and ear size with yours. If yours differ, yours are wrong.

## What was rejected, so it never returns

One stock face on most drawings. One sitting-bear body for every animal, and one body for every person. Everything assembled from circles,
ellipses and rectangles. A small bear head stuck into a scene (hay bale, carpet, swing, sled). Drawings that do not read: read the name, cover
it, and ask whether a five-year-old would say that word. Full list: `docs/makers/FLAGSHIP-ART-REVIEW.md`.

## The construction sheet (every person and animal)

The owner chose to keep this artist because hand-made art gives us control no other tool has: "I don't need generic images. I need the best and customized for our purpose in a smart way." So a person or animal is built, not sketched, and a tool measures the result.

Plan first, then draw. Before any outline, decide the view, the pose and the joints. Then build the drawing from named parts, one top-level `<g id="...">` per part, back to front, and declare it in a `<desc id="construction">` just after the `<rect>`:

```
<desc id="construction">{"class":"animal","view":"profile","pose":"stand","ears":2,"arms":0,"legs":4,
 "joints":{"shoulder_near":[x,y],"elbow_near":[x,y],"wrist_near":[x,y],"shoulder_far":[x,y],"elbow_far":[x,y],"wrist_far":[x,y],
           "hip_near":[x,y],"knee_near":[x,y],"ankle_near":[x,y],"hip_far":[x,y],"knee_far":[x,y],"ankle_far":[x,y]},
 "steps":[["body"],["head","neck"],["leg-front-near","leg-front-far","leg-hind-near","leg-hind-far"],["ear-near","ear-far","tail"],["eye","nose","mouth"]]}</desc>
```

- **class** animal or person. **view** the head: profile or front, nothing in between. **bodyView** the body, only when it differs: a side-on body with its head turned to us is `"view":"front","bodyView":"profile"`. **pose** stand, walk, sit, jump, hang or swim. **ears, arms, legs** the numbers drawn. `ears` other than 2 needs a reason in `earsWhy`.
- **A second figure in one drawing** has its own sheet, `<desc id="construction-<name>">`, with `"prefix":"gran-"` on all its parts and `"holds":["bag"]` for what it holds. The first sheet's steps cover every part of the drawing.
- **Part names:** `head`, `body` (required), `neck`, `ear-near`, `ear-far`, `eye` (profile) or `eye-near`, `eye-far` (front), `nose`, `muzzle` or `beak`, `mouth`, `tail`, `hair`, and any others you need. Limbs: a person or two-legged animal has `arm-near`, `arm-far`, `leg-near`, `leg-far`; a four-legged animal has `leg-front-near`, `leg-front-far`, `leg-hind-near`, `leg-hind-far`; an insect adds `leg-mid-near`, `leg-mid-far` (joints `hip_mid_near`, `knee_mid_near`, `ankle_mid_near` and the same for far). In a front view, near is the viewer's left. Draw each limb whole, even where a nearer part hides it.
- **A held thing** is three groups in this order: `palm-x`, `held-x`, `fingers-x`, so the object passes through the hand.
- **steps** is the order a child would draw it, 3 to 6 steps, every part exactly once. How to Draw can use it directly, so make it a real lesson: big shapes first, details last.

`art-check` measures all of this and prints CONSTRUCTION faults: ears and eyes against the view, a nose off the middle of the eyes, a head not joined to the body, a joint that is not on its limb, limbs of unequal length or width, upper and lower parts out of proportion, a shoulder or hip off the body, a limb growing from the head, a hold in the wrong order, where the fingers do not close, or where the object does not come out of the hand on two sides, a far limb or far ear drawn over the near one on a side-on figure, an elbow or knee at the edge of its limb, feet off one ground line, a plain disc for an animal's head (a tuft on a disc is still a disc), a person's head outside 24 to 42 percent of their height. Run it with `--rig` so a missing sheet is also a fault. A drawing with any CONSTRUCTION fault is not finished. Passing it proves the build, not the beauty: the drawing must still be lovable.

## Format

- One file per drawing: `scripts/worksheet-gen/art-src/drawn/<noun>.svg`, the exact noun you are given.
- `viewBox="0 0 600 600"`, first element `<rect width="600" height="600" fill="#fff"/>`. The subject fills about 80 to 90 percent of the box and
  nothing touches the edge.
- Only black ink `#141414` on white. Shapes are filled white so nearer shapes hide what is behind them. Solid black only for small accents, such
  as pupils and noses. No gray, gradients, shading, hatching, text, images, filters.
- Hand-written `<path>` data with cubic Beziers. A true circle only where the real thing has one, such as a wheel.
- Stroke about 6 to 8 on the outer contour and 3.5 to 5 inside, round caps and joins.
- A child colors this and software numbers its regions: every part is a closed region with no gaps where lines meet, no line crossing through a
  shape, and no region too small for a crayon.
- Each drawing is its own work. No shared kit, body template or reused face.

## How to work, per drawing

1. Think through its real structure and the one or two features that make it readable.
2. Write the paths, render, and look at the render with the Read tool.
3. Redesign what is weak. Do not nudge curves: redraw the shape. Objects usually need two or three rounds, people and animals four to six.
4. Run `node scripts/worksheet-gen/tools/art-check.js <noun,noun>` from the repo root. It prints a technical check per drawing, and writes
   pair.png (the library's nearest picture beside yours) and small.png (each at 96 px) to the folder it names. Read both.
5. Look at every head and face at full size, not only the whole drawing: render it at 500 px or more and Read that. A fault that hides at 290 px is still a fault.
6. Go through the Construction list part by part on the 760 px render (`--big=760`): head, neck, shoulders, each leg, each ear, tail, every line end. Then review against this page: Is it instantly the thing named? Does it hold at 96 px? Is the silhouette strong? Is there any line that does not
   earn its place? Would it sit unnoticed in the library? Does any part repeat another drawing in the batch?

Finish one drawing before starting the next. Be your own harshest critic, and say plainly when a drawing falls short.

## Boundaries

Write only `.svg` files in `art-src/drawn/`, and put any helper of your own in a new folder under `$TEMP/fl/art/`. Never delete anything you did not create.
