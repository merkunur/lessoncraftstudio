## 1. The pitch
"Half the plate is fruit and vegetables — so the plate is a seesaw: the fruit-and-vegetable half on one side, grains and protein on the other. Load only one side and it tips; it sits level, turns and steams only when the two halves match and every part is filled to its notch."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
The plate sits on a little wooden **rocker** — a half-round log under its middle, running from the back of the plate to the front — so it can only rock left or right, like a seesaw. The left half is the fruit-and-vegetable half, the right half is grains and protein. Foods tumble out of a market basket onto the tray with a soft bounce. Drop a carrot into the vegetable section and it settles with a little wobble — and the whole plate rocks down to the left, because that half is now heavier. Add bread and it rocks back. The child is literally balancing a meal: the plate sits level only when the halves match, and it turns and steams only when, on top of that, every section is filled to its little notch. A cookie can sit on the small star saucer beside the plate — the saucer stands on the table, so it does not rock anything — but a second sweet slides straight off the saucer back to the tray. In the everyday-or-sometimes mode the sometimes foods go in a pretty tin with a star on its lid; the lid closes with a clink.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** tell everyday foods from sometimes foods; sort foods into food groups (vegetables, fruit, grains, protein, milk); build a balanced plate: half vegetables and fruit, a quarter grains, a quarter protein, milk on the side.
- **Misconceptions and how the plate pushes back:**
  1. *"Any plate with one vegetable is healthy."* Every food weighs the same, so the rocker reads the plate model's one big rule — half fruit and vegetables — by physics: one carrot against two breads and a fish leaves the plate rocked down on the grains side (1 against 3); a plate of only fruit and vegetables rocks down the other way. Level needs equal halves. The notches then carry the finer rule (vegetables the biggest part) — a level plate with a section still short of its notch rests level but does not turn or steam, and its empty notch dots blink. ⚠ Honest physics: a seesaw has ONE degree of freedom, so it can only measure one rule — the half/half rule — never "every section full"; the notches carry the rest, and the prose never claims the tilt alone shows a balanced meal. (An earlier draft balanced the plate on a single point with four sections; a 2-D tilt cannot tell four quotas apart — an empty plate, or a plate missing two opposite sections, would have sat level.)
  2. *"Some foods are bad."* Sometimes foods are never refused or hidden: one treat sits happily on the star saucer and the plate still levels and steams with it there. Only a SECOND treat slides off ("sometimes", not "every time"). The words are always everyday / sometimes, never good / bad.
  3. *Grouping by colour or shape* (a red tomato put with the red fruit, a yellow banana with the bread) — each section carries a badge food (a library example of its group); a food dropped in the wrong section slides back out with a wobble and its own section breathes.

## 5. The hand
- **Drag a food** (pointerdown): it lifts 6 px with a shadow and follows the finger. Tap-tap works too (tap a food, tap a place).
- **Drop on the right section** (Plate): it settles into the section with a 120 ms wobble, the section's notch dot fills, and the plate **rocks**: `tilt = clamp(3° × (R − L), −8°, +8°)` where L = foods in the vegetable + fruit sections and R = foods in the grains + protein sections (positive = down on the right; spring animation 400 ms). Level exactly when L = R. A food dropped on a section whose notch is already full slides back — with the round tables below this only happens to a wrong food, because every tray food has exactly one notch slot.
- **Drop on the wrong section:** the food sits for 300 ms, then slides out over the rim back to its tray spot with a wobble, and its own section's badge breathes once (it is free — misses count at Check, §7).
- **Milk → the cup:** the cup fills with a white level (drawn).
- **A sweet → the star saucer:** it sits on the saucer. **A second sweet:** it slides off the saucer back to the tray (the first one stays).
- **Everyday mode:** everyday foods → the plate (anywhere on it, no sections shown); sometimes foods → the tin (lid opens, the food drops in, lid closes with a clink). A food in the wrong place slides back.
- **Undo:** any placed food can be dragged back to the tray.
- **Free play:** after success the plate stays live — foods can be lifted and the plate tips and levels again; tapping the levelled plate makes it spin once more with steam.
- Sound (optional): soft thud on landing, a creak as the plate tips, a clink for the tin, a warm "steam" whoosh; TTS speaks a food's name when it is picked up.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing placed** → hint, no miss: Everyday "Drag a food to the plate or the tin." · Plate "Drag a food into its part of the plate."
- **Plate — Check with the plate not level** (a section short of its notch, foods left on the tray that belong on the plate) = a miss:
  - **miss 1:** the plate holds its tilt (or, if level, rests level without turning) and the short section's notch dots blink; the foods still on the tray that belong there lift 4 px · hint, tipped: "The plate is tipping. Which part still needs food?" · hint, level but a notch empty: "The plate is level — but one part still needs food."
  - **miss 2:** each food still on the tray shows a faint outline of itself in its section · hint "Put each food where its outline is."
- **Everyday — Check with foods left or misplaced:**
  - **miss 1:** foods still on the tray wobble · hint "Is it a food for every day, or for some days?"
  - **miss 2:** each remaining food shows a faint outline on the plate or on the tin · hint "Follow the outlines."
- A wrong drop never counts — it slides back by itself (the section's badge breathes, which IS the first help).
- A new move takes the help away until the next Check.
- **Success:** Plate — the plate settles level with a tiny "tock", makes a slow quarter turn and three curls of steam rise; the treat on the saucer gets a small star sparkle · "A balanced plate! Half vegetables and fruit, some grains, some protein — and one treat." (10 foods: "… and milk on the side."). Everyday — the tin lid clinks shut and the plate gives a little shine · "Everyday foods on the plate, sometimes foods in the tin."

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a child choose it again?** Yes — a plate that wobbles and tips with every food and levels into a steaming turn when the meal is balanced; a saucer that politely drops the second sweet.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — balance is made literal (a one-vegetable plate stays tipped), groups are sorted against picture badges, and treats are "sometimes", never "bad".
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Library food pictures, calm wording, no diet claims or national-model claims, no score or timer.
4. **The invention:** a plate on a seesaw rocker — the fruit-and-vegetable half against the grains-and-protein half — that only sits level when the halves match, and only turns and steams when every part is filled to its notch; plus a star saucer that holds exactly one treat.
