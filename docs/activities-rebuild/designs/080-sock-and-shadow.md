## 1. The pitch
"Shadow's cupboard is dark — every word you say lights only the things that fit it, and the little door in the curtain lets just ONE thing through."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **The lamps.** Shadow's cupboard stands behind a glass front in a dim room. Every cubby has a little lamp above it, all off, and the things inside sit in the half-dark (dimmed, but the child can still see them — Shadow, inside, cannot see the coral ring Sock has stuck on the OUTSIDE of the glass). When the child's first word arrives ("cup"), a lamp clicks on above every cup — two warm pools of light. The next word ("small") switches one lamp off with a soft *tick*. Watching your own words turn lights on and off is the toy.
- **The door that only fits one.** When the child presses the speaking tube, every thing that is still lit glides to the little door in the curtain. One thing slides through and lands on Sock's counter with a bump. Two things jam in the doorway side by side, wobbling — funny, never shaming, and exactly what "too vague" means.
- **The memory pegs.** Shadow's memory pegs hang beside the cupboard. Each word arrives as a bead and hops onto a peg. A bead too many knocks the first one off — it rolls away across the floor. The child SEES what "too many words" does.
- **The end of each round**: the fetched thing slides across the counter, bumps Sock's ring, the six lamps run left to right in a little light-chase, and the phrase the child built prints on a paper ticket that spears onto a spike on the counter. After a pass the spike holds a stack of tickets — the child's own clear sentences.
- **Free play**: after a round the chips stay live, so the child can send any word and watch what lights up — a guessing toy ("what will light if I say *small*?"). A shuffle button re-deals the same things into new cubbies.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (SL.K.6, the way kindergarten teachers mean it): say what you mean so a listener who cannot see your world understands it — name the thing, add the detail that tells it apart, express a feeling with its reason ("I feel sad because it is raining"). Speaking audibly is practised with a grown-up (page note); the instrument trains the CLARITY half: choosing the words.
- Misconceptions made visible:
  1. **"The listener knows what I know."** Egocentric reference — "the cup" when there are two cups. Push-back: both cups stay lit and jam in the door. The lit set IS the listener's uncertainty, made of light.
  2. **"More words are always clearer."** Push-back: Shadow has three memory pegs; a fourth bead knocks the first off, and if the first word was the noun, half the cupboard lights up again and jams. Physics, not a scolding.
  3. **"A feeling word is enough"** (or "a reason is enough"). In feeling rounds Shadow holds up a face AND brings a help: a missing feeling leaves the face-hook empty; a missing reason lights all three helps and they jam.
  4. **A wrong word.** A word that fits nothing (saying "ball" when no ball is on the top shelf) turns every lamp off: the dark cupboard says "nothing like that here".

## 5. The hand
1. **Tap a chip** → it hops into the sentence strip (180 ms arc), the strip re-flows the phrase grammatically (120 ms fade), and a bead in the chip's colour appears at the tube's mouth. Tap a word in the strip → it hops back to the tray (undo, any time before the tube is pressed). Drag works too (chip → strip). At most 4 words in the strip (further chips grey out).
2. **Live lamps** (setting "Lamps: live", the default): every time the strip changes, the core recomputes which things fit ALL the strip's words, and the lamps switch within 100 ms (on: bulb fades to gold 140 ms and the glow blooms 200 ms, the thing returns to full colour; off: a 120 ms *tick*, the thing dims). With "Lamps: after I speak" a wooden **pelmet** covers the lamp row and every thing sits at the same dim level until the tube is pressed.
3. **Press the speaking tube** (Check): the beads travel up the tube and into the cupboard (420 ms, a soft whoosh, the tube's segments lighting in sequence) and drop onto Shadow's pegs one by one (90 ms apart). A bead that lands on a full rail knocks the FIRST bead off: it squashes (80 ms), rolls off the rail's end and away across the floor (600 ms); that word in the strip turns grey and struck through. The lamps then compute from the beads ON THE PEGS only.
4. **The door**: every lit thing glides from its cubby to the door (500 ms ease-out).
   - exactly one → it slides through the arch onto Sock's counter (300 ms) and bumps the coral ring;
   - two or more → they jam in the arch side by side (6 px wobble, 3 cycles, 400 ms) and slide back to their cubbies;
   - none → the empty arch flashes its outline once.
5. **TTS** (where a voice exists): pressing the tube speaks the assembled phrase in Sock's voice (pitch 1.1); a long-press on any chip speaks its word.
6. **Feeling rounds**: same hand; the strip assembles "I feel ___ because ___", with "please" as an optional chip that is placed grammatically and changes nothing — kindness is welcome and never graded. The face row lights by the feeling word, the help row by the reason. The help comes through the door; the matching face rises onto the face-hook.
- **Free play**: after success the chips stay live and the lamps keep answering; the shuffle button (a chip-sized button with a shuffle icon) re-deals the same things into new cubbies. Neither counts as a round.

## 6. The rounds
One round = Sock's cupboard with a ringed thing → the child builds a phrase → the tube → exactly one thing must come through. A round is right when the lit set computed from the beads on the pegs is exactly {target}; in feeling rounds, when the face row lights exactly the shown feeling AND the help row lights exactly the right help.

Every round deals **at least one chip that fits the target but does not help** (a true but useless word) and **at least one that does not fit the target**, so tapping every chip never works and tapping only the true words can overflow the pegs.

Things are written `noun·size·shelf` (B big, S small; T top, U bottom). Cubbies are **dealt fresh each pass** (see answer position); the table gives the content, not the places — except round 8, whose next-to relations are part of the round and are dealt as fixed neighbour pairs placed anywhere.

- **Answer position**: every round's layout is **mirrorable**: a pass deals each round with its shelves swapped (T ↔ U, and the shelf chips and the shortest phrase swap with them: "on the bottom shelf" ↔ "on the top shelf") for 4 or 5 of the 9 L1 rounds, chosen so the target is on the top shelf in 4–5 rounds and the bottom shelf in 4–5; columns are dealt by a balanced shuffle (round 8's neighbour pairs move as pairs). Without mirroring the content put the target on the top shelf in 6 of 9 rounds (2/9 per top cubby vs 1/9 per bottom cubby) — the critic measured this and the mirror fixes it. Over a pass the target sits in each of the 6 cubbies at least once, never in the same cubby two rounds running; in L2 the right face and the right help each sit in each of the 3 positions 2–3 times per pass. Chip order is shuffled per round so the shortest-phrase chips are never the first chips in sequence. **Gate**: over 2,000 simulated passes P(target in cubby s) ∈ [0.14, 0.19] for every s and P(target on top shelf) ∈ [0.45, 0.55]; every mirrored round re-checked for its shortest clear phrase; a "tap the first k chips" bot and a "tap every chip" bot each finish < 15 % of rounds.
- **Order**: order-only reshuffle per pass — L1 rounds 1–2 always open (the on-ramp), rounds 3–9 reshuffled; L2 all eight reshuffled; never the same order twice in a row.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Tube with an empty strip** → no miss; the tube's mouth glows; bubble: "Say a word into the tube first."
- **Miss 1** (wrong thing, jam, nothing lit, or overflow) → the apparatus shows what happened (the jam, the dark hatch, the rolled-away bead) AND the pelmet lifts (live lamps) for the rest of the round even when the setting is "after I speak"; the coral ring pulses. Bubble by cause:
  - jam: "Two {pluralNoun} came to the door. Which one does Sock mean? Add a word that tells them apart."
  - nothing lit: "Nothing in Shadow's cupboard fits those words. Look at Sock's thing again."
  - wrong thing came through: "That one has no ring. Which word tells Shadow the difference?"
  - overflow: "Shadow can only hold {pegs} words — one rolled away. Say less!"
  - feeling, face missing: "Shadow knows what to bring, but not how you feel. Add a feeling word."
  - feeling, help jam: "Shadow knows how you feel, but not why. Add what happened."
- **Miss 2** → the stronger showing: every thing that was lit after the last Check gets a **difference badge** pinned to its cubby — the icon of the attribute where it differs from the ringed thing (two nested squares for size, the shelf icon, the next-to icon, or the noun picture). The ringed thing's badge is gold, the others coral, and the chips that would separate them throb gently in the tray. Bubble: "Look where they are different — say that word."
- A new chip tap removes the badges (help taken away until the next Check).
- **Success** → the one thing slides through the door, bumps the ring, the ring turns gold, all six lamps flash once in sequence (480 ms) and the ticket prints: a 220 × 40 paper slip slides out of the counter edge with the phrase and spears onto the spike (360 ms). Bubble: "Just enough words: Shadow brought {phrase}!" (L1) · "Shadow understood: {sentence} — and brought the {help}." (L2).

**Locale traps** (the build authors each locale's phrase builder by hand; one native + pedagogy reviewer reads every reachable phrase per locale in one compact dump):
- **Noun-phrase order and agreement**: de article + adjectives before the noun, inflected (`der kleine Becher`, `auf dem oberen Regal`); fr size before the noun with gender agreement (`la petite tasse`, `le grand chapeau`), place after; es/pt/it the size adjective after the noun (`la taza pequeña`, `a xícara pequena`, `la tazza piccola`; never pre-nominal `gran`, which means "great"); nl `-e` after the definite article (`de kleine beker`, `het kleine …`); sv/no **double definiteness** (`den lilla koppen`, `den lille koppen`) but da single definiteness with an adjective (`den lille kop`) — the reviewer confirms each Scandinavian form; fi has no article and puts case on the place phrase (`ylähyllyllä`, `alahyllyllä`).
- **"next to the X"**: de dative (`neben dem Ball`), fi genitive + postposition (`pallon vieressä`), fr contraction (`à côté du ballon`, `de la`, `de l'`), it (`vicino alla palla`), pt (`ao lado da bola`), es (`al lado de la pelota`, `del`).
- **Feelings and speaker gender**: Sock is one character in every locale; Romance languages need a speaker gender for some feeling adjectives. Rule: use invariant forms where natural (fr `j'ai peur`, `je suis triste`, `j'ai sommeil`; es `tengo miedo`, `tengo sueño`, `estoy triste`; it `ho paura`, `ho sonno`, `sono triste`; pt `estou com medo`, `estou com sono`, `estou triste`); where none exists (angry, happy, surprised) Sock's grammatical gender is fixed per locale and recorded in the locale file (fr *la chaussette* → feminine: `je suis contente`, `je suis en colère`, `je suis surprise`; es *el calcetín* → masculine: `contento`, one of `enfadado`/`enojado` per the es-MX register, `sorprendido`). The face pictures are gender-free.
- **Two different everyday nouns** must exist for mug vs cup (de Tasse / Becher, fr tasse / mug, es taza / tazón, pt xícara / caneca, it tazza / boccale, nl kopje / mok, sv kopp / mugg, da kop / krus, no kopp / krus, fi kuppi / muki) and hat vs cap (de Hut / Kappe, fr chapeau / casquette, es sombrero / gorra, fi hattu / lippis …). If a locale cannot separate a pair, that locale's round swaps in another pair with the same logic (two kinds × two sizes).
- **Reasons are whole clauses authored per locale**, never assembled: de verb-final with a comma (`Ich bin traurig, weil es regnet.`), nl verb-final (`omdat het regent`), sv `för att det regnar`, fi `koska sataa`.
- **Shelf words**: each locale's natural cupboard words (de `oben im Regal`, sv `på översta hyllan`, fi `ylähyllyllä`).
- TTS is reliable in 5 of 11 locales — the lamps, the jam, the rolling bead and the face-hook carry everything with the sound off.
- sv/da/no/fi metadata (new at build): title and intro localized from the frozen EN, the platform age-range grade names (§17.4.2).

## 11. Distinct from
- **Built registry**: no counting table, no peek window (#12 is a counting window), no bounce-back pot (#22 bounces by size; here things jam in a hatch because a phrase fits two).
- **`number-sieve` tool** (clues darken a number field): related in spirit (a set narrows), but there the clue IS a rule card handed to the class; here the child authors the clues as speech for a listener with a memory limit and a one-thing door — the skill is choosing words for someone else.
- **Designed registry**: Row 72 Two-Ended Rod (opposites), 217 Article Caps, 219 Body Map, 225 Sorting Cabinet (a wrong piece jams a drawer — here the jam is caused by a phrase that fits two, not by a wrong piece).
- **home-language-bridge** (picture phrase board) and **feelings-check-in** (a feelings chart): communication with no listener model, feelings with no reason.

## 12. Build plan
- **Core** `LamplightCupboardCore`: `state = { round, strip: [chipId], pegs: [chipId], dropped: [chipId], phase: 'compose'|'travel'|'door'|'done', misses, badges }`. Functions: `fits(thing, chip)` (noun equality, size, shelf, `nextTo` = the thing's horizontal neighbour in the same shelf row holds that noun); `litSet(things, chips)` = things fitting ALL chips (an empty list lights nothing — nothing has been said); `send(strip, memory)` → `{pegs: last memory chips, dropped: the rest}`; `doorResult(lit)` → 'through' | 'jam' | 'none'; `isRight(round, pegs)`; feeling rounds `litFaces(pegs)`, `litHelps(pegs)`; `deal(round, rng)` assigns cubbies and chip order under the balance constraints; `differenceBadges(lit, target)`. Phrase builders: one pure function per locale in the activity file, `phrase(chips) → string`.
