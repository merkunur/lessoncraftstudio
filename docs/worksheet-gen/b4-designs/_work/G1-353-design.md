## 1 Page concept (base, G1)

"Question Words: Who, What, Where, When": seven numbered lanes. Each lane opens with a 56 px picture of the sentence's subject, then ONE short answer sentence in Nunito 800 18 in which the answer constituent is highlighted (coral-soft bar + 3 px coral underline, the `markedSpan`), and under the sentence a strip of the same four question-word chips in the same order on every row. The child reads the coloured words and circles the chip that ASKS for them. From across the room: seven pictures down the left, seven single lines of text each carrying one warm highlight, twenty-eight identical white chips in seven neat rows, nothing else. Top quality = one focal apparatus (the marked sentence), one-line sentences in every locale (measured, §2), chips at the 44 px circle floor, pencil-first (the circle is the only mark), and a key the child cannot read off the picture (the picture is the SUBJECT, the mark is usually somewhere else: §4). The abstract Klasse-2 act "Frage nach dem markierten Satzglied" becomes concrete because the highlight does the pointing and the chips do the asking.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- **The mark is the key; the chips are the choices; they can never be confused.** The mark (`markedSpan`) is a FLAT coral-soft bar (`#FBE3D8`) with a 3 px coral underline and 4 px corner radius, INSIDE the running sentence, in the sentence's own Nunito 800 18 ink. A chip (`.ws-achip`) is a ROUND white pill with a 2.5 px teal border in Baloo 2 700 20, in a strip BENEATH the sentence. Different shape (bar vs pill), different colour family (coral vs teal), different type (Nunito vs Baloo), different place (inline vs strip). The mark is applied only inside `<p data-lcs-sentence>`; `qwChips` never receives a marked label (it throws on a `data-lcs-mark` in its input, and verify() asserts no `[data-lcs-mark]` inside any `[data-lcs-chips]`), so the marking never reaches an answer chip. A boxed variant (2.5 px coral border, radius 8, m: +16 px) was rejected because a bordered rounded box READS AS A CHIP to a six-year-old; a plain underline was rejected because a 2-word constituent under a descender-heavy line ("im Garten") loses its extent in print. The bar gives the extent, the underline survives greyscale (§9).
- **One answer by construction.** Every item is authored as a whole literal with exactly ONE bracketed constituent (`text:"Der Hund schläft [im Garten]."`) and a `kind` that the constituent's TYPE fixes: a person as SUBJECT -> `who`; a thing as OBJECT -> `what`; a place phrase -> `where`; a time phrase -> `when`; a counted quantity -> `howmany`. The panel signs `single:true` per item (the bracketed words are answered by exactly one of the page's kinds in that locale). The code asserts the STRUCTURE: exactly one `[...]` pair, non-empty, not the whole sentence; the row's `kind` in the page's `kinds`; no chip label anywhere in the sentence text (`(?<!\p{L})label(?!\p{L})` with the `u` flag, never `\b`); no item id twice; the question literal (bank) absent from the page DOM.
- **Two-valid is designed out, not reviewed out.** The classic failure ("Der Hund schläft abends im Garten": Wo or Wann) cannot arise because the mark names the constituent and the chip must ask for THAT one. The person-as-object trap (de "Wen", fi "Ketä/Kenet", en "whom") is a data rule: person constituents are bracketed only in SUBJECT position at d2; a locale that wants the object case adds a kind (`who_obj`) with its own chip label at d3 (§6). Animals and things are never `who` (de "Was" for the dog; the panel per locale).
- **The picture does not leak.** At d2 the picture is the SUBJECT's referent, while `what` rows always bracket the OBJECT and `where`/`when` rows bracket an adverbial, so a child who only looks at the picture and circles by picture kind (`pictureBot`: person -> who, object/animal -> what, place -> where) scores exactly the `who` rows: `mix.who <= 2` of 7 = 29 %. The gate runs `pictureBot` over the resolved page and fails above 0.35 (poison: a page with 3 who rows -> FAIL). At d1 the picture IS the marked referent on purpose (the scaffold level, unpublished).
- **Wrong is visibly wrong.** The circled chip sits directly under the highlighted words; the teacher reads bar and circle in one glance. A row with two circles or none is a visible non-answer. Match (b): a question with two lines or an answer with none. Fill (c): a box holding a word that is not the kind of the highlighted words. Sort (d): a phrase written under the wrong head. Write (e): the written question is checked against the stamped literal by the teacher (the model answer ships in the stamp only).

## 6 Locale slot structure

- **Authored language per locale** lives in `data/b4/locales/question-words.<loc>.json` (en in `data/b4/question-words.js`), generated from `i18n/.draft-b4-<loc>.json` by the cloned `tools/apply-b4-locale.js` after `validate-b4-draft.js`. The EN file also holds the LOCALE-NEUTRAL layer (never re-authored per locale): `pools` (persons / objects / places with pinned `pic`, `picOpened:true`, `depicted` on persons) and `items[]` (`{id, stem, kind, pic, markedPic, picOpened:true}`; `pic` = the subject's picture, `markedPic` = the marked referent's picture where it is a person/object/place, absent on `when` items). Each locale file carries:
  - `qwords`: `{kind: label}` for the closed universe `who what where when howmany why how` PLUS any locale-specific kind it needs (fi `what_part:"Mitä"`, `where_to:"Mihin"`, `whose:"Kenen"`; de `who_obj:"Wen"`, `where_to:"Wohin"`; sv writes `where:"Var"`, never "Vart", the panel's call). Labels are the SENTENCE-INITIAL form (capital; es/pt with the accent; da/no/sv/fi/nl/de/fr/it their school form), no question mark (the sort heads add the locale's `qmark`, es `"¿…?"` both signs: `qmark:{pre:"¿", post:"?"}`).
  - `faces`: per face id `{kinds:[...]}` (base d1/d2/d3, fill, sort, ask blocks) = which kinds this locale prints on that face; any kind set the strip guard rejects is refused at build (never wrapped).
  - `items`: `{id: {text, question, short, kind?, single:true}}` per neutral item id: `text` = the answer sentence with ONE `[...]` (de nouns keep the capital; fi the case the sentence needs INSIDE the bracket, e.g. `[puutarhassa]`; sv/da/no the definite where natural, `[i trädgården]`); `question` = the whole question with `{q}` exactly once at the position the language wants (`"{q} schläft der Hund?"`, `"¿{q} duerme el perro?"`, `"{q} does the dog sleep?"`, fi `"{q} koira nukkuu?"`); `short` = the standalone constituent for the sort face (nominative for persons/things, the bare locative for places; <= 11 glyphs or the item is refused on the sort face only); `kind` overrides the neutral kind when the locale's grammar requires it (fi an object thing -> `what_part`); an id absent from the locale file = that item refused there.
  - `strings`: title + instruction per face id (base + 5), the EN pair being the rows module's literal (one source; the gate asserts).
- **Where text sits.** Sentences: Nunito 800 **18** (17 on the write face only, §7e), left-aligned, one line inside 531 (base, fill) or two lines inside 216 (match). Chips: Baloo 2 700 20, nowrap, <= 5 per strip, strip <= 531 (guard + render assertion). Bank pills (fill): Nunito 800 18, one row: 5 pills = en 494 · de 435 · fr 421 · es 476 · pt 487 · it 473 · nl 466 · sv 426 · da 509 · no 460 · fi 508 (m + 32 pad/border per pill + 4 gaps of 10) <= 651 in all 11; the two-row guard (<= 2 x 651) stays as the backstop. Sort heads: Baloo 2 700 20 in `.ws-pill` (m at pad 18 with "?": it "Che cosa?" 129.0, da "Hvem?" 101.1, fi "Missä?" 101.0) <= 201. Starters (ask, write d1): Nunito 700 at `starterFontPx({h, glyphH})` from the measured metrics, inkSoft. Badge: Baloo 2 700 16 white. Font floor on these pages: 16 (the 9 px lint is far).
- **+40 % reserve is structural.** de/fi/pt run 9.2 to 9.6 px/char against en 8.1; the one-line rule holds because the POOL cap is per face (`maxChars`) and the longest legal line (48 x 9.6 + 8 = 469) is under 531 with 62 px spare. The chip strip reserve is the measured 5-chip fi total 512 vs 531. The match face reserves TWO lines per side (2 x 216 = 432 px of text >= de 40 chars 384). The sort tile guard is per tile (§7d).
- **Traps designed as data, not code:** fi inflection (the chip label is the inflected form the sentence requires; the row `kind` names it; a bare "Kuka" never sits under a partitive object); de/fi/en person-as-object (`who_obj` kind or refusal, never "Wer" for "dem Bäcker"); animals are `what` (de "Was", en "what"; the fr/it/es/pt panels choose and sign); de nouns capitalised inside the bracket; sv "var" not "vart"; da "Hv-ord" spelling in the title only (never "hv ord" in no); the es inverted mark lives in the question literal, so the fill face prints "¿" before the box; NO `{name}` slot anywhere (persons are the pool's occupations and `toys/girl`, `classroom/student`, so no name-gender tagging is needed and every text is a whole literal); the `{q}` slot is the ONLY slot (`fillSlots` throws otherwise).

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

- **Alt A, the page-wide legend.** One chip strip at the top (Wer Was Wo Wann numbered 1-4) and a small numeral box on every row; the child writes the number. Rejected: it turns a reading task into a code lookup (the G2-279 colour-code idiom), the box is a `blankNumeralBox` on a letters page, and a one-glance teacher check needs the chip on the row.
- **Alt B, three pictures per row.** A person, an object and a place on every row, one question word printed at the left ("Wo?"), the child circles the picture the word asks for. Rejected: it is picture classification (K-225 / K-235) and never reaches a sentence; it is solvable by picture kind alone; and it inverts the genre (teachers search "which question word", not "which picture").
- **Chosen: the marked answer sentence with a per-row chip strip.** It is the house lane idiom (G1-341 badge + chips, G2-335 inline apparatus, G1-350 lane list) re-cut so the highlight does the pointing, it keeps every sentence on one measured line in all 11 locales, it fits the 5-chip fi strip with 19 px spare, and the same lane carries the fill and write faces by swapping what sits under the sentence.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Picture legibility (opened at 120 and 56).** Places at 56: tent, forest, mountain, cabin, campfire, beach, pool, garden, nest, house, igloo, fireplace, island, lighthouse, church, garage, stairs, door, fence, bed, sofa, sandbox, slide, swing, playground, desk, bench read cleanly; `camping/lake` and `camping/river` both read as "landscape with water" at 56 (one per page, twin group); `around the house/kitchen` is a scene that reads as a kitchen only at 56+ (allowed, never at 44); `At the Supermarket/cart` is a trolley, not a place (excluded from `places`; usable as an object); `hospital/bed` is a hospital bed (a place only in a "hospital" frame; the second `bed` is not pinned). Twin groups, one per page: {lake, river}, {cabin, house}, {campfire, fireplace}, {camping/tent, summer/tent}, {sandbox, playground}. Persons at 56 (all clear; the G1-352 `depicted` table applies): baker farmer firefighter teacher nurse chef florist doctor gardener librarian athlete musician veterinarian + classroom/student + toys/girl. Objects at 56: ball kite robot doll bus bicycle apple banana cake book guitar milk bread backpack key dog cat rabbit all clear (`breakfast/bread` has NO file; `bread` is pinned to `At the Supermarket/bread`). **Unusable, measured:** `police_officer`, `teddy_bear`, `post_office` have NO vocab key (not in the index); `school park farm zoo library hospital supermarket bakery classroom` have NO picture at all, so the place pool is the 28 opened place nouns above and every "at school" style constituent is a text-only `when`/`where` phrase whose row picture is the SUBJECT (allowed: the picture is never the key at d2).
- **`objForms` cannot supply the place phrases.** Measured: of the 28 place nouns, `objForms` holds 3 in en/de/fr/it/sv/da/no, 2 in pt/nl, 0 in es, 29 in fi (and fi stores `part`/`gen`, never the inessive). So every locative phrase ("im Garten", "puutarhassa", "i trädgården") is a panel WHOLE LITERAL inside the item text; the code never builds a place phrase from a noun. This is why the bank is per item, not per noun.
- **Uniqueness beyond structure.** The code cannot judge that "[das Brot]" is a `what` and "[der Bäcker]" a `who`; the panel signs `single:true` per item and states the kind; the critic spot-reads three items per kind per locale. The person-as-object and animal-as-subject traps are §4/§6 data rules; the gate refuses any `who` item whose bracket is not sentence-initial in de/fi/en (a cheap structural proxy the panel can override with `objectPerson:true` on a `who_obj` item).
- **Overflow in long locales.** Base: one line by pool cap (48 x 9.6 + 8 = 469 < 531); chips: fi 5-chip 512 < 531 (m + sum), any inflected fi set re-summed by the guard. Match: 2 lines in 216 (de 40 chars 368 -> 2 lines; verify <= 47). Fill: question <= 30 chars + the 220 cap. Write: 714 at worst chrome -> fi title <= 3 lines (validator). Sort: the tile guard refuses, never squeezes; the shelf asserts <= 3 rows. Ask: 596, no text risk (starters are single words; "Kuinka monta" at the starter font ~ 118 px inside 639).
- **Greyscale.** The coral-soft bar prints as a barely-visible light grey; the 3 px coral underline prints mid-grey and CARRIES the mark in B&W (this is why the mark is bar + underline, §4); the teal chip borders print near-black, so the circled chip is the child's pencil and nothing else on the row is round. The bins' dashed teal top prints dark; dashed grey lines stay legible. The engineer prints one de and one fi base page in greyscale for the critic.
- **Pencil space.** Chips 44 high with 12 px side padding (a pencil circle clears the border); the fill box 40 high x 110..220 (G1 fontChoice 26 fits); bins 179 px lines at gapY <= 58; rulings glyphH 24 (G2 floor) on 48 px rows; the match line zone 103 px between dot centres.
- **Cut lines:** none on any face.
- **9 px floor:** the smallest text is the 16 px badge / the starter font (>= 20 at glyphH 24 by the metrics); the lint cannot fire.
- **What the QA lint catches:** overflow, footer intrusion, palette (only `.ws-*` classes + tokens; the mark uses `coralSoft` + `coral`), the 9 px floor, a blank body (`[data-ws-content]` on every lane, match, shelf, block). **What only the type gate catches:** picture floors (56 / 96 per face), the one-line sentence, the identical chip order on every row, the strip within its column, the chip-not-in-sentence rule, kind balance + `pictureBot <= 0.35`, the mark never inside a chip, match derangement + twins, the uniform gap box + one-row bank, sort tile widths + fillable bins + lines >= tiles, the empty ruling + unprinted question at d2, twin-picture exclusion. **What only a human eye catches:** a bracket on the wrong constituent, a fi chip in the wrong case for its sentence, a de "Wer" over an object person, a sv "vart", a place phrase a child would file under `what`, a `when` phrase that is really a `where` ("at the party"), a picture whose subject reads as the marked thing in print (the panel opens every pinned picture; the critic reads one greyscale render per face per locale).
- **Factory traps recorded:** `sentenceGap` throws on `{q}` and carries verb stamps; `truthChips` is a fixed yes|no pair; `answerBox` without an answer stamps `undefined`; `.ws-lane` at its default padding costs 14 px per row (7 chip rows would not fit); `\b` is ASCII-only, every word test is `(?<!\p{L})...(?!\p{L})` with `u`; `pillChoice` centres and carries no index; a `.ws-blankbox` inside a `<p>` must be `display:inline-block` or it drops to its own line; the K-288 bin SVG needs `width` and `viewBox` both (a bare `height` collapses in print).
