## The skill
Name family members (mother, father, sister, brother, grandmother, grandfather …) from their place on a family tree.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE TREE THAT HOLDS EVERY FRAME. A tree with three branches of frames: grandparents at the top, parents in the middle, the children (one marked "me" with a star) at the bottom; joining lines show who is whose. The child drags family-word cards onto frames; a card snaps only onto the frame its word names (relative to "me"); a wrong card slides back and the line from "me" to the right frame lights, so the relationship is read along the tree. Me-moves setting: the star moves to another child, and the same frames change names (my brother's father is my father) — kinship is a relation, not a label on a person.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Family words as fixed labels on people rather than relations to "me"; mixing up grandparent sides.
- **Pictures:** MISSING: seven family figures as library pictures (grandmother, grandfather, mother, father, older girl, older boy, baby — toys/baby could serve as the baby). Frames, branches and the "me" star are apparatus.
- **Settings:** Words: core (mother, father, sister, brother) / plus grandparents · Me: fixed / moves
- **Rounds:** 9 trees per pass, the structure fixed (one family shape, as K-370 rules), the words' frames shuffled; one right frame per word.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-385, G1-386, G1-387, G2-362, K-370, K-375
- **Distinct from:** No family activity exists.
- **Build risk:** Blocked on family-figure art. Nordic lineage words (farmor/mormor, farfar/morfar) need a fixed grandparent side per frame — the tree must mark the mother's and father's side by branch colour. The clue mode (G1-386: names placed from clues) could be built with abstract name tokens, but without figures it teaches name logic, not family words.

## BLOCKED — needs an operator decision before building
Family words need people who look like a grandmother, grandfather, mother, father, sister, brother and baby; the library has only toys/baby, toys/girl, toys/doll and uniformed occupations, and drawing silhouettes with hair/age cues is hand-drawn characters (rule 5). The design is sound and builds as soon as family figures exist.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 1 "The Family Tree" (family-tree-rows): Families have generations: grandparents, parents, children; family words name each place. — A TREE WITH THREE BRANCH LEVELS: grandparents on the top branch, parents in the middle, children at the bottom. Family-word cards (grandma, dad, baby, aunt) are dropped on a branch; a card on the wrong level slides along the branch to the trunk. The clue setting reads clues ("Mia is Tom's sister") and the child places the name cards; the branch lines connecting people light when a relation is right.
- grade 2 "The Family Tree Path — Who Is Who?" (family-tree-path): Use family words (mother, father, sister, brother, grandmother, grandfather, aunt, uncle, cousin) for relationships. — A three-generation family tree of library people on branches. A riddle ("my mother's sister") is a PATH: the child starts at the "me" child and walks up and across the branches step by step, each step tapping a branch (up to parent, across to sibling, down to child). The person where the path ends lights, and the child picks the family word for them from the bank. Reverse: a person lights; the child builds the path.
