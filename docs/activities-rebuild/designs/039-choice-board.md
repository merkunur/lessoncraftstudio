## 1. The pitch
"A flat shape turned on its edge is a line and slides through the letterbox; a solid turned any way still has thickness and jams — and a box, opened out into its net, becomes flat enough to post."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **A post office counter.** A red post box with a thin letter slot and a parcel shelf beside it. Shapes arrive in a mail bag. The child drags each onto the **letter tray** or the **parcel tray**.
- **The slot test is a show.** Check: the letter tray tips its shapes one by one towards the slot. A flat shape turns on its edge — and shrinks to a thin line — and slips through with a papery *fwip*. A solid turns on its edge and is still fat: it jams in the slot, wobbles, and pops back out with a *bonk*. On the parcel tray, solids stand on the shelf with their depth showing in the side window; a flat shape put there cannot stand — it topples, lies down as a line and slides off the shelf edge.
- **Unfold the box.** In the grade-2 counting tasks a solid jams at the slot — so the child pulls its tab and it opens, face after face swinging down on its hinges, into a flat net on the counter. Count the faces (each tapped face gets a postmark), and the flat net then slides through the slot: the solid became flat.
- **Postage is one stamp per face.** The opened net cannot go through the slot unpaid: every face needs a postmark, so the child stamps face after face (a satisfying inky *thunk*, a little wavy postmark ring) — the curved sheet of a cylinder refuses a stamp and curls up, because it is not a flat face. Then the child says how many stamps were used, and the paid net slides through the slot with a *fwip* — a solid made flat enough to post.
- **Earned show per round:** the post box's flag flips up, the mail drops into the bag inside with a satisfying thump and the bag's drawstring cinches. No timer, score, streak or lives.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- **Skill:** identify shapes as two-dimensional (flat) or three-dimensional (solid) (K.G.A.3, G2-244); count the flat faces of cubes, rectangular boxes, square and triangular pyramids, triangular and hexagonal prisms and cylinders by opening them into nets (G2-242); see everyday objects as those solids (G2-243).
- **Misconceptions and the push-back:**
  - *"A circle and a ball are the same shape"*, *square = cube*, *triangle = pyramid*: every sort round contains twin pairs (circle & sphere, square & cube, triangle & pyramid or cone, rectangle & box); only the flat twin becomes a line on its edge and passes the slot.
  - *A picture of a solid on a flat screen is flat:* the slot does not care that the screen is flat — the solid's edge-on view still has depth (drawn shaded, turned on the turntable), and it jams.
  - *A cube has 3 faces* (counting only the faces you can see): unfolding lays all six faces flat side by side — none can hide.
  - *Counting only the faces you can see* (the faces a 3D drawing hides): solids are drawn see-through with hidden edges dashed (as school books and the worksheets draw them), and the net lays every face side by side — the back and the bottom come out into the light and need their stamps too.
  - *A cylinder's curved side is a face:* unfolding a cylinder unrolls its side into a sheet that is striped as "was curved" and will not take a postmark; the flat faces are the two circles — the worksheets' convention (and edges are not asked for a cylinder, as the worksheets leave it out).

## 5. The hand
- **Sort:** drag a shape from the bag to a tray (or tap it, then tap a tray) → it hops there (180 ms); trays hold up to 6; drag between trays freely. **Tap a shape** → a small lift and wiggle only — it does NOT turn edge-on before Check (that would answer the sort; the turning is the slot's verdict). After success, free play: every shape can be tapped to turn edge-on and back.
- **Check (sort)** → the letter tray tips items to the slot one at a time (each ≈ 600 ms): flat → turns edge-on (scaleY → 0.04 in 220 ms), slides through, *fwip*; solid → turns edge-on but keeps a visible depth, jams (shake ±3 px), *bonk*, pops back to the bag. Then the parcel shelf: solids slide onto it (side window shows their depth); a flat shape topples flat (rotates 90°), shows as a line in the side window, slides off the shelf and back to the bag.
- **Postage mode:** drag the pull tab outward (or tap it, or Enter) → the faces swing down on their hinges one by one (250 ms each, a cardboard *flap* sound) into the net; the cylinder's lids flip off and its side unrolls (a 400 ms roll) into a striped sheet. **Tap a face of the net** → a postmark ring is stamped on it (tap again removes it); tapping the striped sheet makes it curl up for a moment and stamps nothing. **Tap a chip** → selects the count. Check with every face stamped and the right chip → the net slides through the slot; the slot refuses a net with an unstamped face (it bumps and slides back) — the stamps make "count every face" a condition of posting, not a reminder.
- **Turn button** → the solid turns 90° on the turntable (500 ms) so the child can look for hidden faces before opening it; after unfolding it is hidden (the net is flat).
- **Keyboard:** Tab through shapes/trays (Enter moves), the pull tab (Enter opens), the net's faces (Enter stamps), the turn button, the chips.
- **Free play after success:** the child can fold the net back up by dragging the tab inward and unfold it again.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check (sort) with shapes still in the bag** → hint "Put every shape on a tray." (no miss). **Postage mode with no chip**
 → "How many? Tap a number." (no miss); unfold mode before unfolding → "Pull the tab to open it out flat." (no miss).
- **Sort miss** = any shape bounced by the slot or the shelf. **Miss 1:** the bounced shapes, back in the bag, show their edge-on view side by side for 2 s (a line beside a thick block) above the bag. Hint: "Flat shapes turn into a thin line on their edge. Solids stay thick." **Miss 2:** each bounced shape gets an arrow to the tray it belongs on. Hint: "Follow the arrows."
- **Postage miss** = wrong chip. **Miss 1:** the unstamped faces of the net wobble and the stamped ones glow faintly; if fewer faces are stamped than the net has, the net bumps the slot and slides back. Hint: "Stamp every face — look for the back and the bottom." **Miss 2:** every face shows a small numeral in turn (1 … n) as it gets a stamp. Hint: "Count along with the numbers."
- **A new move** clears the showing.
- **Success:** sort — the flag flips, the mail drops into the inner bag, drawstring cinches; bubble "Yes — the flat shapes went through the slot!" · postage — the paid net slides through the slot, "Yes — {n} faces! Opened out, it is flat." · everyday things "Yes — the {object} has {n} faces, like a {solid}!"

## 11. Distinct from

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a post box that swallows flat shapes and spits out solids, boxes that fold open, and a stamp for every face before the parcel can be posted.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — "flat has no thickness" is shown by a shape turning into a line; "a cube has six faces, not three" is shown by unfolding it.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — calm, wordless apparatus, real objects from the library, no score.
4. **The invention, in one sentence:** only something flat fits through the letterbox slot — a solid jams until it is opened out into its flat net.
