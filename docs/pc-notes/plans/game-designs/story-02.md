## 2. Concept & creative hook
**Mo the bear (the troupe's heavy sleeper) napped through the whole story. Now he's awake, blinking, and he KNOWS he missed something good — Pip's light is still buzzing with it. He tugs the child's sleeve: "You saw it. I slept through the whole thing. Tell me — what happened?"** The most universal thing a five-year-old does: tell a friend the thing they missed.
- **THE STORY GOES ABSENT (the panels-dark structural kill):** the moment Mo asks, the panels fold into Pip's lantern and the story-board goes soft cream — **the story now lives ONLY in the child's telling.** There are NO visible panels during the retell → the panel-order cheat is structurally impossible (a child can't match statement→panel-position when there are no panels).
- **EACH BEAT LANDS ON MO'S FACE (the audience-recount, SL.K.4):** a part told true → Pip's glow-mote drifts to Mo, and **Mo's face does the thing** — he gasps at the scary middle, goes soft at the kind end. The child isn't answering Mo; the child is GIVING Mo the story, one warm piece at a time, and watching it land. (Mo's reactions are AFFECT-NEUTRAL until AFTER the child commits — they never leak the order or the content, F-2/F-10.)
- **THE KEY-DETAIL IS WHERE THIS STORY SURPRISES THE SCRIPT (the script-violating fix — the critic's deepest):** the child can't win by knowing "how stories go." Every beat's discriminating detail is where THIS tale DEPARTS from the generic arc — Pip searches for Wren's lost thing because Wren is too sad to move (the searcher is NOT the loser); the lantern went dark because Mo SAT on it (not because it broke). The true statement is the script-UNEXPECTED one; the foil is the generically-plausible one. A child ordering by "lose→search→find" + picking the generic detail picks the FOIL and the lantern stays dim.
- **THE WIN RESTS ON THE KEY-DETAILS (order necessary-not-sufficient):** the order is allowed to be schema-recoverable (kids legitimately know story shape — that's not the skill); the lantern reaches full glow ONLY when the arc AND every script-violating key-detail are true.
- **THE PAYOFF:** the whole arc told true → Mo "HAS" the story now (he turns and retells it to Wren himself, a little garbled + delighted, glow-motes drifting off his fur); a told-light joins the night-sky `GameCollection` (non-numeric AND non-content — a constellation of stories-given-away, NEVER a count). `ctx.correct()`. A wrong part → no fail; Mo gently asks "wait — what happened that MADE that happen?" (a causal nudge, never the answer) + re-tell.
- **Anti-generic statement:** NOT order-the-panels — (a) panels-dark + the `PANEL_ORDER_SOLVER` kill panel-order; (b) the keyword lint + the `SEQUENCE_KEYWORD_SOLVER` kill the keyword cheat; (c) the inherited PLAUSIBILITY/PICTURE/TROPE solvers (Game-58); (d) the script-violating key-details + the win-decisive key-details + the `SCRIPT_SOLVER` kill the world-knowledge/script cheat (the critic's deepest); (e) the reaction-suppression + the `REACTION_LEAK_SOLVER` kill the affect-leak; the gate proves panel-order + keyword + plausibility + script + reaction-leak solvers ALL fail while the retell oracle passes.
