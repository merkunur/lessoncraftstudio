## The skill
Recognise feelings from faces (happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, calm), name them, and connect a situation to a feeling.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE FACE THAT YOU SET. A big round dial (apparatus geometry: a circle, two dot eyes, one curve mouth, two short line brows) with two handles — the mouth handle bends the curve (smile ↔ frown, open), the brow handle tilts the brows (raised, flat, angled down). Setting them morphs the face continuously. A situation card shows a library picture (a lost balloon floating away; a present; a thunderstorm; a birthday cake) or a feeling word. The child sets the dial; each feeling is a REGION of handle space. Check: the dial slides next to the library emotions face of the target feeling. Right: the two overlap and the dial's outline glows. Miss 1: the dial and the library face sit side by side and the part that differs (brows or mouth) pulses. Miss 2: the handle that is wrong shows a faint target. GOOD-OR-HARD mode (K-333): library emotions faces slide to a "feels good" or "feels hard" cloud. TODAY mode: free play, no right answer.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Reading only the mouth (a smile = happy, everything else = sad) and not the eyebrows; thinking some feelings are "bad" to have.
- **Pictures:** emotions (happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, tired, excited, bored); situations: toys (balloon), christmas (present), weather (stormy), bakery (cake); refused: a dog (it can carry any feeling).
- **Settings:** Mode: set the face / match face to word / good or hard / today (explore) · Feelings: 4 / 6
- **Rounds:** 9 situations per pass, each feeling ≥ once, chosen by the reviewer to be unambiguous in 11 locales; tolerance regions measured so neighbours (happy/excited, sad/tired) never overlap; one right feeling per situation.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-319, K-331, K-332, K-333, K-334, K-335
- **Distinct from:** No SEL activity exists. Not a mascot: the face is an abstract dial, like the learning clock's hands.
- **Build risk:** The operator must accept the geometric dial as apparatus (rule 5) — it is three shapes, no hair, no body. Wording "feels hard", never "bad".
