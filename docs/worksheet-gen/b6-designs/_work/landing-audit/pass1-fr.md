# Landing audit pass 1 — fr

1. G1-402: the eight sentences are printed in story order (D'abord, Ensuite, Puis, Enfin) and only the pictures are scrambled, so the child never orders anything - the sequencing is done by the sheet; the task is sentence-to-picture matching only. The title 'Images séquentielles' still fits, but the landing describes it as a reading/matching page.
2. G2-381: the instruction says 'Dessine trois animaux…' but the page has NO drawing space - only three writing lines under 'Des animaux qui vivent ici' (apparatus named but not drawn). The landing tells teachers to let children draw in the scene or on the back.
3. G2-381: the instruction says 'entoure le bon mot de chaque paire' but only ONE pair is printed (humide / sec), and that pair has no question stem ('Ce milieu est…'). The resolved config carries chipPairs: 2, so the hot/cold pair is being dropped (plausibly because a pond is neither hot nor cold) while the string still says 'chaque paire'.
4. G1-407: the header line 'Chaque animal a besoin de nourriture, d'eau et d'un abri' names WATER, but the page has only Nourriture and Abri columns - water is named, never drawn or asked. Landing flags it as an oral point.
5. G1-406: the instruction says 'Dans chaque rangée, barre l'animal…' but each habitat's four animals are laid out as a 2x2 block, i.e. TWO visual rows of two; a literal reader looks at two cards and may cross nothing or cross two. 'Pour chaque milieu' / 'dans chaque groupe' would match the render.
6. G1-398: the sloth card (orange, dark eye mask, sitting upright) reads to French children as a suricate/meerkat - a savanna animal - which creates a plausible second answer (C Savane) for an animal the sheet expects under A Jungle. Also the savanna gets only one animal (lion) of eight.
7. G1-398: the fr habitat pool is forêt / mare / prairie / océan / banquise / savane / jungle; this draw shows Jungle, Mare, Savane, Forêt. 'Jungle' is not the programme term (French school usage is 'forêt tropicale' / 'forêt équatoriale'); minor register point for the fr bank.
8. K-380: two pictograms are nearly identical - the child sneezing (hand to face, small cloud) and the child brushing teeth (hand to face, foam bubbles); neither holds the object. Tissue box and toothbrush are therefore easily swapped. Also the 'children' are adult-proportioned silhouettes while the instruction says 'chaque enfant'.
9. K-382: step 1 (wet hands) and step 4 (rinse) are the same drawing - hands under the running tap - distinguished only by three small bubbles in the basin; a maternelle child can swap 1 and 4 with a defensible reading.
10. G1-403: the scene of the child bent over the sink spitting foam, toothbrush still raised, can honestly be 'pendant' or 'après' - two defensible answers for one picture.
11. G1-404: row 2 shows hands under running water in BOTH pictures; the only difference is soap foam. A child can argue that rinsing with water also removes germs. Also the title names one gesture ('je tousse dans mon coude') while the page tests four (elbow, soap, own cup, tissue in the bin).
12. G2-379: instruction 'la phrase qui dit pourquoi on le fait' - the antecedent is 'chaque bonne habitude' (feminine); 'on la fait' / 'pourquoi on la pratique' / 'pourquoi c'est utile' is the correct French. Also, only the sleep pair sits straight across (row 2 to row 2).
13. G2-378: nobody paints - the brush stays standing in the pot in all four frames while the planks turn orange, so the child has no subject for 'qui peint ?' (the landing suggests deciding it with the class). Word bank 'barrière' is acceptable, though French children often say 'palissade' or 'clôture' for a picket fence.

17. G2-377: the model letter in the margin of each line (i, u, t) looks like the print/script letter, not the cursive exemplar the grey letters use; the child's model is not the letter he traces.
