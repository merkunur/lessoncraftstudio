## The skill
The sun, earth and moon move in patterns; the moon's shape seems to grow and shrink in a regular order.

## The apparatus and its invention
A LAMP, A GLOBE AND A MOON ON A TRACK. A lamp (apparatus) shines from the left; the library Earth sits in the middle; the library Moon rides a ring track around it. The child drags the Moon round the track; a round window "from Earth" shows the Moon's lit part computed from the angle (a white disc and a shadow, geometry). ORDER task (G1-391): phase cards (geometry) are laid on the track in order from a given start, growing or shrinking; a card out of order does not match the window and slides back. NAME task (G2-367): a phase lights in the window and the child picks its name card. DAY/NIGHT task (G2-368): pins with tiny library houses sit on Earth; the child turns Earth and taps day or night for each pin — the lamp decides. Miss 1: the window shows the true phase for the dropped place beside the card; Miss 2: the right place on the track glows.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Thinks the moon changes because the earth's shadow covers it, or that the moon makes its own light.
- **Pictures:** space (earth, moon), miscellaneous (house) for pins; lamp, track and phases are apparatus.
- **Settings:** Task: phases in order / name the phase / day and night · Phases: 4 / 8
- **Rounds:** 9 rounds with different starting phases and directions; name rounds cover each phase once per pass; day/night rounds 3–5 pins; one right answer.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-378, G1-391, G2-367, G2-368
- **Distinct from:** No space activity exists.
- **Build risk:** pt-BR: the lit side is mirrored in the southern hemisphere — a pt setting renders the window mirrored (decided by the pt panel). Phase names ×11 by the native panel.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 2 "The Orbit Lamp — Day, Night and the Moon" (orbit-lamp): Explain day and night by the Earth turning toward or away from the Sun, and name the Moon's phases from where the Moon is on its orbit. — A sun lamp on the left lights one half of a turning Earth (library earth, lit half bright, far half shaded). Pins with small library houses sit on the Earth; the child turns the Earth with a drag and taps day or night for each pin — the lamp decides. Moon mode: the Moon (library moon) can be dragged around the Earth on its orbit ring; a window shows how the Moon looks from Earth at that place (the lit part computed from the angle); the child names the phase or drags the Moon to make a named phase.
