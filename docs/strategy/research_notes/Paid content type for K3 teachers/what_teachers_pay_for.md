# What K-3 teachers and parents actually PAY for (US + Europe, 2024-2026)

> Method note: WebFetch was blocked for every domain in this environment, so every finding below comes from WebSearch result summaries (45 queries run before the session's search budget was exhausted). Facts marked **[summary-only]** were read from a search engine's page summary, not from the page itself, and should be spot-checked before being quoted externally. Prices carry currency + the year the summary attributed them to (or "as summarised 2025/26" when the page was undated). Conversion / subscriber numbers are given ONLY where a source stated them; otherwise "unknown".

## KQ1 — Teachers Pay Teachers: best-selling K-2 categories, formats, prices, what makes a bundle sell

### Takeaway
On TPT the money is in **bundles and product lines** (year-long / mega bundles at $30-$100+, auto-discounted ~20% vs the sum of parts), built from $3-$5 single items that act as discovery funnels; the K-2 sellers named as favourites sell no-prep centres, morning tubs, fine-motor and literacy/math activity sets. Order volume on TPT slid every year 2022-2024 (-2.2%, -5.1%, -1.3%) with a small rebound in Jan 2025; saturation (13M+ products) and ChatGPT are the named causes.

### Cited Findings
- Kindergarten sellers named as WeAreTeachers "favourites": Kindergarten Smorgasboard (bundled sets "at great prices"), The Printable Princess (activity tubs, fine-motor, math+literacy activities), The Moffatt Girls (no-prep bundle resources), Simply Kinder (bundles per subject/topic) **[summary-only]** — [WeAreTeachers](https://www.weareteachers.com/favorite-kindergarten-teachers-pay-teachers/)
- Typical price bands: single items $1-$5; bundles $10-$30; "Mega Bundles" for year-long use $50+ (USD, 2025) **[summary-only]** — [Accio, "Best selling products on TPT 2025"](https://www.accio.com/business/best-selling-products-on-teachers-pay-teachers-2025)
- 2025 TPT demand characterised as shifting toward "ready-to-go", standards-aligned resources plus student behaviour / SEL content **[summary-only, aggregator]** — [Accio](https://www.accio.com/business/best-selling-products-on-teachers-pay-teachers-2025)
- Bundle mechanics: sell each part as a $3-$5 standalone, then bundle so teachers filtering for cheap items discover the line and "upgrade"; single resources $3-$10 vs bundles $30 / $50 / $100+ per sale; TPT auto-discounts bundles 20% and "a 20% discount typically shows clear savings and increases conversions" **[summary-only]** — [Lindsay Bowden, product lines & bundles](https://lindsaybowden.com/podcast/create-product-lines-and-bundles/); [TPT Seller Blog on pricing](https://sellerblog.teacherspayteachers.com/how-to-price-your-tpt-products/); [It's Lit Teaching](https://itslitteaching.com/how-i-made-1000-in-my-sixth-month-selling-on-teachers-pay-teachers/)
- Seller advice: keep resources themed to an end-bundle; expand best-sellers into lines across grade levels, seasons, skills **[summary-only]** — [Miss Becca's Classroom](https://www.missbeccasclassroom.com/2025/01/03/tpt-seller-tips-create-products-that-will-sell/)
- K-2 format vocabulary confirmed on TPT browse pages: morning work / morning tubs / soft starts, sub tubs, decodable readers & passages (marketed as "science of reading aligned"), centres, task cards, assessments, digital practice — [TPT kindergarten category](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse/elementary/kindergarten); [TPT decodable readers](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse?search=decodable+readers+kindergarten); [Natalie Lynn Kindergarten on morning tubs](https://natalielynnkindergarten.com/morning-tubs-in-kindergarten/)
- TPT order counts: 2022 -2.2% vs 2021; 2023 -5.1%; 2024 -1.3%; Jan 2025 +3.5% **[summary-only; source is a seller-SEO blog, methodology not visible]** — [SEOTpreneur](https://seotpreneur.com/your-tpt-sales-slump-might-be-because-of-this/)
- "AI services like ChatGPT are taking potential customers off of the TPT platform"; 13M+ products on TPT in 2025 (vs 151 in April 2006) **[summary-only]** — [Teach Create Sell, "Are TpT sales down 2024"](https://teachcreatesell.com/are-tpt-sales-down-2024/); [It's Lit Teaching, "5 ways selling on TPT has changed"](https://itslitteaching.com/selling-on-teachers-pay-teachers-has-changed/)
- Scale of the buyer base: "more than 7 million educators, including 85 percent of PK-12 teachers in the U.S." use TPT (IXL press release, March 2023); IXL also owns ABCya, Education.com, Rosetta Stone, Vocabulary.com, Wyzant — [IXL press release](https://www.ixl.com/materials/us/press/Press_release__IXL_Learning_acquires_TPT.pdf); [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/strategy-operations/ixl-learning-acquires-teachers-pay-teachers/2023/03)
- Buying calendar: TPT runs site-wide sales 4-6×/year; August is "when the charts start to swing back up" ending the summer slump, with a major back-to-school sale in the last week of August; a "New Year, New Resources" sale in the first/second week of January **[summary-only]** — [TPT Seller Blog, search trends Aug-Oct](https://sellerblog.teacherspayteachers.com/search-trends-on-tpt-in-august-september-and-october/); [Rhoda Design Studio](https://www.rhodadesignstudio.com/summer-sales/); [Lindsay Bowden BTS sale](https://lindsaybowden.com/tpt-back-to-shool-sale/); [timetobuying](https://timetobuying.com/when-does-teachers-pay-teachers-have-sales/)
- Canva: "Canva Education is 100% free... will remain free for K-12 educators"; Canva Education accounts may NOT be used to make TPT products (Canva Free/Pro may); for sellers "Canva can eliminate the need to pay for outside clip art and fonts" **[summary-only]** — [Canva for Teachers](https://www.canva.com/education/teachers/); [Lindsay Bowden, selling on TPT using Canva](https://lindsaybowden.com/selling-on-tpt-using-canva/)

### Inferences
- The K-2 paid categories that persist are those with **prep cost the teacher cannot offload to ChatGPT**: physical-format centres/tubs (print-cut-laminate systems), sequenced decodable sets, assessment/intervention systems, and year-long "everything" bundles. Generic single worksheets are the $1-$3 discovery layer, not the revenue.
- The whole marketplace is drifting down (~-8% orders cumulative 2022-24) while product count explodes → per-product revenue is collapsing far faster than platform revenue; a new entrant cannot win on generic worksheet volume.
- The 20%-auto-discount bundle + $3-5 part is the proven "ladder": cheap entry item → line → bundle. The equivalent for a subscription site is a metered free tier → per-pack purchase → all-access.

### Gaps
- No public TPT category-level sales ranking exists; the "best-selling categories" statements are seller-blog and aggregator opinion, not marketplace data.
- Could not source TPT School Access (purchase-order) pricing or the share of TPT revenue paid by schools vs personal cards (search budget ran out before this query).
- No hard data on clip-art/font seller revenue collapse; only the mechanism (Canva makes them redundant) is documented.

## KQ2 — Subscription products: pricing, what is gated, subscriber numbers (US + Europe)

### Takeaway
Teacher-paid personal subscriptions cluster at **€5-€11 / $7-$16 per month, i.e. ~$45-$130/year**, gated on *volume* (3 free items/month, 3 free activities) and *creation/organisation* features; classroom software with student accounts and data is priced at **$70-$300/classroom/year** and is increasingly school-paid only (Seesaw, Lalilo dropped individual teacher plans in 2025). Twinkl is the scale reference: £78.6m 2024 revenue, ~£98.9m group turnover FY to April 2025, "6 million customers".

## KQ3 — Freemium-to-paid conversion benchmarks and what moves them

### Takeaway
Published benchmarks put EdTech **freemium** conversion at roughly **2.6%-5%** (one source 5-8%), versus **~22-30% for time-limited free trials**; the documented levers used by the teacher products above are hard volume meters (3 items/month, 3 activities ever), creation/editing tools, reporting, and removal of individual free plans altogether.

### Cited Findings
- First Page Sage (80+ SaaS clients 2021-25): average freemium→paid 3.7%, EdTech **2.6%** (lowest), RegTech 5.8% **[summary-only]** — [Userpilot, freemium-to-premium](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/)
- 3-5% "respectable" for self-serve freemium at $5-50M ARR — [Fiscallion](https://www.fiscallion.io/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-saas)
- Education/EdTech trial-to-paid ~24.8%; "25-30% considered healthy"; another aggregator: EdTech freemium 5-8%, trials up to 22% **[summary-only; aggregator quality varies]** — [Nalpeiron](https://nalpeiron.com/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-rate); [productgrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/insights/edtech/free-to-paid/); [Geneo](https://geneo.app/query-reports/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks)
- Why EdTech converts poorly: "the paid trigger (compliance risk, seat-based team use) is sharper" in legal/RegTech; EdTech is consumer-adjacent **[summary-only]** — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/)
- K-12 B2B PLG benchmarks exist (Graham Forman) — [Medium](https://grahamforman.medium.com/key-product-led-growth-plg-measures-and-benchmarks-for-k12-b2b-edtech-companies-7082ffe6c358)
- Observed meters: Education.com 3 items/month; Wordwall 3 activities lifetime; Boom 5 students/1 class; Epic free only in school hours; Squla free only in school hours; Seesaw & Lalilo removed free/individual teacher tiers (2025) — sources in KQ2.
- Wordwall reached 100k paid subscribers (2021) → "600,000+ subscribed educators" on its pricing page; 51M activities created — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/wordwall); [Wordwall price plans](https://wordwall.net/price-plans)

### Inferences
- Wordwall's ratio (600k subscribers vs 51M created activities and 1M daily visitors) is consistent with low-single-digit conversion of registered users, in line with the 2.6-5% benchmark. Exact rate unknown.
- The strongest observed conversion lever in this category is a **lifetime or monthly count meter on the thing the teacher already uses**, not on premium extras — every high-volume freemium here meters volume first and sells creation/organisation second.
- Free-for-teacher / paid-for-parent (Squla, Epic) and free-in-class-hours designs convert the *household*, not the teacher; the classroom becomes the acquisition channel.

### Gaps
- No product in scope publishes its own conversion rate; all figures are cross-industry benchmarks.
- No evidence found isolating which single feature (editable vs answer key vs ad-free) lifts conversion most; only the bundle of meters/features each product chose.

## KQ4 — Who pays (personal card vs school PO), seasonal calendar, price thresholds, European differences

### Takeaway
US teachers spend **~$655-$895/year out of pocket** (2025 surveys) against a median school supply budget of $200; French primary teachers average **€297-€410/yr** personal spend; German teachers ~€585/yr; Austrian €550/yr — so a personal €5-10/month tool is well inside existing spend, while $200+/classroom systems need a PO. Buying peaks in August (back-to-school) with a secondary January peak.

### Cited Findings
- US: average out-of-pocket $895 in 2024-25 (AdoptAClassroom, spring 2025) vs $655 (DonorsChoose 2025); up 49% since 2015; median school-provided supply budget $200; 97% say budget insufficient; supplies +7.3% cost into 2025-26 **[summary-only]** — [AdoptAClassroom 2025 survey](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/2025/06/09/2025-teacher-survey-spending-stats-classroom-needs/); [19th News](https://19thnews.org/2025/08/teachers-spending-school-supplies-debt-crowdfunding/); [NEA](https://www.nea.org/nea-today/all-news-articles/rising-cost-school-supplies-increases-burden-teachers)
- France: SE-Unsa survey of 12,000 teachers (March 2024): 92% spent ≥€300 personal money, a quarter >€500, mostly primary, mean €410; another survey mean €297, median €200; 92.2% of primary teachers use own money **[summary-only]** — [Scolinfo](https://www.scolinfo.net/92-des-enseignants-depensent-plus-de-300-euros-par-an-dargent-personnel-pour-leur-classe/); [Anadolu Agency FR](https://www.aa.com.tr/fr/monde/france-des-enseignants-d%C3%A9noncent-le-recours-croissant-%C3%A0-leurs-fonds-personnels-pour-financer-les-fournitures-scolaires/3772483); [Ekole](https://www.ekole.fr/blog/plus-de-90-des-enseignants-du-1er-degre-financent-eux-memes-des-fournitures-scolaires)
- Germany: Lehrerfreund survey — teachers invest "fast 600 Euro" (€585) per year of private money; Grundschule teachers of 7-8-year-olds feel most obliged (67%) **[summary-only; survey date not visible, likely older]** — [Lehrerfreund](https://www.lehrerfreund.de/schule/1s/lehrer-ausgaben-unterrichtsmaterial/4435); [Betzold blog, was Lehrer selbst bezahlen](https://www.betzold.de/blog/was-lehrer-selbst-bezahlen/)
- Austria: ÖBV/JKU Linz study (2,000+ respondents): €550/yr average personal spend; 99% fund materials such as copies themselves; ≈€70m/yr nationally **[summary-only]** — [kosmo.at](https://www.kosmo.at/550-euro-privat-so-viel-zahlen-oesterreichs-lehrer-fuer-den-unterricht/); [meinbezirk.at](https://www.meinbezirk.at/c-lokales/viele-lehrer-zahlen-unterrichtsmaterial-aus-eigener-tasche_a8560678)
- School-paid patterns: Gynzy (NL) priced by school pupil count; Bingel (BE) per pupil per subject; Worksheet Crafter (DE) school seat packs in 5s; MatematikFessor (DK) school licence 10,000 DKK; Skolestudio (NO) per-pupil packages; Seesaw/Lalilo school-only in 2025; ESGI volume discounts from 2 licences — sources in KQ2.
- Teacher-free / parent-paid: Squla free in class, €10.99/mo at home; Epic free 7-15h school days, $9.99/mo family — sources in KQ2.
- Calendar: TPT August upswing + late-August site-wide sale; early-January "New Year" sale; 4-6 site-wide sales/yr — [TPT Seller Blog](https://sellerblog.teacherspayteachers.com/search-trends-on-tpt-in-august-september-and-october/); [timetobuying](https://timetobuying.com/when-does-teachers-pay-teachers-have-sales/)
- Twinkl DE annual ≈ €5/mo (=€60/yr); Wordwall €45-67.50/yr; Education.com $60-120/yr; Boom $6.99/mo; Starfall teacher $70/yr; Kami $99-149/yr — the personal tier cluster (sources in KQ2).

### Inferences
- The personal-card ceiling implied by the market is **~€50-€70/yr in Europe and ~$60-$120/yr in the US**; everything above ~$150 is packaged as a *classroom licence* with per-student caps and a quote path, signalling PO purchase.
- Germany's Lehrmittelfreiheit covers textbooks, not teacher-made supplementary material, which is why the DE ecosystem is per-download (eduki) and micro-contribution (Zaubereinmaleins) rather than subscription. (Inference — the Lehrmittelfreiheit query itself could not be run.)
- NL/BE/Nordic primary teachers rarely pay personally for pupil-facing platforms because the school licence model is universal there; a teacher-paid product in those markets must be a *teacher tool* (prep, printing, differentiation), not a pupil platform.

### Gaps
- Direct evidence of the "$50/yr personal ceiling" or "$8-10/month" threshold as a stated survey finding — not found; the figure above is inferred from the price clustering.
- Share of TPT/Twinkl revenue from school POs vs personal cards — unknown.
- Nordic and Dutch teacher personal-spend surveys — not searched (budget).

## KQ5 — Categories that have COLLAPSED in willingness to pay (free AI, free curricula, Canva)

### Takeaway
Evidence of collapse is indirect but consistent: TPT order volume fell three years running while listings passed 13M, sellers name ChatGPT as a demand leak, Canva's free education tier removes the need to buy clip art/fonts, and Twinkl's answer has been to launch its own AI (Ari) and grow into a £99m group — i.e. incumbents are absorbing "generation" into the subscription rather than selling it per item.

### Cited Findings
- TPT orders -2.2% (2022), -5.1% (2023), -1.3% (2024); ChatGPT "taking potential customers off the platform"; 13M+ products **[summary-only]** — [SEOTpreneur](https://seotpreneur.com/your-tpt-sales-slump-might-be-because-of-this/); [Teach Create Sell](https://teachcreatesell.com/are-tpt-sales-down-2024/)
- Seller community asking "Is AI going to replace TPT sellers?" (Lindsay Bowden, Facebook) — [Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/thelindsaybowden/posts/is-ai-going-to-replace-tpt-sellersi-get-why-people-are-asking-this-it-feels-like/1747356546789309/)
- Canva Education free for K-12 with "no hidden fees, paywalls, or trial periods"; Canva "can eliminate the need to pay for outside clip art and fonts" **[summary-only]** — [Canva](https://www.canva.com/education/teachers/); [Lindsay Bowden](https://lindsaybowden.com/selling-on-tpt-using-canva/)
- Twinkl launched "Ari" AI and grew group turnover £79m → £98.9m (FY2025) **[summary-only]** — [Education Matters](https://educationmattersmag.com.au/twinkl-ai-empowering-teachers-with-purpose-built-support/); [The Assignment Report](https://www.theassignmentreport.com/news/view,revenue-up-58-at-twinkl_18697.htm)
- Wordwall made its AI generator available to all paid subscribers (2025) — a paid-tier feature, not free — [Wordwall on X](https://x.com/getwordwall/status/1914212901889454534)
- eduki has added "a broad AI suite" alongside the 700k-material marketplace **[summary-only]** — [Campixx](https://www.campixx.de/tool/e-learning/eduki/)
- Free curricula that displace paid: Epic School free in school hours; Squla free in class; Canva free; Freckle free tier; UFLI — **not verified by search (budget)**.

### Inferences
- Commoditised: generic printable worksheets, plain lesson plans, clip art, fonts, single-skill drills — anything ChatGPT + Canva can produce in minutes. Still paid: sequenced systems (decodables, phonemic-awareness routines like Heggerty's 35-week manual), assessment with data (ESGI), self-checking digital practice with reports (Boom), interactive game templates (Wordwall), and time-saving *tooling* (Worksheet Crafter, Twinkl Create).
- Incumbents now sell AI as a *gated feature of the subscription* (Wordwall, Twinkl Ari, eduki), which means "AI-generated" itself carries no premium; the premium is curation + tooling + organisation.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on Twinkl subscriber churn, TPT clip-art category revenue, or UFLI's effect on paid phonics sales.
