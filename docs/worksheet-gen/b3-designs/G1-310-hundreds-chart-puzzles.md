Six numbered cards (2 x 3) each hold one piece: a teal-outlined shape (3x3, plus, L, T) with a creamDeep drop shadow, lifted off the chart. One cell is tealSoft and prints its number; every other cell is white with a grey dashed answer square. The child reasons from the anchor (right = +1, down = +10) and writes the rest. Loose pieces, not fragments drawn on a board: drawn in place the page collapses into G1-129 and the child counts along the board; loose pieces force the 2-D place-value step. The apparatus is numerals only; the instruction is the only sentence.

### F1 : Where Does the Piece Go? (`mode:'place'`, G1)
**Move:** NUMBER -> POSITION: read a fully printed piece, find its place on an almost empty board (tens = row, ones = column), write its numbers into the board. **Layout:** `chartOutline({cell:46, guides:'edges'})` 468x468 centred + 14 + four `chartFragment({printed:'all', cell:48, fontSize:20})` 152x152 in a row, gap 22 (674 <= 675); height 634 <= 722. No badge and no letter on pieces (a letter reads as a chart character; the printed values identify the piece); pieces stamp `data-lcs-piece="k"`; board targets `data-lcs-target="k" data-lcs-answer`, nothing printed there. Pieces never cover a printed guide (`'edges'` = row 1 + column 1; `'rich'` also column 10) and are disjoint. d1: 3 pieces at cell 56, `guides:'rich'`; d3: 4 pieces at cell 48, `guides:'corners'` (the pedagogy's 5 pieces do not fit: 5 x 140 + 48 = 748 > 675 at the 44 floor). **Verify:** each piece's derived `(row(v), col(v))` carries `data-lcs-target=k` with `data-lcs-answer === v`; no target on a guide; targets disjoint (P6); printed values position-consistent; no value in two PIECES (a board target equalling its piece value is the one sanctioned "twice" on this type, exempt from P3); pieces === `d.pieces`. **Query face:** "where does the piece go" / "Wo passt das Teil hin" / "var passar biten".
