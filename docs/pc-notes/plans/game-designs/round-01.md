## 2. Concept & creative hook
**Rolo the gopher tends a long meadow PATH lined with lantern-posts — one at every ten (and on the big trail, one at every hundred). At dusk a little traveler drifts in and settles at an exact spot on the path; in the dark it can't find an exact spot, only the NEAREST cozy lantern — so the child helps Rolo tuck each traveler into the nearest lantern-post to spend the night safe.** The path IS the number-line; the lantern-posts are the multiples; a glowing marker-STONE marks the midpoint.
- **WHY WE ROUND, made felt (the estimation flavor — pedagogy's WHY):** a traveler lost in the dark needs "the nearest lantern," not an exact unfindable number — rounding stops being a digit-trick and becomes an act of getting-someone-safely-to-the-nearest-cozy-stop. That felt need is the motivation, never a separate scored task.
- **THE MIDPOINT IS EARNED, NOT HANDED (the critic's deepest fix — the comparison kill):** the child doesn't get a labeled "45" to compare against. On every decision round they CONSTRUCT the model: name the two lanterns that HUG the traveler's number (which two tens surround 47? = the place-value act) → tap the halfway spot to set the marker-STONE (which reveals "45" only AFTER) → then tuck the traveler into the nearer lantern. A child who can't bracket 47 between 40 and 50, or can't find the halfway, cannot proceed — so place-value reasoning is the ONLY path; a `compare(47, given-45)` shortcut has no given midpoint to read.
- **NEAREST-100 — THE DIGIT-RULE EXECUTIONER:** on the big trail (200↔300, midpoint 250) the TENS digit decides and the ones are NOISE — 247→200 (a last-digit "7→up" reader gets 300, WRONG; vary the ones 0-9 and the answer never moves). Interleaving the ten-trail and the hundred-trail means no single-digit habit survives.
- **THE SCHEMATIC DECIDE-BEAT (the pixel kill):** the path is drawn to-scale while Rolo TEACHES (the reveal shows 47 really does sit past 45) — but at the DECIDE moment the lanterns are spaced EVENLY (schematic), so "which is nearer" can't be eyeballed from a pixel-gap; the child decides from the NUMBER they bracketed, not a measured distance.
- **THE PAYOFF:** the right lantern → it LIGHTS, the traveler curls up safe, the meadow grows brighter (a `GameCollection` lantern-meadow — non-numeric AND non-content, NO count). `ctx.correct()`. A wrong tuck → no fail; Rolo gently shows the distance ("that lantern's farther — look, 47 is past the halfway stone, nearer 50") and the child re-tucks (the nudge TEACHES nearness — F-7).
- **Anti-generic statement:** NOT round-by-the-last-digit — (a) nearest-100 + the `DIGIT_RULE_SOLVER` kill the ones-digit rule; (b) the schematic decide-beat + the `PIXEL_POSITION_SOLVER` kill the pixel-gap eyeball; (c) the construct-then-decide re-sequence + the `MIDPOINT_COMPARISON_SOLVER` kill the handed-midpoint comparison (the deepest fix); (d) the tie taught honestly (equidistance-ack, not ends-in-5); the gate proves digit-rule + pixel + midpoint-comparison + brute-tap + first/last/random solvers ALL fail while the nearness oracle passes.
