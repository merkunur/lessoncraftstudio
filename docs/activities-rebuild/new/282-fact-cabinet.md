## The skill
Organise true facts about an animal under headings (eats, lives, moves, body covering, babies), and compare two animals' facts as same or different.

## The apparatus and its invention
A filing cabinet with five drawers, each with an icon heading (food, home, movement, skin/fur/feathers, babies). Fact cards about the round's animal come off a pile; the child files each in its drawer. A card that is TRUE for another animal but not this one does not fit any drawer and slides to a "not this animal" tray — the child must judge truth too. Two-animal mode: two cabinets side by side and a shared middle drawer; facts true for both go in the middle.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Filing by the word on the card ("lives" in every card) instead of the meaning, and accepting any animal fact as true for this animal.
- **Pictures:** animals / zoo animals / ocean life / birds / insects (one large picture per cabinet); heading icons drawn as apparatus
- **Settings:** Mode: one animal / compare two · Cards: 5 / 8 with false cards
- **Rounds:** ≥ 8 animals per mode with native-checked fact sets; the card order is shuffled and false cards appear at random positions.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-318, G2-339, G2-340, G2-341, G2-342
- **Distinct from:** Atlas's Fact Files reads one fact for one question; Linc's Fact Chain links two facts; Willow compares stories. Sorting facts into headings and comparing animal facts is not taught.
- **Build risk:** Headings are written words (eats · lives · moves · body covering · babies) — reading them is part of the G2 task — plus a library picture where one fits (plate, house, feather). Animal facts must be scientifically right in every locale (one science + native reviewer).
