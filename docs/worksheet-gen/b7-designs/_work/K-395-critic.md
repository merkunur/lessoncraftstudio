## 5 Quality verdict

I would print the base, F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F8 and F10 for my class tomorrow if the pictures looked like the plain sheets I opened: two big line pictures one above the other, a column of boxes to tick, no clutter, a difference a five-year-old can name in every ring, and a fold-and-hold check on the mirror page that children will do twice for fun. What would embarrass me is a rich picture with two chicks floating in the sky, a tulip swapped for a different tulip, or a 7-page whose seven rings sit in the same seven spots on every copy the Level Set programme regenerates, and those are exactly the three places where the designs' numbers did not survive the real composer: the rich copies are still being rebuilt, the same-key swap is now refused everywhere, and the 7-face ships as two honest pairs until a one-pair unit passes the position gate. The two things that must land before anyone builds are the `heroFront` knob (without it the bunny on the word page almost never changes and the word bank quietly teaches "tick everything but the animal") and the engineer's own read of every pinned rich sheet, because every rich number here is a measurement of data that is about to change.
