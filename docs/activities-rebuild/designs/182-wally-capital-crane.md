## 1. The pitch
"Wally's crane will lift a little letter up to a capital — but only if you hang the word on the hook that says WHY: the start of a sentence, a name, a place, a day/month/holiday, or a brand; a word that has no reason slides right back down."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- A real working crane on a building site of words: tap a word, the crane swings over (a smooth 400 ms arc with a creak), the hook drops on its cable, and you choose which of the hook's **reason tags** to clip it to. Up it goes — and on the way up the letter **changes shape** as it crosses the line: *p* stretches into *P*, *t* into *T*, *s* into *S*. Children watch the shape-change every time.
- The decoys make it a detective game: *city* or *Paris*? *blocks* or *Lego*? *summer* or *July*? You must have a reason, and the crane asks for it.
- The earned show: at Check every lifted word is lowered back into the sentence standing TALL; the dashed **cap line** drawn across the writing lines lights up and the tall capitals poke up through it like a little skyline; Wally's crane toots once and the sentence reads.
- Free play: after a cure, tap any capital and the crane gently lifts it again and swings it — tap a lowercase decoy and the crane tries, the hook swings, nothing holds, and the word stays down.

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (L.2.2.a): capitalize holidays, product names and geographic names (and, from L.1.2.a, the start of a sentence, names of people and pets, days and months in en).
- Misconceptions and the push-back:
  - *"Capitalize important or big words"* (Christmas Celebrations, my Dog) → a word lifted on a hook that cannot hold it slides down the cable at Check and its capital shrinks back into the small letter: the hook tests the REASON, not the feeling.
  - *"Only names of people get capitals"* → place, special-day and brand hooks hold *Texas*, *Monday*, *Lego*; a child who leaves them down sees the gap in the skyline at Check.
  - *"Capital = bigger letter"* → the crane shows the capital as a different SHAPE (g→G, a→A, e→E), not a magnified small letter.
  - Locale-specific (§9): English capitalizes days and months, Swedish and Finnish do not; German capitalizes every noun. Each locale's crane carries only its own hooks — a Swedish *måndag* has no hook to hang on.

## 5. The hand
- **Tap a word** → the trolley runs along the jib to above it (300 ms, 100 ms response), the hook lowers to the word (200 ms), and the reason ring opens (5 tags fan out around the hook, 60 ms stagger).
- **Tap a reason tag** → the hook clips the word's FIRST LETTER and lifts it up the cable; crossing the x-line the letter morphs to its capital (180 ms cross-fade + stretch), and it settles back in place standing tall, with the chosen tag hanging small above it. Two-word names (*San Diego*, *New York*, *Lake Erie*) are two taps (two letters).
- **Tap a lifted word** → the crane lowers it (undo): the letter shrinks back.
- **Tap outside / the word again with the ring open** → the ring closes (no lift).
- **Check** → the crane tests every hook (§7).
- Keyboard and sound: a creak on swing, a soft clunk on clip, a little tick when the letter crosses the line.

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
- **Check with nothing lifted** → bubble **"Tap a word that needs a capital and choose why."** No miss.
- **The test (every Check)**: the crane runs along the sentence; every lifted word's tag is tested: the right reason → the tag flashes and the capital stays; a wrong reason or no reason at all → the word slides down the cable and shrinks back (300 ms), its tag swinging empty. Words that needed a capital and were not lifted stay small, and the cap line above them shows a GAP in the skyline.
- **Miss 1** → after the test, each wrongly lifted word keeps a faint tag outline of the reason the child chose with a small `?` drawn in it; each gap in the skyline gets a soft hook-shadow above the word that needs a lift (the COUNT of missing capitals is shown, not the reasons). Bubble **"Look at the gaps in the skyline. And check your reasons: is it really a name, a place, a day, month or holiday, or a brand?"** (de: **"Kann *der, die* oder *das* davor stehen?"** — authored in de.)
- **Miss 2** → the hook-shadows show the reason icon each word needs; wrong lifts' tags show the right icon (or an empty hook if no reason exists). Bubble **"These hooks show the reason. Lift the words again."**
- A new lift clears the help until the next Check.
- **Success** → every capital stands tall, the cap line lights end to end, the crane toots and the sentence reads. Bubble **"Every capital has its reason: “{sentence}”"** + a one-line reason list in the bubble (e.g., "On — start · Monday — day/month/holiday · Texas — place").

## 8. Settings
1. **Level**: One special name (L1, default) · Start and names (L2) · Tricky words (L3).
2. **Reasons**: choose a reason (default) · lift only — the ring is skipped and any lift counts as long as the word needs a capital (for younger children or EAL starters; the test still drops wrong lifts).

  - **fr / es / it / pt / nl**: start · name · place · day/month/holiday (FEASTS only: fr *Noël*, *Pâques*; es *Navidad*; it *Natale*; pt *Natal*, *Páscoa*; nl *Kerstmis*, *Pasen*) · brand.   - Brands: only internationally known product names (Lego); each locale picks two it knows (the reviewer confirms they are capitalized brands there). ⚠ In sv/da/no *lego* is commonly written small as a generic noun ("bygga med lego") — the Nordic author confirms or picks another brand; a brand that the locale writes small is a DECOY there, never a target.
  - **Landmark and geographic names (L3 rounds 1, 3, 9)**: English capitalises the generic word inside the name (*Lake Erie, Eiffel Tower*); **fr/es/it/pt keep the generic word small** — *le lac Érié, la tour Eiffel, la torre Eiffel, a torre Eiffel* — and **de/nl/sv/da/no/fi write one compound** (*Eiffelturm, Eiffeltoren, Eiffeltornet*). So the en round 9 inversion ("tower IS part of the name") is en-only; in fr/es/it/pt the small *tour/torre/lac/lago* is the decoy beside the capital name. Never translate these rounds.
  - The capital morph uses the locale's letters (å→Å, ø→Ø, ä→Ä, é→É, ñ→Ñ, ç→Ç); Dutch *ij* at a sentence start capitalizes as a pair (*IJsland*) — nl rounds avoid initial *ij* except one deliberate L3 round.

## 12. Build plan
- **Core** `capital-crane-core.js`: per-locale `HOOKS[loc]` (ids + icons); round `{ id, level, tokens:[{w, needs?:reason[] }] }` — a word's `needs` is the list of reasons that make it capital (usually one; a first-word name may list two); state `{ lifts: Map(tokenIndex → reason) }`; `test(state)` → per token `held|slid|gap`; `isRight = every token with needs is lifted with a reason ∈ needs AND no token without needs is lifted`. The capital form is derived (`toLocaleUpperCase(loc)` of the first letter, plus nl *ij* pair rule).
- **Gate** `verify-capital-crane-core.js`: every round ×11 — every `needs` reason exists in that locale's HOOKS; no sv/da/no/fi round expects a day/month/holiday capital; no fr/es/it/pt/nl round expects a day or month capital; de: every noun token needs Nomen (the oracle reads a native-authored noun list per round, not the round's own tags) and every non-noun is unlifted; ≥ 1 decoy per L1/L3 round; L2 capitals-per-round not constant; target position spread (no word index > 35 % of a locale's targets); unique-longest-word tell ≤ 35 %; the cured text equals the native author's cured sentence.
- **Mutations**: accept any reason · allow lifting decoys · special-day hook present in sv · days capitalized in fr · capital by bigger font instead of the real uppercase letter · grade before Check · help persists after a new lift · skyline gap not shown for an unlifted target · same order twice.
- **Risks**: product names are trademarks — keep to *Lego* / *Oreo*-class household names already in the old deck; the gate refuses any brand not on a reviewed per-locale list.
- Pictures: none. BLOCKED: nothing.

## 13. The four questions
1. **Choose it again?** Yes — a crane, a hook, letters that change shape in mid-air, and decoys to outwit.
2. **Teacher on the board?** Yes — "every capital needs a reason" is the rule teachers chant; the hooks make the reasons explicit and per-locale correct.
3. **Parent trust?** Correct rules for the child's own language (no English capitals forced on Swedish months), no scores, calm.
4. **The invention:** the Reason Hooks — Wally's crane only lifts a letter to a capital if you hang the word on the hook that says why, and a word with no reason slides back down.
