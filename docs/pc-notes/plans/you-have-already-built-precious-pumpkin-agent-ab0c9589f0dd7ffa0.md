## 1. GRADE RULING — **årskurs 1**

**Ruling: åk 1.** Decisive.

I reached this without reference to the German Klasse 1 decision; it coincides, but the German
reasoning (Zahlenraum bis 20 / Zehnerübergang is Klasse 1 in every Bundesland's Lehrplan) is not
evidence about Sweden and I did not use it.

### Why, in Swedish terms

**(a) Lgr22 cannot decide the year, and does not pretend to.** The matematik centralt innehåll is
stated once for the whole åk 1–3 band. The two clauses this activity sits under —
*hur talen kan delas upp* (how numbers can be split) and *centrala metoder för beräkningar med
naturliga tal vid huvudräkning* — are band-level. Lgr22 tells me the content belongs to lågstadiet.
It does not and cannot tell me the year. The progression does.

**(b) The Skolverket Bedömningsstöd i taluppfattning places it at the end-of-åk-1 marker.** This is
the mandatory diagnostic in åk 1 and it is the closest thing Sweden has to a codified year-by-year
map. As I use it in class, the ladder runs: secure addition/subtraktion **inom 10** at the first
avstämning; **uppdelning av tal och tiokamrater used to cross the ten inom 20** at the next; **inom
100** after that. Bridging ten within 20 is therefore an åk-1 target that is *checked* at the åk-1/åk-2
seam — not an åk-2 introduction.

**(c) The textbooks split, and the split is informative rather than fatal.**
- **Favorit matematik 1B** — introduces *tiotalsövergång inom 20* in the spring term of åk 1. (Finnish
  lineage; the original *Tuhattaituri* does the same.)
- **Singma 1B** — "göra tio" / *tiotalsövergång* is grade 1. (Singapore lineage; make-ten is a Grade 1
  strategy there by design.)
- **Prima Matematik** and **Matte Direkt Safari** — slower: åk 1 secures 0–20 largely *utan*
  övergång, and the övergång is consolidated in 2A.

So: the two internationally-derived series (which are the two most common in Swedish classrooms
right now) put it in åk 1; the two older Swedish series treat it as åk-1-late/åk-2-early. **No
series puts it later than early åk 2, and none puts it earlier than åk 1.** An åk-1 chip is correct
for half the market and correct-as-revision for the other half. An åk-2 chip would be *wrong* for
Favorit and Singma classes — a teacher would skip a card labelled åk 2 that her class needs in
March of åk 1.

**(d) The catalogue ladder settles it.** The existing sv rungs are:

| rung | slug | content |
|---|---|---|
| förskoleklass | `tiokompisar-gor-10` | bonds to 10 |
| **? — this card** | — | **bridge the ten inom 20** |
| åk 2 | `addition-med-tiotalsovergang-inom-100-ak-2` | bridge the ten inom 100 |

Placing this at åk 2 would put two structurally different rungs on the same year and leave åk 1
with nothing between "tiokompisar" in F-klass and "inom 100" in åk 2 — a two-year hole across
exactly the transition where Swedish children most need scaffolding. The three-rung shape
**F-klass: bonds → åk 1: bridge inom 20 → åk 2: bridge inom 100** *is* the canonical Swedish
progression, in every series, including the slow ones (they just run it slightly late).

**(e) Standing project rule applies:** the grade chip is a *placement* signal, not a capability
claim. åk 1 tells a teacher where this sits in the sequence. It does not assert that every åk-1
child can do it in October.

### ⭐ (f) DIRECT ANSWER — **No. Within-20 and within-100 cannot both sit at åk 2.**

Asked explicitly, so answered explicitly: **the within-20 rung has to be åk 1 for the ladder to read
as a progression at all.** Three reasons, in the order I would give them in a staff room.

**They are not two difficulties of one thing. They are two different objects.**
Bridging ten *inom 20* has **one** ten, and it is a **place** — the child can see it, point at it,
stand on it. That is why a number line works. Bridging *inom 100* has **many** tens, and the ten is
no longer a place but a **unit** — for 47 + 8 the child must first work out *which* ten she is
crossing (50, not 10), which presupposes tvåsiffriga tal and positionssystemet, i.e. that a *tiotal*
is a thing you count in. Lgr22 lists that understanding separately from *hur talen kan delas upp*.
A child can be completely secure on 8 + 6 and completely lost on 47 + 8, and in åk 1 most are.
Putting both at åk 2 asserts they are one skill, and they are not.

**A parent sees only the two chips, and two identical chips make the within-20 card look like the
remedial version of the other one.** This is the part I would press hardest. If both read *åk 2*,
the only thing separating them on screen is *inom 20* versus *inom 100* inside the title — and a
range difference under the same year chip does not read as **sequence**, it reads as **difficulty
within one year**. The parent concludes the within-20 card is the easy variant for children who are
behind, picks the åk-2-appropriate one, and never opens the other. So: **if I ruled åk 2, the honest
answer to "what then distinguishes them?" is *nothing a parent can see* — and that is precisely the
argument against ruling åk 2.** The grade chip is the only sequencing signal the catalogue has at
browse level; spending it on a distinction the two cards do not have destroys it.

Note this is the same harm class I refused in §2, running the other way. There I refused to **split**
a bucket; here I refuse to **collapse** one. Both defects are read by a teacher as "the navigation
does not know what it is doing."

**It would empty åk 1 of the thing åk 1 is.** Swedish åk 1 is, more than anything else, the year of
talområdet 0–20. If the catalogue's åk-1 arithmetic rung is blank, the gap is not a missing card —
it is a missing **year**, sitting between two occupied rungs. A ladder may not skip a rung in the
middle; that is the one thing a ladder cannot do and still be a ladder.

### ⭐ (g) The 3/3 locale split is not a tie to be broken — it is evidence, and it resolves for åk 1

de / fr / nl at year one; es / pt / it at year two. Not a vote, and I would not treat it as one even
if voting were allowed here. But the split is not random, and the reason it happened tells me which
side Sweden is on.

**The three year-two locales all have a curriculum that explicitly defers**, and the three year-one
locales all have one that explicitly codifies within-20 in year 1 (de Zehnerübergang in Klasse 1;
fr CP *franchir la dizaine*; nl *rekenen over het tiental tot 20* in groep 3). Lgr22 does **neither**
— åk 1–3 is one undifferentiated block — so Sweden cannot inherit either side's reasoning and falls
to progression plus ladder, which is what §1(b)–(d) did.

But there is a structural fact that decides which side Sweden *resembles*, and it is the one the
split is actually tracking:

> **Sweden has a compulsory förskoleklass, and the catalogue has already spent a rung on it.**

`tiokompisar-gor-10` occupies F-klass. In the year-two locales, bonds-to-ten and first work within 20
are compressed into **year 1** — there is no earlier rung to carry the bonds — which is exactly what
pushes secure within-20 bridging out into year 2. Sweden does not have that compression, because the
bonds already left åk 1 and went to förskoleklass. **The Swedish ladder has one more rung than the
Romance ladders, and that extra rung at the bottom is what frees åk 1 to be the within-20 year.**

nl is the direct structural parallel and it is worth naming: groep 3 is the Dutch **first formal
school year**, preceded by groep 1–2 (the kleuters), which is functionally förskoleklass. Dutch put
the bonds in the kleuterjaren and *rekenen over het tiental* in groep 3 — the same three-rung shape
I am ruling for Sweden. Sweden belongs with de/fr/nl on the structure, not with es/pt/it.

### Recorded caveat
Prima/Safari classes will meet this in åk 2 as consolidation. That is fine and does not change the
chip. If the landing copy ever wants to say so, the honest sentence is *"introduceras i slutet av
åk 1 och befästs i åk 2"* — never "för åk 2".

### Slug consequence
The sibling åk-2 card already fixes the naming convention. The sv slug should parallel it exactly,
with **inom 20** as the distinguishing token — it is also a real Swedish teacher search string:

> `tiotalsovergang-inom-20-tallinje-ak-1`
> (or `addition-och-subtraktion-med-tiotalsovergang-inom-20-ak-1` if the longer form is wanted)

No cannibalisation risk against `…-inom-100-ak-2`: different range, different year, different query.

## 2. STRAND RULING — **keep "Algebra" on this card. File the defect at family scope.**

I have to give you two answers here, and they point in opposite directions, so let me separate the
mathematics from the operational call.

### The mathematics is not close

For Lgr22 åk 1–3, **Algebra contains exactly two things**: *mönster i talföljder och geometriska
mönster*, and *likhetstecknets betydelse* (that `=` means the two sides have the same value).
That is the whole of it. Everything about *how you compute* — *hur talen kan delas upp*, *centrala
metoder för beräkningar vid huvudräkning* — sits under **Taluppfattning och tals användning**.

`ten-stones` is a **huvudräkningsstrategi**. It has no equality-sign content and no pattern content.
Under Lgr22 it is Taluppfattning och tals användning, and no Swedish lågstadielärare would say
otherwise. The auto-map CCSS *Operations & Algebraic Thinking* → *Algebra* is a false friend: the
English strand name contains the word "algebraic", and the Swedish strand named "Algebra" means
something narrower and different. It is precisely the kind of artefact that tells a Swedish teacher
"this is Common Core with Swedish words on top", which is the one impression this rebuild exists to
prevent.

### But the ruling is: do not change this card alone

**Keep "Algebra".** Reason, and I'll say plainly which harm is worse:

The activities index groups cards by **grade band → localized strand**. If I override this one card,
a Swedish teacher browsing åk 1 sees `tiotalsövergång inom 20` sitting alone under *Taluppfattning
och tals användning* while `gör talet` (K.OA.A.3), `hitta det saknade talet` (1.OA.D.8) and
`para ihop tal upp till 20` (2.OA.B.2) — four cards that are the same *kind* of thing to her — sit
under *Algebra*. She does not read that as "one card is correctly filed". She reads it as **the
navigation is broken**, and she stops trusting the categories entirely. A uniformly wrong label
reads as a taxonomy choice she disagrees with; a split reads as data rot. The second is worse,
because the first costs you an argument and the second costs you the browse surface.

So: **the wrong-but-uniform harm is the lesser harm, and it is the one to accept today.**

### What I want filed instead

A family-scope commission, and it is cheap. But ⚠ **it is not a blanket flip**, and that is exactly
why it must not be started from one card:

- **Move to Taluppfattning och tals användning** (unambiguous — these are computation methods):
  `1.OA.C.6` (this card), `K.OA.A.4`, `1.NBT.*`, `2.NBT.*`, `2.OA.A.*`, `1.OA.A.*`.
- **Genuinely borderline — argue each one:** `1.OA.D.8 hitta-det-saknade-talet` (8 + ⬚ = 11) and
  `2.OA.B.2 para-ihop-tal-upp-till-20`, *if* they are framed as equalities rather than as recall.
  Lgr22 Algebra really does own *likhetstecknets betydelse*, and a missing-addend card presented as
  "make both sides the same" is legitimately Algebra. A missing-addend card presented as "what do
  you add" is Taluppfattning. **The framing decides, not the CCSS code.**
- **Stays Algebra:** anything about *mönster i talföljder*.

That per-card judgment across ~17 cards is a real commission with a native panel, and doing it one
card at a time is how you end up with the split index I just refused. File it; do not start it here.

## 3. TERMINOLOGY — the six strategies in Lgr22 / lågstadiet register

Marked **[E]** established classroom/textbook Swedish, **[C]** coined by me for this card,
**[P]** the phrase is established but there is no established *noun*.

### make-a-ten / bridging ten
- **Teacher/metadata term: `tiotalsövergång`** **[E]** — universal, in every series and in the
  Skolverket material. Use it in `page_title`, `page_intro` and the slug. ⚠ Qualify it: unqualified
  *tiotalsövergång* reads åk-2/inom-100 to a Swedish teacher, because that is the commonest
  collocation. Write **`tiotalsövergång inom 20`**.
- **Child-facing strategy name: `Göra tio`** **[E]** — this is Singma's own name for the strategy
  and children meet it as those two words. Prompts should say **"Gör tio först"**.
- **Child-facing verb for the act: `gå över tiotalet`** **[E]** (not *"hoppa över tiotalet"* —
  ⚠ *hoppa över* means **to skip / to omit**, the exact opposite; the Italian panel hit the identical
  trap with *saltare il dieci*. If the frog verb must be *hoppa*, it needs a destination:
  **"hoppa till tiostenen"**, **"hoppa förbi tiotalet"**.)
- ⚠ Compound morphology: *tiotal + s + övergång*, singular first element — `tiotalsövergång` is
  correct as written; never *tiotalövergång*, never *tiotalsöverföring*.

### the golden 10 stone
- **`tiostenen`** **[C]** (the metaphor is the site's; the compound is natural Swedish).
  With the gold pointed at: **`den gyllene tiostenen`**.
- ⚠ **Gender: *en sten* → *tiostenen*, NOT *tiostenet*.** Plural *tiostenar*, def. pl. *tiostenarna*.
- ⚠ **`gyllene`**, invariable — not *gyllne*, not *guldig*. `guldstenen` is also perfectly natural and
  warmer for seven-year-olds; if the linguist prefers it, I have no pedagogical objection — but
  **pick one and never mix them**, because the child has to learn that this one stone has one name.
- ⚠ Three things must stay three words and must never be interchanged:
  **the stone = `tiostenen`** · **the act = `att gå över tiotalet`** · **the bond = `tiokamraterna`**.
  Mixing them is the single most likely way this card ends up sounding foreign.

### number bonds to ten — and the `tiokompisar` collision
- **Use `tiokamrater`** **[E]** here. **This is a helpful ladder, not a collision — because I am
  choosing the sibling word deliberately.**
- Both `tiokamrater` and `tiokompisar` are fully established and Swedish teachers treat them as
  synonyms. But: `tiokamrater` is the term in **Favorit matematik** and in the **Skolverket
  bedömningsstöd**, i.e. the more "school" register, which is the right shift for åk 1 after a
  förskoleklass card. And the F-klass card has already spent `tiokompisar` in its slug.
- Result: the sv catalogue reads as a genuine progression —
  *"i förskoleklass lärde du dig **tiokompisarna**; nu använder du **tiokamraterna** för att gå över
  tiotalet"* — with **no slug collision and no chip collision.** Reusing `tiokompisar` in the slug
  would have been the confusing option; reusing the *concept* under its sibling name is the ladder.
- ⚠ Never write *tiovänner*, *kompisar till tio*, *tio-par*. Not Swedish classroom usage.

### anchor — commutativity / start from the larger addend
- **Mode label: `Störst först`** **[P]**. The full classroom phrase is **"börja med det största
  talet"** **[E]** — every Swedish teacher says this; there is no noun.
- ⚠ **Do NOT coin `bytesuppgift`.** German has *Tauschaufgabe*; Swedish does **not** have an
  equivalent noun, and inventing one would read as translated-from-German. If a noun is
  structurally required by the layout, the least-bad option is **`byt plats på talen`** as a short
  imperative label **[E as a phrase, C as a label]** — but I prefer `Störst först`.
- **`kommutativa lagen`** **[E]** exists but is åk 4+ register. Never put it in front of the child.
  It may appear in the teacher-facing `page_intro`.
- ⚠ See defect **A3** below — as currently written this round does not actually test commutativity,
  so the label needs to change *with* the prompt, not before it.

### relation — the inverse operation
- **Mode label: `Omvänd uppgift`** **[P]** — the phrase *"den omvända uppgiften"* is normal teacher
  Swedish. **`motsatt räknesätt`** **[E]** is the other established phrasing.
- **For the displayed 8 + 6 = 14 / 14 − 6 = 8 pair, the right word is `talfamilj`** **[E]** —
  Singma's term, and it is exactly what that block renders. Use it: *"Det är en talfamilj."*
- **`omvänd räkneoperation`** **[E]** — formal, fine in teacher-facing prose, too heavy for the child.
- ⚠ Never *omkastad uppgift*, never *baklängessumma*, never *bakvänd uppgift*.

### equiv — near-doubles
- **`nästan dubbelt`** **[P]** — the phrase is used; `dubbelt` **[E]** is fully established (Singma,
  Favorit) and children meet *dubbelt* early.
- ⚠ **I am NOT confident there is an established Swedish *noun* for a doubles fact.** *"en dubbel"*
  is not natural; *"dubbeluppgift"* would be a coinage; *"dubblor"* I have seen but would not stake
  a card on. **Do not let a linguist coin one to fill a layout slot — ask them to check Singma and
  Favorit first.** German's *Nachbaraufgabe* has no Swedish counterpart and must not be calqued
  (*grannuppgift* is not a thing).
- Safest shipping shape: label the option buttons with the **equation itself** (`7 + 7`) and carry
  the concept in the prompt — *"Vilket **dubbelt** ligger bara ett steg ifrån?"* No noun needed.
- ⚠ See defect **A4** — this round has a mathematical problem that terminology cannot fix.

### `tallinje` — **CONFIRMED, correct.**
`en tallinje, tallinjen, tallinjer` — this is the Skolverket / Lgr22 term and the only right one.
⚠ Never **`nummerlinje`** (a calque; wrong). ⚠ Never **`talrad`** or **`talföljd`** — those mean a
number *sequence*, a different object that belongs to the Algebra strand.
Related and correct: **`hoppa på tallinjen`**, **`talhopp`**, **`tallinjen 0–20`**.

### Two extras the ensemble will hit
- **The frog's name.** ⚠ *Lily* reads to a Swedish child as *lilja* / *näckros* — and the pond
  decoration in the code is literally a lotus (🪷). The English name is a pun on water-lily that
  simply is not there in Swedish; it just reads as a foreign girl's name attached to a frog. The
  Dutch panel renamed for this exact reason. Recommend the sv panel **rename**, check the new name
  against the shipped sv character lexicon across the other tools, and note that *en groda → grodan*
  is common gender.
- ⚠ **`Rätt` / `Fel` are banned** in this project's sv register (they mean *correct/incorrect about
  your work*). The existing string set already avoids them — keep it that way. **"Vi tittar en gång
  till"** is the right shape for `sayAgain`.

## 4. AUDIT OF THE ENGLISH SOURCE

The design *intent* is genuinely good — the six modes map cleanly onto the four strategies 1.OA.C.6
actually names (making ten, decomposing to a ten, the add/sub relationship, equivalent known sums)
plus two strategy-*selection* modes. And one design decision deserves protecting explicitly, see
**S1**. But the execution has defects, and five of the eleven rounds currently carry **no evidence**
that the child used any strategy at all.

### ⭐ A1 — SEVERE. Five of eleven rounds cannot fail. Four are two-option coin flips with a free retry.

From `ten-stones-core.js`:
```js
options: round.options || (round.cog === 'findten' ? ['yes','no']
                        : (round.cog === 'anchor'  ? [round.a, round.b] : null))
```
So `anchor` and `findten` each render **exactly two buttons**. And in `ten-stones-activity.js`
`_choose()`, a wrong tap costs nothing — it says *"Let's look again"*, reshuffles, and re-renders:

```js
_choose: function (choice) {
  if (Core.gradeChoose(this.round, choice)) { this._correct(); return; }
  this.msg = api.t('sayAgain'); this._opts = shuffle(...); this.render();
}
```

**With two options and a free retry, a wrong answer tells the child the answer.** `anchor-3-8`,
`anchor-9-2`, `findten-6-3`, `findten-8-6` are all solvable in **at most two taps, by anyone**.
`equiv-7-8` has three options → at most three taps. `relation-8-6` is not graded at all (see A5).
That is **5 of 11 rounds** that a child who has learned nothing completes at the same speed as a
child who has learned everything.

⚠ **The core's own cheat-proof does not cover this.** The docblock's "GATE-CAN'T-CHEAT PROOF" lists
SPIN-TO-TEN-SEARCHER / ONE-BIG-HOP / COUNT-BY-ONES / GLOW-CHASER / OVERSHOOT — every one of those is
a *bridge*-family solver. The proof is real and the bridge family is genuinely strong (there is no
per-hop grader, so there is no gradient to search — that is a good piece of design). **But the
CHOOSE family was never adversarially modelled, and it is trivially brute-forceable.** A
BRUTE-FORCE-THE-CHOICE solver would score 100% on 5 of 11 rounds. That is a hole in the tool's own
stated proof and I would file it as such.

**Fix (any one, in order of preference):** (i) give `anchor` and `findten` a third option; (ii) on a
wrong choose, do not re-offer — advance and re-present the round later; (iii) at minimum, more
rounds per mode so the coin flip is diluted across the pass.

### ⭐ A2 — SEVERE. `anchor` provably cannot discriminate. It does not teach commutativity.

`correctChoice()` for anchor is `Math.max(round.a, round.b)`. The prompt asks *"which number should
Lily stand on to reach ten quickest?"*.

For two addends both ≤ 10, **the larger addend is *always* the one nearer to ten.** The two rules —
"pick the larger" and "pick the one closest to ten" — are the *same predicate over this entire
input space*. They cannot be separated by any round you could add. Which means:

- A child who has never heard of commutativity and simply **always taps the bigger number** scores
  100% on both anchor rounds.
- The round therefore produces **zero evidence** about the strategy it is named for, and the mode
  name `anchor` (start from the larger addend / commutativity) is a claim the round does not support.
- ⚠ Worse: **the prompt never tells the child that swapping is allowed.** Shown `3 + 8`, a
  seven-year-old has no reason to believe she may start at 8. The whole commutative insight — the
  thing that makes this round worth existing — is left unstated, and the child is instead rewarded
  for a proximity heuristic.

**Fix — and it is a rewrite, not a wording tweak.** Make the commutativity explicit and *then* ask:

> *"3 + 8 är samma sak som 8 + 3. Vilket tal ska grodan ställa sig på?"*

Now the round teaches something true (you may start from either; start from the one nearest the
ten) and says it out loud. Keep the mode, change the claim: the honest label is **`Störst först`** /
*byt plats på talen*, and the prose should own that "nearest the ten" and "the larger" coincide here
rather than pretending they are different tests.

⚠ Cross-locale note: the **nl** prompt already smuggles in *"Denk aan de grootste eerst"* (think
largest first) — the hint that the en/de/fr/es/pt/it prompts all lack. So the Dutch panel had
already noticed this and patched it locally. sv should fix it at the source, not inherit the
English.

### ⭐ A3 — SEVERE. `equiv-7-8`'s option set is rigged, and the round's own rule admits the answer it hides.

The core's rule is mathematically correct and general:
```js
if (Math.abs(sum - 2 * opts[i]) === 1) return opts[i];
```
sum = 15. Offered options are `[7, 5, 9]` → only 7 satisfies it (|15 − 14| = 1). ✅ by the code.

**But 8 also satisfies it**: |15 − 16| = 1. `8 + 8 = 16`, one *too many* — a completely legitimate
near-double anchor for 7 + 8, and the one many children reach for first because 8 is the larger
addend. **It is deliberately not offered, and the round gives the child no way to say it.** So a
child with the *correct and more sophisticated* insight finds no button for it, taps something else,
and is told to look again.

That is not a distractor problem, it is a mathematics problem: **7 + 8 has two valid near-double
anchors, and the round asserts it has one.**

- Also: **`5 + 5 = 10` is not a distractor, it is noise** — it is 5 away, nowhere near "one away".
  And `9 + 9 = 18` is 3 away. Neither probes any misconception. The one option that *would* probe a
  misconception (8) is the one excluded.
- The wording *"Which DOUBLE is just one away from helping?"* is not parseable by a seven-year-old
  in any language. "Just one away from **helping**" asks about the usefulness of a fact, not about
  a number. And `DOUBLE` is shouted in caps as a term the round never defines.

⚠ **And the apparatus is idle.** Every other round uses the number line. This one is a text question
sitting on top of a number line that does nothing. But the line *could* carry it beautifully — two
equal hops of 7 to 14, then one more to 15 — which is exactly what the strategy looks like.

**Fix, in order:** (i) re-render it on the line (best; the strategy becomes visible and the mode
earns its place); or (ii) choose a sum with a **unique** near-double — `6 + 7 = 13` (only 6 and 7
qualify… ⚠ both qualify again; `2d = 12` or `14`) — in fact **every** consecutive-addend sum has two
near-double anchors, so this is structural: use a **non-consecutive** pair where only one double is
one away, or accept both and let the round have two right answers; or (iii) **drop `equiv` from the
Swedish build.** Near-doubles *is* legitimate åk-1 Swedish content (Singma teaches *dubbelt* and
nästan-dubbelt), so I do not want to lose it — but as shipped it is the weakest of the eleven and
the only one that is mathematically unsound.

### ⭐ A4 — SEVERE. `8 + 6` is three of the eleven rounds, and one of them is dead.

The pool is: `8+6`, `7+5`, `6+3`, `13−4`, `3+8`, **`8+6`**, `7+8`, `9+4`, `15−7`, `9+2`, **`8+6`**.

`8 + 6` appears as `maketen-8-6` (band 1), `findten-8-6` (band 2) and `relation-8-6` (band 3).

**`findten-8-6` is therefore non-diagnostic by construction**: the child bridged 8 + 6 herself in
band 1 and is now asked in band 2 whether 8 + 6 needs the ten stone. She does not need to reason;
she remembers. And with two options and a free retry (A1) she could not have failed anyway.

`bandOrder()` shuffles *within* band and preserves band order, so band 1 always precedes band 2 —
the ordering makes this reliable, not occasional.

**Fix:** give `findten` fresh numbers. And see A7 for what those numbers should be.

### ⭐ A5 — `relation-8-6` is unfalsifiable and contradicts the equation chip on screen.

```js
_affirm: function () { this.msg = ...; this.solved = true; this.crossed++; ... }
```
One button, labelled *"Yes — same stones!"*, no grading, `crossed++`. The inverse relation is
**told**, not discovered. It counts as a completed crossing on the pond progress bar exactly like a
bridge round the child had to think through.

⚠ **And there is a visible contradiction.** `_eqChip()` renders `8 + 6 = ?` with the answer hidden
until solved — but on this round the *prompt* says `8 + 6 = 14` and `_renderRelation()` prints
`8 + 6 = 14` two lines below the chip. The same screen says `= ?` and `= 14` simultaneously.

**Fix without an engine change:** make the child *predict* the landing before the backward hop —
reuse the existing spinner: `14 − 6 = ⬚`. That converts a reveal into a task and removes the chip
contradiction (the chip can show the addition as solved and the subtraction as open).

⚠ **Fix with an engine change — flag for the developer before anyone specs it:** the obvious richer
task is "which subtraction belongs to this talfamilj?" — but a fact family has **two** subtractions
(`14 − 6 = 8` *and* `14 − 8 = 6`), and a three-option single-answer grader would mark one of the two
correct answers wrong. That is a lovely lesson and a grader hazard at the same time. Do not spec it
without deciding which.

### ⭐ A6 — Two of the eight bridge sub-answers arrive **pre-filled with the correct value**.

The spinner opens at 1 (`this.spin = bp ? Math.max(0, Math.min(9, 1)) : 0;`) and resets to 1 again
at the remainder phase (`this.phase='remainder'; this.spin = 1;`). The bond values in this pool are:

| round | to-ten | remainder |
|---|---|---|
| `maketen-8-6` | 2 | 4 |
| `maketen-7-5` | 3 | 2 |
| `maketen-9-4` | **1 ← pre-answered** | 3 |
| `decompose-13-4` | 3 | **1 ← pre-answered** |
| `decompose-15-7` | 5 | 2 |

So **`maketen-9-4` is solvable by pressing "Hop!" twice with no input at all on the first bond** —
and it is a **band-3** round, the hardest band. `decompose-13-4`'s remainder phase is likewise
pre-answered.

**Fix: open the spinner at 0.** No bond in this pool is 0, so 0 is never accidentally correct; and
`8 + 0 = 10` is *visibly* false, which invites the child to move it. One-character change, closes the
whole class.

### ⭐ A7 — The most important case in a make-ten activity is missing: landing **exactly on** the ten.

`crossesTen()` returns false when the sum lands on 10 (`start < 10 && target > 10` is false for
target = 10). Pedagogically that is the right verdict — *she lands on the stone, she does not need
to rest on it* — and `facts.isNonCrossingGuard` shows the core is aware of the non-crossing case.

But **there is no such round in the pool.** The two `findten` rounds are a clear no (6 + 3, never
reaches ten) and a clear yes (8 + 6, obviously past it). The genuinely interesting third case —
**`6 + 4`, or `13 − 3`** — is exactly where a child's understanding of *what the stone is for*
becomes visible, and it is absent. In Swedish terms it is the case that separates a child who knows
*tiokamraterna* from one who has memorised "big numbers need the stone".

**Fix:** add `findten` rounds at `6 + 4` (lands on) and one fresh crossing (e.g. `7 + 6`), retire
`findten-8-6`. That fixes A4 and A7 in one edit and starts to dilute A1.

### A8 — `findten`'s question is not the question.

*"Will Lily need to **rest** on the Ten Stone?"* / *"Yes — rest on the stone"* / *"No — hop straight
there"*. The mathematical question is **does the jump cross the ten**; "rest" makes it sound like a
choice she might make either way, and "hop straight there" implies she *could* have used the stone
and declined. For 6 + 3 she never reaches ten at all — "straight there" is not a decision, it is a
fact about the numbers.

**Swedish fix — and it is better than the English:** build it on the established term.
> **"Går hoppet över tiotalet?"** — *Ja, jag måste stanna på tiostenen* / *Nej, jag hoppar direkt*.

That is the actual mathematical question, in the actual Swedish vocabulary, and it removes the
"resting is optional" reading.

### A9 — `decompose`'s first half is given away, and the mode is misnamed.

*"13 − 4. Hop back to the Ten Stone: 13 − ⬚ = 10."* The displayed frame `13 − ⬚ = 10` **forces** the
first answer — there is exactly one number that makes it true, and finding it is a within-10
subtraction, not a decomposition. Then the second phase asks 4 − 3 = 1 as an unconnected question.

The decomposition the mode is named for — **splitting the subtrahend**, 4 = 3 + 1 and 7 = 5 + 2 — is
never asked, never shown, and never named. The child does two easy steps and the *reason* they are
those two steps is invisible.

**Fix (no engine change):** name the split in the prompt so the child sees the 4 come apart.
> *"15 − 7. Dela upp 7: först tillbaka till tiostenen, sedan resten."*
`Dela upp` is the Lgr22 verb (*hur talen kan delas upp*) — this also strengthens the strand argument
in §2. And it makes the two phases one strategy instead of two chores.

Only **2 of 11** rounds are subtraction, and the subtraction bridge is the *harder* half in Swedish
practice (`15 − 7` is a classic åk-1/åk-2 diagnostic item). **Recommend at least 3.**

### A10 — Band ordering does not track difficulty.

Band 1 = `maketen-8-6`, `maketen-7-5`, `findten-6-3` (no subtraction at all).
Band 3 contains `maketen-9-4`, whose to-ten bond is **1** — the easiest bridge in the pool, and the
one that needs no input (A6). Bands should open with the small remainders, not close with them.

### A11 — Hardcoded English shipped to **all eleven locales** (live i18n defect, not sv-specific).

```js
minus.setAttribute('aria-label', 'less');
plus .setAttribute('aria-label', 'more');
svg('svg', { ..., 'aria-label': 'a number line from 0 to 20' });
```
Three English strings reach every locale's screen reader. This is the recorded "speaking English to
children" class, in the accessibility channel where no visual gate can see it.

### A12 — There is no `sv` anywhere in the engine's language branches.

`numWord()` has en/de/fr/es/pt/it/nl and **no sv** → Swedish aria-labels and TTS would read
*"eight plus six"*. `eqWord` (line 146) has no `sv` → falls through to `" equals "`. The anchor
aria-label (line 208) has special cases for de and nl and none for sv.

Required for the sv build:
```js
var WORDS_SV = ['noll','ett','två','tre','fyra','fem','sex','sju','åtta','nio','tio',
                'elva','tolv','tretton','fjorton','femton','sexton','sjutton',
                'arton','nitton','tjugo'];
```
⚠ **`arton`**, not *aderton* (archaic). ⚠ **`ett`**, not *en* — the counting word is neuter.
⚠ `eqWord` for sv = **`' är '`**. ⚠ anchor aria = **`'Ställ dig på ' + numWord(o)`** → *"Ställ dig på
nio."* ⚠ `hop` = **`'Hoppa!'`**; `stand` = **`'Ställ dig på'`**.

### A13 — `instruction` is a half-authored dead string.

`instruction: { en:'', de:'', pt:'', it:'', nl:'' }` — empty in five locales, **absent in fr and es**,
while `title` and `prompt` both have fr. Either it is used and two locales fall through, or it is
used by nobody and should go. This is the recorded dead-string class; worth a line to the developer.

### A14 — `page_intro` framework leakage (already governed, restating for the sv build).

The en intro ends *"Common Core 1.OA.C.6."* The other locales are inconsistent: **it** cites
*Indicazioni nazionali* ✅, **nl** cites *SLO-kerndoelen* ✅, **es** cites *SEP* on an es-MX
locale ✅, **de** and **fr** cite **no framework at all** ❌. For sv: **Lgr22, name only, no CCSS
code**, per the SERP-copy gate.

### ✅ S1 — One design decision to protect explicitly.

`_renderLine()` draws ticks and labels at **0, 5, 10, 15, 20 only** — there are no unit marks. That
is what stops the number line from rewarding **räkna ett i taget**, which is the immature strategy
that make-ten exists to replace. On a unit-marked line every bridge round would be solvable by
counting stones and the whole activity would collapse.

**Do not add unit ticks to the Swedish build**, and do not let a "make the line clearer for young
children" review add them. It is the load-bearing restraint in the design.

## 6. SUMMARY OF THE THREE RULINGS

1. **GRADE = åk 1.** Bridging ten *inom 20* is the end-of-åk-1 target in the Skolverket
   bedömningsstöd and is taught in åk 1 by Favorit matematik 1B and Singma 1B; the catalogue ladder
   (F-klass *tiokompisar* → **åk 1 inom 20** → åk 2 inom 100) requires it, and åk 2 would leave åk 1
   empty. Prima/Safari meet it as åk-2 consolidation — that is a caveat, not a reclassification.
2. **STRAND = keep "Algebra" on this card, and file a family-scope commission.** Lgr22 Algebra in
   åk 1–3 is *only* mönster i talföljder and likhetstecknets betydelse; this is a huvudräkningsstrategi
   and belongs to *Taluppfattning och tals användning*, unambiguously. But a lone corrected card
   splits the strand-grouped index across two buckets and reads to a teacher as broken navigation —
   worse than a uniformly wrong label. Fix all ~17 together, per card, because `1.OA.D.8` and
   `2.OA.B.2` are genuinely arguable as equality content.
3. **TERMINOLOGY:** `tiotalsövergång inom 20` [E] (metadata) / **`Gör tio`** [E] (child) ·
   **`tiostenen`** [C], *en sten* → **-en** not -et, `gyllene` invariable · **`tiokamrater`** [E] —
   deliberately the sibling of the F-klass `tiokompisar`, so it is a **ladder, not a collision** ·
   **`Störst först`** [P], ⚠ never coin *bytesuppgift* · **`omvänd uppgift`** [P] + **`talfamilj`**
   [E] · **`nästan dubbelt`** [P], ⚠ no established Swedish noun for a doubles fact — do not let one
   be coined · **`tallinje` CONFIRMED**, never *nummerlinje*, never *talrad*.
   ⚠ *hoppa över tiotalet* means **skip the ten** — the opposite. Rename the frog.
4. **AUDIT:** 14 defects, 4 severe. Headline: **5 of 11 rounds cannot fail** (four 2-option coin
   flips with a free retry + one ungraded reveal), and the core's own cheat-proof only ever modelled
   the bridge family. `anchor` is **provably** unable to discriminate — for addends ≤ 10 "the larger"
   and "nearest to ten" are the same predicate, so a child who always taps the bigger number scores
   100%. `equiv-7-8` hides `8 + 8`, which the core's own rule accepts. `8 + 6` is three of eleven
   rounds. Two bridge sub-answers open pre-filled. And **there is no round that lands exactly on
   ten** — the single most valuable case in a make-ten activity.
