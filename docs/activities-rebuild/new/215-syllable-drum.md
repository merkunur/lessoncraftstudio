## The skill
Count the syllables in a picture word by beating them, and sort pictures by syllable count.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE BEAD RAIL THAT SPLITS THE WORD. A big library picture on a stand; under it an empty bead rail; beside it a round beat pad. The child says the word and taps the pad once per syllable — each tap drops a bead onto the rail with a small bounce (tapping a bead on the rail removes it). Check: the picture's written word (the gated split from approved-words-<locale>.json) slides down and breaks into its syllables, one syllable landing over each bead. Right: every bead wears a syllable and the beads ripple left to right. Miss 1: a bead with no syllable sits dark and rolls off the end, or a syllable with no bead hangs over an empty dip — the child sees which part was not beaten. Miss 2: the word lies over the rail already split with dots between syllables and the child beats again. SORT MODE (K-234): three or four shelves carrying 1, 2, 3, 4 beads (no numerals); the child drags each picture to a shelf and the shelf lays the split word along its beads — a 3-syllable word on the 2-bead shelf leaves one syllable hanging off the end and slides back.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Counting letters or sounds instead of syllables; counting the first beat only.
- **Pictures:** animals, farm-animals, fruits, vegetables, vehicles, toys, clothing — ONLY nouns present in approved-words-<locale>.json (gated splits); da: the policy_managed:false pool only.
- **Settings:** Mode: beat and count / sort to shelves · Syllables: 1–3 / 1–4
- **Rounds:** 9 words per pass, ≥ 2 of each count (1–3 or 1–4); every pass includes ≥ 2 length lures (a long 1-syllable word and a short multi-syllable word) so letter-counting fails visibly; shelf order fixed 1-2-3-4 (the count is the content); one right count.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-233, K-234
- **Distinct from:** The Syllable Builder blends given syllables into a word; this is the other direction (split and count).
- **Build risk:** fr: the mute-e ruling is open (§20.9) — the fr pool excludes words ending in a mute e until it is ruled; sv uses the school-split carve-out (§A.13.57). Distinct from the syllable-splitter tool only because the rail checks the child's count — keep the pad visually unlike the tool's drum. Legible without sound: the child says the word, the reveal is written.
