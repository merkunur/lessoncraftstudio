## 1 Page concept (base)

**"The washroom hooks."** Across the top of the page runs a teal peg rail with five round pegs; from each peg hangs a cream plaque on two strings, and on each plaque a faceless child is in the middle of a habit, **with the tool taken out of the picture**: hands held out under a running tap (no soap), a fist at the mouth with foam bubbles (no toothbrush), lying under a blanket beside a moon and two sleep zigzags (no bed), a hand at the head with three messy tufts (no comb), both hands cupped at the nose with a sneeze burst (no tissue). Along the bottom stands a shelf plank with five drawn tools in a deranged order. The child draws a line from the dot under each plaque down to the dot of the tool that child needs.

Why this is the top page: (1) the washroom hook row with a picture per child is the one image every K child in all 11 countries already owns (Handtuchhaken, kapstok met symbolen, knagerække), so the page reads as "our washroom", not as a test; (2) ONE focal apparatus (rail above, shelf below, the lines between), generous whitespace in the line zone, no words on the apparatus at all (the base is identical in 11 locales); (3) **answer-hiding is pedagogical, not cosmetic**: because the tool is removed from the habit picture, the child must name the SITUATION and recall its tool ("she is foaming at the mouth, she needs a toothbrush"), which is the genre's real teaching point; a pictogram holding its toothbrush would reduce the page to shape-matching; (4) pencil-first: 5 lines, big coral dots, nothing to write.

## 4 Answer-hiding + uniqueness

- The page prints each habit's SITUATION and the tools, never a line and never the tool inside the habit. Stamps: plaque `<div data-lcs-habit="wash-hands">`, its pictogram `<g data-lcs-pictogram="habit" data-lcs-pose data-lcs-props="tap,water,basin">`, each prop `<g data-lcs-prop="tap">`; shelf item `<div data-lcs-tool="soap">`. No `data-lcs-answer` anywhere.
- **verify():** (1) re-derive the pairing from the gate's OWN table `HABIT_TOOL = {wash-hands:soap, brush-teeth:toothbrush, sleep:bed, comb:comb, blow-nose:tissue, sun:hat}`, never from the page; (2) every habit on the page has exactly one tool on the shelf and every tool (except the d3 spare) exactly one habit; (3) **tool leak:** no `[data-lcs-prop]` inside a plaque is any tool present on the shelf (parsed from the markup, and the pictogram gate measures the render); (4) the pedagogy's rule: toothpaste never on the same page as toothbrush; (5) order: tool column index ≠ habit column index for every pair, order ≠ reversed, not all offsets equal; over 20 seeds + the shipped seed, no column holds its own tool > 25 % (it is 0 % by construction) and no offset value exceeds 60 %.
- A wrong answer is visible to the teacher: every line is a straight pencil line from a plaque dot to a tool dot; a wrong pairing lands on a tool whose picture has nothing to do with the plaque's situation.

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **"The day from sunrise to moon"** (a sun-to-moon arc with each habit placed at its time of day). Rejected: it teaches WHEN, which is daily-routine ORDER (the "ma journée / mi rutina" cluster the SEO panel said must be assigned to one family; story-sequencing is its natural owner) and it prints a time, i.e. a quantity that differs by country (pt-BR brushes after every meal; the lock bans numbers).
2. **"One big bathroom mirror"** (the child in a large central mirror, five habits around the frame, lines to tools at the edges). Rejected for the base: one scene with radiating lines crossing the figure, and a faceless child in a mirror reads eerie at K. The mirror survives where it is TRUE: F3, where brushing happens at the mirror, as a frame for the steps, not as a scene.
3. (also considered) **"Habit passport with stamps"**: rejected, a passport of stamps is a reward economy ("good child") the no-shaming rule points away from, and its grid duplicates F6.

**Recommendation: the washroom hooks.** It is the child's own room, not a metaphor; the rail + shelf give one apparatus and a clean line zone; removing the tool from the habit makes the base a thinking task; the peg-and-plaque signature carries through F2, F4, F6 (and yields to the mirror on F3 for a reason), so the six faces read as one family from across the room while each keeps a different apparatus for its own query.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Pictogram legibility at K size** (sleep lying, comb tufts, blow-nose vs brush-teeth): the two mouth-level poses are separated by TWO hands at the NOSE + burst ABOVE vs ONE hand at the MOUTH + bubbles BELOW; the pictogram gate measures the anchors; the human eye check on the 124 px render at 100 % and at 50 % print scale is mandatory (a gate cannot say a silhouette "reads"). If `lie` does not read as sleep at d2 size, the base swaps `sleep` for `drink ↔ glass of water` (data, not code).
- **Tool leak:** a context prop that IS the tool (soap in `wash-hands`) would give the answer; gated both in the markup (prop list ∩ shelf tools = ∅) and by the render gate.
- **Two right answers:** bubbles fit soap AND toothpaste (toothpaste is banned from the base); towel fits wash-hands too (towel never on the base shelf at d2; d3's spare tool must not fit any plaque: the gate's own table lists `towel` as fitting `wash-hands`, so the d3 spare is drawn from tools that fit no plaque on that page).
- **Greyscale:** ink 54 vs teal 80 are close; nothing depends on that difference: answers ride on POSE, PROP SHAPE and presence, white props on ink bodies differ by ~200. Coral dots (luma 151) print mid-grey, still visible as solid disks.
- **Long locales:** only F5 and F6 carry text; budgets above; the base family is text-free. The instruction must name only apparatus on the page ("line", "boxes", "circle", "tick"); F3 says boxes because there are boxes.
- **Pencil space:** base line zone ≥ 160; numeral boxes 72 × 56; circles have ≥ 20 px margin; tick squares 48.
- **No cut lines** on any face (nothing is cut).
- **9 px floor:** smallest text 18 px. **What the lint catches:** overflow, footer intrusion, font floor, off-palette hex. **What only a human eye catches:** whether each silhouette reads as the habit, whether the calm twin on F4 looks shamed, whether a reason literal secretly fits two habits in its language.

## 10 Summary

1. Concept: the kindergarten washroom hook rail; every habit hangs on a peg, tools stand on a shelf below, the child draws lines.
2. The base removes the tool from each habit picture, so the child reads the situation and recalls its tool: zero words on the page in 11 locales.
3. ONE art source: a new profile-view `primitives/habit-pictogram.js` (figures, 20 props, tool product views, a hands close-up), zero library pictures (wine glass, tea cup, first-aid tissue box opened and rejected).
4. Faces: hand-washing steps on rails (5, hands close-up), brushing teeth in four mirrors (no mouth-rinse step), stop the germs in four pairs (sleeve-wipe pair rejected), reasons match with anti-tell literal rules, an open week chart.
5. Everything is designed to 677 px, K/G1/G2 floors met, answers re-derived from pose/prop tables, greyscale-safe by shape.
