## 1. The pitch
"Pip's bakery coin box only swallows a coin when its counter already says what the box will hold with that coin inside — so you count on before every coin you drop, and the counter will not turn past the price."

## 2. Why a child will want it again
- **A box that waits for your number.** On the bakery counter stands a tin coin box with a slot on top, a little brass flap over the slot, and a counter window on its front with chunky number wheels you click round like a bike lock. Hold a coin over the slot and the flap lifts a crack, *waiting*. Click the wheels to the number the box will hold with your coin in it, let go of the coin — and if you counted on right, the flap swings open, the coin drops with a deep *clonk* and you see it land in the box's glass side window. Count on wrong, and the flap snaps shut and **flips the coin back out** onto the cloth with a *tock* — the wheels roll back to what the box really holds. It is a toy that checks every single step of your counting, by itself, with no words.
- **The price pin.** A coral pin sits on the counter at the price. The wheels **clack against it and will not turn further** — so a coin that would take you past the price simply cannot be said, and cannot go in. Children try it on purpose ("what if I put the quarter in?" — clack).
- **The purse is real.** The coins lie on a felt cloth, shuffled, exactly as they came out of the purse — some to spare, but not endless. Spend the little coins first and you can find yourself with only coins that are too big (§3): the box teaches that you start with the biggest coin that fits.
- **The earned show.** When the box holds exactly the price, the wheels spin a full turn and click home on the price, the bell on the lid *dings*, the box's little front drawer slides open and the bakery treat you paid for (a library picture: cookie, croissant, cupcake, doughnut, pretzel, waffle, bagel, brownie) slides out onto a paper napkin. Then the coins in the glass window hop once each in the order they went in, and above each one floats the number the child turned for it — **25, 35, 40, 41** — the child's own count replayed as a little parade.
- **Free play**: before the first round and after every success the box has no price pin — feed it any coins from the cloth and count as high as you like (the slot still only opens to the right number; that is the toy). The tip-out handle pours everything back with a rattle.
- No timer, score, streak or lives; nothing is sold to the user (the bakery is pretend).

## 3. The skill and the misconception
- Skill (2.MD.C.8; G1-211, G1-228–231, G1-232, G2-295): make a given amount with coins, **counting on by each coin's worth** as you go (25 … 35 … 40 … 41); make it with the fewest coins; find how much more is needed to reach a price; decide which collection is worth more.
- Misconceptions and the push-back (all by the mechanism, none by a message):
  1. **Counting coins instead of value** (dime → "one more") → the child turns the counter one on for the dime and drops it: the slot refuses it and flips it back. Only the coin's **worth** opens the flap. (Measured: a "coins-as-ones" player — counts every coin as 1 — finishes **0 of 16** level-1/2 rounds in every family, because no purse holds as many 1-coins as its price.)
  2. **Starting with the small coins** → the purse is finite, so pennies and small coins spent first leave you with coins that only overshoot. Measured over level 1: a "smallest-first" player (always drops the smallest coin that still fits under the pin) **gets stranded in 4 of 8 rounds** (EURR and NOK 3 of 8; BRL 4 of 8) and **never** in the other half, while a "biggest-first" player finishes **8 of 8 rounds in every family**. In level 2 (fewest coins) smallest-first either strands or ends with too many coins in **8 of 8 rounds in every family**, and biggest-first gives the fewest set 8 of 8. So "start with the small coins" is not forbidden — it simply runs out of road half the time, which is the honest truth about a real purse. (Small-first also asks for harder sums: 7 + 25 instead of 25 + 7; the wheels show the carry.)
  3. **Overshooting** (dropping a coin "to be safe") → the price pin stops the wheels at the price; a coin whose true new total is past the price cannot be counted on, so it cannot go in. Overshoot is not a later red mark — it is physically impossible, and the child feels exactly where it happens.
  4. **Coin size = worth** → coins are drawn at their real relative diameters (§4): the small dime opens the flap for "+10", the big nickel only for "+5"; in euro the 1 € coin is smaller than the 50 c coin, in sv the 10 kr smaller than the 2 kr, in da the 10 kr smaller than the 2 kr and 5 kr.
  5. **How much more = the price** (top-up rounds) → the box already holds Pip's coins and its wheels already show their total (e.g. 35); the child counts on from 35, not from 0, and the success sentence writes 35 + 15 = 50.
  6. **More coins = more money** (compare rounds) → each tray is counted into the box; the box prints a slip with its total; R7 is won by fewer coins and R8 by more coins, in every family (measured).

## 5. The hand
- **Turn the counter**: tap ▲/▼ on a wheel (one step, 90 ms roll with a ratchet *tick*) or drag a wheel up/down (one digit per 36 px). The ones wheel carries like an odometer: ▲ on 9 rolls it to 0 and nudges the tens wheel on one (the carry is visible). The counter can never go **below** what the box already holds (the wheels bump softly) and never **past the price pin** (they clack against the coral pin and shiver, 150 ms; the pin flashes).
- **Drop a coin**: drag a coin from the cloth to the slot (it follows the finger at 1.1×, the flap lifts a crack as it nears), or tap a coin (it lifts and glows) and then tap the slot. On release over the slot the box decides within 50 ms:
  - counter = what the box holds + the coin's worth → **swallow**: flap swings open (120 ms), the coin drops through (200 ms, *clonk*), lands in the glass window; the counter stays where the child put it — it is now the box's total.
  - counter = what the box holds (not turned) → nothing happens but a nudge (§7, no miss): the flap stays a crack open, the wheels wiggle.
  - any other number → **spit**: the flap snaps shut, the coin flips back to its place on the cloth (300 ms arc, *tock*), the child's number flutters out of the window on a little coral paper slip and fades (600 ms) so it is seen, and the wheels roll back to what the box holds (250 ms).
- **Give-back lever**: pull it (tap or drag down) → the last coin in pops back up out of the slot onto the cloth (250 ms) and the wheels roll back by its worth. Repeat to take more back, newest first (a real slot box can only give back what is on top).
- **Tip-out ring**: tap → the box tips 30° and pours every coin back onto the cloth (400 ms rattle); the wheels roll to the start (0, or Pip's amount in top-up rounds).
- **Compare rounds**: tap a tray to choose it (it lifts with a ring), then count the trays into the box one after the other — any order; when a tray is empty the box prints its slip (*ratchet-zip*, 400 ms), pins it in the holder with the tray's colour, and tips itself out for the other tray.
- **Free play** §2. **Keyboard**: Tab → the wheels (↑/↓ change the focused wheel, ←/→ move between wheels) → the cloth coins (←/→ choose, Enter = drop into the slot) → the lever (Enter) → the tip-out ring (Enter). **Sound** (optional, never needed): tick, clonk, tock, clack, ding. **TTS** (optional): tap the price tag to hear the price; tap a coin to hear its name (en "dime" — never its worth in en).

## 7. Check, the misses, the success
There is no Check button: **each drop is a check** — the box answers every coin within 50 ms, and the round ends by itself when the box holds the price.
- **Drop with the counter not turned** (or tapping the slot with no coin) → the flap stays a crack open, the wheels wiggle once; bubble: "Turn the counter first: what will the box hold with this coin in it?" (no miss)
- **Miss 1** (wrong count on a coin) → the spit (§5): the coin flips back, the child's number flutters out on a coral slip, the wheels roll back to the box's total and that total pulses once. Bubble: "The box holds {in}. Count on what the {coin} is worth, then drop it again."
- **Miss 2** (wrong again on the same coin) → the box shows the count-on: the wheels tick forward **by themselves** from {in} in the coin's natural steps — tens first, then a five, then ones (a quarter from 35: 45, 55, 60; a 2 kr from 13: 14, 15) — with a *tick* per step, the coin shows a ghost of its worth on its face (in en too), then the wheels roll back to {in} for the child to do it. Bubble: "Watch: {in} … {steps}. Now you turn it and drop the {coin}."
- **The pin** (wheels clack at the price; not a miss) → the pin flashes and the held coin wobbles; bubble: "The counter stops at {price}. The {coin} is too much — try a smaller coin."
- **Stranded** (revealed the first time the wheels jam at the pin, or the cloth is empty, while no coins left on the cloth can make the rest; not a miss) → the remaining coins dim slightly and the give-back lever bobs; bubble: "The coins left can't make {gap} more. Pull the lever to take coins back." Stranded a second time in the round → the box tips out and the cloth lays its coins in a row biggest-first, left to right (one showing); bubble: "Start with the biggest coin that fits, then count on."
- **Fewest level, exact but too many coins** → the bell does not ring; in the glass window the coins that could be one coin glow inside one bracket with a ghost coin beside it. Bubble: "Exactly {price}! Can you pay it with fewer coins?" Second time: the ghost coin also appears on the cloth, ringed. Bubble: "These coins make one {coin}. Pull the lever and use it."
- **Compare, wrong prediction** (shown after both slips are printed) → both slips stand side by side, the bigger total's tray glows, the two coin piles are lined up under their slips. Bubble: "{big} is more than {small}. More coins is not always more money."
- A new move (a turned wheel, a dropped coin, the lever) takes any showing away until the next miss.
- **Success** → the full spin of the wheels, *ding*, the drawer slides out the treat onto the napkin, the count-on parade over the glass window. Bubble: L1 "**You counted {chain}: exactly {price}!**" · L2 "**{price} with just {n} coins: {chain}.**" · L3 top-up "**{in} + {added} = {price}. You needed {added} more.**" · L3 compare "**{big} is more than {small} — you picked the tray with more money!**" ({chain} = the child's own turned numbers, e.g. "25, 35, 40, 41"; money formats §9.)

## 13. The four questions
1. **Would a 5–8-year-old choose it again?** Yes — a tin box with clicking number wheels that swallows a coin with a clonk or flips it back at you, a pin you can bang the wheels against, and a treat sliding out of the drawer: it is a toy that plays back, and the parade shows the child their own counting.
2. **Would a teacher put it on the board?** Yes — the mechanic IS counting on by coin value: every coin must be counted on before it goes in, the pin makes overshoot impossible, a real purse makes "start with the biggest coin" something the class discovers, and levels 2–3 cover fewest coins, how much more and which purse has more. One right answer per round (exactly the price), no running total, nothing to guess by position.
3. **Would a parent trust it?** Yes — real coins of the child's own country at real sizes, calm wordless apparatus, library treats, works silently on a phone, nothing sold.
4. **The invention in one sentence:** the coin box's slot only opens when its counter already says what the box will hold with your coin inside, and the counter cannot turn past the price.

## 14. Critic review (2026-10-17)
Scores, first redesign (The Price Glass) → this redesign (The Say-It Slot): invention 4 → 5 · fun 5 → 5 · completeness 5 → 5 · correctness 5 → 5 · distinctness 4 → 5 · reality 5 → 5.

**Why the Price Glass was replaced.** Its band-in-a-glass ("a coin melts into a band as tall as its worth") is a *magnitude* model of money — the same idea as new 270's Wallet Bar, where coins unroll into a bar as long as their worth. The previous review admitted it ("value-as-a-magnitude is shared with 270 … the hidden line is the invention, not the band") and capped invention and distinctness at 4. There was a second, quieter flaw: the glass did the adding. The child dropped coins and the bands stacked themselves; the arithmetic happened in the apparatus, and the child only judged a height — so the skill the standard names, counting on coin values, was never in the child's hand.

**What replaces it.** The redesign removes every magnitude: no band, no bar, no scale, no running total. The only number on the stage is the one the child turns. The invention is a **counting-on lock**: the slot accepts a coin only when the counter already reads what the box will hold with that coin in it, so each coin is a small checked act of counting on (35 … +10 … 45). Two more pieces of the same mechanism carry the other two misconceptions the operator named:
- the **price pin** makes overshoot physically impossible — the wheels clack and stop, so a too-big coin cannot be counted on and cannot go in;
- the **finite purse** makes "small coins first" honestly risky: measured, smallest-first is stranded in 3–4 of 8 level-1 rounds per family, biggest-first never, and in level 2 smallest-first always misses the fewest set. Nothing forbids small-first; it just runs out of road, as it does with a real purse.
"Counting coins instead of value" is refused coin by coin (a coins-as-ones player finishes 0 of 16 rounds in every family).

**Checked against the registry and the tools.** No registered invention is a money slot keyed to a counted-on total. The nearest machines either count for the child (row 92's lamp, the Money Mat tool), check a finished total (row 164's tag dial), or advance a board by one hop (row 141's flip pads). Row 51's lock opens on a syllable combination; nothing there is counted. The four sibling money rows now use four different models: 164 sorts by worth, 165 is a counting-on lock, 166 is conservation, 270 is length.

**Correctness.** Every table was regenerated and measured by `work-s165/m165.js` with errors 0: real coins per country (nl/fi no 1/2 c, es whole pesos, pt no 1 centavo, sv no 20 kr coin, no no 2 kr coin, kroner in da/no/sv); every L1 price made at least two ways from a purse with spare coins, with no coin equal to the price; every L2 fewest set unique; L3 gaps varied, with an overshoot coin in each add-purse wherever the coin set allows; compare R7/R8 balanced in all 8 families. All eight old level-1 targets per locale are kept. Three values changed, each for a stated reason: NOK L2 7 → 17 (no 2 kr coin, so there was no real alternative to the fewest set), and the USD/EURR/BRL top-ups (so the "how much more" answers no longer repeat).

**Fun and reality stay at 5.** The box is a tactile, sound-optional toy with an immediate answer to every coin, the pin is a thing children bang on purpose, the treat drawer is the earned show, and the parade replays the child's own count. It works at 704/360/320 with no Check button, the box and cloth sit above the fold, the pictures are opened library treats, and the coins are each country's real coins at their real sizes.
