## 1 Page concept (base)

**"Tag the plant."** Gardeners put a tag on every plant; here the child writes the NAME TAGS. One plant (600 px tall, the whole left of the page) grows in a see-through soil box, so the roots are as visible as the flower: the concept every K-3 misconception list starts with ("plants have no roots / roots are just strings") is answered by the apparatus itself. Six coral tags (1-6) hang on threads from the six parts; each thread ends in a small teal ring round the exact part (one seed, not the pod; the pod tip, not a leaf). On the right a cream label card holds six numbered writing rows. The child reads a tag number, looks where its thread goes, and writes that part's name on the row with the same number, choosing from the six-word bank on top.

Why this over the obvious leader-lane diagram (argued in §8): the tags leave the plant UNCOVERED and the writing all in one tidy card (one hand position, one column to check for the teacher); the plant keeps 320 px of width instead of the 270 a two-sided lane layout leaves (K-354 measured 195 + 7 + 270 + 7 + 195); and the plant is botanically complete (flower AND fruit AND seed on one plant, which a bean or pea plant genuinely shows at the same time: new flowers keep opening while earlier pods ripen), so the 6-part G1 set (NGSS 1-LS1-1, BNCC EF02CI06 "raiz, caule, folhas, flores e frutos") is honest without a second picture. Top quality = one focal apparatus, generous margins, everything the child writes in one card, coral used ONLY for the tags (the "look here" signal).

## 8 Two alternatives + recommendation

1. **The labelled diagram with lanes (the obvious one).** A plant in the middle, three writing lanes each side, leader lines, bank on top (the K-354 grammar). Rejected: it is visually the body figure with a plant in it (the two families blur on the hub and across the room); two lane columns leave the plant ~270 px wide, so the seed and pod shrink below reading size; six leaders fanning from a narrow stem cross the leaves they do not point at.
2. **The herbarium sheet.** The plant pressed flat on a botanist's sheet with tape strips and a specimen label in the corner. Charming and editorial, and I kept its best idea (one label card instead of lanes), but rejected the conceit: a pressed plant has no soil, and "roots grow underground" is the first thing the K-2 plant unit teaches. The root-view box keeps the roots visible AND underground.

**Recommendation: the root-view plant with garden tags.** One apparatus reused by five of six faces (full plant, stages, pot, section, part icons all from one file, so the whole family looks drawn by one hand), coral used only as the pointer, a single writing column, and a botanically complete plant (flower + fruit + seed on one stem) that makes the 6-part G1 label set honest.

## 9 Risks, mitigations, print check

- **Thread ambiguity** (the seed thread crosses the pod skin; stamen/pistil threads cross back petals in the section): each thread ends in a ring round ONE part and the gate proves point-in-shape; a human must still look at the 20-seed sweep at 100 % zoom. Only a human sees "which does this point at" as a child does.
- **Long locales:** bank one row except possibly de with articles (still 718 <= 722); job sentences capped at 70 chars by the validator (2 lines); chip words capped at 11 chars; fi 4-line title leaves 677 and every stack here is <= 669 except face b at its 104-row setting (rows are `minmax(96px,1fr)`, so 636 at 677).
- **Greyscale:** the only dark fills are teal (seeds, anthers, roots, outlines); coral tags print mid-grey with a white numeral (the water-cycle marker precedent); tealSoft leaves vs white petals separate by shape and outline, never by hue alone. Nothing on any face is identified by colour.
- **Pencil space:** base rows 199 × 58 with glyphH 30 (G1); numeral boxes 60 × 52 (face c) and 52 × 48 (face d) hold a 26 px numeral.
- **Botany a human must sign off:** the pod-as-fruit (children expect "fruit = apple"; the landing and the panel's word choice carry it: pt "fruto", not "fruta"); exclusions of celery/corn/potato; the flower-becomes-fruit claim in face c (true: the ovary becomes the fruit).
- **The seed is 14 px** at h 600 (under the G1 44 px element floor). It is a pointed-at part, not a target the child marks; if the critic rules it too small, raise the seed r to 8 and the pod width to 34 (the gate re-checks clearances).
