## The skill
Compose complete, true sentences about a picture with a starter and a word bank.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE CAPTION THAT CHECKS ITSELF AGAINST THE PICTURE. A scene grid (apparatus: a floor and a wall) holds 3–4 library objects in placed relations (a cat on a bed, a ball under a chair). Below, a caption strip and word tiles: starters, nouns from the word bank (with their article from LCSGrammar), position tiles (on, under, next to, in front of) and an end mark. The child builds a sentence; the tiles snap into the only grammatical order frames the engine allows (prepSentence prefix · expected · suffix), so word order and articles are always right — the child's job is the MEANING. Tap Read: each named object lights when it is named and the relation is tested against the scene. True: the caption fills one of three lines on the caption card. False: the named object wobbles at its real place and the false tile lifts. A sentence without a position tile or an end mark does not light. Three different true sentences fill the card; a repeat is refused.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Writing a list of words instead of a sentence, or writing something not in the picture.
- **Pictures:** around the house / furniture / pets / toys placed by the apparatus in a scene grid
- **Settings:** Starters: given / free choice · Sentences: 2 / 3 per picture
- **Rounds:** 9 scenes built from library objects in randomized relations, each with ≥ 4 different true sentences; the word bank shuffled; no single answer to copy.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G2-278, G2-299, G2-300
- **Distinct from:** Wiggles' Sentence Builder unscrambles a GIVEN sentence; Bramble's Parking Tower places vehicles by position words. This composes original sentences and checks them for truth.
- **Build risk:** fi postpositions and de dative (handled by LCSGrammar, with its refusals); G2-300 free writing is not reproduced — the activity composes from tiles.
