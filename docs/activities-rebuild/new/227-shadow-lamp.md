## The skill
Match a picture to its outline (shadow), and recognise a whole picture from a small part of it.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE BEAM THAT DRAWS THE OUTLINE. A wall carries 3–4 dark silhouettes; a lamp stands at the side. The child drags a library picture into the lamp's beam; the picture's real shadow is cast and slides onto the wall over the silhouette it is dropped near. If it is the same shape the shadow sinks exactly into it (edges vanish); if not, the mismatched edges stay bright where shadow and silhouette disagree — the child SEES what differs (ears, tail, handle). Part-to-whole setting: the lamp is a narrow spotlight that shows only a part of a hidden picture on the wall; the child drags the whole picture into the beam and the lit part must coincide.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Matching by colour or by one feature (any four-legged animal for any four-legged shadow).
- **Pictures:** animals, farm animals, kitchen tools, vehicles, toys (silhouettes generated from the same library picture by alpha mask — no new art)
- **Settings:** Mode: whole shadow / part only · Look-alikes: easy (different kinds) / close (same family: cat–dog–fox)
- **Rounds:** 9 rounds; silhouette positions shuffled, the right one in each wall slot ≤ 40 %; "close" rounds always include a same-family distractor; one right answer.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** K-057, K-069
- **Distinct from:** No activity matches outline to object; the Peek Window hides and shows a GROUP for counting, not a part of one picture for identification.
- **Build risk:** Silhouettes from alpha masks must survive pictures whose webp has a white (non-transparent) background — check the library alpha per noun.
