## The skill
Predict and test whether things float or sink; see that size or weight alone does not decide, that shape can make clay float, and draw a conclusion from a record of tests.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE TUB THAT TELLS THE TRUTH. A clear tub of water (apparatus) with a surface line and a sand floor. The child predicts by dragging a library object to the surface band or to the floor; on release it falls in, splashes and settles where it really goes — bobbing at the surface or resting on the floor, sliding away from the predicted place if the prediction was wrong. The prediction stays as a faint ghost beside the result, so the child compares. BALANCE setting (G1-408): two objects first sit on an apparatus balance that tips to the heavier; then both go in — the heavy log floats, the light key sinks. CLAY setting (G2-382): a grey clay lump (geometry) with a shape slider ball → flat → bowl → boat; only the hollow boat floats, though the clay is the same. RECORD setting (G3-400, merged test-tank): a question card ("Do heavy things always sink?"), six objects predicted and tested; each fills a row of a record card (prediction · result · match mark); then the child picks the conclusion the record supports from three — a conclusion the data contradicts is shown crossed against the row that disproves it.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** Big things sink, small things float; heavy = sinks without exception.
- **Pictures:** Float: camping (log), fruits (apple, lemon), toys (ball, boat, balloon), kitchen-tools (sponge), miscellaneous (leaf, pinecone), at-the-supermarket (water bottle, empty with cap). Sink: around-the-house (key, hammer, spoon if metal), tools (nail, bolt, hammer), beach (rock), accessories (ring). Refused: acorn (varies), grape, plastic spoon, coin (no picture).
- **Settings:** Mode: predict and test / balance first / shape the clay / record and conclude · Objects: everyday / tricky (big floater vs small sinker)
- **Rounds:** 9 objects per pass, floaters and sinkers balanced, ≥ 3 tricky pairs; record rounds: 7 object sets of 6, each chosen so the naive rule (heavy sinks / big sinks) fails at least once; conclusion options shuffled; one right answer from a reviewed float/sink table.
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-204, G1-399, G1-408, G2-382, G2-383, G3-400, K-384
- **Distinct from:** No sink/float activity exists.
- **Build risk:** Every object's behaviour must be checked against its PICTURE (the spoon must look metal; the bottle must look empty and capped) — the G1-204 plastic-spoon defect is the precedent.
- **Grade span (settings):** K (record setting: 3)

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 1 "The Float Tank" (float-tank): Predict, then test, whether an object floats or sinks; heavy does not mean it sinks. — A WATER TANK WITH A PREDICTION LINE: the child drags an object to the shelf above the tank and first sets the prediction marker (top half = float, bottom = sink), then lets go. The object falls in and settles: floats at the surface or rests on the bottom. A tally board records predicted vs real. Balance setting: two objects on a scale first show which is heavier; then both go in the tank, and the heavier one floats (log vs coin) to break the "heavy sinks" idea.
- grade 2 "The Water Tank — Sink or Float?" (water-tank): Predict and test whether objects sink or float, and see that changing a shape (a clay ball into a boat) can make it float. — A glass water tank with a prediction shelf (sink side / float side). The child sorts objects onto the shelf, then drops each in: it sinks to the floor or bobs at the surface (true to the material). Clay mode: a clay lump with a shape slider — ball → flat → bowl → boat; the child shapes it and drops it; only the hollow boat floats, though the clay is the same.
- grade 3 "The Test Tank" (test-tank): Run a fair test: predict, test, record, and draw a conclusion about why things sink or float. — THE TEST TANK: a glass tank of water and a shelf of library objects. A question card is chosen ('do heavy things always sink?'). The child drags each object onto a prediction board (sink / float column), then drops it in the tank: it sinks or floats, and a record card fills in the 'what happened' column beside the prediction with a match/mismatch mark. After 6 objects the child picks the conclusion that the record supports from three; a conclusion the data contradicts is shown crossed against the row that disproves it (a big log floats).
