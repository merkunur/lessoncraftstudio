## The skill
Tell pets, farm animals and wild animals apart, and match animals to their homes.

## The apparatus and its invention
THE ANIMAL THAT WALKS TO ITS HOME. A landscape strip of three or four library place pictures standing like signposts (PETS–FARM–WILD: house · haystack · forest; HABITATS: forest · lake · sea · snowy mountain; HOMES: nest · birdhouse · house · water). The child drags an animal to a place; on release it walks there. Right: it settles in front of its place and three need badges above it (food · water · shelter, apparatus icons) fill. Miss 1: it stops, one or more badges stay empty, it turns and walks back; Miss 2: its real place gives a small wave. WHO-DOES-NOT-LIVE-HERE mode (G1-406): a place holds four animals, one wrong — tap the one whose badges are empty. WHAT-IT-EATS mode (G1-206, G1-407): drag the animal onto plants / animals / both. ADAPT mode (G2-380): a feature card (a written sentence: thick fur, fins, long neck) is dropped on the animal it describes; one animal is left over.

- **Misconception the apparatus makes visible:** All animals live on farms; a fish in a nest; wild = dangerous.
- **Pictures:** Animals: animals, farm-animals, pets, forest-creatures, ocean-life, zoo-animals, birds, winter (penguin, seal, walrus). Places: miscellaneous (house), thanksgivinng (haystack), camping (forest, lake, river, mountain), beach (water, island), winter (icicle as the snowy sign), spring (nest, birdhouse, garden). MISSING: beehive, kennel, burrow, desert, barn.
- **Settings:** Mode: pet–farm–wild / animal homes · Animals: 6 / 9
- **Rounds:** 9 animals per pass, places balanced, place layout fixed per mode (the place is the content); one right place from a reviewed table (rabbit, parrot, ferret refused in pet/wild — cultural).
- **Worksheet types it covers:** G1-202, G1-206, G1-398, G1-406, G1-407, G2-380, G2-381, K-204, K-209, K-383
- **Distinct from:** No habitat activity exists; Mother and Baby is offspring.
- **Build risk:** Desert animals (camel) are refused until a desert place picture exists. Pet vs farm vs wild decided per locale by the reviewer.

## Merged into this instrument (cover each as a setting or round set)
- grade 1 "The Habitat Map" (habitat-needs): Animals live where their needs are met: food, water and shelter; each habitat suits some animals and not others. — A FOLDING LANDSCAPE: a strip of habitats (pond, forest, ocean, desert, arctic, farm). Each animal dragged into a habitat shows three need badges above it (food, water, shelter) that fill if the habitat provides them; in the wrong habitat one or more stay empty and the animal looks to the habitat where it belongs. The "who does not live here" setting fills a habitat with four animals, one wrong, and the child taps the one whose badges are empty. The "what it eats" setting asks the child to drag the animal's food into its shelter (plants / other animals / both).
- grade 2 "The Habitat Diorama — Who Lives Here?" (habitat-diorama): Match animals and plants to the habitat they live in and the body feature that helps them live there. — Three habitat dioramas side by side (ocean, desert, arctic/forest/pond — built from library backgrounds and pictures). Animal cards are dragged into a diorama; a right fit makes the animal settle and its helpful feature flashes (thick fur, fins, hump, webbed feet); a wrong fit makes it shiver/overheat/gasp with a small icon and slide back. Adaptation mode: a feature card ("has thick fur and small ears") is dropped on the animal it describes, one animal left over.
